## Summary
Evan Watt teaches on the doctrine of the church and the body of Christ, addressing the false doctrine held by many King James Bible colleges that the church is only local and never universal. Through careful examination of Scripture and proper context, he demonstrates that the body of Christ is a spiritual body encompassing all believers worldwide, entered through spiritual baptism by the Holy Spirit, not water baptism.

## Scripture
Ephesians 4:4-6 — "There is one body and one spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling; one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all who is above all and through all in you all."

## Key Themes
- The body of Christ is spiritual and universal, not merely local
- Proper word usage and context are essential to understanding Scripture
- The church began with the revelation of the mystery to Paul, not at Pentecost
- Spiritual baptism by the Holy Spirit, not water baptism, enters believers into the body of Christ
- Unity in the one body of Christ is central to God's design
- Right division of Scripture is necessary for proper doctrine

## Notes

### Word Use and Definition
- The Greek word *ekklesia* (church) means "called out assembly" but context determines specific meaning
- The word "church" appears in different contexts: the nation of Israel in the wilderness (Acts 7:38), local assemblies, and the universal body of Christ (Colossians 1:18)
- False teachers deliberately use "universal church" mockingly, associating it with Catholicism
- The body of Christ is unique to Paul's epistles and is often avoided by those who deny its existence

### Church Origin
- Matthew 16:18 refers to the kingdom church under the gospel of the kingdom, in the future tribulation period—not the church which is the body of Christ
- The "local church only" doctrine teaches Baptist perpetuity: that Jesus planted the first independent fundamental Baptist church during His earthly ministry and has preserved it continuously
- The body of Christ began when the mystery was revealed to Paul (Ephesians 3)
- The mystery revealed that Gentiles would be "fellow heirs and of the same body" through the gospel (Ephesians 3:6)
- Matthew 16 is under the dispensation of the kingdom; Ephesians is under the dispensation of grace—they cannot be the same church

### Baptism and Entry into the Body
- Water baptism is not required to be baptized into the body of Christ
- Romans 6:3 describes spiritual baptism into Christ's death and resurrection, not water baptism
- 1 Corinthians 12:13 clearly states: "By one Spirit are we all baptized into one body"
- God baptizes believers into the body of Christ instantly when they believe
- Water baptism today is a picture and testimony of salvation, not a means of entry into the body
- The "one baptism" of Ephesians 4:5 refers to the spiritual baptism that matters in the current dispensation

### The Problem of Local-Church-Only Doctrine
- This false doctrine creates disunity by claiming believers in different locations are not part of one body
- It contradicts Ephesians 4:3-6, which emphasizes unity in one body
- It makes no sense of practices like reading missionary letters, praying for distant believers, and rejoicing with those far away
- If the body is only local, believers have no real connection to the rest of God's people worldwide

### Application
- Believers must work to keep unity in the one body of Christ
- Give up non-essential matters to maintain peace and unity
- Resolve conflicts peacefully as members of one body
- Recognize that the body needs to be "fitly joined together"
