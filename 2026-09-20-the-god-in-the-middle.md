## Summary
Youth Pastor Andrew Watt draws on a harrowing car accident during his vacation in Tennessee — and three biblical accounts — to remind believers that God is never absent in the middle of life's trials. Whether in a fiery furnace, a storm-tossed sea, or the chaos of everyday struggle, God is present, active, and sufficient. The sermon culminates in the image of Christ hanging on the middle cross, completing salvation for all who trust in Him.

## Scripture
Daniel 3:19-25 — Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego are cast into the fiery furnace by Nebuchadnezzar, yet a fourth figure — like the Son of God — is seen walking with them in the midst of the fire.

Mark 6:45-51 — Jesus sees His disciples toiling and rowing against the wind in the middle of the sea and comes to them, walking on the water.

Mark 4:35-41 — A great storm threatens to sink the disciples' boat while Jesus sleeps; He rebukes the wind and waves, then challenges their lack of faith.

John 19:16-18 — Jesus is crucified between two others, placed in the midst.

Colossians 2:10-14 — Believers are complete in Christ, whose death on the cross blotted out the record of sin against them.

## Key Themes
- God's presence in the middle of trials, whether self-caused or not
- Providence seen in small, unexpected details (a tree, cell reception, a cancelled Turo car, a student nearby)
- Faith versus fear — the disciples' panic contrasted with God's calm sovereignty
- The sin-cursed flesh versus the saved soul and spirit
- Christ on the middle cross as the ultimate expression of God meeting humanity in its need
- The world watching believers in the fire, just as Nebuchadnezzar watched the Hebrew boys

## Notes
**Opening illustration — car accident in Tennessee:**
- Pastor Watt and his wife drove off a mountain road near Sevierville, TN during a vacation in the Smoky Mountains.
- Their car was totaled due to frame damage, but both were unharmed.
- A tree stopped the car from rolling further — though it damaged the frame, it prevented a far worse outcome.
- They had unusually strong cell reception at the exact spot of the accident, enabling them to call for help.
- No rental cars were available within 50–100 miles due to a NASCAR race and a major car show in Pigeon Forge.
- God provided: hotel rewards points covered lodging during a sold-out weekend; a Turo BMW reservation led unexpectedly to a suitable Jeep; Zach Simpson (a church member attending Crown College, 16 minutes from the car return location) drove Pastor Watt partway home; a cousin met him in Kentucky; his mother drove him the rest of the way to Michigan, arriving home at 5:35 the same day as the Sunday evening service.

**Point 1 — God in the fiery furnace (Daniel 3):**
- Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego were thrown bound into a furnace heated seven times hotter than normal.
- It was not the Hebrew boys who observed God's presence — it was the pagan king Nebuchadnezzar.
- Application: the world is watching believers in the fire; our testimony and God's presence are visible to those outside the faith.
- Even in the worst trials — those that may alter life forever — believers need not wonder where God is. He is right in the midst.

**Point 2 — God meeting the disciples in the storm (Mark 6 & 4):**
- In Mark 6, Jesus sees His disciples toiling and rowing from the shore and comes to them on the water.
- In Mark 4, Jesus is asleep in the boat — so unconcerned by the storm that He is resting — yet able to rebuke it instantly.
- Analogy: Pastor Watt learned in the NICU (his daughter was born prematurely and spent weeks there) to watch the nurses' reactions rather than panic at alarms — the nurses' calm was reassuring. Similarly, Jesus' sleep in the storm should have been the disciples' cue that there was no ultimate danger.
- Many believers are toiling and rowing in health struggles, parenting, marriage, work, and relationships — feeling they are going nowhere. God sees them and will come to meet them.
- His daughter, now almost five years old and saved, is a testimony to God's rescue. A cousin was also in the NICU for 168 days and survived.

**Point 3 — Christ on the middle cross (John 19; Colossians 2):**
- Jesus was crucified in the midst — between two sinners, before sinners, murdered by sinners.
- Colossians 2:10-14: believers are complete in Christ. The circumcision made without hands speaks of the soul and spirit being separated from the sin-cursed body at salvation — no longer bound to die with it.
- Believers should not be surprised or despairing when the flesh still wars — Romans 7 tension ("O wretched man that I am") is real, but salvation is secure.
- The darkness of the world — evil, war, violence, lust, deceit — does not catch God off guard. When Christ hung on the cross, the world literally went dark. He is not surprised by sin.
- Closing reminder: you don't have to doubt God because the world is dark, your strength is weak, your sins are great, or your faith is small. You are complete in Christ because He died for you right in the middle.

**Closing:**
- Altar call extended to any who are not saved.
- Pastor Watt acknowledged this was not the sermon he had planned to preach upon returning from vacation; the experience itself became the message.
