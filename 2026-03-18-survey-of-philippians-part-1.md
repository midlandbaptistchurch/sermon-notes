## Summary
Pastor Jim begins a survey of Philippians by establishing the historical context of Paul's second missionary journey to Philippi and the founding of the church there. He emphasizes that Philippians teaches about unity in the body of Christ, contrasting the sterile doctrinal nature of Ephesians with the personal, affectionate tone of Philippians.

## Scripture
Philippians 1:1 — Paul addresses the saints, bishops, and deacons at Philippi, showing the proper structure of a local church.
Acts 16 — The founding of the Philippian church through Paul's ministry to Lydia, the slave girl, and the Philippian jailer.

## Key Themes
- The importance of rightly dividing Scripture (2 Timothy 2:15)
- The seven-fold unity of the body of Christ (Ephesians 4:4-6)
- The proper structure of local churches (bishops, deacons, saints)
- The personal, affectionate nature of Paul's relationship with the Philippian church
- Unity versus denominationalism in Christianity

## Notes

### Historical Background
- Paul visited Philippi around 52-53 AD on his second missionary journey
- Philippians was written around 62-63 AD while Paul was imprisoned in Rome
- The book has 4 chapters, 104 verses, and 2,183 words
- Philippi was named after Philip II of Macedonia, father of Alexander the Great

### The Church Trilogy
Pastor Jim identifies three related books that deal with the church:
- **His body** (the universal body of Christ)
- **The congregation** (local churches)
- Philippians fits into this framework as addressing unity within the body

### Paul's Second Missionary Journey to Philippi
- Paul was forbidden by the Holy Spirit from going into Asia or Bithynia
- He received the Macedonian vision at Troas
- First converts: Lydia (seller of purple), the slave girl with divination spirit, and the Philippian jailer
- These became the foundational members of the church

### The Seven-fold Unity (Ephesians 4:4-6)
1. One body
2. One Spirit
3. One hope
4. One Lord
5. One faith
6. One baptism
7. One God and Father

### Church Structure
From Philippians 1:1, the proper makeup of a local church includes:
- **Saints** (all believers)
- **Bishops** (overseers of ministry)
- **Deacons** (servants who assist leadership)

### Rightly Dividing Scripture
- 99% of Christians are confused because they don't rightly divide Scripture
- Confusion comes from not understanding the distinction between what applies to Israel, Gentiles, and the body of Christ
- Paul is God's spokesman for this dispensation (Romans 11:13)
