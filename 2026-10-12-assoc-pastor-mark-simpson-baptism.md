## Summary
Pastor Mark addresses the controversial topic of water baptism, arguing that while it was commanded for Israel during the gospels and early Acts, it is not a requirement for believers in the church age. He emphasizes that the confession of sins in John's baptism was collective national repentance, not individual confession of every sin. The sermon distinguishes between water baptism (a Jewish sign) and spirit baptism (placement into the body of Christ), and shows how Romans 6 uses baptism metaphorically to teach believers about dying to sin and walking in newness of life—the real application of baptism for today.

## Scripture
Matthew 3:1-17 — John the Baptist preaches repentance, baptizes people who confess their sins, and baptizes Jesus who came up "out of the water."

Joel 2:28-31 — Prophecy of the Holy Spirit being poured out, extending through the tribulation period.

Romans 5:12-21 — Adam's sin brought death to all; Christ's obedience brings life, with grace abounding over sin.

Romans 6:1-6 — Believers are baptized into Christ's death metaphorically, buried with him, and raised to walk in newness of life.

Genesis 22:1-14 — Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac pictures Christ's substitutionary death.

## Key Themes
- **Confession in John's Baptism**: The confession of sins during John's baptism was a collective national repentance for Israel turning away from God, not an itemized list of individual sins.
- **Water Baptism vs. Spirit Baptism**: Water baptism was a Jewish sign during the transition period; spirit baptism is the placement of believers into the body of Christ at salvation with no water involved.
- **Baptism Not Commanded for Today**: Paul said "Christ sent me not to baptize" (1 Corinthians 1:17), and there is no clear command in scripture for water baptism by immersion in the body of Christ.
- **Romans 10:9-10 Context**: Confessing with the mouth was specifically for Israel turning from law to grace, not a universal requirement for salvation which is by belief alone.
- **Death, Burial, Resurrection Not Yet Revealed**: Jesus' baptism could not have pictured death, burial, and resurrection since this gospel had not yet been revealed during His earthly ministry.
- **Romans 6 Application**: Baptism imagery in Romans 6 teaches sanctification—dying to the flesh and being raised to walk in newness of life—not water baptism.
- **Satan's Deception**: Satan did not understand that killing Christ would result in His resurrection and victory over death, hell, and the grave (1 Corinthians 2:8).

## Notes

### Confession of Sins in Matthew 3
- People were baptized "confessing their sins" (Matthew 3:6)
- This was not confessing every individual sin (which would be impossible to remember them all)
- The confession was collective/national—Israel as a nation turning from their rebellion and accepting John's message about the coming Messiah
- They were separating themselves from the rest of the nation that rejected the message
- This ties into Israel's specific situation, not a pattern for today

### Romans 10:9-10 and Confession
- "If thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved" (Romans 10:9)
- Context: Paul is speaking specifically to Israel (Romans 10:1 - "my heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they might be saved")
- Israel had to confess/turn from the law (which was right but is now wrong) to grace (which is now right)
- This is not about praying a prayer to be saved
- For the body of Christ: Ephesians 1:13 gives the pattern—hearing, understanding, trusting, believing, then sealed—no confession required

### Water Baptism and Jesus
- Matthew 3:16: Jesus "went up straightway out of the water"
- People assume this means He went under the water (immersion)
- But death, burial, and resurrection had not been revealed yet at this point in time
- The Holy Spirit was prophesied to be "poured out" (Joel 2:28-31), suggesting pouring rather than immersion
- There is no place in the Bible that explicitly commands baptism by immersion
- The Greek word for baptism means "to be identified with," not "to immerse"

### Paul and Baptism
- Paul said "Christ sent me not to baptize" (1 Corinthians 1:17)
- Paul baptized some people but was glad he hadn't baptized more
- If Paul stopped healing, raising the dead, speaking in tongues, and doing miracles, he also stopped emphasizing baptism
- There is no clear command for believers today to be water baptized

### Circumcision and Baptism Parallel
- No one argues Christians should be circumcised today
- Timothy got circumcised to reach the Jews (transitional accommodation)
- Titus refused to be circumcised to make the point it wasn't required (both were right because it was during the transition)
- Similarly, baptism was part of the Jewish system and transitional period
- The question is: where does it transfer to everyone else? (Answer: it doesn't as a requirement)

### Romans 6 and Spiritual Baptism
- Romans 6:3-5 uses baptism as a metaphor for sanctification
- Believers are already saved and in the body of Christ when Paul writes this
- "Baptized into his death...buried with him by baptism into death...raised up from the dead...walk in newness of life"
- This pictures dying to the flesh and being raised to live righteously
- This is what should be taught to new believers—not getting wet, but living out the baptism metaphor by making right choices
- Verse 2: "How shall we that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?"
- The emphasis is on practical sanctification—telling sin "no" and choosing righteousness

### Baptism as a "Sacred Cow"
- To Baptists, water baptism is extremely important (a "sacred cow")
- Most believe in two "church ordinances": baptism and the Lord's Supper
- But the Bible speaks of ordinances without clearly establishing baptism as an ordinance for the body of Christ
- The text of the Bible in context is the ultimate authority, not tradition or denominational teaching
- People often defend baptism because they want to believe it so badly, rather than following what scripture actually says

### Satan's Pride and Madness
- 1 Corinthians 2:8: "Which none of the princes of this world knew: for had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory"
- Satan wanted to eliminate the Messiah to prevent Him from becoming King
- He didn't realize that killing Jesus would lead to resurrection and defeat of death, hell, and the grave
- Satan's pride and corruption prevented him from seeing God's plan
- Job 1:6-12: Satan came to present himself before the Lord with the angels, accusing Job
- Revelation 12:10: Satan is "the accuser of our brethren...which accused them before our God day and night"
- Satan has been running his mouth since before Genesis 1:2, in his madness continuing to oppose God despite knowing the outcome

### Genesis 22 and Christ's Sacrifice
- Abraham was commanded to sacrifice Isaac, his only son (Genesis 22:2)
- Isaac was likely about 33 years old (same age as Christ at crucifixion), not a child
- Isaac willingly allowed himself to be bound and placed on the altar
- Abraham said "God will provide himself a lamb" (Genesis 22:8)
- God stopped Abraham and provided a ram caught in the thicket
- This pictures God's original plan: Christ would be sacrificed in the temple
- Because of Israel's rebellion, He was crucified instead, but Jesus knew this would happen

### Death Reigning from Adam
- Romans 5:12: "By one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin"
- Romans 5:17-19: Adam's offense brought death; Christ's obedience brings life
- Christ dealt with sin past, present, and future for all mankind
- Even for Israel, they would still need to go through the tribulation period, enter the kingdom under the new covenant
- After Satan's release and final rebellion, the great white throne judgment, then new heaven and new earth

### Transition Period Difficulties
- Timothy got circumcised; Titus did not—both were right during the transition
- Similarly with baptism—it was practiced during the transition but is not commanded for today
- All transitional things are difficult to navigate
- The key is understanding what applies to which dispensation
