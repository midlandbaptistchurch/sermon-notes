## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on dealing with temptation by emphasizing the need for a purposeful plan to anticipate and overcome temptation. He explores both internal deceptions (self-deception) and external threats (false teachers and bad doctrine), drawing on biblical warnings from Paul about being aware of our vulnerabilities and the schemes of our adversary.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There is no temptation taking you but such as is common to man; but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it."

## Key Themes
- The importance of having a plan to deal with temptation
- Guarding against self-deception
- Recognizing false teachers and doctrinal error
- Knowing yourself and your spiritual enemy
- The danger of being deceived by smooth talk and false doctrine
- Holding fast to sound doctrine
- Studying to show yourself approved unto God

## Notes

### The Need for Intentional Planning
- Temptation often creeps up subtly; we must preview the temptation risks in our lives
- God promises to provide a way to escape temptation, but we must be alert and prepared
- Many Christians lack an orderly, analytical approach to dealing with temptation

### Self-Deception: The Internal Threat
- "Let no man deceive himself" (1 Corinthians 3:18)
- We are often our own worst deceiver
- Temptation to be ashamed of being tempted is itself a form of deception
- Inordinate ambition—desire for power and personal satisfaction rather than serving God—can lead us astray
- We must balance healthy ambition with humility and realistic self-assessment
- Galatians 6:3 warns against thinking too highly of ourselves

### External Deception: False Teachers and Bad Doctrine
- Satan disguises himself as an angel of light; his ministers do the same (2 Corinthians 11)
- False teachers may operate out of ignorance or deliberate deception
- Some teach truth mixed with wrong doctrine (like a diamond in a silicon setting)
- We must "rightly divide" Scripture to separate truth from error
- Romans 16:17 warns against those who cause divisions through contrary doctrine
- Ephesians 4:14 describes being "carried about with every wind of doctrine by the sleight of men"
- Many false teachers are themselves deceived but won't admit error due to pride, reputation, or financial stake

### The Importance of Sound Doctrine
- Titus 1:9 emphasizes holding fast to the faithful word as taught
- Young pastors should seek training that teaches sound doctrine
- Both academic understanding and practical application of Scripture are essential
- Being a Christian leader means being an example of how to live according to God's Word
- "Study to show thyself approved unto God" means both rightly dividing Scripture and living it out

### Strategic Awareness
- Sun Tzu's principle applies spiritually: "If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the results of a hundred battles"
- We must understand our own vulnerabilities and the nature of spiritual opposition
- Many Christians struggle in marriages and relationships because they fail to study and prepare themselves
