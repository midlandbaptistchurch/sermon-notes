## Summary
Drawing from John 9, the account of a man born blind whom Jesus healed, Craig McKinley explores how God's plans are sovereignly orchestrated long before we encounter our circumstances — and how questioning and reasoning can undermine our ability to simply trust Him. The sermon calls believers to move past doubt and to respond as the healed man did: with plain, humble faith — "Lord, I believe."

## Scripture
John 9 (with background from John 8) — The account of a man born blind who is healed by Jesus, then questioned by neighbors, his parents, and the Pharisees, before ultimately confessing faith in the Son of God.

## Key Themes
- God's sovereign providence over our circumstances
- The danger of questioning and reasoning away what God is doing
- Simple, childlike faith over intellectual doubt
- God's glory being made manifest through trials
- The cost of belief and social pressure to deny Christ

## Notes
- **Context in John 8–9:** Jesus has just confronted the Jewish religious leaders, who pick up stones to kill him. While fleeing through the crowd, he passes by the man born blind — a moment Craig emphasizes was no coincidence but divinely orchestrated.
- **God's long-range plan:** The healed man is estimated to be at least 20–25 years old (his parents say he is "of age," John 9:21). Craig points out that God's plan for this man's healing was set in motion decades before — a reminder that God knows our circumstances long before we face them.
- **The miracle itself:** Jesus spits on the ground, makes clay, anoints the man's eyes, and tells him to wash in the Pool of Siloam (rediscovered archaeologically in 2004, about a quarter mile from the temple). The man obeys without question and returns seeing.
- **Reactions of doubt:** Neighbors, the man's parents, and the Pharisees each respond with questions rather than wonder. The Pharisees ultimately conclude the man was never blind at all, and cast him out of the synagogue — a severe punishment involving social, economic, and personal loss.
- **Being cast out of the synagogue** in that culture meant exclusion from religious life, community shunning, loss of income, family, and potential death — explaining the parents' fearful response.
- **The final encounter:** Jesus seeks the man out after he is cast out and asks, "Dost thou believe on the Son of God?" The man's answer is simply: "Lord, I believe" (John 9:38).
- **Alistair Begg illustration:** Craig plays a clip from Scottish preacher Alistair Begg about the thief on the cross — a man with no Bible study, no baptism, no doctrinal training — whose only basis for heaven is: "The man on the middle cross said I could go." The point: faith does not require exhaustive answers, only trust in Christ.
- **Key application:** When we go through difficult circumstances, we are not the only ones affected — God's glory and his work in the lives of many others are at stake. Rather than demanding explanations, believers are called to ask, "How do you want your name made manifest in this situation?"
- **Over 8,000 promises in Scripture** were referenced, with five read aloud: James 1:12, Galatians 6:9, Isaiah 26:4, Psalm 34:19, and Romans 4:21.
