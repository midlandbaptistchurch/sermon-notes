## Summary
Jeremy Huston challenges believers to take inventory of their lives and evaluate what truly matters in light of eternity. Using Paul's example from Philippians 3, he calls Christians to identify things worth discarding, departing from, dedicating themselves to, and even dying for. The central message is that our lives should count for Christ, not for temporal pursuits that will have no eternal value.

## Scripture
Philippians 3:1-16 — Paul reflects on his past credentials and accomplishments, then declares that he counts them all as loss compared to knowing Christ and pressing toward the goal of eternity with Him.

## Key Themes
- Recognizing what truly counts for eternity versus what is merely temporal
- Taking honest inventory of where we are spiritually
- Prioritizing what matters most in our relationship with God
- Forgetting both past failures and past accomplishments to focus forward
- Pressing toward the mark with fervency to finish strong
- Living for God's glory rather than personal achievement
- Stewardship of our lives, time, resources, and talents
- The reality of standing before the judgment seat of Christ

## Notes

**What Should Not Be Counted**
- Things that do not belong to us—our lives have been bought with a price and belong to God
- Vain pursuits that will fade away when we die
- Personal accomplishments and credentials as our source of worth
- Things that draw us away from Christ and eternity

**What Should Be Counted**
Paul demonstrates three categories of inventory:
- Things worthy of discarding: practices and possessions that don't matter for eternity
- Things worthy of departing from: not necessarily sinful but competing for priority with what matters most
- Things worthy of dedication: the Gospel, the church, witness, bearing fruit, and living for Christ

**Five Principles from Philippians 3:13 for Pursuing What Matters Most**

1. **Frame of Reference**: "I count not myself to have apprehended." Honest assessment of where we really are spiritually. There is always room for growth, and we should not settle thinking we've arrived.

2. **First Things First (Prioritizing)**: "This one thing I do." We must prioritize spiritual over carnal, eternal over temporal, heavenly over earthly. Our relationship with God must matter more than anything else.

3. **Forgetfulness**: "Forgetting those things which are behind." Release both past accomplishments (to avoid complacency) and past failures (to avoid condemnation). Focus on moving forward, not dwelling in regret.

4. **Forward Focus**: "Reaching forth into those things which are before." Always recognize there are greater heights in God to pursue. Maintain an eternal goal and perspective.

5. **Fervency to Finish Strong**: "I press toward the mark for the prize." Maintain singular focus on pleasing God and living for His high calling. Keep eternal values in view.

**Balancing Earthly and Eternal Life**
We fulfill our roles as parents, providers, and citizens while maintaining eternal priorities. Colossians 3:1-4 teaches us to set our affections on things above while living faithfully on earth. Godliness is profitable for both this life and the life to come.

**Making Life Count at Every Stage**
Whether young, middle-aged, or senior, we can make our lives count for Christ. The illustration of the man who abandoned plans to build a mountain house to establish the Jehovah Jireh Foundation demonstrates how redirecting resources toward God's kingdom created eternal impact—hundreds of churches planted and established with foundational support.

**The Ultimate Questions**
- Will this matter five seconds after I die?
- Does this count when I stand before the judgment seat of Christ?
- Am I living for eternity and its values, or only for temporal concerns?
- What is my life accomplishing for the cause of Christ?
