## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a study through Ephesians 5, focusing on verse 20 and the call to give thanks always. He argues that genuine thankfulness is inseparable from the peace of God, and that believers must cultivate the discipline of being thankful not only for blessings but also for the people God has placed in their lives.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:20 — "Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ." Examined alongside Colossians 3:14–17 and Philippians 4:6–9, which connect the peace of God with a life of gratitude.

## Key Themes
- Peace as the foundation for true thankfulness
- Gratitude in all circumstances, including suffering
- Being thankful for other members of the body of Christ
- Spiritual discipline and intentional prayer for others
- The Christian's responsibility to cultivate habits of gratitude

## Notes
**Peace and Thankfulness Are Inseparable**
- Colossians 3:15 links letting the peace of God rule in your heart directly with being thankful.
- You cannot manufacture genuine thankfulness without first having the peace of God.
- The peace of God is available to believers but must be actively received — it requires faith, trust, and saturation in the Word (Col. 3:16).

**Thankfulness in Suffering**
- Philippians 4:6–7: God's answer to prayer is often not the removal of difficulty but the granting of peace that passes understanding.
- Suffering may arise from: (1) our own choices (sowing and reaping), (2) the choices of others, or (3) God's chastening for our growth.
- Looking ahead to the judgment seat of Christ, believers will one day be thankful for trials endured with grace.

**Being Thankful for Others — A Pattern in Paul's Letters**
Simpson surveys six Pauline passages where Paul expresses thanks to God for specific people:
1. **Ephesians 1:16** — "Cease not to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers."
2. **Philippians 1:3** — "I thank my God upon every remembrance of you."
3. **Colossians 1:3** — "We give thanks to God and the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, praying for you always."
4. **1 Thessalonians 1:2** — "We give thanks to God alway for you all, making mention of you in our prayers."
5. **2 Thessalonians 1:3** — "We are bound to thank God always for you, brethren... because that your faith groweth exceedingly."
6. **Philemon 1:4** — "I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers."

Paul's consistency across letters and churches shows he had an intentional system for remembering and praying for people. Believers are called to develop a similar habit.

**Practical Discipline**
- Being thankful for others requires initiative and a plan — it does not happen automatically.
- Driving past someone's house, scrolling a church directory, or any moment of remembrance can become an opportunity to pray and give thanks.
- All believers are responsible for the full range of Christian duties (cf. Romans 12), not only those that come naturally to their personality.
- Introverts and extroverts alike must find ways to stay connected to and grateful for the body of Christ.
