## Summary
Pastor Simpson continues examining transitional passages in Paul's writings, showing how some instructions were specific to the transition period from law to grace and cannot be directly applied today. He demonstrates this through Romans 14 (dietary laws and observing days), 1 Corinthians 7 (marriage and singleness during "the present distress"), and 2 Corinthians 8-9 (the collection for poor saints in Jerusalem). While these passages contain transitional elements, they also reveal timeless grace principles about Christian liberty, stewardship, and the body of Christ.

## Scripture
Romans 14:1-23 — Paul addresses disputes over food and observing special days during the transition from law to grace, teaching believers not to judge one another over these matters while emphasizing that all will stand before God's judgment seat.

1 Corinthians 7:25-40 — Paul advises remaining single "for the present distress" and because "the time is short," giving permission rather than commandment during this specific transitional period.

2 Corinthians 8-9 — Paul explains the collection for the poor saints at Jerusalem (who had sold everything in preparation for the tribulation that didn't come), using the Macedonians' sacrificial giving as an example while teaching principles of cheerful, purposeful giving.

## Key Themes
- **Understanding Transitions**: Not everything in Paul's writings applies directly today because some instructions were given during the transition from law to grace when signs, wonders, and the "Jew first" priority were still operative.
- **Christian Liberty and Unity**: During transitions, believers at different levels of understanding should extend grace to one another rather than judging, as seen in the food and days controversy in Romans 14.
- **The Judgment Seat of Christ**: Both sides of a dispute will give account to God individually, making unity and avoiding stumbling blocks more important than being "right" on secondary matters.
- **Transitional vs. Universal Principles**: While 2 Corinthians 8-9 addresses a specific historical occasion (helping Jerusalem saints who sold everything), it contains universal principles about sowing and reaping, cheerful giving, and giving according to what one has.
- **Stewardship Enables Ministry**: Proper financial stewardship and living within one's means is necessary to be able to give when needs arise, rather than expecting God to bless poor financial decisions.

## Notes

### Introduction: The Challenge of Transitions
- Paul had to transition people away from teachings that were once right (the law, teachings of Christ on earth preparing for tribulation)
- Paul's unique challenge: telling people (including Peter, James, John) that what Jesus personally told them was now changing
- He had to help them transition away from signs and wonders used to reach Jews
- Some passages in Paul's writings cannot be directly applied today because they address transitional situations

### Romans 14: Food, Days, and Christian Liberty
- Romans 1:16 says "to the Jew first and also to the Greek" - but we are not commissioned that way today
- The weak in faith ate only herbs because they couldn't accept freedom from dietary laws
- The strong could eat all things, understanding their liberty
- Paul's instruction: don't judge each other over these matters (v. 3-4)
- One esteems one day above another (Sabbath, feast days), another esteems every day alike
- "Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind" (v. 5) - during transition, both positions were acceptable
- Key principle: "None of us liveth to himself" (v. 7) - teaching about the body of Christ
- "We shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ" (v. 10) - changes perspective on disputes
- "Every one of us shall give account of himself to God" (v. 12)
- Don't put a stumbling block in a brother's way (v. 13)
- "To him that esteemeth any thing to be unclean, to him it is unclean" (v. 14) - true during transition, but compare 1 Timothy 4:3-5 where Paul definitively states every creature of God is good
- Don't grieve your brother with your food; don't destroy him with your meat for whom Christ died (v. 15)
- "The kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost" (v. 17)
- Follow after things which make for peace and edification (v. 19)
- "He that doubteth is damned if he eat, because he eateth not of faith: for whatsoever is not of faith is sin" (v. 23)
- Contrast with 1 Timothy 4 where false teachers forbid marriage and meats, having their conscience seared - no ambiguity there

### 1 Corinthians 7: Marriage and Singleness
- Context: "the time is short" (v. 29) - referring to the limited time to reach Jews with signs and wonders
- "The present distress" (v. 26) - specific historical situation
- Paul's burden for reaching his people led him to recommend singleness for fuller devotion
- "I speak this by permission, and not of commandment" (v. 6)
- "Are thou bound unto a wife? seek not to be loosed. Art thou loosed from a wife? seek not a wife" (v. 27)
- "But if thou marry, thou hast not sinned" (v. 28)
- "They that have wives be as though they had none" (v. 29) - doesn't match Ephesians 5:22-33
- Wife bound while husband lives, but "she is happier if she so abide, after my judgment: and I think also that I have the Spirit of God" (v. 39-40)
- Paul's unique perspective influenced by going to the third heaven and experiencing what he couldn't tell others
- Genesis 1:28 (be fruitful and multiply) is still operative, but Paul's specific burden and situation influenced this advice
- Understanding Paul's heart and the transitional context prevents misapplication

### 2 Corinthians 8-9: The Collection for Jerusalem Saints
- Historical context: Acts 2 - believers sold everything and laid it at the apostles' feet
- They were preparing for the tribulation period that didn't come
- Economically, selling everything with no way to replace it or earn interest meant they would run out
- God was going to supernaturally provide (like manna in wilderness) but the dispensational change left them destitute
- Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5) died for not giving everything - shows the seriousness of the commitment expected then
- Paul saw the need and organized a collection from churches in Macedonia and elsewhere
- Logistical challenges: transporting valuable goods/gold from Macedonia to Jerusalem by boat, securing it, trusting messengers

#### 2 Corinthians 8: The Macedonian Example
- "The grace of God bestowed on the churches of Macedonia" (v. 1)
- "In a great trial of affliction the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality" (v. 2)
- They gave "to their power...and beyond their power" - willing to put themselves in difficulty (v. 3)
- "First gave their own selves to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God" (v. 5) - not just charity but understanding body of Christ responsibility
- Titus sent to complete the collection in Corinth (v. 6)
- "I speak not by commandment, but by occasion of the forwardness of others, and to prove the sincerity of your love" (v. 8) - not a command, but an occasion
- Christ became poor that we might be rich (v. 9)
- "Herein I give my advice" - not commandment, advice (v. 10)
- The Corinthians had pledged a year ago but hadn't followed through (v. 10)
- "Now therefore perform the doing of it" - get it done (v. 11)
- "For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath, and not according to that he hath not" (v. 12)
- "For I mean not that other men be eased, and ye burdened" (v. 13)
- "By an equality, that now at this time your abundance may be a supply for their want, that their abundance also may be a supply for your want: that there may be equality" (v. 14) - not socialism, but bringing them up to a starting point
- Reference to manna gathering: "He that had gathered much had nothing over; and he that had gathered little had no lack" (v. 15) - extra turned to worms
- Titus chosen because he's trustworthy and strong enough to get the job done
- Titus "being more forward, of his own accord, he went unto you" (v. 17) - operating in grace, making the need his own
- Sent with another trusted brother to avoid any accusation about handling abundance (v. 18-22)
- "Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men" (v. 21)

#### 2 Corinthians 9: Principles of Giving
- "Show ye to them, and before the churches, the proof of your love" (8:24)
- Paul had boasted to Macedonia about Corinth's readiness, and their zeal provoked others (v. 2)
- Sent brethren ahead "lest our boasting of you should be in vain" (v. 3)
- "Lest haply if they of Macedonia come with me, and find you unprepared, we (that we say not, ye) should be ashamed" (v. 4) - Paul's diplomatic pressure
- Paul had to balance: showing the urgent need, holding them to their word, but not commanding, encouraging the right spirit
- "He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" (v. 6) - sowing and reaping principle
- "Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver" (v. 7)
- Purpose in heart, not grudgingly, not of necessity - these are the guidelines, not specific amounts
- "God is able to make all grace abound toward you, that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work" (v. 8)
- "He that ministereth seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness" (v. 10)
- "The administration of this service not only supplieth the want of the saints, but is abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God" (v. 12)
- "The experiment of this ministration" (v. 13) - only use of "experiment" in the Bible
- This was a one-time experimental/occasional ministry for a specific historical need
- "Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift" (v. 15)

### Application: Transitional vs. Universal Truth
- This was not about missions giving - it was about a specific need during the transition
- Universal principles embedded: sowing/reaping, cheerful giving, purposing in heart, God's ability to provide
- The Titus principle: when you see the need, make it your own and do what needs to be done
- Cannot apply this to say you must give sacrificially even if you can't feed your family
- Must practice good stewardship (Dave Ramsey principles) to be prepared to give when needs arise
- If you're poor because of foolish spending and not working hard, giving more won't fix it - violates sowing and reaping
- Prepare ahead of time through wise stewardship to be able to give when opportunities come
- Dave Ramsey understands stewardship but misses this passage - tries to make gospel passages work instead
- With understanding of this passage, his teaching would align better with Scripture

### General Principles for Reading Paul's Apostolic Epistles
- Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians, 1 & 2 Thessalonians were written during Paul's journeys
- These contain more transitional elements than prison epistles (Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians) and pastoral epistles (1 & 2 Timothy, Titus)
- By prison and pastoral epistles, things are more settled, less about signs and transitional practices
- Look for universal truths embedded in transitional passages (judgment seat, sowing/reaping, body of Christ)
- Some verses are true throughout (Romans 14:10-12); others are specifically transitional (Romans 14:14 vs. 1 Timothy 4:4-5)
- Next study will cover 1 & 2 Thessalonians where Paul discusses both rapture and second coming - important to distinguish
