## Summary
Pastor Andrew Watt examines biblical love as a core component of Christian faith that has been hijacked by worldly definitions. Using Romans 13:8-10 as the foundation, he demonstrates how God simplified 613 Old Testament commandments into two: love God and love others. He argues that true biblical love must be sacrificial, costly to the giver yet free to the receiver, and genuine—contrasting sharply with the world's counterfeit version that demands acceptance without truth. The sermon challenges believers to examine whether their love for fellow church members, all saints, and even their neighbors reflects the genuine, sacrificial love Christ demonstrated.

## Scripture
Romans 13:8-10 — Paul teaches that owing no man anything but to love one another fulfills the law, as all commandments (not committing adultery, not killing, not stealing, not bearing false witness, not coveting) are comprehended in loving your neighbor as yourself. Love works no ill to his neighbor; therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.

## Key Themes
- **Love as the Fulfillment of the Law**: God condensed 613 commandments to 10, then to 2 (love God and love others), showing love as the foundation of all biblical commands
- **Sacrificial Nature of Love**: Biblical love requires laying down one's life for friends, as Christ demonstrated by dying for us while we were yet sinners
- **Love's Cost and Freedom**: Love comes at a cost to the giver but is free to the receiver, unlike worldly love that coerces or manipulates
- **Genuine Love Without Hypocrisy**: True love must be unfeigned (without dissimulation), offering no strings attached, no clauses, no hidden contracts

## Notes

### The Simplification of God's Commands
- God gave 613 commandments in the Old Testament
- These were condensed to 10 commandments
- Jesus further simplified to 2 commandments in Matthew 22:35-40
- First commandment: Love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, and mind
- Second commandment: Love your neighbor as yourself
- All the law and prophets hang on these two commandments
- First half of 10 commandments deals with relationship with God
- Second half deals with relationship with others

### The Hijacking of Biblical Love
- Love has been redefined by the world as "love is love" (circular definition)
- American Christianity has separated from biblical Christianity
- The American God differs from the God of the Bible
- Many claiming to be Christians are missing the core ingredient of biblical love
- People may have correct dress, music, and church attendance but lack genuine love
- Love is not acceptance—this is a worldly counterfeit

### Point 1: Love is Sacrificial
- John 15:13: "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends"
- Romans 5:8: "God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us"
- Ephesians 5: Husbands are to love wives as Christ loved the church and gave himself for it
- Love is not coercing someone to do something they don't want (especially relevant for teenagers)
- True love may require laying down your rights or ego for another's benefit
- Too many believers would rather go to court than sacrifice for a brother in Christ
- Conflict resolution requires someone to sacrifice being "right" for the sake of relationship

### Point 2: Love Comes at a Cost to the Giver but is Free to the Receiver
- Words of affirmation are easy and cost nothing (words are 100% free)
- Actions demonstrate genuine love more than words alone
- Pain is the price of love—it will cost something to truly love
- Examples of love's cost: heartbreak when children disobey, grief when loved ones die, pain when friends make poor choices
- 2 Corinthians 12:15: Paul willing to "spend and be spent" for the Corinthians, though the more he loved them, the less he was loved
- Parents experience this: the more they discipline (love), the less they may be loved in return
- 2 Corinthians 8:7-8: Paul challenged the Corinthian church to prove the sincerity of their love through financial giving
- Jesus Christ paid the ultimate price tenfold, a hundredfold
- John 3:16: God so loved the world
- Worldly love asks "What do I gain?" but biblical love asks "What can I give?"
- Sharing the gospel may cost you socially or emotionally, but that's because you love people

### Point 3: Love is Genuine
- People are tired of fraud, especially in this digital age with AI and scams
- Romance scams exploit people's desire for love and companionship
- 2 Corinthians 6:6: Paul ministered "by love unfeigned" (genuine love)
- Romans 12:9: "Let love be without dissimulation" (without hypocrisy)
- Genuine love has no strings attached, no clauses, no contracts
- Story of Nate Bargatze's father: experienced true love for the first time when saved by Jesus Christ
- His father grew up in an abusive home, was abandoned and attacked by a dog as a child
- Many people have experienced manipulation, abuse, and false love
- When they encounter genuine love from Christ (and believers), it is freeing
- Challenge: coworkers and neighbors should experience genuine love from believers
- Church members we disagree with are the same people Jesus bled and died for

### Application Questions
- How's your love to your fellow church members going?
- How's your love to all saints going?
- How's your love toward your neighbors (defined in Luke 10 as anyone, even those different from you)?
- Do you love somebody enough to tell them about Jesus Christ?
- Do you love somebody enough to forgive them?
- Do you love somebody enough to allow them an opportunity that maybe hurts you a little bit?
- When's the last time you expressed love to somebody where it cost you something?
