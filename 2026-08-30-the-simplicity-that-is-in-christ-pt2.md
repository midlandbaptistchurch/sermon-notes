## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues his series on the simplicity that is in Christ, focusing primarily on the simplicity of salvation in the current dispensation of grace. Drawing a contrast between the "deep things of God" (1 Corinthians 2:10) and the "simplicity that is in Christ" (2 Corinthians 11:3), he argues that salvation today is received by grace through faith alone — not by works — and that this stands in deliberate contrast to what was required under the Law in previous dispensations. The sermon traces four biblical instances of the question "What must I do to be saved?" to demonstrate how the answer changed across dispensations, arriving at Paul's simple answer: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ."

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 11:3 — Paul's warning that believers' minds should not be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ, used as the anchor text for the series.

1 Corinthians 2:10 — "The Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God," establishing the tension between the deep things of God and the simplicity in Christ.

Additional passages referenced: Ephesians 1:13; Romans 6:23; Romans 3:23; Romans 11:6; Romans 2:13; James 1:25; Matthew 24:13; Matthew 19:16; Luke 3:9–11; Acts 2:37–38; Acts 16:30; Galatians 3:19.

## Key Themes
- The balance between pursuing the deep things of God and appreciating the simplicity that is in Christ
- Salvation in the dispensation of grace is by grace alone, through faith alone, apart from works
- Dispensational distinctions: what was required for salvation or acceptance before God differed under conscience, law, and grace
- Four biblical answers to "What must I do?": (1) share with others (Luke 3, under law/John the Baptist); (2) keep the commandments and sell possessions (Matthew 19, under law/Christ's earthly ministry); (3) repent and be baptized (Acts 2, Peter to Israel at Pentecost); (4) believe on the Lord Jesus Christ (Acts 16, Paul to the Philippian jailer)
- The danger of mixing dispensations and imposing works-based requirements on salvation today
- Eternal security as a distinctive feature of the current dispensation of grace
- The five aspects of a believer's life in Christ: Salvation, Sanctification, Study, Service, Soul-winning

## Notes
- Pastor Payne frames the sermon as Part 2 of a series, resuming from the prior week where he introduced 2 Corinthians 11:3 and the five S-words summarizing a believer's life in Christ.
- The "deep things of God" in 1 Corinthians 2 are identified with the mystery — things hidden before the foundation of the world, now revealed to believers in the current age.
- The simplicity of salvation is tied specifically to the dispensation of grace, said to begin around 45 A.D. with the conversion and ministry of the apostle Paul.
- Pastor Payne emphasizes that Paul is the apostle to the Gentiles (Romans 11:13) and that the twelve apostles' instructions were directed to Israel under a different dispensational framework.
- Works-based salvation teaching (baptismal regeneration, keeping commandments, etc.) is critiqued as applying scriptures from other dispensations to the present age.
- Salvation today: grace (Ephesians 2:8), gift and free gift (Romans 6:23; Romans 3:24), not of works (repeated throughout Paul's epistles), received by belief/trust (Acts 16:31).
- Eternal security is presented as unique to the dispensation of grace; believers are sealed in Christ until the day of redemption.
- The rapture is identified as the close of the current dispensation of grace.
- The sermon closes with a pastoral appeal for assurance of salvation.
