## Summary
In this ninth installment of the series on temptation, Hugh Taylor focuses on 1 Corinthians 10:13 and God's provision of an escape route from sin. The sermon emphasizes that temptation is universal and common to all people, but God provides both a way to escape the sin that follows temptation and the strength to bear it. Taylor stresses the importance of self-examination, recognizing our vulnerabilities, and supporting one another through mutual faith.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There had no temptation taken you, but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape that ye may be able to bear it."

Romans 12:3 — On thinking of ourselves with proper humility

2 Corinthians 13:5 — On self-examination whether we be in the faith

Romans 1:11-12 — On mutual comfort and establishment through shared faith

Philippians 1:27 — On standing fast in one spirit and striving together for the gospel

## Key Themes
- God's faithfulness in providing escape from temptation and sin
- The three types of temptation: trial/test, provocation, and enticement
- Self-examination with humility regarding both weaknesses and strengths
- Understanding temptation as a precursor to sin, not sin itself
- The importance of mutual faith and supporting one another
- How our vulnerabilities change throughout life's seasons
- Starting each day by "putting on the new man" spiritually
- The responsibility to minister to and comfort one another in the body of Christ

## Notes

### The Three Dimensions of 1 Corinthians 10:13
The verse contains three "buts" that establish God's care in temptation:
1. **First "but"** — All temptation is common to mankind; we're all in the same boat
2. **Second "but"** — God is faithful and got in the boat with us through Christ
3. **Third "but"** — God provides both a way to escape sin and strength to bear the temptation

### Three Definitions of Temptation
- **Trial or test** — Testing our commitment (e.g., deciding whether to attend church in bad weather)
- **Provocation** — Being provoked either toward judgment or toward love and good works
- **Enticement** — The enticing pull toward sin or evil, or more broadly, toward action

Temptation is the precursor to sin; we are not escaping temptation itself, but escaping the sin that follows it.

### Developing an Escape Plan
The first step in dealing with temptation is having an escape route and plan. This begins with **previewing temptation risks**:
- Recognize that temptation shifts throughout life; what tempts us changes with age and circumstances
- Be realistic about declining physical abilities while maintaining moral vigilance
- Continuously assess where we are vulnerable and where we need to tighten our spiritual armor

### Self-Examination with Humility
Romans 12:3 instructs us not to "think of [ourselves] more highly than [we] ought to think, but to think soberly." Key aspects:
- We have inherent value and worth because of Christ, not because of ourselves
- Our strengths can become our greatest vulnerabilities if they lead to pride
- Regular self-examination helps us understand where we need God's grace most
- We should begin each day by "putting on the new man" spiritually before engaging with the world

### Mutual Faith and Corporate Responsibility
Romans 1:11-12 emphasizes that our faith is not isolated:
- We come together to impart spiritual gifts to one another
- By ministering to others, we are comforted ourselves through mutual faith
- Standing fast in "one spirit" means unity of attitude and heart (Philippians 1:27)
- We are responsible not only for our own temptation management but for helping others bear theirs
- The body of Christ functions by iron sharpening iron — we need each other's faith to stay strong

### Practical Application
- When facing temptation, "pray without ceasing" — use your thought life as a constant prayer
- Don't just attend church; come with intention to minister and be ministered to
- Acknowledge that spiritual leaders and faithful members all need comfort and encouragement
- Recognize that resisting temptation is not just about personal holiness but about corporate witness to the gospel
