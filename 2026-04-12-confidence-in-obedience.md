## Summary
Youth Pastor Andrew Watt examines what it means to live with confidence in obedience to God. Using Paul's letter to Philemon as a case study, he explores how God can have such confidence in believers that He asks them to do more than what is requested. The sermon challenges listeners to move beyond casual Christianity and commit to genuine obedience, mortifying the flesh and bringing every thought into captivity to Christ.

## Scripture
Philemon 10-21 — Paul appeals to Philemon regarding his runaway slave Onesimus, expressing confidence that Philemon will not only obey but do even more than requested.

## Key Themes
- God's confidence in our obedience
- The tension between grace and obedience
- Mortifying the flesh and spiritual warfare
- Freedom from sin through Christ's obedience
- The contrast between Adam's disobedience and Christ's obedience
- Bringing thoughts into captivity to Christ
- Practical daily obedience vs. crisis-driven faith

## Notes

### The Example of Philemon
- Paul had such confidence in Philemon's obedience that he knew Philemon would do even more than asked
- This raises the question: How much confidence does God have in our obedience?
- Philemon's act of forgiving and receiving Onesimus showed obedience beyond the law's requirement

### Biblical Examples of Disobedience
- **Pharaoh** (Exodus 5:2): Lacked fear of God, asked "Who is the Lord that I should obey his voice?"
- **Israel in the wilderness** (Joshua 5:6, Judges 2:14-19): Continually doubted God despite His proven power; God would deliver them into the hands of enemies when disobedient, then raise up judges for deliverance
- Pattern of negotiating with God during crises but returning to disobedience once trouble passed

### Grace and Obedience (Romans 6:14-19)
- We are not under law but under grace, yet this does not give license to sin
- We are freed from slavery to sin to become servants of righteousness
- The infirmity of the flesh remains even for believers—emotions and desires often lie to us
- We must choose to obey, yielding our members to righteousness rather than uncleanness

### Adam's Disobedience vs. Christ's Obedience (Romans 5:19, Ephesians 2:1-10)
- By Adam's disobedience, many were made sinners; by nature we are children of wrath
- By Christ's obedience, many are made righteous
- We are raised up in Christ and seated in heavenly places, moving from natural wrath to spiritual righteousness
- Yet we remain trapped in physical bodies that are naturally inclined toward disobedience

### The War Against the Flesh (2 Corinthians 10:3-5, Colossians 3:1-10)
- Though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh; our weapons are spiritual
- We must bring every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ
- Cultural busyness and focus on physical needs (retirement, education, home repairs) can distract from spiritual development
- We must mortify our members: fornication, uncleanness, evil affection, covetousness (which is idolatry)
- Continuous, intentional war against the flesh through the power of the Holy Spirit is necessary

### The Challenge of Grace
- Many live under grace while remaining disobedient, mistakenly thinking grace cancels consequences
- The epistles contain many commands that require obedience: study the Bible, flee youthful lusts, follow righteousness, preach the Word, be instant in season and out of season
- Shallow faith that only seeks God during crises rather than in daily obedience

### Call to Action
- Seek things above where Christ sits, not earthly things
- Set affections on things above, not on the earth
- Make "absolute war" with the flesh through mortification and the power of the Holy Spirit
- Commit to obedience that exceeds the minimum requirement—go above and beyond
- Become a church and individuals of whom God can confidently say, "I know they will obey and do even more than I ask"
