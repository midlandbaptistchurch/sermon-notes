## Summary
Pastor Simpson unpacks Galatians 6:1-10, emphasizing the balance between bearing one another's burdens and allowing people to prove their own work. He explores how spiritual maturity requires discernment to distinguish between helping someone and enabling destructive behavior. The sermon challenges believers to understand that under grace, we reap what we sow, and that fruitful ministry requires both compassion and wisdom.

## Scripture
Galatians 6:1-10 — This passage addresses how believers should restore those overtaken in sin, bear one another's burdens, and understand the principle of sowing and reaping.

## Key Themes
- Restoration with meekness and humility
- Bearing burdens vs. enabling dependency
- Personal responsibility and proving one's own work
- The principle of sowing and reaping under grace
- Discernment in ministry
- Avoiding weariness in well-doing

## Notes

### Restoring Others with the Spirit of Meekness (Galatians 6:1)
When a believer is overtaken in a fault, restoration should be done in meekness. The warning in this verse is not that you might fall into the same sin, but that you could lose the spirit of meekness—patience, gentleness, and humility—while ministering to others. This requires the fruits of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, and temperance.

### Bearing Burdens and Allowing Others to Prove Themselves (Galatians 6:2-5)
There is a critical balance in Christian ministry:
- **Bearing burdens:** Help others who are struggling or in need.
- **Proving one's own work:** The person being ministered to must also take responsibility for their own growth and change.

The illustration of giving fish versus teaching someone to fish applies here. At some point, the person must do their own work and bear their own burden. True ministry produces visible fruit—change in character, spirit, and gratitude.

### Sowing and Reaping Under Grace (Galatians 6:7-9)
God is not mocked. Whatever a person sows, they will reap. This principle operates under grace, where our choices—not circumstances—determine our spiritual harvest. Those who sow to the flesh reap corruption; those who sow to the spirit reap eternal life. Believers must not grow weary in well-doing, trusting that in due season they will reap if they do not faint.

### Discernment in Ministry
Pastor Simpson shared personal experiences of learning when to help and when to step back. Key insights include:
- Distinguish between genuine ministry and being used by others
- Recognize when people are not willing to do their part
- Invest in those who want to move forward, not just the "squeaky wheels"
- Accept that sometimes stopping the enabling will cause people to leave
- Understand that grace includes boundaries, not endless giving

The story of ministering to Andrew and Darla illustrates how proper investment in willing believers yields eternal fruit, whereas endless assistance without change produces nothing.

### Thanksgiving and Communication (Galatians 6:6)
When helped, believers should communicate gratitude and support those who teach them in good things. True gratitude flows from genuine transformation, not just receiving assistance.
