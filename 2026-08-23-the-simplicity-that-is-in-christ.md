## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne preaches from 2 Corinthians 11 on the danger of being corrupted away from the simplicity that is in Christ, drawing on Paul's concern for the Corinthian church as they entertained false teachers preaching another Jesus, another spirit, and another gospel. The sermon focuses primarily on the simplicity of salvation in the current dispensation of grace — that God saves freely, by grace, through faith alone, apart from works — and contrasts this with how God dealt with humanity under the conscience and the law in previous dispensations.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 11:1-4 — Paul pleads with the Corinthian church to bear with him as he warns them against false apostles who are leading them away from sincere devotion to Christ, expressing his godly jealousy for them as their spiritual father who desires to present them as a pure bride to Christ.

## Key Themes
- The simplicity of the gospel and salvation in the dispensation of grace
- Godly jealousy and Paul's pastoral concern for the Corinthians
- The danger of false teachers preaching another Jesus, another spirit, or another gospel
- Salvation by grace through faith, not by works
- Distinguishing between dispensations and their differing requirements (conscience, law, grace)
- The kindness of God illustrated through David and Mephibosheth
- Being "in Christ" versus being "without"

## Notes

### Paul's Plea and Godly Jealousy (2 Cor. 11:1-2)
- Paul implores the Corinthians to bear with him in a degree of apparent foolishness as he prepares to commend himself — necessary because the very church he founded was rejecting his ministry.
- His jealousy is not self-serving but "godly jealousy" — concern for what is best for them in their relationship with God.
- He uses the illustration of a father presenting his daughter as a pure virgin bride to her husband, applying it to presenting the church to Christ at the judgment seat.
- Supporting references: Romans 7:4 (believers are already married to Christ); 1 Corinthians 12:27 (believers are the body of Christ); 1 Corinthians 4:15 (Paul as spiritual father who begat them through the gospel).

### Warning Against False Teachers (2 Cor. 11:3-4, 13-15)
- Paul warns that the serpent beguiled Eve through subtlety, and similarly false teachers can corrupt believers' minds from the simplicity that is in Christ.
- Three corrupting dangers: another Jesus (attacking Christ's deity, the Trinity, or his sole sufficiency as Savior), another spirit (counterfeit spiritual influence), another gospel (a different message of salvation).
- Pastor Payne noted that churches in the surrounding area illustrate this — groups denying the Trinity or the Jesus of Scripture, and churches promoting agendas contrary to biblical teaching.
- Satan's ministers transform themselves as ministers of righteousness (2 Cor. 11:13-15).
- The battle is a battle of the mind (cf. 2 Cor. 10:4-5 — casting down imaginations, bringing every thought captive to Christ).

### The Simplicity of Salvation
- Salvation = being saved = being rescued from the impending danger of dying without Christ and facing eternal damnation.
- Acts 4:12 — there is salvation in no other name than Jesus Christ.
- We are in the dispensation of the grace of God (Ephesians 3:1-2).
- Grace defined biblically: the love and kindness of God appearing to all men (Titus 2:11; Titus 3:4); God commending his love in Christ dying for sinners (Romans 5:8).
- The story of Mephibosheth (2 Samuel 9) illustrates the kindness of God: lame, hiding in Lo-debar (a place of nothing), brought to the king's table for Jonathan's sake — a picture of God finding us spiritually lame and setting us at his table by grace.
- Salvation is a **gift** — Romans 6:23; Romans 5:17-18 (the free gift came upon all men unto justification of life).
- It is a **free** gift — Romans 3:23-24 (justified freely by his grace); Ephesians 2:8-9 (by grace through faith, not of works).
- We receive it simply by **believing** — 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 (the gospel: Christ died, was buried, rose again); Ephesians 1:13 (after you heard and believed, you were sealed).

### Salvation by Grace Contrasted with Works in Other Dispensations
- Pastor Payne emphasized that Paul's repeated statements of "not of works" (Ephesians 2:8-9; Titus 3:5; 2 Timothy 1:9) imply that in other times and dealings of God, works *were* required.
- The dispensation of conscience (Adam through the Law): God told Cain "if thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted" — the requirement was doing right according to conscience (Genesis 4:7; Romans 2:14-15).
- The dispensation of law: Israel received the Mosaic law and responded "all that the Lord hath said we will do and be obedient" (Exodus 24:4-7) — their entire religious system was one of doing and working.
- James 2:21 — Abraham justified by works (offering Isaac) — applying to Israel's dealings with God, not the present dispensation of grace.
- Future dispensations also involve commandment-keeping: Revelation 12:17; 14:12 (tribulation saints keep the commandments of God and have the faith/testimony of Jesus); Revelation 22:14 (those who do his commandments have right to the tree of life).
- The confusion of many today comes from mixing these dispensational requirements together — adding baptism, commandment-keeping, sacraments, or other works to the simple gospel of grace.

### Closing Appeal
- Do not let your minds be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ regarding salvation.
- Today, in the dispensation of grace: it is grace, faith, gift, free — simply believe and trust that Christ died in your place, was buried, and rose again.
- God's love and kindness has gone out to find us, lame and in Lo-debar, to bring us to his table.
