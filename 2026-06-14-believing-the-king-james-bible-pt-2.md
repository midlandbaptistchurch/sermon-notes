## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues his multi-week Sunday School series making the case for the King James Bible as the inspired, preserved Word of God in the English language. He presents a foundational backdrop for the study, arguing that Satan works to complicate and confuse the Bible issue while God intends His Word to be simple, complete, and practically equipping for every believer. The lesson emphasizes that the hope of the entire human race hinges on having the word of truth, and calls believers to a passionate, committed relationship with Scripture.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 11:3; 2 Timothy 3:16; John 1:14,17; John 8:31-32; John 14:6; Matthew 7:13-14 — These passages are used to establish Satan's strategy to corrupt Scripture, God's design for Scripture to thoroughly equip believers, and the centrality of truth as found in Christ and His Word.

## Key Themes
- Satan's strategy to complicate and corrupt the Bible issue
- God's desire for Scripture to be simple, straightforward, and equipping
- Integrity as the central value connecting all reasons to believe the KJV
- Truth as the foundation of human hope, rooted in Christ
- The danger of spiritual indifference toward the Word of Truth
- Passionate personal commitment to the King James Bible

## Notes
**Overview of the First Edition Title Page**
- Taylor briefly revisits the first edition KJV title page, noting its symbolic imagery: God the Father at the top, the Lamb (God the Son) below, the Apostle Paul depicted holding the sword of the Lord.

**Speaker's Purpose and Disclaimers**
- Goal: inform listeners to a level of credibility so they can believe the KJV, defend it, and help others see its truth.
- Much of the content is a collation of other scholars' work.
- The KJV is not presented as a point of fellowship but as a point of ministry — illustrated by a visiting church experience where a key word difference in a non-KJV translation undermined an entire sermon.

**Backdrop Issue 1 — Satan Wants to Corrupt the Issue (2 Corinthians 11:3)**
- Satan uses complexity, confusion, and deception to dissuade believers from trusting any single Bible.
- Just as the serpent beguiled Eve by perverting God's word, Satan seeks to corrupt minds "from the simplicity that is in Christ."
- The danger: believers become dependent on a pastor's or scholar's interpretation rather than studying for themselves (2 Timothy 2:15).

**Backdrop Issue 2 — God Wants It Simple and Straightforward (2 Timothy 3:16-17)**
- All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness.
- The word "throughly" (not "thoroughly") in verse 17 is noted as significant — a distinction used to illustrate the precision of KJV word choices.
- Scripture is the complete tool to equip the believer for every area of life: marriage, parenting, work, church membership.

**All Three Main Reasons Relate to Integrity**
- Integrity defined from Webster's: "the entire unimpaired state of anything… moral soundness or purity, incorruptness, uprightness, honesty."
- Drawn from Proverbs 20:7 (the just man walketh in his integrity; his children are blessed after him).
- Without the right book, one's integrity is subject to impurity and degradation.

**Backdrop Issue 3 — The Pursuit of Truth Between God and Man**
- The hope of the entire human race hinges on having the word of truth.
- Illustration: faith as a lever, truth as the fulcrum — faith resting on truth can move and fix the things that weigh on a believer's life.
- Key verses on truth: John 1:14, 17 (grace and truth came by Jesus Christ); John 14:6 ("I am the way, the truth, and the life"); John 17:17 ("Thy word is truth"); John 8:31-32 (continuing in the Word leads to knowing truth that makes us free).
- Taylor notes John 8:32 as likely the most misquoted verse in the Bible.

**Spiritual Indifference and Opposition (Matthew 7:13-14)**
- Most people do not deeply care about the word of truth beyond salvation and personal comfort.
- There is a mountain of opposition material against the KJV online and in academia — identified as Satanic smoke screen.
- Believers are called to hang on to the Word of Truth with the same passion as wedding vows.
