## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on temptation by examining three biblical definitions of the term. This sermon focuses on temptation as a test or trial from God, and temptation as provocation or incitement—both directed at God and among believers. Taylor emphasizes the importance of enduring sound doctrine and living worthily, particularly in observing the Lord's Supper with proper reverence.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "But God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that you are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape that ye may be able to bear it."

## Key Themes
- Temptation as a test or trial (Genesis 22, 2 Timothy 4)
- Temptation as provocation or incitement (Psalm 78, Romans 11, Hebrews 10:24)
- The danger of provoking God through unworthy observance of the Lord's Supper (1 Corinthians 11:27-29)
- Living worthily as ambassadors of Christ
- Self-examination before participating in communion
- Learning from Israel's failure in the wilderness (1 Corinthians 10:1-12)
- The importance of enduring sound doctrine in a world opposed to God's truth

## Notes

### First Definition: Temptation as Test or Trial
- God tested Abraham in Genesis 22, proving his faith and God's faithfulness
- In the age of grace, God still tests believers through trials that strengthen our faith
- Paul exhorts believers to "watch in all things" as our first defense against temptation (2 Timothy 4)
- We are running a "gauntlet"—a spiritual marathon where we must stay focused on God's Word despite worldly distractions
- The world uses temptation as a marketing tool to thwart God's will
- Example: Brother Chris working in the oil fields faces extreme temptation to abandon his faith, yet his perseverance encourages the church

### Second Definition: Temptation as Provocation or Incitement
- This type of temptation often involves provoking God or others, usually through sin (Psalm 78:17-18)
- However, provocation can be positive—Paul provoked the Gentiles to emulation through his ministry (Romans 11:13-14)
- Believers should provoke one another "unto love and to good works" (Hebrews 10:24)
- Our behavior and example should draw others toward Christ and godly living
- The church should be distinguished by community and mutual care, unlike the self-serving world

### Israel as a Warning Example
- Israel experienced God's blessing but failed through lusting after evil, idolatry, fornication, and murmuring (1 Corinthians 10:1-12)
- These failures were recorded as examples for us, written for our admonition
- We must "take heed lest we fall" (1 Corinthians 10:12)
- Just as Israel tempted (provoked) Christ in the wilderness, we face the same temptation

### The Temptation to Partake of the Lord's Supper Unworthily
- To eat the bread and drink the cup unworthily makes one guilty of the body and blood of the Lord (1 Corinthians 11:27)
- Unworthy participation brings damnation—not necessarily hell, but a miserable, judged lifestyle
- Many in Corinth became weak, sickly, or died due to unworthy participation (1 Corinthians 11:30)
- We must examine ourselves before partaking: Am I coming in reverence? Do I understand what Christ did? Will I dishonor His sacrifice?
- Even choosing not to attend communion when we can is disrespectful to what Christ accomplished

### The Way of Escape
- Self-examination before spiritual acts prepares us to resist temptation
- We should pray and examine ourselves before any situation, not just at communion
- Enduring sound doctrine enables us to resist the temptation to provoke God
- Living as worthy ambassadors of Christ in all interactions—with spouses, children, neighbors, and even strangers in traffic
- We should prepare ourselves beforehand so that when temptation comes, we are ready to handle it properly
