## Summary
Hugh Taylor explores the concept of a "scriptural selfie"—examining ourselves through the lens of Scripture rather than through smartphones or social media. He walks through six key biblical principles for spiritual self-assessment: thinking soberly of self, examining ourselves, sanctifying ourselves, esteeming others better than ourselves, showing ourselves approved of God, and accounting for ourselves before Christ.

## Scripture
Romans 12:3 — Paul exhorts believers to think soberly of themselves according to the measure of faith God has given them, not thinking more highly than they ought.

## Key Themes
- **Self-Examination**: Looking critically at ourselves through Scripture to identify areas for spiritual growth
- **Humility**: Thinking rationally and reasonably about ourselves rather than being puffed up with pride or self-condemnation
- **Sanctification**: Purging ourselves of sin and distraction to be ready for God's use
- **Submission to Others**: Esteeming others better than ourselves and submitting ourselves one to another
- **Accountability**: Living with the awareness that we will give account before Christ's judgment seat
- **Credibility**: Living authentically so our example matches our words

## Notes

### Think Soberly of Self (Romans 12:3)
- Sober-mindedness means being rational, responsible, and reasonable—not being under the influence of passion
- Not thinking more highly of ourselves than we ought, but also not thinking too little (which is a hidden form of pride)
- Requires honest reflection: Who am I? What do I stand for? What do others see in me?

### Examine Self (1 Corinthians 11:28)
- Self-examination allows us to detect and correct problems early, like early cancer detection
- We need accountability partners and counselors who can provide additional perspective (Proverbs 11:14)
- Being part of a faith community provides built-in accountability to help us keep the faith

### Sanctify Self (2 Timothy 2:19)
- Once we identify issues through examination, we must address them and purge ourselves of iniquity
- We must watch against becoming entangled in the affairs of this life, even good things
- Our priorities and focus often get "balled up"—we need to keep God's mission central
- Both capability and credibility depend on our sanctification
- We are examples to others simply by being part of the church

### Esteem Others Better Than Self (Philippians 2:3)
- Scripturally, no one is better than anyone else—though some may be better off materially or in talent
- Christ is our model: He emptied Himself and took the form of a servant
- Submitting ourselves one to another means seeking to be a blessing rather than just receiving blessings
- This is hard work and often fatiguing, but necessary for Christian growth
- True spiritual growth begins when we come to church to be a blessing, not just to get one

### Show Self Approved of God (2 Timothy 2:15)
- We must study Scripture and rightly divide it to understand how it applies to us
- The "so what test": Does this passage pass the test of applying to my life and behavior?
- We should understand why we practice our faith the way we do (prayer, giving, worship, etc.)
- God's approval, not man's commendation, is what matters

### Account for Self at the Judgment Seat of Christ (Romans 14:10-12)
- Every believer will stand before Christ's judgment seat
- This is about our works and stewardship, not the judgment of our sins (already paid for in Christ)
- What we do with our abilities, opportunities, and time will be evident
- If we hold ourselves accountable now, we'll give a better account then
- Daily remembrance that our actions either build something of value or represent a loss of reward
