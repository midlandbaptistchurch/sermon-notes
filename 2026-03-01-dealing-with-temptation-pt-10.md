## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on dealing with temptation by emphasizing the importance of self-examination and humility in recognizing our weaknesses. He teaches that while temptations are common to all people, God provides a way of escape through Christ. The sermon focuses on practical steps for resisting temptation: examining ourselves honestly, learning from others' mistakes, and remaining alert to spiritual adversaries.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it."

Also referenced: Romans 12:3, 2 Corinthians 13:5, Galatians 6:1, 2 Corinthians 10:12, 1 Thessalonians 3:4-5, Ephesians 6, 2 Corinthians 11:13, John 1:1-2

## Key Themes
- Self-examination with humility
- The authority of Scripture in daily life
- Learning from others' faults without pride
- Spiritual alertness to adversaries
- The way of escape God provides
- Godliness as preparation for eternity

## Notes

### The Foundation: Scripture as Authority
Taylor opens with personal reflections on his approaching 75th birthday and the importance of biblical authority in family and life decisions. He emphasizes that the Bible is not merely a book but a finite expression of an infinite God, deserving reverence and serving as the final authority in all matters—not just church matters, but in all of life's conduct.

### Three Key Steps in Dealing with Temptation

**1. Examine Ourselves with Humility**
- Romans 12:3 warns against thinking of ourselves more highly than we ought
- We should think highly of ourselves in the context of Christ in us, not in isolation
- 2 Corinthians 13:5 calls us to examine ourselves honestly, apart from our natural tendency to protect our ego or "save face"
- True progress and growth require honest, humble self-assessment

**2. Learn from Others' Mistakes**
- Galatians 6:1 instructs spiritual people to restore those overtaken in faults with meekness
- Restoration is labor-intensive; we should address problems early rather than waiting for crisis intervention
- 2 Corinthians 10:12 warns against comparing ourselves with others or commending ourselves
- We must recognize that others' faults could become our own; the tempter seeks to make their mistakes our mistakes
- Even strong believers—married couples, parents, church leaders—experience spiritual stumbles

**3. Be Alert to Adversaries**
- 1 Thessalonians 3:4-5 shows Paul's concern that tribulation might tempt believers to abandon faith
- Satan works through various means: unseen spiritual forces and also through human agents
- 2 Corinthians 11:13 describes false apostles and deceitful workers who disguise themselves as righteous
- Satan can appear as an "angel of light," making evil seem attractive
- Some adversaries are knowingly evil; others are easily manipulated or deceived
- We must maintain spiritual alertness and healthy skepticism without becoming cynical

### The Purpose of This Teaching
Taylor emphasizes that dealing with temptation is central to our identity as believers and our distinction from the world. Those given great spiritual opportunity (the "great door effectual" open to the church) have greater responsibility. We must handle temptations effectively to be ready for the judgment seat of Christ and to have something of eternal value to present to Him.

### The Way of Escape
God does not remove temptation, but He provides a way to escape the sin that follows temptation. The escape is not escape from temptation itself, but from yielding to it. Every believer faces the choice: stop at temptation and refuse it, or step into sin.
