## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a study in Ephesians 5, examining what the Bible teaches about music within the body of Christ. Drawing on Ephesians 5:18-19 and Colossians 3:16-17, he emphasizes that singing together is not optional but a scriptural necessity for the church, meant to bring glory to God and edify believers through teaching and admonishing one another. He closes with an Old Testament illustration from the story of Obed-Edom and the Ark of the Covenant to highlight the blessing that comes from making music before the Lord.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:18-19 — Paul's command to be filled with the Spirit, expressing that fullness through psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in your heart to the Lord. The parallel passage in Colossians 3:16-17 is also examined alongside it.

## Key Themes
- The biblical basis for music in the church (Ephesians 5:18-19; Colossians 3:16-17)
- Being filled with the Spirit and letting the Word of Christ dwell richly are inseparable
- Music must glorify God and edify the body through teaching and admonishing
- The necessity of corporate, congregational singing — not just individual worship
- Making melody "in your heart" as an act of intimacy and worship toward the Lord
- Discerning spiritual music from carnal music
- The role of a choir in helping a local church maintain conservative, doctrinally-grounded music
- Old Testament illustration: Obed-Edom and the blessing that came from hosting the Ark of the Covenant

## Notes
- This appears to be a continuation of a prior week's message on the same passage.
- **Ephesians 5:18-19 and Colossians 3:16-17 are parallel passages**: "Be filled with the Spirit" (Ephesians) corresponds to "Let the Word of Christ dwell in you richly" (Colossians) — the two cannot be separated.
- The phrase "speaking to yourselves" in both passages refers to corporate, mutual teaching and admonishing within the gathered church body, not merely personal devotion.
- **Psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs**: three categories covering the full range of biblical and spiritually-derived music.
- The word *melody* appears four times in the Bible — once in the New Testament (Ephesians 5:19) and three times in the Old Testament (twice in Isaiah, once in Amos). Its first Old Testament use is negative (judgment on Tyre), illustrating that music can serve either godly or ungodly purposes.
- "Making melody in your heart to the Lord" is described as a term of **intimacy and worship** — coupling grace, melody, music, and message as an offering to God.
- Corporate singing provides an opportunity to offer praise with other members of the body in like faith — something that cannot be replicated in individual worship alone.
- Simpson reflects on watching a church service from Mooresville, NC, noting that some churches are strong in music but weak in Bible teaching, and others vice versa — both elements are needed.
- **Obed-Edom** (Gittite, mentioned in 1–2 Chronicles and Samuel): when David attempted to move the Ark of the Covenant and Uzza was struck dead for touching it, the Ark was placed in Obed-Edom's home for three months. God blessed his household. Simpson references a sermon by Steve Jarrett suggesting Obed-Edom likely played music before the Ark during those months. Obed-Edom later went with the Levites when the Ark was properly transported and served as a doorkeeper at the temple, suggesting his musical faithfulness was recognized and rewarded.
- Practical application: engage actively during congregational singing — read the words, focus on the message of the hymns, and understand that worship is taking place, not merely a warm-up to the sermon.
