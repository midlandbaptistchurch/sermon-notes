## Summary
Assoc. Pastor Mark Simpson continues the "Guidelines for Grace" series by examining the biblical meaning of "perfect" — arguing it refers to completeness and maturity rather than sinless flawlessness. Drawing primarily from 2 Corinthians 13:11, he walks through the related commands to be of good comfort and of one mind, connecting them to doctrinal unity, spiritual security, and living out God's good, acceptable, and perfect will as described in Romans 12:2.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 13:11 — Paul's closing exhortation to the Corinthian church: "Finally, brethren, farewell, be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace; and the God of love and peace shall be with you."

## Key Themes
- The biblical meaning of "perfect" as completeness and maturity, not sinless perfection
- Being of good comfort — giving and receiving spiritual support and assurance
- Doctrinal unity (being of one mind) as distinct from uniformity in personality or preferences
- Proving God's good, acceptable, and perfect will (Romans 12:2)
- Spiritual maturity enabling believers to receive deeper wisdom
- The CRIBS framework for understanding what believers received at salvation

## Notes

### The Word "Perfect" in Scripture
- The English word "perfect" in the Bible carries the sense of completeness or maturity, not moral flawlessness as commonly understood today.
- Under grace, perfection (completeness) is presented as an attainable goal when the word is understood in its biblical context.

### Be of Good Comfort (2 Corinthians 13:11)
- "Good comfort" involves security, support, and assurance — not merely emotional consolation.
- Referenced 1 Thessalonians 5:11: "comfort yourselves together, and edify one another."
- Believers must both give comfort to others and allow themselves to be ministered to; those in ministry roles can become out of balance by only giving and not receiving.

### Be of One Mind
- One-mindedness pertains primarily to **doctrine**, not personality, preferences, or spiritual gifts.
- Discussed in connection with 1 Corinthians 1:10: "that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment."
- A rabbit trail on 1 John 1:9 and Colossians 2 illustrated the importance of understanding forgiveness and sanctification correctly as part of this doctrinal unity.

### The God of Love and Peace (2 Corinthians 13:11)
- God's peace is an inward, stable reality independent of outward circumstances.
- Connected to Philippians 4 and the consideration of one's salvation.
- **CRIBS framework** (from Colossians 2 / related passages) — five things received at salvation:
  - **C** — Circumcision made without hands (spiritual circumcision, Colossians 2)
  - **R** — Regeneration (Titus 3:5)
  - **I** — Indwelling of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6)
  - **B** — Baptism into the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:13)
  - **S** — Sealing of the Spirit (Ephesians 1:13; 4:30)

### Proving God's Perfect Will (Romans 12:2)
- Romans 12:2 calls believers to "prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God."
- Rejected the idea that there is a separate "perfect will" versus a "permissive will" of God; instead, proving God's will is an ongoing process of applying biblical principles.
- Practical test: Will this activity stand at the judgment seat of Christ? Does it bring God glory?
- Being in the Word together as a body, studying in context, is itself an example of the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God in practice.

### Perfect Unity and Spiritual Maturity (1 Corinthians 2:6)
- "Howbeit we speak wisdom among them that are perfect" — those who are mature enough to receive deeper biblical truth beyond basic milk.
- Contrasted with the carnal/babe condition described in 1 Corinthians 3:1.
- The goal is to be the kind of believer to whom deeper wisdom can be spoken.

### Closing Prayer
- Prayer that the congregation would embrace biblical perfection — understood as maturity, wisdom, understanding, and completeness — rather than being discouraged by a misunderstood definition of the word.
