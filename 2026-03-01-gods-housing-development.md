## Summary
Hugh Taylor traces God's "housing development" through four phases: Jacob's starter house at Bethel, the portable tabernacle, Solomon's temple, and the modern church as God's dwelling place. The sermon emphasizes that believers today are responsible for building and maintaining God's house—the body of Christ—with the promise of future transformation and renovation.

## Scripture
Psalm 127:1 — "Except the Lord build the house, they labour in vain that build it." The sermon also covers Genesis 28 (Jacob at Bethel), Exodus 25-26 (the tabernacle), 1 Chronicles 28 and 2 Chronicles 5 (Solomon's temple), and 1 Corinthians 6:19 (believers as temples).

## Key Themes
- God as master builder and developer
- Four phases of God's dwelling: starter house (Bethel), mobile home (tabernacle), footstool house (temple), church house (believers)
- The house within a house: inner sanctum in tabernacle and temple; believers within the church
- Our responsibility as God's construction crew
- Spiritual growth and sanctification as ongoing building work
- Future renovation and transformation of believers
- The grafting of Gentiles into God's covenant promises to Israel

## Notes

### Phase 1: Bethel—God's Starter House
- Jacob's encounter at Bethel (Genesis 28) marked God's first "house" on earth—a humble campsite
- Jacob anointed a stone pillar and named the place Bethel ("House of God")
- Despite its rustic simplicity, the presence of God made it profoundly significant
- Jacob's vow and caretaking showed his commitment to maintaining this sacred space

### Phase 2: The Tabernacle—God's Mobile Home
- As Israel grew, God needed a more suitable dwelling—an ornate, portable tent
- The tabernacle featured an outer tent and an inner sanctum (the Most Holy Place) where God's presence dwelt
- The tribe of Levi was appointed as caretakers and ministers (Numbers 3:5)
- God's glory filled the tabernacle, visible to all the people (Leviticus 9:23)

### Phase 3: Solomon's Temple—God's Footstool House
- The temple represented God settling down in one place, putting down roots in Israel
- Despite David's desire to build it, God chose Solomon for the task (1 Chronicles 28:2-6)
- More elaborate and opulent than the tabernacle, with the Most Holy Place at its center
- When completed, God's glory filled the temple so powerfully that priests could not stand to minister (2 Chronicles 5:12-14)

### Phase 4: The Church—God's Present Dwelling
- Today, believers are individually temples of the Holy Ghost (1 Corinthians 6:19)
- The church is both individual believers and the corporate body of Christ (1 Timothy 3:15, Ephesians 1:22-23)
- A house within a house within a house: the believer (inner), the local church (middle), the universal church (outer)
- All believers are co-laborers with God, responsible for building upon the foundation laid by Christ (1 Corinthians 3:9)

### Our Responsibilities as God's Construction Crew
- Study and "measure twice, cut once"—rightly divide the word of truth (2 Timothy 2:15)
- Purge ourselves and become vessels fit for the Master's use (2 Timothy 2:21)
- Keep faithfully showing up for work and increasing in the knowledge of God (Colossians 1:10)
- Build toward the mark of the judgment seat of Christ (Philippians 3:13)
- Take heed how we build, following God's blueprint

### Future Renovation and Transformation
- God will complete the work we begin, transforming our mortal bodies into immortal ones (1 Corinthians 15:51-53)
- We are the culmination and masterpiece of God's housing development (Ephesians 3:20)
- Believers will give God glory throughout all ages, world without end

### Excursus: The Grafting of Gentiles into God's Promises
- Through Romans 11, the sermon explains how Gentiles are grafted into the olive tree of God's covenant with Israel
- We partake of the root and fatness of the tree; we must not boast against the natural branches (the Jews)
- The root—God's covenant with Abraham and the nation of Israel—is the foundation of our faith and Western civilization
- God still remembers His promises to Israel and considers them beloved for the fathers' sake (Romans 11:28)
- We reap the blessings God promised to give through Jacob to all families of the earth
