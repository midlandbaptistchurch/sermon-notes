## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on dealing with temptation by examining the third step: preventing temptations through practical safeguards. He emphasizes making no provision for the flesh, maintaining marital intimacy as a protection against temptation, and developing an attitude of contentment to resist the love of money and power.

## Scripture
Romans 13:12-14 — "Let us cast off the works of darkness and put on the armor of light."
1 Corinthians 7:1-5 — Guidance on marital relationships and intimacy as a safeguard against fornication.
1 Timothy 6:6-10 — "Godliness with contentment is great gain" and warning against the love of money.

## Key Themes
- Prevention of temptation through practical safeguards
- Making no provision for the flesh
- The importance of marital intimacy in resisting temptation
- Developing contentment as protection against desires for wealth, power, and prestige
- Understanding personal sin triggers
- Maintaining focus on Christ as our armor of light

## Notes

### Step Three: Preventing Temptations
Taylor explains that while we cannot eliminate all temptations, we can put safeguards in place to minimize their effects. This involves understanding what our specific temptations and sin triggers are, and taking steps to avoid providing opportunities for the flesh to be tempted.

### Making No Provision for the Flesh (Romans 13)
Based on Romans 13:12-14, the key is to "make no provision for the flesh" by putting on the armor of light. This means consciously choosing not to expose ourselves to situations or influences that would weaken our resistance to sin.

### Maintaining Marital Intimacy (1 Corinthians 7)
Taylor emphasizes that maintaining a strong, intimate marital relationship is a biblical safeguard against temptation. He shares personal experiences about how separation from his wife taught them that extended time apart (beyond two weeks) made them vulnerable. He stresses that:
- Spouses have shared ownership of each other's bodies
- Physical and emotional intimacy should be preserved and prioritized
- Husbands should keep their wives as the primary object of their affections
- Wives should present themselves as attractive to their husbands
- This intimacy is the only licensed outlet for sexual expression

### Developing Contentment (1 Timothy 6)
Taylor teaches that contentment combined with godliness is a great safeguard. The desire to be rich—whether in money, power, prestige, or influence—opens us up to temptation. Rather than pursuing these things, we should:
- Seek contentment and godliness instead
- Withdraw from influences that promote inordinate desire for wealth or power
- Remember that "the love of money is the root of all evil"
- Allow God to guide us into positions of responsibility rather than seeking them for ourselves
