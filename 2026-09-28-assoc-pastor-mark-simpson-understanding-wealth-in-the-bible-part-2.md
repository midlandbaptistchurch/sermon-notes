## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson examines Christ's teachings about wealth in the Gospels, demonstrating that these commands were specifically for those preparing to enter the Tribulation period, not for believers under grace today. He contrasts the requirement to sell all possessions and depend entirely on God's provision during Christ's earthly ministry with Paul's grace-dispensation teaching that allows believers to work, save, and have wealth while being good stewards. The sermon emphasizes the importance of rightly dividing Scripture to understand when commands apply to specific time periods versus universal principles.

## Scripture
Matthew 19:16-30 — Jesus tells the rich young ruler to sell all he has and give to the poor to have treasure in heaven; teaches that it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven.

Luke 12:13-34 — The parable of the rich man who built bigger barns; Jesus commands His disciples to take no thought for their life, what they shall eat or wear, but to seek first the kingdom of God.

Luke 16:19-31 — The account of the rich man and Lazarus, showing the rich man in hell and Lazarus in Abraham's bosom.

1 Timothy 6:3-19 — Paul addresses false teaching about gain being godliness, instructs those who are rich not to trust in uncertain riches but to be rich in good works.

## Key Themes
- **Dispensational Context of Wealth Commands**: Jesus' commands to sell all possessions were specifically for those preparing to enter the Tribulation period, not for believers under grace today.
- **The Poor Saints at Jerusalem**: These believers were poor because they had obediently sold all they had and laid it at the apostles' feet, but the program changed and they didn't get their possessions back.
- **Tribulation Period Requirements**: During the Tribulation, having wealth means either taking the Mark of the Beast or hoarding resources, making it impossible for a rich person to endure to the end.
- **Grace Dispensation Principles**: Under grace, believers are allowed to work, save for retirement, and possess wealth while being good stewards and not trusting in uncertain riches.

## Notes

### The Poor Saints at Jerusalem (2 Corinthians 8-9)
- 2 Corinthians 8 and 9 deal with giving to the poor saints at Jerusalem
- These believers were in grave poverty because they had obeyed and sold all they had and laid it at the apostles' feet
- The program changed but they didn't get their stuff back
- Paul took up large offerings from various churches to help get these folks back on their feet
- 2 Corinthians 8:14 contains the phrase "now at this time" - indicating this was a temporary, transitional situation
- This equality mentioned in verse 14 doesn't apply to believers today as a universal principle
- 2 Corinthians 9:6 - "He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully" - principle of sowing and reaping that applies under grace

### Transitional Time Periods (1 Corinthians 7)
- 1 Corinthians 7:29 says "the time is short" and "they that have wives be as though they had none"
- This doesn't apply to Ephesians 5:22-33 teaching on marriage
- Verse 26 refers to "the present distress" - a specific time period when Paul could still go to the Jew first and do signs, miracles and wonders
- During this short window, believers needed to approach things differently
- The disciples left their families to follow Christ during His three and a half years of earthly ministry
- They would have had a thousand years with them after the tribulation period to make up for it

### The Rich Young Ruler (Matthew 19:16-30)
- Verse 16-17: Young man asks what good thing he must do to have eternal life; Jesus says "keep the commandments"
- Romans 11:6 shows that salvation used to involve works but now is by grace alone: "if by grace then it is no more of works...otherwise grace is no more grace"
- Verse 20: The young man honestly testified he had kept all these commandments from his youth
- People got to Abraham's bosom by keeping the law - they weren't perfect but followed the law
- Verse 21: Jesus commanded him to sell all he had, give to the poor, and follow Him - this was not a test but a command
- Verse 22: He went away sorrowful because he had great possessions
- Verse 23-24: Jesus says a rich man shall hardly enter the kingdom of heaven; it's easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle
- A camel cannot go through the eye of a needle, so Jesus is saying it's impossible for a rich man to go through the tribulation period and endure to the end
- Verse 27: Peter responds foolishly, saying "we have forsaken all and followed thee, what shall we have therefore?"
- Verse 29: Jesus promises that everyone who has forsaken houses, family, or lands for His name's sake shall receive a hundredfold and inherit everlasting life
- Verse 30: "But many that are first shall be last and the last shall be first" - a rebuke to Peter's boastful attitude

### Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus
- Joseph of Arimathea had wealth but didn't leave everything to follow Christ
- Nicodemus came to Jesus by night, not letting everyone know
- They were kind of on the fence - good men who eventually came along, but weren't fully following at that time
- They were trying to figure out what to do without being in denial

### Seek First the Kingdom (Matthew 6 and Luke 12)
- Matthew 6:25-34 and Luke 12:22-34 teach to take no thought for life, food, clothing
- Matthew 6:19: "Lay up not for yourselves treasures upon the earth" - don't have savings
- Matthew 6:24: "No man can serve two masters"
- Matthew 6:33: "Seek ye first the kingdom of God and his righteousness and all these things shall be added unto you"
- "All these things" refers to food, clothing, shelter - basic provision
- Verse 34: "Take therefore no thought for the morrow"
- This was the command for those going into the Tribulation period, not for believers today
- God was going to take care of them the same way He takes care of animals and lilies
- This is what He did when Israel came out of Egypt and went into the wilderness before entering the Promised Land
- People try to apply this to their lives today but can't without taking it out of context

### The Parable of the Rich Man and Barns (Luke 12:13-21)
- Verse 15: "Take heed, beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth"
- Verses 16-19: A rich man's ground brought forth plentifully, so he built bigger barns to store his goods for many years
- He said to his soul: "take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry"
- What he had in his barns was essentially retirement savings
- Nothing says he stole or did anything wrong - he worked hard and was fruitful, a good steward
- Verse 20: God called him a fool and said his soul would be required that night
- Verse 21: "So is he that layeth up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God"
- In the tribulation period, they weren't supposed to have retirement or inheritance to leave to children
- Proverbs says it's good to leave an inheritance to children and grandchildren, but not in the tribulation period

### The Rich Man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19-31)
- Verse 19-21: A rich man clothed in purple and fine linen; a beggar named Lazarus at his gate full of sores
- Verse 22: Lazarus died and was carried by angels to Abraham's bosom; the rich man died and was buried
- Abraham's bosom was in the heart of the earth, where those who followed the law went before Christ's death
- The rich man's only recorded sin was having wealth
- Verse 23: In hell he lifted up his eyes and saw Abraham afar off and Lazarus in his bosom
- Those in hell could see Abraham's bosom, which may have been part of their punishment
- Verse 24: He asked for Lazarus to dip his finger in water to cool his tongue because of torment in flame
- Verse 25: Abraham said he had received good things in his lifetime while Lazarus had evil things; now reversed
- This isn't just about having good things versus bad things on earth
- The rich man was disobedient by keeping his wealth when he should have given it up
- Lazarus had nothing and was where he was supposed to be - a picture of dependency
- Lazarus was laid at the gate full of sores, probably couldn't work due to physical ailment, at the mercy of others
- This is a literal account, not a parable, because Jesus uses the proper name Lazarus
- Most parables don't use proper names
- One of the most vivid descriptions of hell is given over the sin of having wealth
- Verse 26: A great gulf is fixed between them so none can pass

### The Widow's Mite (Mark 12:38-44)
- Verse 41-42: Many rich people cast in much money; a poor widow threw in two mites
- Verse 43-44: Jesus said the widow cast in more than all the others because they gave from abundance but she gave all her living
- Logically, she didn't put in more amount than the wealthy people
- Her gift was more valuable because of her obedience - she got rid of everything
- It was easier for her because she had little; harder for the wealthy
- Lazarus had it easy when told to give up everything because he had nothing
- Zacchaeus the tax collector paid people back and gave away his wealth
- The amount wasn't important except that she gave all of it
- Ananias and Sapphira died immediately because the amount wasn't important, only that they held back part

### The Deceitfulness of Riches (Matthew 13:18-23)
- Matthew 13 contains seven parables dealing with Israel's rebellion (13 is the number of rebellion)
- Verse 22: Seed sown among thorns represents one who hears the word but "the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the word, and he becometh unfruitful"
- Of the four types of soil, only the last one (good ground) brings forth fruit
- In Bible college, the assignment was to determine which of the four was saved
- The correct answer is only the last one - the other three are wrong
- The third one received the word but when bad things happened, he was done
- Under grace, bad things can happen but we're still saved even if we backslide
- But these people had to endure to the end to be saved (Matthew)

### Paul's Teaching on Wealth (1 Timothy 6:3-19)
- Context: Dealing with masters and servants who both got saved but maintained their social positions
- Verse 1-2: Servants under the yoke should count masters worthy of honor; those with believing masters should serve them even better
- Ephesians 6 teaches servants to obey and work for masters as they would for Christ
- Verse 5: Some were teaching that gain is godliness - having more wealth showed more godliness
- Verse 7-8: "We brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. And having food and raiment let us therewith be content"
- Verse 9: "They that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare...which drown men in destruction and perdition"
- This refers specifically to servants who couldn't become rich because they couldn't own anything - they were owned by the master
- Verse 10: "The love of money is the root of all evil" - applies in this context to servants
- Verse 17-19: Instructions for "them that are rich in this world"
- They are NOT told to sell all they have and give to the poor
- They are told not to be high-minded or trust in uncertain riches but in God
- They should do good, be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate
- They are laying up a good foundation for "the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life"
- This shows the rich are allowed to have wealth but should use it to be a blessing and help others
- Servants weren't allowed to have wealth, but those who were rich could keep it under proper conditions

### Paul's Contentment in All Circumstances (Philippians 4:11-18)
- Verse 11-12: "I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound"
- Paul could do both - have abundance or barely enough to get by
- Verse 13: "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me"
- The church at Philippi was the only one that gave to Paul financially
- Verse 18: "I have all, and abound: I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things which were sent from you"
- God was pleased with what they gave Paul, but Paul was the one using it
- Because of their giving, Paul was abounding rather than being abased
- Most people would rather have abundance than barely enough to get by
- If going through the tribulation period, they would only get a certain amount of manna like in the wilderness
- Understanding the other side of dispensational truth explains itself

### Questions About Abraham's Bosom
- Question raised: Is Abraham's bosom in the heart of the earth or right outside of hell?
- Answer: We know it's in the heart of the earth, but exact relationship to hell is unclear
- There was a great gulf fixed between them where one couldn't get to the other
- But there was visibility - those in hell could see Abraham's bosom
- Speculative drawing: Hell might be in the center (bottomless) with the earth spinning, and Abraham's bosom in the heart of the earth nearby
- Could be off to the side or surrounding hell - we don't know exactly
- We just have the facts that there was a great gulf and they could see each other

### Why Give to the Poor When Entering Tribulation?
- Question: Why would Jesus tell them to give their stuff to the poor if He's trying to encourage people not to have any stuff?
- Answer: This was still during His earthly ministry before Acts 2, before He died on the cross
- Matthew is a transitional book transitioning from the law to the Messiah and into the tribulation period
- Similarly, Acts is transitional, and Hebrews transitions them into the tribulation period
- They were to give it to the poor but get where they needed to be and be ready
- John the Baptist preached "repent, the kingdom is at hand" - meaning it would happen in their lifetime
