## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a series on the "Guidelines of Grace," exploring how believers are called to live not under the law but according to grace through biblical principles. Drawing heavily from Romans 12 and Ephesians 4–5, he teaches that grace does not give specific commands for every situation but instead equips believers with principles that shape purpose, priorities, and ultimately a passionate Christian life. The sermon emphasizes personal ownership of one's faith and the importance of renewing the mind through Scripture.

## Scripture
Romans 12; Ephesians 4–5; 2 Corinthians 5 — These passages form the backbone of the sermon, providing practical principles for Christian living under grace, including the ministry of reconciliation, renewing of the mind, brotherly love, and walking circumspectly.

## Key Themes
- Living under grace rather than the law
- The role of biblical principles in guiding Christian conduct
- Purpose: glorifying God and the ministry of reconciliation (2 Corinthians 5:18)
- Renewing the mind (Romans 12:2) as foundational to Christian living
- Personal ownership of one's faith — making Scripture "yours"
- Moving from knowledge to understanding to wisdom
- Passion and fervency in serving the Lord
- Overcoming evil with good; eternal perspective in suffering

## Notes

### Living Under Grace vs. Law
- Believers are not under the Mosaic law; they live according to grace.
- The Gospels (Matthew–John) were written under the law as Christ prepared Israel for the kingdom; Paul's epistles contain the direct instructions for the church age.
- Grace does not eliminate responsibility — it increases it, calling believers to discern right action through principles rather than a checklist.

### Purpose Under Grace
- Primary purpose: glorify God.
- Secondary/practical purpose: the **ministry of reconciliation** (2 Corinthians 5:18) — believers are entrusted with the word of reconciliation so that others may hear and believe the gospel.
- Romans 10:13–15 reinforces this: "How shall they hear without a preacher?"
- Being "approved of God" is also a stated purpose for the believer's life and works.

### Principles from Romans 12
- **Verse 2:** Be transformed by the renewing of your mind — God does not dictate every decision; He calls believers to prove His will through a renewed mind.
- **Verse 9:** Let love be without dissimulation (hypocrisy); abhor evil, cleave to good.
- **Verse 10:** Be kindly affectioned with brotherly love; in honor, prefer one another — serve the needs of others in the body of Christ above your own.
- **Verse 11:** Not slothful in business; fervent in spirit, serving the Lord — take your spiritual work seriously and with zeal.
- **Verse 12:** Rejoicing in hope — the resurrection and rapture are the believer's hope; purposefully bringing this to mind maintains the right frame of mind through hardship.
- **Verses 13–15:** Distributing to the necessity of the saints; given to hospitality; bless those who persecute you.
- **Verse 17–21:** Recompense no man evil for evil; live peaceably with all men; avenge not yourselves; overcome evil with good.
  - Believers are not to seek personal revenge; rewards come from God, not from getting even with others.
  - Paul's example: he suffered shipwreck, beatings, and persecution without fighting back, keeping his focus on edifying the body and winning souls.

### Principles from Ephesians 4–5
- **Ephesians 4:22–23:** Put off the old man; be renewed in the spirit of your mind — the third reference in the sermon to the critical importance of right thinking.
- **Ephesians 4:26–27:** Do not give place to the devil — Satan does his greatest damage in the believer's mind; keeping Christ on the throne of the mind leaves no room for him.
- **Ephesians 4:30:** Grieve not the Holy Spirit of God — walking in the flesh and fellowshipping with darkness grieves the Spirit.
- **Ephesians 5:10–16:** Have no fellowship with unfruitful works of darkness; walk circumspectly (360-degree awareness), not as fools but as wise; redeem the time because the days are evil.
- **"Awake thou that sleepest"** (Ephesians 5:14): A call to examine whether one is spiritually alive and engaged or merely going through the motions.

### Purpose → Principles → Priorities → Passion
Pastor Simpson outlined a progression central to the series:
1. **Purpose** — Know why you exist as a believer.
2. **Principles** — Learn and internalize scriptural guidelines that shape conduct.
3. **Priorities** — Principles help establish what matters most (eternal over temporal).
4. **Passion** — The outcome of the first three; becoming zealous, steadfast, and unmovable in the work of the Lord (1 Corinthians 15:58).

### Personal Ownership of Faith
- A domineering, pressure-based pastoral approach may produce quicker outward results but does not develop mature, principle-driven believers.
- God's design under grace is liberty — believers must choose for themselves based on the Word, not merely external pressure or guilt.
- There must come a point where faith becomes personally owned: "I do this because I said so — and I said so based on what Scripture says."
- The goal is for knowledge to become understanding, and understanding to become wisdom, so that right living flows naturally from a scripturally renewed mind.
- Study (2 Timothy 2:15) is not merely academic — it is so that scriptural truth becomes lived wisdom.
