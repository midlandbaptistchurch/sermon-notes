## Summary
Hugh Taylor explores 1 Corinthians 10:13 as a foundational verse for Christian growth, examining temptation as a universal human experience. He emphasizes that temptation is common to all believers, that being tempted is not sinful, and that God provides a way to escape every temptation. Taylor uses personal examples and biblical references to illustrate how temptation manifests in daily life and how understanding God's promises equips us to resist it.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There is no temptation taking you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that you are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it."

## Key Themes
- Temptation is universal and common to all humanity
- Being tempted is not sin; yielding to temptation is
- God provides both a limit to temptation and a way of escape
- Temptation often strikes in our homes and with our closest relationships
- Understanding God's strategic and tactical will helps us resist temptation
- Spiritual growth requires remembering foundational truths about temptation
- God has given believers a complete toolkit to overcome temptation

## Notes

### The Tale of Three Buts
Taylor identifies three occurrences of "but" in 1 Corinthians 10:13, each serving a distinct purpose:
- First but: Temptation is common to all mankind
- Second but: God is faithful and will not allow temptation beyond our ability
- Third but: God provides a way of escape

### First But: Temptation is Common to All
- No temptation is unique to any individual; all are common to humanity
- Temptation is dangerous, subtle, and often disguised as appealing
- It is a marketing tool used in culture through media and advertising
- Temptation threatens God's tactical will in our individual lives
- Believers encounter temptation almost from birth through adulthood
- The verse emphasizes that not everyone is tempted by every temptation, but any temptation someone faces is not unique to them

### Personal Examples of Temptation
- Overcoming alcoholism: Taylor shares his testimony of victory over temptation to drink
- Anger and harsh speech: Acknowledges ongoing struggle with grumpiness and lashing out, particularly with family
- Incident with granddaughter: A specific moment when he angrily responded to a spilled drink, a memory his granddaughter still recalls years later
- Family discipline: Discusses how his son Travis overcame teenage rebellion by recognizing the predetermined outcome of disobedience

### Three Biblical Meanings of Temptation
1. **Trial or Test**: Temptation can mean to test one's faith, as seen in Genesis 22 with Abraham being tested to offer Isaac
   - Abraham's faith was proven through the test
   - Hebrews 11:17 clarifies that God's testing proved Abraham's belief in God's promises
   - God provides a way to pass the test through faith in His promises

2. *(Meanings two and three are introduced but not fully developed in this sermon)*

### God's Strategic and Tactical Will
- God's strategic will is His overall plan for creation, which will happen regardless of human response
- God's tactical will is His plan for each individual, family, church, and community
- Believers participate in God's strategic will by living according to His tactical will in our daily lives
- Temptation thwarts God's tactical will and prevents us from being active participants in His strategic plan for eternity

### Key Principles
- Being tempted is not sinful; it is a common human experience
- Temptation should not surprise or defeat believers because it is expected
- Spiritual smugness about overcoming one temptation can leave us vulnerable to others
- We cannot be immune to temptation on our own; we depend on God's power
- Remembering God's promises is essential to resisting temptation
- Parents and grandparents have a responsibility to teach younger generations how to handle temptation
- God has given believers an extensive toolkit for dealing with temptation victoriously
