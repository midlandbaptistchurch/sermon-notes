## Summary
Missionary Bryan Baggett preaches from 2 Corinthians 12 on how God's grace is not merely forgiveness but a sustaining presence and power that carries believers through suffering. Rather than always removing the problem, God's grace provides everything needed to endure and grow. Baggett argues that human weakness is not an obstacle to God's work but actually the very space in which His power shows up most clearly.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 12:7-11 — Paul's account of his thorn in the flesh, his three-fold plea for its removal, and God's response: "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness."

## Key Themes
- God's grace as a sustaining presence, not merely forgiveness
- Suffering and weakness as a meeting place with God
- Dependence on Christ rather than self-reliance
- God's power made visible through human inadequacy
- Surrender as a daily, ongoing posture before the Lord
- Opportunities for ministry found within personal crisis

## Notes

### Context: Paul's Thorn in the Flesh (v. 7)
Paul describes a serious, unspecified affliction — a "thorn in the flesh" — and beseeches the Lord three times to remove it. Baggett does not speculate on what the thorn was, noting that its specific nature is less important than what God says in response to it.

### The Promise: "My Grace Is Sufficient" (v. 9)
- Grace here means more than forgiveness; Baggett defines it as a *sustaining power and presence* — God walking through the trial with us, not merely meeting us on the other side.
- Illustrated by a missions trip to Venezuela where Baggett and his wife discovered that the Yacuana language had no word for "grace" and the concept of graciousness was absent from the culture — underscoring how foreign and radical biblical grace truly is.
- God's grace does not always remove the problem but always provides what is needed to endure and grow through it.

### God's Power Made Perfect in Weakness (v. 9)
- God's strength shows up most clearly when we are most aware of our own inadequacy.
- Baggett recounted two vivid personal illustrations:
  1. **The hospital trauma room**: After accidentally amputating the tip of his toe, Baggett found himself in a Vanderbilt ER. Because of neuropathy he felt no pain, allowing him to remain focused as the attending nurse revealed he had recently moved from California and felt God was trying to get his attention. The suffering became an unexpected ministry opportunity.
  2. **The Lebanese family dinner in Napa Valley**: While on deputation, Baggett was unexpectedly seated at dinner with a large Lebanese family and questioned about his faith for three hours. He found himself quoting scripture with a fluency and accuracy he could not explain in his own strength — an experience he and his wife attributed entirely to God working through him.
- Both stories illustrate the principle: weakness creates space for God's power to operate visibly.

### Paul's Shift in Perspective (v. 9b)
- Paul moves from *pleading* for the thorn's removal to *glorying* in his infirmities.
- He is not saying he loves pain or welcomes more suffering; he is saying he loves *what pain produces* — a deeper dependence on Christ.
- This is not manufactured humility but a genuine realization: "I need Him to do everything He has asked me to do."

### Spiritual Embrace: The Power of Christ Resting Upon Us (v. 9c)
- "Rest upon me" (the power of Christ) carries the meaning of *tabernacling* or *dwelling* — Christ's power settling down on us.
- This happens when we stop straining in our own strength and intentionally surrender space to Him.

### Weakness as a Meeting Place with God (v. 10)
- Paul is not saying weakness itself is strength; he is saying *dependence on God* is strength.
- God's power works in spite of difficulties, infirmities, and distractions — not because of personal capability.
- Baggett's mother, battling cancer during infusion treatments, prayed for her nurse — who then wept at her bedside and poured out her own struggles. Grace in suffering became a conduit of ministry to others.

### Practical Application
- Surrender is not a one-time event but a daily re-offering of ourselves to the Lord.
- Every day holds unknown people and moments where God may choose to work through a yielded believer.
- The resources of God's grace are not depleted by being drawn upon; they remain fully sufficient for every person who needs them.
- Baggett closed with a time of personal reflection, inviting listeners to identify areas where they are striving in their own strength and to lay those before the Lord.
