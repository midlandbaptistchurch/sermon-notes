## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the "Members in Particular" series by examining spiritual gifts from Romans 12, focusing on the seven gifts Paul lists and their application in church life today. Using the metaphor of a physical body and its parts, Taylor emphasizes that each member of the body of Christ has a specific role and responsibility, and that all members should strive to develop all seven gifts—not just one—to strengthen the body. The sermon particularly explores what it means to "rule with diligence" as a leader in the church, family, or workplace, balancing transparency with the need to protect innocent parties through graceful and merciful decision-making.

## Scripture
**Romans 12:3-8** — Paul exhorts believers to think soberly of themselves and explains that all members of the body have different offices and gifts, yet all are essential to the functioning of the whole.

**1 Corinthians 12:27** — "Now you are the body of Christ and members in particular."

## Key Themes
- **Members of the Body of Christ** — Each believer has a particular role and is necessary to the body
- **Spiritual Gifts** — The seven gifts in Romans 12 (prophecy, ministry, teaching, exhortation, giving, ruling, and mercy) should be sought after and developed by all believers
- **Leadership and Diligence** — Rulers (leaders in any context) must exercise their authority with steady, faithful application and care
- **Grace and Mercy** — Grace is not permission to sin but a responsibility to live in a way that demonstrates God's character; mercy must accompany diligent rulership
- **Unity and Interdependence** — No part of the body can say it is unimportant; all members need one another
- **Transparency in Leadership** — Leaders should explain their decisions and reasoning to those they lead, though sometimes confidentiality and protection of the innocent require discretion

## Notes

### The Seven Gifts of Romans 12:6-8
1. **Prophecy** — To foretell future events or to preach and instruct in religious doctrine; in modern times, applies to preaching and explaining Scripture
2. **Ministry** — To serve and care for people
3. **Teaching** — To explain Scripture and make truth understandable
4. **Exhortation** — To encourage and urge others toward good
5. **Giving** — To give generously and with simplicity
6. **Ruling** — To lead and exercise authority with diligence
7. **Mercy** — To show compassion with cheerfulness

### Key Points on Leadership and Rulership
- Leaders are responsible for "ruling with diligence"—steady application and constant effort without laziness or delay
- Diligence in rulership includes steady, faithful application to organizational business (whether in church, family, work, or civil government)
- A ruler should be transparent with those under their authority so they understand the "why" behind decisions
- However, a diligent ruler must sometimes withhold information to protect innocent parties from unnecessary worry or harm
- When conflict arises in an organization, it is the ruler's responsibility to address it according to scriptural guidelines
- A ruler should consult with advisors (like deacons) but ultimately bears the responsibility for decisions
- Protecting the innocent is sometimes more important than publicly punishing the guilty
- Members of an organization should bring concerns to their ruler privately rather than creating division by spreading rumors or partial information
- All believers, regardless of their specific gifting, should seek to develop these seven gifts and manifest them in their lives

### Application to Pastor Jim Payne
Taylor holds up the church pastor as an example of someone who has developed all seven gifts, serving as "the glue" that holds the church together and keeps it focused on its mission.

### Four Areas of Government
Taylor references four spheres where rulership applies:
1. Self-government (ruling oneself)
2. Family government (parental authority)
3. Church government (pastoral and elder leadership)
4. Civil government (civil authority)
