## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne opens a survey of the book of Colossians by placing it within its historical and canonical context as one of Paul's prison epistles, written to a church Paul had never personally visited. He traces Paul's ministry timeline from his imprisonment in Acts 28 through his later letters, and introduces the doctrinal framework of Colossians as a letter of correction — warning believers against philosophy and vain deceit — with a focus on the preeminence of Jesus Christ.

## Scripture
Colossians 1:1-8 — Paul's salutation and opening to the saints and faithful brethren at Colossae, a church he did not found personally but about which he had deep concern.

## Key Themes
- Colossians as one of Paul's five prison epistles
- The historical and geographical setting of Colossae within the region of Asia (modern-day western Turkey)
- The church at Colossae founded by Epaphras, who was saved through Paul's ministry in Rome
- Paul's letter trilogies: Romans/Corinthians/Galatians (the cross) and Ephesians/Philippians/Colossians (the risen Christ)
- Colossians written as a letter of correction against false philosophy and tradition
- The preeminence of Jesus Christ as the central purpose of God's plan
- Paul's apostleship and the mystery revealed to him for the Gentiles
- The call to faithfulness in handling the Word of God

## Notes
- Colossians is one of four prison epistles written during Paul's first Roman imprisonment (Acts 28), alongside Ephesians, Philippians, and Philemon.
- Paul had never personally visited Colossae; he writes to people who had not seen his face in the flesh (Colossians 2:1).
- Epaphras traveled to Rome, was saved through Paul's ministry, returned to Colossae, and planted the church there — possibly meeting in the home of Philemon.
- Paul's ministry timeline: first imprisonment → release → ministry in Crete (Titus) and Ephesus (Timothy) → writes 1 Timothy and Titus → re-arrested → writes 2 Timothy (suggested as the last book of the Bible written, c. 67 AD).
- The city of Ephesus served as a hub; during Paul's roughly three-year ministry there (Acts 19), the Word of God spread throughout all of Asia, reaching cities including Colossae, Hierapolis, and Laodicea.
- Paul writes Colossians to warn the believers: "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ" (Colossians 2:8).
- Letter trilogies framework:
  - Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians → centered on the cross and the gospel of salvation
  - Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians → centered on the risen, exalted Christ and the body of Christ
- Ephesians and Colossians described as "twin sisters" in doctrinal content.
- The nature and scope of Colossians is *correction* — bringing believers back to the sufficiency of Christ.
- God's overarching will: that in all things Christ might have the preeminence (Colossians 1:18).
- Paul identifies himself as an apostle by the will of God (Colossians 1:1); his unique role is as apostle to the Gentiles, entrusted with the revelation of the mystery of the body of Christ (Ephesians 3:1-6).
- The letter is addressed to "the saints and faithful brethren" — all believers are saints (set apart), but Paul writes especially with the faithful in mind, those who handle the depth of the Word with care (cf. 1 Corinthians 4:1-2; 2 Timothy 2:2).
- Grace and peace in the salutation reflect the satisfaction of God the Father with the atoning work of Christ.
- This is Part 1 of a multi-week series on Colossians.
