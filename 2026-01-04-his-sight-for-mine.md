## Summary
Evan Watt explores Luke 15's three parables—the lost sheep, lost coin, and prodigal son—to challenge listeners to see others through God's eyes rather than their own. He emphasizes that just as God values and seeks the lost, we must learn to love sinners and forgive fellow believers by adopting Christ's perspective of worth and compassion.

## Scripture
Luke 15 — Three parables Jesus tells to publicans, sinners, and the self-righteous Pharisees and scribes, illustrating God's love for all people and his desire to seek and save the lost.

## Key Themes
- Seeing others through God's eyes, not our own perspective
- God's love and mercy extend to all people
- The danger of self-righteousness (the elder brother)
- Loving the lost by recognizing their value to God
- Forgiving fellow believers as Christ forgave us
- Humility as the foundation for Christ-like perspective
- The Gospel of Luke's emphasis on Jesus coming for everyone

## Notes

### The Gospel of Luke's Emphasis
Luke's gospel uniquely emphasizes that Jesus came for all people. Key passages include Luke 2:10 (good tidings for all people), Luke 2:29-32 (salvation prepared for all people), and Luke 3:6 (all flesh shall see God's salvation). This context is essential for understanding the three parables.

### Understanding Parables
A parable is not just "an earthly story with a heavenly meaning"—it's more specifically "an illustration of a specific point in a specific context." The three parables don't stand alone; they work together building toward a greater point.

### The Lost Sheep and Lost Coin
The first two parables establish that:
- A shepherd leaves 99 sheep to find 1 lost sheep, carries it home with rejoicing
- A woman lights a candle and searches diligently for 1 lost piece of silver, rejoices when found
- The comparison: Just as you see the sheep and coin as precious, God sees sinners as precious
- Heaven rejoices when even one sinner repents

### The Parable of the Prodigal Son
The longest parable introduces three characters:

**The Younger Son:**
- Asks his father for his inheritance immediately (implying he wishes his father dead)
- Wastes his substance with riotous living
- Falls into poverty and shame, feeding pigs
- Comes to himself and determines to return home, rehearsing his confession
- His father sees him far off, runs to embrace him, and restores him without letting him finish his confession
- The father celebrates with a feast

**The Father:**
- Represents God's love and acceptance
- Runs to embrace his wayward son
- Cuts off the son's self-deprecating confession with forgiveness and restoration
- Shows that God receives repentant sinners immediately and completely

**The Elder Brother:**
- Represents the self-righteous Pharisees and scribes
- Has always obeyed and served faithfully
- Becomes angry and refuses to join the celebration
- Argues that he deserves more than his brother
- Is characterized by pride, self-righteousness, and comparison
- The parable's real challenge is directed at the elder brother's heart

### Application: Two Practical Ways to Apply This Truth

**1. Loving the Lost**
- See them through God's eyes, not through judgment and condemnation
- Remember: "Hate the sin, love the sinner" is impossible without seeing them as God does
- A sinner is "a soul that has been purposefully created" and "that he came for and that he died for and that he loves"
- Recognize their inherent value and worth in God's sight

**2. Forgiving Fellow Believers**
- Forgiveness goes against human nature; it requires seeing through God's eyes
- God sets the standard for forgiveness: "Even as God for Christ's sake have forgiven you" (Ephesians 4:32)
- "Even as Christ forgave you so also do ye" (Colossians 3:13)
- Do not wish judgment on those who hurt us—someone has already been judged and punished for all sin: Christ
- Look at others as God looks at them: through the lens of Christ's forgiveness

### The Core Message
We easily become the elder brother—self-righteous, judgmental, comparing ourselves to others. To follow Christ means:
- Humbling ourselves (Philippians 2:3-5)
- Adopting Christ's mind, which emptied itself and took the form of a servant
- Recognizing that without God we are the same as those we judge
- Understanding that Christ came specifically for sinners (1 Timothy 1:15)
