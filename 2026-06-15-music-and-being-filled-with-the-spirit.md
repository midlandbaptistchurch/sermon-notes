## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues an ongoing study in Ephesians, focusing on Ephesians 5:18-19 and the connection between being filled with the Holy Spirit and the role of music in the Christian life. He draws a parallel between Ephesians 5:18-19 and Colossians 3:16, arguing that psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs are not merely worship tools but genuine teaching instruments. Simpson challenges the congregation to develop doctrinal awareness when engaging with Christian music, recognizing that many songs — while well-intentioned — contain theological inaccuracies or misapplied scripture.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:18-19 — Paul's instruction to be filled with the Spirit, expressed outwardly through speaking to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, and making melody in the heart to the Lord. The parallel passage in Colossians 3:16 is also examined.

## Key Themes
- Being filled with the Holy Spirit is a progressive, choice-driven process for believers today
- Yielding to righteousness is a prerequisite to being filled with the Spirit
- Psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs function as teaching and admonishing tools, not merely worship aids
- Christian music should be held to the same doctrinal standard as preaching
- Believers have a responsibility to discern theological accuracy in the songs they sing and hear
- The Word of Christ dwelling richly (Colossians 3:16) and being filled with the Spirit (Ephesians 5:18) are parallel and complementary commands

## Notes
- Simpson recaps the previous week's teaching on the word "filled," distinguishing Spirit-filling under grace from Spirit-filling in the kingdom program
- Being filled with the Spirit today involves the believer's active choice, not a sovereign overwhelming as seen in Acts
- Three negative examples of "being filled" are cited: sorrow (John 16:6), wrath (Luke 4:28), and envy (Acts 13, implied)
- Paul's prayers for believers include being filled with: fruits of righteousness (Philippians 1:11), the fullness of God (Ephesians 3:19), and knowledge of His will (Colossians 1:9)
- Simpson observes that rightly divided, doctrinally sound Christian music is extremely rare — even rarer than doctrinally sound preaching
- He uses the example of the song referencing the "midnight cry" as an instance of a scripturally misapplied lyric (applying a Second Coming reference to the Rapture context)
- The word "mansion" appearing frequently in hymnody despite limited biblical usage is noted as an example of doctrinal imprecision in music
- Simpson does not call for abandoning hymns with imperfections, but urges the congregation to develop awareness and discernment
- He announces a brief multi-week study on music in the Bible to follow
- Closing prayer asks God for direction, discernment, and the ability to "prove all things and hold fast that which is good"
