## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne examines 1 Thessalonians, emphasizing that this letter focuses on the coming of Christ for the church and our deliverance from the wrath to come. He contrasts Romans and Galatians (which teach about the cross and justification) and Ephesians and Colossians (which teach about the church as the body of Christ) with 1 and 2 Thessalonians, which specifically address Christ's coming for His church. The sermon emphasizes that believers are not appointed to wrath but to salvation from the tribulation period.

## Scripture
1 Thessalonians 1:10 — "And to wait for his son from heaven, whom he raised from the dead, even Jesus, which delivered us from the wrath to come."

## Key Themes
- **Deliverance from Wrath**: Believers are not appointed to the wrath of the tribulation period but will be caught up before God pours out His judgment on the earth.
- **The Rapture vs. The Revelation**: The rapture (Christ coming for the church) is distinct from the revelation (Christ's second coming to earth); only Paul teaches about the rapture.
- **The Lamb of God's Dual Nature**: Christ as the Lamb represents both sacrificial atonement and the executor of God's wrath and vengeance.
- **The Thessalonian Church as a Model**: Despite being new believers who faced great persecution, the Thessalonians became an example of faith, love, and hope to all believers in Macedonia and Achaia.

## Notes

### Background and Geography of Paul's Second Journey
- Paul was shamefully treated at Philippi (1 Thessalonians 2:2), referring to being thrown in jail for casting out a spirit of divination
- After Philippi, Paul went to Thessalonica, then Berea, Athens, and finally Corinth
- Paul was only in Thessalonica for less than a month (about three weeks/three Sabbath days)
- While at Athens, Paul sent Timothy and Silas back to Thessalonica
- Timothy and Silas returned to Paul at Corinth with a sterling report and a love offering
- This financial support stirred Paul to give his full effort to ministry instead of tent-making

### The Three Great Doctrines in Paul's Letters
- **The Cross**: Found in Romans and Galatians (justification and doctrine)
- **The Church**: Found in Ephesians, Colossians, and Philippians (the body of Christ)
- **The Coming**: Found in 1 and 2 Thessalonians (Christ's coming for the church)

### Distinguishing the Rapture from the Second Coming
- 2 Thessalonians 1:7 speaks of Christ being "revealed from heaven with his mighty angels" (the second coming)
- 2 Thessalonians 2:1 speaks of "the coming of our Lord and by our gathering together unto him" (the rapture)
- The rapture is when Christ stops in the clouds; the second coming is when He returns all the way to earth
- This dispensation began with Christ stopping in the clouds (Acts 9, saving Paul) and will end with Christ stopping in the clouds (calling up the church)

### Salvation from Wrath
- 1 Thessalonians 5:9: "For God hath not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation"
- This salvation is deliverance from the wrath of the tribulation period, not from sin
- Wrath is not hell; wrath is the expression of God's anger, vengeance, and judgment poured out during the tribulation
- Hell is a place; wrath is an event

### Commendation of the Thessalonian Church
- Paul thanked God for their "work of faith, labor of love and patience of hope" (1 Thessalonians 1:3)
- Patience of hope means waiting for something with expectation of it happening
- New believers should be taught about the hope of Christ's coming early in their discipleship
- 1 Thessalonians 1:4: "knowing, brethren beloved, your election of God"—they were elected to serve Him
- The gospel came to them "not in word only, but also in power and in the Holy Ghost and in much assurance" (1:5)

### The Thessalonians' Testimony
- They became "followers of us and of the Lord" (1:6)—Paul is the pattern
- They "received the word in much affliction" (1:6), referring to persecution described in Acts 17
- They became "ensamples to all that believe in Macedonia and Achaia" (1:7)
- Their faith "sounded out" not only in Macedonia and Achaia but "in every place" (1:8)
- They "turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God" (1:9)

### The Context of Idol Worship
- In Roman and Greek culture, there were multifarious pagan gods and heathen practices
- Temples filled with prostitutes where men performed immorality under the guise of worship
- Marriage was looked at as something to take or leave
- Society was terrible at this time
- Some would embrace Christianity but try to keep their idols too
- The Thessalonians genuinely turned from idols, which Paul commended

### "Behold the Lamb of God"
- John 1:29: "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world"
- John the Baptist could not have been thinking about the cross (he didn't know about it yet)
- The Lamb of God has a twofold understanding: sacrificial and wrathful
- Revelation 6:16: People will hide "from the wrath of the Lamb"
- Christ as Lamb represents both humble sacrifice and executor of wrath

### The Day of the Lord and Wrath
- Zephaniah 1:14-15: "The great day of the Lord is near...That day is a day of wrath"
- Isaiah 13:9: "Behold, the day of the Lord cometh, cruel both with wrath and fierce anger, to lay the land desolate: and he shall destroy the sinners thereof out of it"
- When Christ sets up His kingdom, He will "destroy the sinners" and remove sin from the world
- The kingdom will be righteous and sinless

### Taking Away Sin from the Earth
- Matthew 13:39-41: At the end of the world, angels will "gather out of his kingdom all things that offend and them which do iniquity"
- The tares (children of the wicked one) will be "gathered and burned in the fire"
- This is the removal of sinners from the earth before the kingdom is established

### Luke 17 and the Revelation
- Luke 17:30: "Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed"
- "Revelation" means He is revealed (every eye shall see Him, Revelation 1:8-9)
- Luke 17:34-36: "One shall be taken, and the other shall be left"—this occurs both at night and during the day (because the earth is round)
- This is NOT the rapture; traditional Baptist doctrine incorrectly interprets this as the rapture
- Luke 17:37: "Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together"—the taken ones are gathered for judgment

### The Supper of the Great God
- Revelation 19:17-18: An angel invites "all the fowls that fly in the midst of heaven" to "the supper of the great God" to eat "the flesh of kings...captains, and mighty men"
- This is God preparing a meal of human flesh for the birds
- The Lord collects the wicked, deposits them in a place of judgment, and the birds eat their flesh

### Cleanup After Armageddon
- Ezekiel 39:12-15: It will take seven months to cleanse the land after the battle of Armageddon
- Men will be employed continually to pass through the land burying bones
- When they see a man's bone, they will set up a sign until it's buried in the valley
- The flesh will already have been eaten by the birds

### The Wrath to Come
- John the Baptist said to the Pharisees and Sadducees: "O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?" (Matthew 3:7)
- Isaiah 61:2: "To proclaim the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God"
- When Jesus read this scripture in the synagogue, He only read the first part (the acceptable year) and stopped
- The day of vengeance is still to come

### Our Hope
- 1 Thessalonians 1:10: We "wait for his son from heaven...which delivered us from the wrath to come"
- We have not been appointed to wrath
- Before wrath is poured out, Christ will call us up: "Come up hither"
- We will be caught up "in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump"
- We turn to God from idols to serve the living and true God and to wait for His dear son
