## Summary
In this fifth installment of the Survey of Colossians series, Pastor Jim Payne works through Colossians chapter 2, examining Paul's conflict on behalf of the Colossian church and his desire that believers reach a full assurance of understanding in the mystery of Christ. The sermon identifies four false teachings Paul warns against — philosophy, legalism, mysticism, and asceticism — and closes with an emphasis on the believer's completeness in Christ and the importance of being rooted, built up, and established in the faith.

## Scripture
Colossians 2 — Paul's appeal to the Colossian church to be knit together in love, to acknowledge the mystery of God found in Christ, and to guard against teachings that undermine Christ's all-sufficiency.

## Key Themes
- The supremacy and all-sufficiency of Christ as Head of the body
- The mystery of God revealed through Paul's gospel
- Full assurance of understanding as the goal of the local church
- Four false teachings to guard against: philosophy, legalism, mysticism, and asceticism
- Completeness in Christ — believers need nothing added to what he has done
- Being rooted, built up, and established in the faith
- The importance of rightly dividing the Bible and judging all teaching

## Notes
**Context of Colossians among the Prison Epistles**
- Ephesians, Philippians, and Colossians are the Prison Epistles, focused on the doctrine of the church — the body of Christ.
- Earlier epistles (Romans, Corinthians, Galatians) focused on the cross; the Prison Epistles focus on the church.
- Colossians specifically emphasizes the person, preeminence, and supremacy of Christ as Head of the body.
- Colossians is described as a corrective epistle: Ephesians = doctrine, Philippians = reproof, Colossians = correction.

**Background: Epaphras's Report to Paul**
- Epaphras traveled from Colossae to Rome to give Paul a firsthand report of the church.
- Paul had never visited Colossae personally but felt deep conflict and concern for them based on this report.
- This report prompted Paul, by revelation and inspiration, to write the epistle.

**Colossians 2:1 — Paul's Conflict**
- Paul opens chapter 2 with "for I would that ye knew what great conflict I have for you," connecting back to his description of his ministry in 1:24–25.
- His conflict was a more than 20-year engagement on behalf of the body of Christ.
- His goal: that their hearts might be comforted, being knit together in love.

**Full Assurance of Understanding (2:2)**
- Paul's desire is not emotionalism but that believers reach "all riches of the full assurance of understanding."
- This leads to "the acknowledgement of the mystery of God, and of the Father, and of Christ."
- Acknowledgement means owning the truth — not just knowing it, but claiming it.
- In Christ "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (2:3).
- The mystery was received by Paul through special revelation (seeing Christ directly) and is received by believers today through the Spirit working through Scripture.

**Four False Teachings Paul Warns Against**
1. **Philosophy (2:8)** — "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy." Earthly, sensual wisdom that appeals to the senses and exalts man rather than Christ. Reflected today in self-focused, philosophical preaching.
2. **Legalism (2:16–17)** — "Let no man therefore judge you in meat or drink, or in respect of a holy day, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days." Legalism is the effort to combine grace with works — the idea that grace alone is not enough. The law was a shadow pointing to Christ.
3. **Mysticism (2:18)** — "Let no man beguile you of your reward in a voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into those things which he hath not seen." Seeking hidden knowledge through angels or spiritual experiences. Pastor Payne noted that the ministry of angels has changed in this dispensation — God's message today comes through His word, not angelic visitation.
4. **Asceticism (2:20–23)** — "Touch not, taste not, handle not." Rigid self-denial and bodily neglect that has "a show of wisdom" but offers no true spiritual benefit. Examples include monastic practices and extreme physical penance.

**Completeness in Christ (2:9–10)**
- "In him dwelleth all the fullness of the Godhead bodily" — the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit all manifest in Christ.
- "And ye are complete in him, which is the head" — because all fullness is in Christ and believers are in him, nothing need be added.

**Walking in Christ (2:6–7)**
- "As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him."
- Believers are to be rooted (agricultural metaphor) and built up (construction metaphor) in him.
- "Established in the faith as ye have been taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving."
- To be established: first receiving the foundation, then firmly fixing oneself upon it.

**Romans 16:25 — Paul's Gospel and the Mystery**
- Pastor Payne concluded by turning to Romans 16:25, where Paul speaks of being "able to establish you according to my gospel and the preaching of Jesus Christ according to the revelation of the mystery."
- Understanding Paul's gospel in the full context of the Bible — including the distinction between the Gospel of the Kingdom and Paul's gospel — is the key to rightly interpreting all of Scripture.
- The preaching of Christ according to the mystery means: Christ rose not to sit on David's throne for the church, but to justify believers freely forever.

**Responsibility to Judge Teaching**
- Every believer has a responsibility to judge what comes from any pulpit, including their own pastor's.
- A preacher who does not understand the mystery and rightly divide the Bible will introduce skewed error — not just in major doctrines but in nuances throughout.
