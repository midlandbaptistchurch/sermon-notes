## Summary
John Moe, a former long-time member of Midland Baptist Church, shares an update on his Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, detailing his work as an integrated chaplain serving law enforcement, fire, EMS, and dispatch personnel in Clinton County, Michigan. He recounts specific ministry moments — including a deputy's conversion, a Bible study for officers, critical incident debriefs, and a veteran's funeral — illustrating the unique access an integrated chaplain has to minister Christ to first responders. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, John challenges the congregation to appreciate the unrelenting calling of the 0.69% of Americans who serve as first responders and to pray for workers in this mission field.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focuses especially on verse 6, "for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing," using the phrase "attending continually" to describe the unrelenting burden borne by law enforcement and first responders and to frame their work as a God-ordained calling.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of earning trust from the inside out
- Sharing the gospel clearly in every ministry context, including Bible studies, funerals, and critical incidents
- The mental and emotional toll of first responders "attending continually" upon others' worst days
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership (John's chaplaincy and Sue's Victim Support Team volunteer work)
- Prayer and financial support needs for the Whole Armour ministry

## Notes
**John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy**
- Former long-time member of Midland Baptist; moved to Lansing in 2004 to run a business.
- Wrote the civil service exam for Michigan State Police in 1989 but pursued agriculture instead; reflects that God needed to mature his character first.
- Met a lieutenant through business in 2010; began civilian ride-alongs and accumulated ~300 hours in a patrol car (2010–2013).
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013; recognized for his Christian witness and asked to become chaplain in 2014.
- Undertook extensive training: police academy, public safety courses, SWAT basic school (2022), Marine Patrol state academy (2025), and became a fully deputized deputy.
- Currently serves Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, and Clinton County Special Operations Team as primary assignments, with open doors to several additional agencies, fire departments, EMS, and 911 dispatch — a reach of over 350 people.

**Sue Moe's Role**
- Volunteers with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST).
- Deployed to critical incident scenes (suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support families.
- Her presence at scenes complements John's chaplaincy, building relationships with officers' spouses and opening doors for dinner fellowships and deeper pastoral care.
- Prayed aloud with a grieving family — including the sheriff, a sergeant, and a deputy — at the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old who drowned after breaking through ice on a pond.

**Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy**
- Ceremonial chaplains are often pastors who appear only for official functions with no day-to-day relational presence.
- John's integrated model — sworn reserve status, deputy credentials, SWAT membership, and peer assistance team leadership — places him organically within the agencies, earning trust that opens doors for genuine ministry.
- Key quote: "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust. So when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

**Key Ministry Moments Shared**
- *Deputy's conversion and baptism:* A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to say he had accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning before the Sunday evening service.
- *Bible study for deputies (Jan–March 2026):* Eight deputies attended regularly — the first such study ever conducted in the county. Participants brought multiple Bible versions; John gently demonstrated differences while keeping the focus on the gospel. Final session included a full gospel presentation from Genesis to the New Testament. One deputy, Greg, wrestled with the possibility of his grandparents' murderers being forgiven — a conversation John is still gently continuing.
- *Four of those deputies attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church* the Sunday before this service and heard a gospel presentation.
- *MSU active shooter (2023):* Clinton County SWAT was first tactical boots on the ground. John conducted a critical incident stress debrief the following week for team members who had cleared the student union and encountered deceased students.
- *Ice drowning:* Grandfather and five-year-old lost; John diffused with deputies on scene while Sue prayed with the entire family and law enforcement present.
- *Veteran's suicide:* Son of a county firefighter (and brother of the SWAT medic) died by suicide. John conducted the funeral, delivering a clear gospel presentation to a mixed, largely unchurched crowd despite the service beginning with a song containing profanity — because the family chose it.
- *SWAT pre-raid prayer:* Before a coordinated multi-agency arrest operation for a cold-case hunting murder, John's SWAT commander asked him to pray. He led ~75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies in prayer for safety and justice.
- *Three-hour gospel conversation:* During a recent SWAT call-out, John sat in a gear truck with a non-operational team member for three hours, sharing the Lord.
- *Plane crash, Bath Township:* Responded and conducted a diffusal with law enforcement and firefighters on scene.
- *Drunk driver hitting a family of 16:* Six hours on scene; prayed with deputies, a lieutenant, and firefighters; months of follow-up pastoral care for the first-arriving deputy.
- *Marital counseling for officers:* Two young officers with new babies called John for marital help; he introduced the concept of the five love languages as a practical entry point.

**Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement**
- Verse 1: All authority is ordained of God.
- Verse 2: Resisting governing authority is resisting God's ordinance.
- Verse 3: Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil.
- Verse 4: The ruler is a "minister of God" — the word minister is used twice — carrying the sword to execute wrath on evildoers.
- Verse 6 (key focus): "They are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing." The phrase "attending continually" captures the unrelenting, around-the-clock, holiday-or-not, sick-or-well nature of the first responder's call.
- Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (law enforcement, fire, EMS, dispatch), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- John's mission statement: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

**Ministry Needs**
- John turned down a VP of Business Development role and a full-time community relations officer position with benefits in order to maintain the flexibility required by chaplaincy.
- Receives approximately 15 hours/week of hourly compensation from Bath Township; shares $5,000 in state Marine Patrol funding with another part-time deputy — otherwise largely self-funded.
- Financial partnership and, above all, prayer support are needed.
- Urgent prayer need: finding a Bible-believing chaplain candidate to apprentice and eventually expand the ministry to agencies already requesting coverage.
- Prayer cards available at the visitor's desk.
