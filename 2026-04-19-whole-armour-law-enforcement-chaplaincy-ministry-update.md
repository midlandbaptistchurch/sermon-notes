## Summary
Missionary John Moe, a law enforcement chaplain serving in Clinton County, Michigan, presented a ministry update to Midland Baptist Church on the evening of April 19, 2026. He described his journey from civilian ride-alongs to becoming a sworn reserve officer, SWAT-certified operator-chaplain, and county Marine Deputy, explaining how his integrated model of chaplaincy — serving from the inside rather than the periphery — has opened doors to share the gospel with officers, deputies, and their families. His wife Sue also shared how her volunteer work with the County Victim Support Team complements John's chaplaincy by ministering directly to families at critical incident scenes.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on God-ordained governmental authority, with particular emphasis on verse 4 (rulers as "ministers of God" bearing the sword) and verse 6 ("attending continually upon this very thing"), which John used to articulate both the divine calling of law enforcement officers and the unrelenting weight of their service.

## Key Themes
- The integrated law enforcement chaplain model versus ceremonial chaplaincy
- Law enforcement officers as God-ordained ministers (Romans 13)
- Earning trust as the foundation of effective ministry
- The gospel presented clearly in unlikely settings
- Wives and volunteers as essential ministry partners
- Critical incident stress debriefing and pastoral care
- The "attending continually" burden carried by first responders
- Connecting the called to the One who called them

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family grew up in the church and youth group before moving to Lansing in 2004.
- Owns a business in Lansing, now managed by his son; previously held two corporate traveling positions.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 and scored well but pursued an agricultural business career instead.
- In 2010, met a township lieutenant through business and began civilian ride-alongs; logged approximately 300 hours in a patrol car by 2013.
- 2013: Became a sworn reserve officer.
- 2014: Asked by a sergeant to serve as police chaplain; initially said no out of humility, then accepted after prayer.
- 2014–2015: Attended police academy and a public services chaplain academy.
- 2016: Withdrew from academy for a year due to a house fire; resumed afterward.
- 2017: Appointed to the county SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster.
- 2022: Completed basic SWAT school; now a basic SWAT-certified operator-chaplain-quartermaster.
- 2023: Began serving as chaplain for the county sheriff's office.
- 2025: Attended state training academy for Marine Patrol; became a fully deputized deputy.
- Also runs the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office (a sergeant and three deputies focused on officer wellness).
- Currently serves Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations Team, and is frequently called by three or four other county agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 dispatch center — more than 350 people across those organizations.

### Sue Moe's Ministry — Victim Support Team
- Sue was motivated to join the County Victim Support Advocates Team after her nephew died of a fentanyl/heroin overdose in Daytona Beach at age 32; she was struck by the disconnect between law enforcement notification and family support.
- Has served as a VST volunteer for approximately four years despite no prior grief or trauma training (she is a first-grade teacher).
- Responds to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents, sometimes for three to four hours at a time.
- Her presence at scenes has opened relationships with deputies' wives and families, creating ministry inroads for the couple together.
- At one critical incident (a grandfather and five-year-old who fell through ice, both fatally), Sue prayed aloud with the grieving family, the sheriff, a sergeant, and a deputy all present in the room — a moment John witnessed from the doorway.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- A ceremonial chaplain is typically a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart; they may only be called in extreme situations and often lack relational trust with the team.
- An integrated chaplain is organically embedded: sworn status, SWAT membership, peer assistance team leadership — ministry flows from the inside out.
- John's view: if chaplaincy is not actively looking for opportunities to point people toward Christ, it is falling short of its purpose. He expressed frustration with the International Conference of Police Chaplains' stance against chaplains being specific about their faith.
- He has found a more compatible organization, Heroic Deeds (based in Ohio), which is openly Christ-centered; he plans to attend a meeting with them in August.

### Ministry Fruit and Stories
- A deputy named Ryan called John after pulling over on the road and trusting Christ as Savior; John was his first phone call. John and Sue attended Ryan's baptism the morning of this presentation.
- John led an eight-week Bible study for the county (January–mid-March 2026), the first ever held in that county. Eight deputies attended regularly, representing a wide spectrum of religious backgrounds (Catholic, Church of God, Wesleyan/Nazarene, modern evangelical, and some with no clear salvation).
  - Night one focused on the authority and objective truth of Scripture, using the King James Bible while engaging those who brought other versions.
  - The final night included a clear gospel presentation moving from Genesis through the New Testament.
  - One deputy, who had attended church his whole life, said he had never heard that justification is entirely Christ's work at the cross — not human merit. John distinguished justification (100% Christ's work) from sanctification (the ongoing work of rooting out the world).
  - Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, challenged whether God could forgive their killer. John engaged honestly and pastorally, acknowledging his own human response while pointing to God's grace.
  - Four of the Bible study deputies attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church the Sunday before this presentation; two of them are not yet saved.
- John conducted a Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD) for the Clinton County SWAT team after they were first responders at the Michigan State University active shooter event.
- Responded to the scene of a drowning involving a grandfather and five-year-old; followed up with the family over subsequent days.
- Ministered to a firefighter whose veteran son died by suicide; conducted the memorial service (standing room only, approximately 150 attendees), including a clear gospel presentation to a largely unchurched crowd.
- Counseled two young officers separately on marital strain following the birth of a child, including a discussion of the five love languages.
- Responded to a plane crash in Bath Township (three fatalities); the plane would have crashed on John's property had it flown five more seconds.
- Responded to three officer-involved shootings in the county.
- Responded to a crash where a drunk driver struck a family of 16, killing two instantly; spent six hours on scene including evidence collection, and prayed with deputies, a lieutenant, and firefighters before follow-up debriefing that lasted months.
- At a coordinated multi-agency takedown (approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies) related to a Bath Township murder cold case, the SWAT commander asked John to pray before they rolled out; the entire room bowed their heads.
- Two weeks before this presentation, accompanied a light-duty deputy on a call-out; they spent three hours talking about the Lord in the truck.
- Received an inquiry from a neighboring county agency asking him to serve as their chaplain as well — he currently has no capacity to take on additional agencies.

### Romans 13 — The Divine Call of Law Enforcement
- Verse 1: All governing powers are ordained of God.
- Verse 2: Resisting governing authority is resisting God's ordinance.
- Verse 3: Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil.
- Verse 4: The ruler is "a minister of God" — bearing the sword not in vain, executing wrath on evildoers. Law enforcement officers are ministers in this biblical sense.
- Verse 6: "Attending continually upon this very thing" — John emphasized the unrelenting nature of first-responder service: no holidays, no weather exemptions, no pause for personal hardships.
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first-responder role (firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- John's mission statement: *The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the One who did the calling — to take the chapel into their work environment and be an ambassador for Christ in this unique mission field.*

### Ministry Needs
- Financial: John receives partial hourly compensation from the township for approximately 15 hours per week in a community relations/chaplaincy hybrid role, and shares $5,000 in state funding for the Marine Deputy role. No health benefits or retirement. Turned down a corporate VP role and a full-time law enforcement position with benefits in order to maintain the flexibility his calling requires.
- Prayer: The greatest need is for prayer support.
- Personnel: It is extremely difficult to find people called and suited to law enforcement chaplaincy. John is already serving fire departments that need a chaplain and cannot cover all the need himself. He asked the church to pray for the Lord to raise up additional chaplains.
- Prayer cards are available at the visitors desk.
