## Summary
John Moe, a former longtime member of Midland Baptist Church now serving as a full-time integrated law enforcement chaplain in Clinton County, Michigan, gave a ministry update on Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy. He shared how God called him from a business career into this unique mission field, described the scope of his ministry to police, sheriff's deputies, SWAT teams, fire departments, and 911 dispatchers, and explained how his wife Sue partners with him through the county Victim Support Team. Anchoring his message in Romans 13:1-6, John emphasized that law enforcement officers are God's ministers "attending continually" and that his calling is to connect those officers to the God who called them.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on governing authorities as ministers of God, with particular focus on verse 6: "for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing" — used to illustrate the unrelenting, divinely ordained nature of law enforcement service.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as ministry (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — building trust from the inside out
- Gospel witness and evangelism within law enforcement communities
- The weight of continual vigilance carried by the 0.7% of the population who serve as first responders
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership (John's chaplaincy + Sue's Victim Support Team work)
- Critical incident stress debriefing and peer support for officers
- Connecting the called to the One who called them

## Notes

### John's Background and Call
- Former business owner in Lansing; stepped away from corporate work two years prior when God made his chaplaincy calling unmistakably clear.
- Wrote the civil service exam for the Michigan State Police in 1989 but pursued an agricultural business career instead; God used that detour to mature him for this ministry.
- Began ride-alongs in 2010 after meeting a lieutenant through his business; completed approximately 300 hours in patrol cars from 2010–2013.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013 and was asked to become a chaplain in 2014.
- Training milestones: police academy, public safety academy, appointed to county SWAT team (2017), basic SWAT certification (2022), Sheriff's Office chaplain (2023), fully deputized Marine Deputy (2025).

### Scope of Ministry
- Primary agencies: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 Dispatch Center.
- Over 350 people across these organizations have open access to his ministry — and growing.
- Serves as Peer Assistance Team coordinator, providing proactive care for officers after difficult calls or personal hardships.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- Ceremonial chaplains are often pastors whose name appears on an org chart but who have no ongoing relational presence.
- John's integrated model means he holds sworn status, serves on the SWAT team, and coordinates peer assistance — giving him an organic, trusted presence from the inside out.
- Trust is described as essential: "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust so that when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

### Sue Moe's Role — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Sue volunteers with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates, responding to critical incidents (completed suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support surviving family members on scene.
- Her relational presence with families — including praying with them — complements John's ministry to the officers on the same scenes.
- Her involvement has opened doors for John to build relationships with deputies' spouses, strengthening the overall ministry reach.
- Example given: at a scene where a grandfather and five-year-old child died after falling through ice, Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, and a deputy — in prayer with the grieving family.

### Evangelism Highlights
- Conducted an eight-week Bible study in early 2025 for county deputies — the first ever in Clinton County. Eight deputies attended regularly.
  - Week 1 established the authority of Scripture and objective truth as a foundation.
  - Subsequent weeks included studies on Gideon, the divine calling of law enforcement, and a clear gospel presentation in the final session.
  - One deputy wrestled with the idea of grace covering even his grandparents' murderer — John walked through the gospel with him and continues to follow up.
  - Four deputies who attended the Bible study came to Solid Rock Baptist Church's Friend Day the following Sunday.
- A deputy named Ryan called John while pulled over on the side of the road to share that he had just accepted Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning before the evening service.
- Conducted a funeral for a veteran (son of a county firefighter) who died by suicide — gave a clear gospel presentation to a crowd that included rough, non-churched attendees.
- Led prayer for approximately 75 law enforcement officers from local, state, and federal agencies before a coordinated SWAT operation to arrest suspects in a five-year-old murder case.
- Had a three-hour conversation about the Lord with a team member while waiting in a gear truck during a recent SWAT call-out.

### Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM)
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- Conducted a debrief for the SWAT team after responding to the Michigan State University active shooter event (team was first tactical boots on ground).
- Also debriefed after incidents including: a drunk driver striking a family of 16, a plane crash in Bath Township, officer-involved shootings, and the grandfather/child ice tragedy.
- Provides ongoing follow-up care — one case involved months of support for a deputy first on scene at the drunk-driving incident.

### Romans 13:6 — "Attending Continually"
- John focused on the phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting, unpaid-holiday, every-weather, every-circumstance nature of law enforcement service.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (including firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, and dispatchers), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- Core calling statement: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs Shared
- Partial compensation recently granted by Bath Township for community relations work (~15 hours/week); Marine Deputy role shares $5,000 in state funding between two part-time deputies.
- Turned down a Vice President role and a full-time police community relations position in order to maintain flexibility to respond wherever and whenever needed.
- Prayer needs: financial support, and urgently, God to raise up additional Bible-believing chaplains — John is being asked to expand into more agencies than he can serve alone.
- Neighboring county and additional township agency have recently inquired about his services.
- Midland Baptist Church is noted as one of the ministry's early and ongoing supporters.
