## Summary
John Moe, a former Midland Baptist member and founder of the Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, shares an update on his integrated chaplaincy work across multiple law enforcement agencies in Clinton County, Michigan. Drawing on Romans 13:1-6, he explains the biblical basis for law enforcement as a divine calling and describes his ministry's goal of connecting officers to the God who called them. The evening also features his wife Sue's account of her parallel role as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates team.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focuses particularly on verse 6, which describes civil authorities as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using this phrase to highlight the unrelenting weight borne by first responders and the spiritual need that creates.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and biblical mandate of law enforcement (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — being present from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for effective ministry
- Sharing the gospel clearly in a secular mission field
- The role of a husband-and-wife ministry team
- The weight of continual vigilance borne by first responders
- Prayer and financial support needs for chaplaincy ministry

## Notes

### John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- Long-time member of Midland Baptist before relocating to Lansing in 2004 to start a business.
- Wrote the civil service exam for the Michigan State Police in 1989 but pursued an agricultural business career instead.
- Beginning in 2010, he built a relationship with a local police lieutenant, eventually completing approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs between 2010 and 2013.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013, then was invited to serve as chaplain in 2014 after his Christian witness was noticed by a sergeant.
- Subsequent training milestones: police academy attendance, appointed to Clinton County SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster (2017), Basic SWAT certification (2022), chaplain for the sheriff's office (2023), and fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy (2025).

### Current Ministry Scope
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team, and the county Peer Assistance Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 Dispatch Center.
- Over 350 people across these organizations have an open door to his ministry, and the number is growing.

### Sue Moe's Role
- Serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST).
- Responds to scenes of critical incidents — completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other tragedies — to support surviving family members.
- Her presence at scenes complements John's chaplaincy: she builds relationships with deputies' spouses, which in turn opens doors for couples' ministry and fellowship.
- A highlight example: at a scene where a grandfather and five-year-old child drowned after breaking through pond ice, Sue gathered the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members — and led them in prayer.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- John distinguishes between a "ceremonial" chaplain (often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has little ongoing relationship with officers) and an "integrated" chaplain who is organically embedded within the agencies.
- His sworn reserve and deputy status, SWAT membership, and peer assistance coordinator role give him a "seat at the table" and the trust necessary for effective ministry.
- Key principle: "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust. So when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

### Bible Study with Deputies
- Ran an eight-week Bible study January through mid-March of the current year; eight deputies attended fairly regularly — a first for the county.
- Participants brought a wide range of backgrounds (Catholic, Nazarene/Wesleyan, modern evangelical).
- Week one established the authority of Scripture and objective truth as a foundation. Subsequent weeks built toward a clear gospel presentation.
- On the final night, gave a full gospel presentation tracing from the Garden of Eden through the New Testament. One deputy realized for the first time that salvation is based entirely on Christ's work at the cross, not on personal good works.
- Another deputy wrestled with the question of whether the murderer of his grandparents could be forgiven — an ongoing pastoral conversation.
- Four of the Bible study deputies attended the Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church (John's current home church, pastored by his son-in-law).

### Critical Incident Stress Management
- Certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- Conducted a formal debrief for the Clinton County SWAT team following their response to the Michigan State University active shooter event, during which team members were exposed to deceased students.
- Has conducted debriefs for numerous other incidents, including a fatal pond drowning and a drunk-driver crash that struck a family of sixteen.

### Other Ministry Moments Highlighted
- Led a group prayer of approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies before a coordinated multi-location arrest operation (a cold-case murder in Bath Township).
- Conducted a funeral for the son of a county firefighter — a veteran who died by suicide — giving a clear gospel presentation to a mixed, largely unchurched crowd.
- Provided pastoral care to officers navigating marriage difficulties after the arrival of a new baby.
- Had a three-hour gospel conversation with a SWAT team member while waiting in a gear truck during a call-out.
- A deputy who pulled over and accepted Christ while driving later invited John and Sue to his baptism, which they attended the morning of this presentation.

### Romans 13:6 — "Attending Continually"
- John draws particular attention to the phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" as a description of the unceasing demands placed on first responders.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (law enforcement, fire, EMS, dispatch), yet they attend continually to the worst moments in the lives of the other 99.3%.
- "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs
- **Prayer:** Most urgent need. Specifically for the ministry's ongoing gospel opportunities, for John and Sue's strength and safety, and for God to raise up additional Bible-believing chaplains to meet the growing demand.
- **Financial support:** John works approximately 98% full-time in chaplaincy. He receives partial compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township in a community relations officer role and shares $5,000 in state Marine Patrol funding with another part-time deputy. He turned down a full-time law enforcement position and a corporate VP role in order to maintain the flexibility the ministry requires.
- **Prayer cards** are available at the visitor's desk.
- A neighboring county agency has already contacted him about serving there — he currently lacks capacity to expand further without additional support and/or a co-laborer.
