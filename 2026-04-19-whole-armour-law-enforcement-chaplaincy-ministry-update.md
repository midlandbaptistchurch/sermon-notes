## Summary
John Moe, along with his wife Sue, presented an update on their Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, sharing how God called John from a business career into integrated law enforcement chaplaincy serving Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, the Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team, and several other agencies. John emphasized that his goal is to build trust from the inside out—being a sworn officer and deputy himself—so that he can minister the gospel effectively to officers and their families at their most vulnerable moments. The evening message culminated in a reading of Romans 13:1-6, highlighting law enforcement as a divinely ordained calling and the chaplain's role as connecting the called to the One who called them.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John used this passage to establish the biblical foundation for law enforcement as a God-ordained institution, with particular focus on verse 6 and the phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" as a description of the unrelenting weight borne by first responders.

## Key Themes
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy: being present from the inside out rather than on the periphery
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry in law enforcement
- Romans 13 and the divine calling of law enforcement officers
- The weight of "attending continually" — the emotional and spiritual toll on first responders
- Gospel proclamation in unconventional settings (Bible studies, funerals, SWAT callouts, critical incident debriefs)
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership (John in chaplaincy; Sue in Clinton County Victim Support Team)
- The need for prayer and financial support for chaplaincy work

## Notes

### John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John and Sue were longtime members of Midland Baptist before relocating to Lansing in 2004 to start a business.
- John had an early interest in law enforcement, having scored well on the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989, but pursued an agricultural business career instead.
- Beginning in 2010, John did approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs with a local police department. In 2013 he became a sworn reserve officer, and in 2014 he was asked to become a chaplain.
- He attended a police academy, became a SWAT-certified operator and chaplain/quartermaster (2017, with basic SWAT school in 2022), began serving as chaplain for the Clinton County Sheriff's Office in 2023, and became a fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy in 2025.

### Integrated Chaplaincy Model
- John contrasted "ceremonial" chaplains (often a pastor whose name appears on an org chart with little daily relationship) with "integrated" chaplains who are embedded within the agencies they serve.
- His sworn status and active participation in SWAT callouts, peer assistance coordination, and Marine Patrol give him organic access and earned trust.
- He serves over 350 people across Bath Township Police, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, the Special Operations Team, several additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance, and the County 911 Dispatch Center.

### Sue's Role — Victim Support Team
- Sue volunteers with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST), responding to critical incidents such as suicides and accidental deaths to support families on scene.
- Her presence on scenes has opened doors for relationship-building with deputies' spouses, creating additional ministry opportunities as a couple.
- A powerful moment was shared: at the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old who broke through ice and drowned, Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members — in prayer.

### Gospel Ministry Examples
- **Deputy's conversion and baptism:** A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to say he had just accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism the very morning of this presentation.
- **Bible study with deputies:** From late January through mid-March of the presentation year, John led an eight-week Bible study with eight deputies — a cross-section of Catholic, Wesleyan, and nominally Christian attendees. He established the authority of Scripture in week one, then built toward a clear gospel presentation in the final session. He addressed a deputy's deep struggle with the possibility that the murderer of his grandparents could be forgiven and in heaven, walking through the nature of grace and justification.
- **Veteran's funeral:** John conducted a funeral for a firefighter's son (a veteran who died by suicide), delivering a clear gospel presentation to a mixed, largely unchurched crowd despite the service opening with explicit music chosen by the family.
- **SWAT callout prayer:** Before a coordinated multi-agency arrest operation involving approximately 75 officers (local, state, and federal), John's SWAT commander asked him to pray. John led the assembled officers in prayer for safety and justice.
- **Critical Incident Stress Debriefs (CISD):** John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF). He conducted a debrief for his SWAT team following the Michigan State University active shooter event and has conducted many others, including after a drunk driver struck a family of 16.

### Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- John read through Romans 13:1-6, noting the cascading structure of God's authority flowing to human government and law enforcement.
- Verse 4 identifies the officer as "a minister of God" and one who "beareth not the sword in vain."
- Verse 6's phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" became John's central point: less than 0.7% of the U.S. population serves as first responders, yet they bear unrelenting vigilance over the worst moments in the lives of the other 99.3%.
- **Key statement:** "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs
- John receives partial compensation (~15 hours/week) from Bath Township for a community relations officer role; Marine Patrol pay is shared state funding split between two part-time deputies.
- He turned down a VP-level corporate offer and a full-time police department role in order to maintain the flexibility required for on-call chaplaincy.
- **Prayer requests identified:**
  1. Wisdom and strength for John and Sue as every call involves tragedy.
  2. A Bible-believing chaplain to help meet growing demand (neighboring county and additional agencies have reached out).
  3. Financial support for the ministry.
  4. Continued open doors and trust within law enforcement agencies.
