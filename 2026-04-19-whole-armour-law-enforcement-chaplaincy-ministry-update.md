## Summary
Missionary John Moe, joined by his wife Sue, presented a ministry update on his work as an integrated law enforcement chaplain serving multiple agencies in Clinton County, Michigan. He described the unique model of integrated chaplaincy — being embedded within law enforcement as a sworn officer, SWAT team member, and peer assistance coordinator — and how that trust-based presence opens doors for the gospel. John and Sue shared specific accounts of ministering to officers and families in the aftermath of tragedies, conducting Bible studies, presenting the gospel at a funeral, and witnessing a deputy come to saving faith.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John walked through this passage to establish the divine mandate behind law enforcement, emphasizing verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting weight carried by first responders, and the chaplain's calling to connect officers to the God who called them.

## Key Themes
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being organically embedded in law enforcement rather than being a name on an org chart
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry
- The divine calling of law enforcement officers (Romans 13)
- The unrelenting burden of first responders — less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in first responder roles
- Connecting the called to the one who called them
- The role of a chaplain's spouse in victim support ministry
- Sharing the gospel boldly in a pluralistic and secular environment
- Justification by Christ alone versus works-based religion

## Notes
- John Moe has a long history with Midland Baptist Church; his family attended when the current building was under construction. He and his wife moved to Lansing in 2004.
- His path to chaplaincy began in 2010 through ride-alongs with a township police lieutenant, leading to 300+ hours in a patrol car by 2013, when he became a sworn reserve officer.
- A sergeant recognized John's consistent Christian witness and asked him to become chaplain in 2014. John initially declined but sensed clear confirmation from the Lord.
- Timeline of ministry development:
  - 2014: Appointed chaplain; began police academy and public services chaplain academy training
  - 2016: House fire caused a one-year withdrawal from the academy
  - 2017: Appointed to county SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster
  - 2022: Completed basic SWAT school; certified operator chaplain
  - 2023: Began serving as chaplain for the county sheriff's office
  - 2025: Attended state training academy for Marine Patrol; became a fully deputized deputy
- Current responsibilities include: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team, plus frequent calls from 3-4 other police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and county 911 dispatch — over 350 people across these organizations.
- John runs the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office, providing support to officers after difficult calls or personal hardships.
- **Sue Moe** serves as a volunteer on the county Victim Support Team (VST), responding to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents to support families. Her motivation came from the fentanyl overdose death of her nephew four years prior.
- Sue's VST work creates relationship pathways with deputies' wives and has led to opportunities for prayer and gospel conversations at scenes.
- **Key ministry accounts shared:**
  - A deputy named Ryan called John as his first phone call after pulling over and accepting Christ alone while driving. John and Sue attended his baptism the morning of this service.
  - After the Michigan State University active shooter event, John conducted a critical incident stress debrief for SWAT team members who had cleared the scene.
  - At a scene where a grandfather and five-year-old drowned after breaking through pond ice, Sue prayed with the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and the grieving family.
  - A firefighter's son, a veteran, died by suicide. John ministered to the father, conducted the funeral (standing room, ~150 attendees), and delivered a clear gospel presentation despite the secular setting.
  - Conducted an 8-week Bible study for 8 deputies; presented the full gospel on the final night. One deputy had never heard that justification is by Christ alone. Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled deeply with the doctrine of grace.
  - Four deputies from the Bible study attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church; two are unsaved.
  - Prayed with all officers on scene at a mass casualty drunk driving incident involving a family of 16 (two fatalities).
  - Led prayer for ~75 law enforcement officers from multiple agencies before a coordinated SWAT operation to apprehend a murder suspect.
  - Responded to a plane crash in Bath Township that nearly struck his own property.
  - Involved in three officer-involved shootings since joining the county.
- John expressed frustration with the International Conference of Police Chaplains (ICPC) for discouraging chaplains from sharing their specific faith. He is exploring involvement with "Heroic Deeds," an Ohio-based organization that takes a more explicitly Christ-centered approach.
- He turned down a vice president role at a coatings company and a full-time law enforcement community relations position with benefits in order to maintain the flexibility his chaplaincy calling requires.
- Ministry needs: financial support (partial compensation only from township and marine deputy roles), prayer support, and prayer for God to raise up additional cop chaplains to meet growing demand.
- Statistic cited: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first responder capacity (full-time, paid-on-call, and volunteer firefighters; local/state/sheriff/federal law enforcement; EMTs, paramedics, and dispatchers).
