## Summary
John Moe, a former Midland Baptist member now serving as an integrated law enforcement chaplain in Clinton County, Michigan, gave a ministry update on Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy. He shared how God called him from a business career into full-time chaplaincy work with police departments, a county sheriff's office, a SWAT team, fire departments, and EMS. Anchoring the presentation in Romans 13:1-6, John challenged the congregation to appreciate the unrelenting burden carried by the 0.69% of the population who serve as first responders, and described his mission as connecting those called to law enforcement to the One who called them.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focused especially on verse 6, which describes civil authorities as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using the phrase to illustrate the unceasing weight of vigilance borne by law enforcement and first responders.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — being present from the inside out
- Building trust with officers as a foundation for gospel ministry
- The partnership of John and Sue Moe in chaplaincy and victim support
- Sharing the gospel clearly in a secular and spiritually diverse mission field
- The unrelenting burden of first responders (only 0.69% of the U.S. population)
- Connecting the called to the One who called them

## Notes

### John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John and Sue Moe were longtime faithful members of Midland Baptist before relocating to Lansing in 2004 to run a business.
- John had an early interest in law enforcement, scoring well on the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989, but pursued an agricultural business career instead.
- Beginning in 2010, he built a relationship with a local police lieutenant through his business, eventually doing approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs from 2010–2013.
- In 2013 he became a sworn reserve officer; in 2014 he was asked to become a chaplain — a title he initially found humbling and intimidating.
- He has since undergone extensive training: police academy, public safety coursework, basic SWAT school (2022), and Marine Patrol state training academy (2025), becoming a fully deputized deputy.

### Scope of Ministry
- Primary agencies: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Additionally called upon by three or four other police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and County 911 Dispatch.
- Over 350 people across organizations with open doors for ministry — and growing.
- Serves as Peer Assistance Team coordinator, providing support to officers after difficult calls or personal struggles.
- Certified debriefer for Critical Incident Stress Management (ICISF) — conducted debriefs after the MSU active shooter event, a fatal pond incident involving a grandfather and five-year-old, officer-involved shootings, a plane crash, and a drunk-driving incident involving a family of sixteen.

### Sue Moe's Role
- Sue volunteers with Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST), responding to scenes of critical incidents — completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other tragedies — to support surviving family members.
- Her presence on scenes has opened relational doors with deputies' families, creating synergy with John's chaplaincy.
- Example shared: at the scene of the grandfather and child pond fatality, Sue gathered the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and the grieving family in prayer.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- Ceremonial chaplains may be listed on an org chart but have little real relationship with officers.
- Integrated chaplaincy means being organically present — sworn reserve status, deputy credentials, SWAT membership — so that trust is built from the inside out.
- Example: a deputy John had ministered to over time called him from the roadside after accepting Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism the morning of this service.

### Bible Study with Deputies
- January through mid-March of the presentation year, John led a Bible study with eight deputies — the first ever conducted in Clinton County.
- Participants brought a wide range of backgrounds (Catholic, Wesleyan/Nazarene, modern evangelical). John took a measured approach, beginning with the authority of Scripture and culminating in a clear gospel presentation on the final night.
- One deputy, whose grandparents had been murdered, wrestled openly with whether their killer could be forgiven — a pastoral conversation John described as ongoing.
- Four deputies who attended the Bible study came to the friend day at Solid Rock Baptist Church the Sunday before this service.

### Funerals and Pastoral Ministry
- John conducted the funeral for the son of a county firefighter — a veteran who died by suicide — after the family requested him. He gave a clear gospel presentation despite the service beginning with a secular song chosen by the family.
- He has also walked with officers through marital difficulties and an infertility journey, illustrating the depth of trusted pastoral relationship he has developed.

### Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Call of Law Enforcement
- Verse 4 identifies the officer as a "minister of God" — not a ceremonial title but a functional calling to uphold good and restrain evil.
- Verse 6 — "attending continually upon this very thing" — carries the weight of unceasing, around-the-clock vigilance regardless of weather, holidays, personal circumstances, or national conditions.
- Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (law enforcement, fire, EMS, dispatch), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the One who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs and Prayer Requests
- Financial support: John receives limited hourly compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township for community relations work; Marine Deputy role shares only $5,000 in state funding between two part-time deputies. He turned down a VP role and a full-time officer position to maintain flexibility for chaplaincy.
- Prayer for additional chaplains: John has more open doors than he can fill and is unable to accept new agency requests. He urgently needs a Bible-believing co-laborer suited to this ministry context.
- Prayer for wisdom, safety, and endurance for John and Sue as every call they receive represents someone's tragedy.
