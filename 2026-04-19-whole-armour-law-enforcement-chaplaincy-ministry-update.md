## Summary
Missionary John Moe, an integrated law enforcement chaplain serving Clinton County, Michigan, gave a ministry update to Midland Baptist Church describing his work as chaplain for multiple law enforcement and first responder agencies. He shared stories of gospel witness, critical incident debriefing, Bible studies with deputies, and the unique calling to connect law enforcement officers to the God who called them. His wife Sue also spoke about her volunteer ministry with the Clinton County Victim Support Team.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John Moe focused particularly on verse 6, emphasizing that law enforcement officers are "God's ministers attending continually" upon their calling, highlighting the unrelenting weight of vigilance carried by the less than one percent of the population who serve as first responders.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being present from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry among officers
- The weight of "attending continually" borne by first responders
- Evangelism and clear gospel presentation in unexpected settings
- The role of a chaplain's spouse in victim support ministry
- Critical incident stress debriefing as pastoral care

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family grew up in the church before moving to Lansing in 2004.
- Owns a business in Lansing now run by his son; previously traveled extensively for corporate work.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued an agricultural business career instead.
- In 2010, met a lieutenant through business connections and began civilian ride-alongs, accumulating approximately 300 hours in a patrol car by 2013.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- Called to become a police chaplain in 2014 after a sergeant recognized his consistent Christian witness.
- Completed chaplain academy training, was appointed to county SWAT team in 2017, earned basic SWAT certification in 2022, and became a fully deputized Marine Deputy in 2025.
- Currently serves Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, and Clinton County Special Operations Team as primary responsibilities, with open doors to three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and County 911 dispatch — over 350 people total.
- Runs the peer assistance team for the sheriff's office.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- Ceremonial chaplains are often local pastors whose names appear on an org chart but who have little ongoing relationship with the team.
- Integrated chaplaincy means being organically embedded — sworn reserve status, deputy role, SWAT team membership, and peer assistance coordinator — so that ministry flows from the inside out.
- Trust is earned one minute and one relationship at a time; that trust creates a "reservoir" so that when officers face something overwhelming, a safe and trusted resource is close at hand.

### Ministry Highlights Shared
- **Deputy's Salvation:** A deputy called John after pulling over on the side of the road and trusting Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning before the evening service.
- **Bible Study:** Conducted an 8-week Bible study for county deputies (January–mid-March 2026); eight deputies attended regularly — the first such Bible study ever held in the county. Participants brought various Bible versions; John used differences in translation to point toward the King James Bible. Concluded with a clear gospel presentation. One deputy who had attended church his whole life realized for the first time that salvation is by Christ's work alone, not human effort. Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled openly with whether God could forgive a killer — an ongoing conversation.
- **Friend Day:** Four of the Bible study deputies attended Solid Rock Baptist Church's Friend Day the previous Sunday; two are not yet saved.
- **Michigan State University Active Shooter (prior event):** Clinton County Special Operations Team was first on the ground; John conducted a critical incident stress debrief for the team the following week.
- **Grandfather and Five-Year-Old through Ice:** Both lives were lost; John responded to the scene while Sue's VST team ministered to the family inside. Sue asked if she could pray with the grandmother, who responded, "Would you please — it's the only thing that's going to get us through this." Sue led the entire room, including the sheriff and deputies, in prayer.
- **Firefighter's Son's Suicide (veteran):** John spent two hours with the grieving father, prayed with him, and was ultimately asked to conduct the funeral — a standing-room-only service of approximately 150 people. Despite an unconventional setting (including a secular opening song chosen by the family), John delivered a clear gospel presentation.
- **Bath Township Plane Crash (end of 2025):** Three lives lost; the crash occurred near John's own property. He conducted a diffusal with law enforcement and firefighters on scene.
- **Three Officer-Involved Shootings:** John responded to each, escorting officers away from the scene to decompress.
- **Family of 16 Hit by Drunk Driver:** Two killed instantly; John assisted with evidence collection for six hours and prayed with deputies, the lieutenant, and firefighters on scene. Months of follow-up debriefing ensued.
- **SWAT Raid on Murder Suspect:** Before rolling out on a coordinated multi-agency operation involving approximately 75 officers, John's SWAT commander asked him for "a word" and the entire room bowed in prayer.
- **Marriage and Relationship Counseling:** Two young officers separately approached John about marital struggles after having a baby; he walked them through practical and biblical guidance including the five love languages.
- **Infertility Support:** Walking alongside a couple dealing with infertility issues.

### Sue Moe's Ministry — Victim Support Team
- Motivated by the fentanyl overdose death of her nephew four years ago and the gap she observed between law enforcement notification and family care.
- Volunteers with Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team, responding to critical incidents such as completed suicides and accidental deaths.
- Deployed to scenes for three to four hours, building relationships with deputies and their families.
- Her relational approach has opened doors for gospel conversations; VST colleagues have commented on her prayer life and asked to meet for lunch.
- The synergy between her VST role and John's chaplaincy has opened doors to deputies' families that would not otherwise be accessible.
- Encouraged Midland County residents that Midland County also has a VST and she is available to talk.

### Romans 13 and the Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- Romans 13:1 — All governing powers are ordained of God.
- Romans 13:2 — Resisting governing authority is resisting God's ordinance.
- Romans 13:3-4 — Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil; the one in authority is a "minister of God" who bears the sword not in vain, a "revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil."
- Romans 13:6 — "They are God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing." John emphasized "attending continually" as describing the unrelenting, round-the-clock, holiday-regardless, weather-regardless, personal-circumstance-regardless vigilance of law enforcement.
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in some first responder capacity (firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers); this fraction is "attending continually" upon the worst day of the other 99.3%.
- John's mission statement: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs
- **Prayer** is the primary request — especially as every call-out means a tragedy has occurred.
- **Financial support:** John receives partial hourly compensation from the township (approximately 15 hours/week, community relations role) and part-time Marine Deputy pay; no benefits or retirement. He turned down a full-time law enforcement role and a corporate VP role to remain available for the calling.
- **Prayer for additional chaplains:** The need far exceeds John's capacity; a neighboring township agency recently requested he serve as their chaplain, which he had to decline. He is asking the church to pray for the Lord to raise up others called to law enforcement chaplaincy.
- Fresh prayer cards are available at the visitors desk.
