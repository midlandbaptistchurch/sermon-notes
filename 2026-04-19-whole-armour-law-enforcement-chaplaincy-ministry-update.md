## Summary
John Moe, a former Midland Baptist member and founder of the Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, presented a Sunday evening ministry update on his work as an integrated law enforcement chaplain in Clinton County, Michigan. He shared how God led him from a business career through ride-alongs and reserve officer service into full-time chaplaincy with multiple police, sheriff, SWAT, fire, and emergency services agencies. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, John challenged the congregation to appreciate the divine calling of first responders and to pray for those who attend continually to the worst moments in other people's lives.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focused especially on verse 6, "for they are God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using it to describe the unrelenting burden carried by law enforcement and first responders, and to frame the chaplain's role as connecting officers to the God who called them.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and authority of law enforcement (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — building trust from the inside out
- Evangelism and gospel presentation in a law enforcement context
- The ministry partnership of husband and wife (John's chaplaincy and Sue's Victim Support Team work)
- Critical incident stress debriefing and pastoral care for first responders
- The weight of continual vigilance borne by less than 0.7% of the U.S. population
- Prayer and financial support needs for the ministry

## Notes
- John and Sue Moe were longtime members of Midland Baptist before relocating to Lansing in 2004.
- John's law enforcement journey began in 2010 with civilian ride-alongs, progressing to sworn reserve officer (2013), chaplain for Bath Township PD (2014), Clinton County SWAT chaplain/quartermaster (2017), basic SWAT certified operator (2022), Clinton County Sheriff's Office chaplain (2023), and fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy (2025).
- The ministry, Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy, now has open-door relationships with over 350 personnel across multiple agencies including Bath Township PD, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations Team, several other county agencies, fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 Dispatch Center.
- Sue Moe serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST), responding to critical incidents alongside John's chaplaincy work. Their combined presence at scenes creates a powerful husband-wife ministry witness.
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF). He has conducted debriefs following the MSU active shooter event, a fatal pond accident involving a grandfather and five-year-old, officer-involved shootings, a drunk-driving crash involving a family of 16, and other incidents.
- In early 2026, John led an eight-week Bible study for eight county deputies — the first of its kind in the county. The final session included a clear gospel presentation covering justification by faith and the cross of Christ.
- One deputy who came to faith during John's ministry was baptized the morning of this presentation, with John and Sue present.
- John also conducted a funeral for a firefighter's son (a veteran who died by suicide), giving a clear gospel presentation to a unchurched mixed crowd.
- John was invited to pray over approximately 75 officers from multiple agencies before a coordinated multi-location arrest operation related to a long-unsolved murder case in Bath Township.
- Key distinction emphasized: integrated chaplaincy (organically embedded in the agency) versus ceremonial chaplaincy (pastor on an org chart with little relational access).
- Romans 13:6 insight: the phrase "attending continually" describes officers responding regardless of weather, holidays, personal health, or family occasions — an unrelenting vigilance shared by only 0.7% of the U.S. population on behalf of the other 99.3%.
- John is connected with Heroic Deeds, an Ohio-based chaplaincy organization that takes a Christ-forward approach, and plans to attend their meeting in August.
- Ministry needs shared: prayer support, financial partners (partial compensation only from township community relations role and shared state Marine Patrol funding), and prayer for the Lord to raise up additional Bible-believing chaplains to meet growing demand.
- Midland Baptist is noted as one of the original supporting churches of this ministry.
