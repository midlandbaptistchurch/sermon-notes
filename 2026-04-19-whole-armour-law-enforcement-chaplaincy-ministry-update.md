## Summary
John Moe, a former Midland Baptist member and full-time law enforcement chaplain, presents an update on his Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry serving Clinton County, Michigan. He shares how God called him from a business career into integrated chaplaincy work alongside police, sheriff's deputies, SWAT teams, fire departments, and EMS. Drawing on Romans 13:1-6, John connects the divine calling of law enforcement officers to their role as God's ministers and describes his mission to connect the called to the One who called them.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on God-ordained governmental authority, used here to frame law enforcement officers as God's ministers "attending continually" upon the work of justice and public safety.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as ministry (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — building trust from the inside out
- Sharing the gospel in a unique mission field
- The unrelenting weight of continual vigilance carried by first responders
- Husband-and-wife partnership in ministry (John's chaplaincy and Sue's Victim Support Team)
- Critical incident stress management and pastoral care for officers
- Connecting the called to the One who called them

## Notes

### John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- Former business owner in Lansing; stepped away from corporate work two years prior after persistent sense of God's calling into law enforcement chaplaincy.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989; pursued agriculture/business instead, believing God needed to mature his character first.
- Beginning in 2010, built relationships with local law enforcement through civilian ride-alongs (~300 hours, 2010–2013).
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013; appointed as chaplain in 2014.
- Attended police academy; appointed to Clinton County SWAT team (2017); completed Basic SWAT School (2022); became a fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy (2025).
- Primary agencies served: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and County 911 Dispatch — over 350 people with open doors to minister to.

### Sue's Role — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Sue serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates team.
- Activated at critical incidents (completed suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to provide immediate support to families.
- Her presence at scenes builds relationships with officers' spouses, creating social access that deepens the ministry.
- Illustrated by a moving account of Sue leading an entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members — in prayer at the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old child lost through the ice.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- Ceremonial chaplain: a pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has no relational presence with the team.
- Integrated chaplain: embedded within the agency through sworn status, SWAT membership, and peer assistance team roles — ministry flows organically from within.
- "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust. So when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

### Gospel Ministry in the Field
- Conducted an eight-week Bible study for county deputies (January–March of the ministry year); eight deputies attended regularly — first of its kind in the county.
- Diverse group (Catholic, Wesleyan, modern evangelical backgrounds); John used differences between Bible versions as a gentle apologetic for the King James Bible while keeping the focus on the gospel.
- Final session: a clear gospel presentation tracing Scripture from the Garden of Eden through the New Testament. Key moment — one deputy raised the hard question of whether the unrepentant murderer of his grandparents could be in heaven, leading to a lengthy pastoral conversation about grace, forgiveness, and justice.
- Four deputies who attended the Bible study visited Solid Rock Baptist Church's Friend Day the following Sunday, where a clear gospel message was preached.
- Shares the gospel at funerals, critical incident scenes, and one-on-one conversations in patrol trucks during SWAT call-outs.
- Key conviction: "If we're not sharing, looking for opportunities, building relationships, and nudging and pointing and bringing these guys and gals toward Christ, what are we doing?"
- Critical of chaplaincy organizations that discourage sharing one's faith; connecting with Heroic Deeds (Ohio-based organization that puts Christ forward as the answer).

### Testimony of a Deputy's Salvation and Baptism
- A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to share that he had just accepted Christ.
- On the morning of this service, John and Sue attended that deputy's baptism at his home church alongside his family and pastor.

### Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM)
- Certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- Conducted a formal debrief for the Clinton County SWAT team after they were first tactical responders to the Michigan State University active shooter event.
- Led debriefs and pastoral care following: a family of 16 struck by a drunk driver, a plane crash in Bath Township, officer-involved shootings, and the ice pond tragedy involving a grandfather and five-year-old.
- Conducted a coordinated arrest prayer for approximately 75 officers of multiple agencies (local, state, federal) before hitting the home of a suspect in a long-unsolved hunter murder case in Bath Township — invited by his SWAT commander to pray over the assembled team.

### Funeral Ministry
- Conducted the funeral of a veteran who died by suicide — son of a county firefighter and brother of a SWAT team medic.
- Navigated a non-church-attending family's wishes while ensuring a clear gospel presentation was woven into the service.

### Romans 13:6 — "Attending Continually"
- The phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" (Romans 13:6) captures the unrelenting nature of first-responder service: no pause for weather, holidays, illness, anniversaries, or personal crisis.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as a first responder (including firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, and dispatchers), yet they attend to the worst moments of the other 99.3%.
- "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the One who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs and Prayer Requests
- Financial support: partially compensated (~15 hrs/week) by Bath Township in a community relations role; Marine Patrol deputy role shares only $5,000 of state funding between two part-time deputies; most chaplaincy work remains uncompensated.
- Turned down a VP-level corporate offer and a full-time police department role to maintain the flexibility the ministry requires.
- **Prayer requests:**
  1. Faithful financial support for the ministry.
  2. A Bible-believing, gospel-focused chaplain to come alongside and help meet the growing demand — neighboring counties and agencies are requesting coverage he cannot provide alone.
  3. Continued fruit among the officers, deputies, and families he serves.
