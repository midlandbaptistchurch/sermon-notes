## Summary
John Moe, founder of Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry and former longtime member of Midland Baptist Church, shares an update on his integrated chaplaincy work serving law enforcement, fire, EMS, and 911 dispatch personnel in Clinton County, Michigan. Drawing on Romans 13:1-6, John explains the divine calling behind law enforcement and the unique role of an integrated chaplain who ministers from within the ranks rather than from the outside. He and his wife Sue together describe recent ministry encounters including Bible studies with deputies, critical incident stress debriefs, funerals, and Sue's volunteer work with the County Victim Support Advocates team.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on God-ordained governmental authority, used here to establish the divine calling of law enforcement officers and to highlight verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting weight carried by first responders.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as established in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — ministering from within the ranks to build trust
- The gospel as the central purpose of chaplaincy ministry
- The unrelenting burden borne by the 0.69% of the population who serve as first responders
- Husband-and-wife partnership in ministry (John in chaplaincy, Sue in Victim Support Advocacy)
- Critical incident stress management and peer care for officers
- The importance of prayer and financial support for chaplaincy work

## Notes
- John gave background on his path to chaplaincy: a 1989 civil service exam score, a career in agricultural business, and nearly 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs beginning in 2010 before becoming a sworn reserve officer in 2013 and a chaplain in 2014.
- Current roles include: chaplain and reserve officer at Bath Township Police Department; deputy and chaplain at Clinton County Sheriff's Office; chaplain, quartermaster, and basic SWAT-certified operator on the Clinton County Special Operations Team; Peer Assistance Team coordinator; and Marine Patrol deputy (fully deputized 2025).
- John also serves in an unofficial capacity for several additional agencies: three to four other police departments, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 Dispatch Center — reaching over 350 people.
- Sue Moe serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates team, responding to critical incident scenes to support families. Her presence at scenes has opened relational doors with deputies' spouses and extended the ministry's reach.
- John conducted the first-ever Bible study at the Clinton County Sheriff's Office (January–March of the current year), with eight deputies attending regularly. The study covered the authority of Scripture, the divine calling of law enforcement (including a study on Gideon), and concluded with a clear gospel presentation.
- During that Bible study, one deputy named Greg wrestled openly with the question of whether the murderer of his grandparents could be forgiven and enter heaven. John engaged the question honestly, affirming both God's justice and the sufficiency of the cross.
- Four deputies who attended the Bible study subsequently attended a Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church in Lansing, where Pastor Gregory Nathan delivered a gospel presentation.
- John described a deputy named Ryan who pulled over while driving, accepted Christ, and was later baptized — an event John and Sue attended that morning (April 19, 2026).
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF) and has conducted debriefs following major events including the MSU active shooter incident, a plane crash in Bath Township, three officer-involved shootings, a five-year-old and grandfather drowning tragedy, and a drunk-driver collision involving a family of 16.
- John conducted a funeral for the son of a local firefighter — a veteran who died by suicide — and delivered a clear gospel presentation to the mixed and largely unchurched crowd.
- Romans 13:6 emphasis: the phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" means waiting on or taking care of without pause — day or night, holidays or anniversaries, in any weather or national crisis. John applied this to the spiritual and emotional toll on first responders and the need for chaplaincy care.
- Key statistic cited: less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (including firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, and dispatchers), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- Ministry needs shared: prayer for more Bible-believing chaplain candidates to meet growing demand; financial support given the largely volunteer/low-compensation nature of the work; John recently turned down both a Vice President of Business Development offer and a full-time law enforcement community relations role in order to maintain the flexibility the ministry requires.
- Closing prayer called the congregation to their own "attending continually" — on right doctrine, on rooting out worldliness, and on unhindered communion with God.
