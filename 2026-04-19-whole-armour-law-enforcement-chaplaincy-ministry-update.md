## Summary
Missionary John Moe, along with his wife Sue, presented a ministry update on their work with Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy in Clinton County, Michigan. John described his journey from civilian business owner to fully integrated law enforcement chaplain serving multiple agencies, SWAT, and a county sheriff's office, while Sue shared her role on the County Victim Support Team. Together they illustrated how presence, trust, and the gospel are woven into the daily realities of first responders and the families they serve.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on civil government as ordained by God, with particular focus on verse 6 ("they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing"), which John used to highlight the unrelenting weight carried by law enforcement officers and the divine calling behind their work.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — ministry from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel opportunity
- The toll of "attending continually" on first responders
- Husband-and-wife partnership in ministry
- Sharing the gospel clearly in secular and difficult settings
- The need for prayer and financial support for chaplaincy ministry

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family grew up in the church before relocating to Lansing in 2004.
- Owned and operated a coatings/corrosion business; son now runs the company.
- Wrote the civil service exam for Michigan State Police in 1989 but took a civilian career path; God used that time to mature his character and faith.
- Beginning in 2010, he did approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs with a township police agency.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- Asked to become chaplain in 2013–2014; initially declined out of humility, but quickly sensed clear divine leading and accepted.

### Training and Roles
- Attended a public services chaplain academy (2014–2015); withdrew for a year due to a house fire in 2016; completed in 2017.
- Appointed to Clinton County SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster in 2017.
- Completed basic SWAT school in 2022 — certified as a basic SWAT operator/chaplain/quartermaster.
- Began serving as chaplain for the Clinton County Sheriff's Office in 2023.
- Attended the state training academy for Marine Patrol in 2025; became a fully deputized deputy.
- Runs the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office.
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 dispatch center — over 350 people across these organizations.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- A "ceremonial" chaplain is often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has little relational presence with the team.
- An "integrated" chaplain is embedded from the inside — sworn status, shared training, and daily relational presence create organic ministry opportunities.
- John emphasized that chaplaincy must include pointing people toward Christ, not merely being a neutral "God figure."
- Expressed frustration with the International Conference of Police Chaplains (ICPC) for discouraging chaplains from sharing specific faith convictions.
- Noted interest in a Christ-forward organization called Heroic Deeds (based in Ohio), with a planned meeting in August.

### Sue Moe — Victim Support Team Ministry
- Motivated to apply after her nephew died of a fentanyl/heroin overdose in Daytona Beach and she witnessed a gap in compassionate family notification and support.
- Serves as a volunteer on the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team, responding to scenes of suicide, accidental death, and other critical incidents.
- Has been on scenes for three to four hours at a time, building relationships with deputies and their spouses.
- Her relational access to officers' wives has opened doors for dinner fellowships and deeper gospel conversations.
- At a tragic scene involving a grandfather and five-year-old who went through ice on a pond (both deceased), Sue gathered the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, and a deputy — for prayer at the family's request.
- Colleague on the VST team remarked: "When you pray, you act like you're talking to somebody" — leading to lunch and gospel conversations.

### Ministry Highlights and Critical Incidents
- **Deputy's salvation:** A deputy who had been attending church called John while driving, pulled over, and accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism the very morning of this presentation.
- **Bible study at the sheriff's office:** Ran an eight-week Bible study January–mid-March 2026; eight deputies attended fairly regularly — the first such study ever conducted in the county. Participants came from Catholic, Church of God, Nazarene/Wesleyan, and non-church backgrounds. The final session included a clear gospel presentation. One attendee said he had never heard that justification is entirely Christ's work, not human effort. Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled openly with the idea that their killer could be forgiven — an ongoing pastoral conversation.
- **Four deputies from the Bible study attended Friend Day** at Solid Rock Baptist Church (John's son-in-law's church in Lansing); two are not yet saved.
- **Michigan State University active shooter event:** Clinton County SWAT was first tactical team on the ground; John conducted a Critical Incident Stress Debrief (CISD) the following week for team members who encountered deceased students.
- **Grandfather and child lost through ice:** Responded to scene; Sue's VST team ministered to the family inside while John checked on deputies outside. Sue led the room in prayer.
- **Firefighter's son (veteran) — suicide:** Spent two hours with the grieving father; eventually conducted the standing-room-only funeral (~150 attendees) and delivered a clear gospel presentation despite the service including explicit music chosen by the family.
- **Bath Township plane crash:** Responded to scene; conducted a rapid "defusel" with all law enforcement and firefighters present; three lives were lost; the crash site was within approximately five seconds' flying distance from the Moe property.
- **Three officer-involved shootings** in the county since John became involved; responded to each, escorted officers from scenes for decompression.
- **Family of 16 struck by a drunk driver:** Two killed instantly; worked evidence collection for six hours; prayed with deputies, lieutenant, and firefighters on scene; months of follow-up ensued.
- **Murder investigation SWAT callout:** On the night a five-year cold-case murder suspect was identified and multiple simultaneous hits were executed, John led approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies in prayer before they rolled out — at his SWAT commander's invitation.
- **Infertility, marital counseling, injury support:** Has been trusted by young officers for deeply personal conversations including marital adjustment after a new baby and significant physical injuries.

### Romans 13:6 — "Attending Continually"
- John focused on the phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" as describing the unrelenting, around-the-clock, holiday-and-illness-regardless vigilance of first responders.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as a first responder (full-time, paid-on-call, and volunteer firefighters; local/state/sheriff/federal law enforcement; EMTs, paramedics, and dispatchers).
- That less-than-three-quarters-of-a-percent of the population responds to the worst day of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: *to connect the called to the one who called them* — bringing the chapel into the law enforcement world and serving as an ambassador for Christ in this unique mission field.

### Ministry Needs
- **Financial support:** Receives partial hourly compensation from Bath Township (~15 hrs/week) as a community relations officer and partial compensation as a Marine Deputy (shared $5,000 in state funding). No health benefits or retirement from chaplaincy work. Turned down a full-time law enforcement community relations role with benefits to preserve flexibility for his calling.
- **Prayer:** Requests faithful prayer for himself and Sue; every phone call they receive signals that a tragedy has occurred.
- **Personnel:** Actively seeking someone called to law enforcement chaplaincy to help meet the growing demand — they do not need to pursue the sworn/SWAT track, but must have the right heart and temperament for this ministry.
- Prayer cards available at the visitors' desk.
