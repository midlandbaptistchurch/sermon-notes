## Summary
Missionary John Moe, a law enforcement chaplain serving in Clinton County, Michigan, presents a ministry update on Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy. He describes his journey from civilian ride-alongs to becoming a sworn reserve officer, deputy, SWAT-certified chaplain, and peer assistance team coordinator, explaining how an integrated presence within law enforcement agencies allows him to build trust and share the gospel with officers, deputies, and their families. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, he challenges the congregation to appreciate the divine calling of first responders who "attend continually" on the worst days of those they serve, and to pray for and support this unique mission field.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John Moe expounds on this passage to establish the God-ordained authority of law enforcement, highlighting verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting vigilance required of first responders and the spiritual weight they carry.

## Key Themes
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being organically embedded within law enforcement agencies to build trust and gospel access
- The divine calling of law enforcement — Romans 13 frames officers and deputies as ministers of God ordained to uphold civil order
- Evangelism through relationship — sharing the gospel with officers, conducting Bible studies, and presenting the gospel at funerals and critical incidents
- The weight of continual vigilance — fewer than 0.7% of the U.S. population serves as first responders, attending to the worst moments of the other 99.3%
- Connecting the called to the One who called them — the chaplain's core mission of linking officers' vocational calling to God as the author of all authority
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership — Sue Moe's volunteer work with the County Victim Support Team as a complement to John's chaplaincy

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; moved to Lansing in 2004 and planted/attended Solid Rock Baptist Church
- Owned a business in Lansing; stepped back two years ago when the Lord clearly called him to full-time law enforcement chaplaincy
- Scored well on the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued an agricultural business career instead; God used those years to mature his character and faith
- 2010: Met a lieutenant through business, began civilian ride-alongs; logged approximately 300 hours in a patrol car from 2010–2013
- 2013: Became a sworn reserve officer at Bath Township Police Department
- 2014: Appointed as police chaplain after a sergeant recognized his consistent Christian witness; initially said no but quickly sensed clear confirmation from the Lord
- 2014–2015: Attended police academy and a public services chaplain academy
- 2016: Withdrew from academy training for a year due to a house fire; returned the following year
- 2017: Appointed chaplain and quartermaster to the county SWAT team
- 2022: Completed basic SWAT school; became a basic SWAT-certified operator chaplain
- 2023: Began serving as chaplain for the Clinton County Sheriff's Office
- 2025: Attended the state training academy for Marine Patrol; became a fully deputized deputy

### Current Ministry Responsibilities
- **Primary agencies:** Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team
- **Additional open doors:** Three to four other county police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, County 911 dispatch center
- **Total reach:** Over 350 individuals across these organizations
- **Peer Assistance Team:** Coordinates a team of one sergeant and three deputies to monitor officer wellbeing after rough calls or personal difficulties
- **Community Relations Officer role:** Recently began receiving partial hourly compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township; no benefits or retirement
- **Marine Deputy:** Fully deputized, working part-time between Memorial Day and Labor Day; shares $5,000 in state funding with another part-time deputy

### Sue Moe's Victim Support Team (VST) Ministry
- Inspired by the death of her nephew from a fentanyl/heroin overdose in Daytona Beach four years prior, which revealed the gap between law enforcement notification and family support
- Applied to and was accepted by Clinton County's Victim Support Advocates Team despite no formal grief or trauma training
- Responds to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents; often present for three to four hours alongside deputies
- Builds relationships with officers' wives, opening doors for couples to socialize and receive ministry together
- Clinton County Sheriff is a born-again believer; county leadership is supportive of chaplain involvement
- Prayed with an entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members — at the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old lost through pond ice
- VST colleagues have noticed her manner of prayer and initiated follow-up conversations about faith

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- **Ceremonial chaplain:** Often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart; called only in extreme situations; limited relational access
- **Integrated chaplain:** Embedded within agencies through sworn status, SWAT membership, peer assistance role, and regular presence; ministry flows from the inside out
- John's integrated roles create a "reservoir of trust" so that when officers face crises they have a safe, trusted, Bible-believing resource immediately available

### Gospel Ministry in Action
- **Deputy Ryan's salvation:** A deputy who had been attending church called John to report he had pulled over on the side of the road and trusted Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism the morning of this service
- **County Bible study (January–mid-March):** First-ever Bible study conducted for county personnel; eight deputies attended fairly regularly; cross-section of Catholic, Church of God, Wesleyan, and non-churching backgrounds
  - Week 1: Established the authority of Scripture as an objective foundation
  - Subsequent weeks: Studies on Gideon and the divine calling of law enforcement
  - Final session: Clear gospel presentation tracing redemption from Genesis through the New Testament
  - One deputy realized for the first time that salvation is based entirely on Christ's work, not human effort; had never heard this in years of churchgoing
  - Another deputy struggled with the idea that his grandparents' murderer could be forgiven; John affirmed the humanity of the struggle while holding to the Bible's teaching on grace
  - Four of the eight Bible study deputies attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church; two are unsaved
- **Veteran's funeral:** Conducted a standing-room-only funeral (~150 attendees) for a firefighter's son who died by suicide; the family was non-churching but open to God; John included a clear gospel presentation despite an unconventional service atmosphere
- **SWAT pre-operation prayer:** Before a coordinated multi-agency raid to arrest a murder suspect (Bath Township hunter case), John's SWAT commander asked him to lead prayer; approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies bowed their heads together
- **Plane crash (Bath Township):** Responded to scene; conducted a group defusing with law enforcement and firefighters; the crash site was near John's own property
- **MSU active shooter response:** SWAT team was first tactical unit on scene; John conducted a formal Critical Incident Stress Debrief the following week for all team members exposed to deceased students
- **Drunk driver incident:** A family of 16 was struck; two killed instantly; John did evidence collection, stayed on scene six hours past midnight, prayed with deputies, lieutenant, and firefighters, and provided months of follow-up
- **Officer marriage counseling:** Two different young officers with new babies sought John's counsel about marital strain; trust level reflects the depth of relationship built over years
- **Infertility support:** Walking alongside a couple dealing with infertility, praying with and ministering to them
- **Three-hour gospel conversation:** An injured deputy on light duty rode in John's gear truck during a SWAT callout two weeks prior; they talked about the Lord for three hours

### Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM)
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF)
- Debriefs are structured, closed-door group sessions limited to personnel present at a specific incident; no notes taken; designed to help the brain process trauma
- Has conducted debriefs for the MSU shooting, the pond drowning of a grandfather and child, officer-involved shootings, and other major incidents

### Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- Verse 1: All governmental authority is ordained by God
- Verse 2: Resisting lawful authority is resisting God's ordinance
- Verse 3: Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil
- Verse 4: The officer is called a "minister of God" twice — for good and as a revenger of evil; the sword is not borne in vain
- Verse 6: "Attending continually" — officers serve without pause regardless of weather, holidays, personal anniversaries, illness, or national conditions; this is the weight John sees accumulating in his officers over time
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first responder capacity (firefighters paid and volunteer, law enforcement at all levels, EMS, dispatchers)
- That 0.69% responds to the worst day of the other 99.3% of the population
- John's mission: Connect the called officer to the God who called them

### Ministry Needs
- **Prayer:** Primary need; every call John and Sue receive signals someone's tragedy or crisis
- **Financial support:** Ministry is largely uncompensated; John turned down a VP role at a coatings company and a full-time police department position with benefits in order to maintain the flexibility his calling requires
- **Additional chaplains:** Neighboring agencies have requested chaplain coverage John cannot provide; he appeals for prayer that God would raise up more people called to law enforcement chaplaincy
- **Presentation/website approval:** Ministry visibility (Facebook, website, prayer card, presentation) required approval from a chief, SWAT commander, and county sheriff due to use of official patches
- Prayer cards available at the visitors' desk
