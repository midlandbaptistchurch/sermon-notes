## Summary
Missionary John Moe, an integrated law enforcement chaplain serving in Clinton County, Michigan, presented a ministry update to Midland Baptist Church on the work of Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy. He shared how God led him from a career in business through reserve officer service into full-time chaplaincy among law enforcement, first responders, and their families. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, he emphasized the divine calling of law enforcement officers and the unique mission field they represent, highlighting the critical need for prayer, financial support, and additional chaplains.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on governing authorities as ministers of God, with particular focus on verse 6 and the phrase "attending continually," which John Moe used to illustrate the unrelenting weight carried by law enforcement and first responders.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement officers as ministers of God (Romans 13)
- Integrated versus ceremonial chaplaincy — being present from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry among officers
- The toll of "attending continually" on first responders and the need for pastoral care
- Evangelism in the law enforcement mission field
- The partnership between John's chaplaincy and Sue Moe's Victim Support Team (VST) ministry
- Justification by Christ alone versus works-based religion

## Notes

### Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John Moe and his wife Sue have a long history with Midland Baptist Church; their family grew up in the church before relocating to Lansing in 2004.
- John has an agricultural business degree and owned a company in Lansing, which his son now runs.
- He wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 and scored well but pursued business instead; he reflected that God used those intervening years to mature his character.
- In 2010, through a business contact, he began civilian ride-alongs with a township police agency — approximately 300 hours over three years.
- In 2013 he became a sworn reserve officer. In 2014 a sergeant recognized his consistent Christian witness and asked him to serve as chaplain; he initially said no but prayed about it and quickly sensed God's clear direction.
- He attended a public services chaplain academy, went through SWAT school (basic SWAT certified operator in 2022), was appointed to the county SWAT team in 2017, became a chaplain for the sheriff's office in 2023, and was fully deputized as a Marine Deputy in 2025.
- He also coordinates the peer assistance team for the sheriff's office.

### Scope of Ministry
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 dispatch center.
- Estimates over 350 people across organizations to whom he has open doors for ministry.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- John distinguished between a "ceremonial" chaplain (often a pastor whose name appears on an org chart but has little relational presence) and an "integrated" chaplain who is organically embedded within the agency.
- His sworn status, SWAT role, and peer assistance coordinator role give him a seat at the table and build the trust necessary for effective ministry.
- His philosophy: build a reservoir of trust so that when officers face crisis, there is a safe and trusted resource close at hand.

### Sue Moe — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Sue serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team, responding to scenes involving completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents.
- Her motivation came after her nephew died of a fentanyl/heroin overdose in Daytona Beach; she was struck by the lack of personal connection between law enforcement and grieving families at notification.
- As a former first-grade teacher, she brought compassion and relational skills to the role.
- Her VST work complements John's chaplaincy: she ministers to families on scene while he ministers to the officers, and her relationships with deputies' wives have opened doors for deeper family ministry.
- A notable moment: at a scene involving a grandfather and five-year-old who fell through pond ice (both deceased), Sue asked the grandmother if she could pray; the grandmother replied, "Would you please — it's the only thing that's going to get us through this." Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, and a deputy — in prayer.

### Ministry Highlights and Critical Incidents
- Conducted a critical incident stress debrief (CISM — International Critical Incident Stress Foundation certified) for the SWAT team following the Michigan State University active shooter event.
- Responded to the scene of a grandfather and young child who drowned after falling through ice; conducted a diffusal with deputies and followed up with the family for days afterward.
- Ministered to a firefighter whose veteran son completed suicide; despite reluctance, John conducted the funeral service for approximately 150 attendees and gave a clear gospel presentation.
- Responded to a scene where a family of 16 was struck by a drunk driver (two killed instantly, most others hospitalized); assisted with evidence collection, prayed with deputies and firefighters on scene, and provided months of follow-up.
- SWAT callout for the arrest of suspects in a long-unsolved hunter murder case in Bath Township: the SWAT commander asked John to pray before approximately 75 officers from multiple agencies rolled out.
- A deputy called John to say he had pulled over on the road and accepted Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism the very morning of this presentation.
- Led a Bible study for eight deputies from January through mid-March — the first such study in the county — covering the authority of Scripture, the story of Gideon, the divine calling of law enforcement, and a full gospel presentation. Four of those deputies later attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church.
- One Bible study attendee, who had grown up attending church, realized for the first time that salvation is based entirely on Christ's work, not personal merit. Another deputy wrestled with the concept of grace when confronted with the possibility that his grandparents' murderer could be forgiven.

### Romans 13 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- Verses 1-6 establish that governing authorities are ordained of God.
- Verse 4 describes the officer as "a minister of God" bearing the sword as "a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil."
- John focused on verse 6: "they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing" — the phrase "attending continually" captures the unrelenting, around-the-clock, holiday-or-not vigilance of first responders.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first responder capacity (full-time and volunteer firefighters, law enforcement at all levels, EMS, dispatchers), yet this small group responds to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: connect the called officer to the one who called them — God, the author of all authority.

### Ministry Needs
- Financial support: John receives partial hourly compensation from Bath Township for approximately 15 hours per week in a community relations/chaplaincy role, and some compensation as a part-time Marine Deputy (sharing $5,000 in state funding). There are no health benefits or retirement associated with these roles. He turned down a full-time law enforcement community relations position with benefits in order to preserve flexibility for chaplaincy.
- He also turned down a vice president of business development role from a former corporate contact.
- Prayer is the primary request: for John and Sue personally, for the officers and families they serve, and for the Lord to raise up additional chaplains to meet the growing need. He noted it is nearly impossible to find people called to law enforcement chaplaincy.
- A neighboring county township agency recently asked John to serve as their chaplain — a request he currently cannot fulfill due to capacity.
- He is exploring the "Heroic Deeds" organization (based in Ohio), which takes a Christ-centered approach to chaplaincy, as a potential alternative to the International Conference of Police Chaplains (ICPC), whose guidelines discourage specific faith sharing.
