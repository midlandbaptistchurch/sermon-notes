## Summary
John Moe, former Midland Baptist member and founder of the Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, shares an update on his integrated chaplaincy work with law enforcement, SWAT, and emergency services in Clinton County, Michigan. He recounts specific ministry moments — including a deputy's salvation, a Bible study with officers, funerals, critical incident debriefs, and on-scene prayer — to illustrate how trust and presence open doors for the gospel among first responders. Anchoring his message in Romans 13:1-6, John challenges the congregation to pray for and support the 0.69% of Americans who serve as first responders, attending continually upon others' worst days.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focuses especially on verse 6, where law enforcement officers are described as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using this passage to frame the divine calling of law enforcement and the weight of unrelenting public service.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and authority of law enforcement, rooted in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being present from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry among first responders
- The husband-and-wife ministry partnership of John and Sue Moe
- Sharing the gospel clearly in secular and spiritually diverse settings
- The unrelenting burden borne by the 0.69% of Americans who serve as first responders
- Prayer and financial support for chaplaincy ministry

## Notes
**John Moe's Background and Call**
- Long-time former member of Midland Baptist; moved to Lansing in 2004 to start a business
- Wrote the civil service exam for Michigan State Police in 1989; pursued agriculture business instead
- Beginning in 2010, began ride-alongs with a local police lieutenant; accumulated ~300 hours of civilian ride-alongs by 2013
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013; recognized for living out his faith organically among officers
- Officially became a police chaplain in 2014 after persistent encouragement from a sergeant
- Went through police academy training; appointed to county SWAT team in 2017; completed basic SWAT school in 2022
- In 2023, began serving as chaplain for the Clinton County Sheriff's Office
- In 2025, became a fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy

**Scope of Ministry**
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team
- Also regularly called upon by 3–4 other police agencies, 2 fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the County 911 Dispatch Center
- Over 350 people across organizations have open doors to his ministry, and growing
- Serves as Peer Assistance Team coordinator — proactively checking on officers after difficult calls or personal struggles

**Sue Moe's Role**
- Volunteers with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST)
- Responds to critical incident scenes (suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support grieving families
- Her presence at scenes complements John's chaplaincy — she builds relationships with officers' spouses, opening doors for couples' ministry
- Example: At a tragic scene where a grandfather and five-year-old drowned after falling through pond ice, Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and family members — in prayer

**Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy**
- Ceremonial chaplains are often local pastors whose names appear on an org chart but who have no real relational access
- Integrated chaplaincy means being sworn in, working alongside officers, earning trust from within
- John's sworn reserve and deputy status, SWAT membership, and peer assistance role give him a seat at the table and a reservoir of trust
- Quote: "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust. So when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

**Key Ministry Moments Shared**
- *Deputy's salvation:* A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to share that he had just accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning before the evening service.
- *Bible study:* January–March of the current year, John led an 8-week Bible study for county deputies — the first ever in the county. Eight deputies attended regularly. Topics included the authority of Scripture, the divine calling of law enforcement (Gideon), and a clear gospel presentation on the final night. One deputy wrestled openly with forgiving the unresolved murderer of his grandparents; four of the deputies later attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church.
- *Critical incident debriefs (CISM):* John is certified through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF). He conducted a debrief after the Michigan State University active shooter event, after a drowning tragedy, and following a drunk driver striking a family of 16, among others.
- *Firefighter's son's funeral:* John conducted the funeral for a veteran who died by suicide — son of a firefighter in the county. Despite the family being non-churchy and the service beginning with a rap song containing profanity, John gave a clear gospel presentation to the assembled crowd.
- *SWAT pre-raid prayer:* Before a coordinated multi-agency raid to arrest suspects in a cold-case murder, John's SWAT commander turned to him and asked for "a word." John led approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies in prayer.
- *Marital counseling:* Two young officers separately approached John about marital struggles after having a new baby; he counseled them using the five love languages framework and pastoral guidance.
- *Plane crash response:* Responded to a plane crash in Bath Township; conducted a diffusal with law enforcement and firefighters on scene.

**Bible Study Approach and Gospel Clarity**
- Attendees brought many Bible translations; John used that as an opportunity to gently demonstrate differences and the clarity of the King James Bible
- Prioritized gospel presentation over Bible version debate, recognizing most attendees were not saved
- Clear gospel: justification is entirely the work of Christ at the cross; sanctification is the believer's subsequent work of rooting out the world
- Refused to adopt the posture of large chaplaincy organizations (e.g., International Conference of Police Chaplains) that discourage sharing one's faith; exploring partnership with "Heroic Deeds" ministry out of Ohio, which is Christ-forward

**Romans 13:6 — "Attending Continually"**
- The phrase "attending continually upon this very thing" describes the unrelenting, around-the-clock vigilance of law enforcement
- Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (law enforcement, fire, EMS, dispatch combined)
- That 0.69% attends continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%
- John's core mission statement: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

**Prayer and Support Needs**
- Prayer for John and Sue as every phone call they receive signals a tragedy
- Prayer for someone with a Bible-believing background to come alongside as an additional law enforcement chaplain — John cannot meet all the demands alone
- Financial support: John turned down a VP-level corporate role and a full-time police community relations position to remain flexible and available; partial compensation from the township (~15 hrs/week) and shared state marine patrol funding (~$5,000) leave a significant gap
- Midland Baptist is noted as one of the first and ongoing supporters of the ministry; prayer cards available at the visitor's desk
