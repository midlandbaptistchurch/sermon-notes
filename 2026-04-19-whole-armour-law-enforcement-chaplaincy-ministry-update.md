## Summary
Missionary John Moe, a law enforcement chaplain serving in Clinton County, Michigan, presented a ministry update on his integrated chaplaincy work with police departments, the county sheriff's office, a SWAT team, and multiple other first-responder agencies. He shared how God providentially led him from a business background into full-time chaplaincy, described the difference between ceremonial and integrated chaplaincy, and recounted specific ministry encounters including a deputy's salvation, a Bible study with officers, a funeral service, and critical incident stress debriefings. His wife Sue also shared her parallel ministry as a volunteer on the county Victim Support Team. The evening concluded with a reading and reflection on Romans 13:1-6 and an appeal for prayer and financial support.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John Moe used this passage to ground the divine calling of law enforcement, highlighting verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting vigilance required of first responders, and verse 4's identification of governing authorities as "ministers of God."

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as ministry (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel witness
- Evangelism and gospel presentation in unconventional settings
- Supporting first responders' mental and spiritual health
- The partnership of husband and wife in ministry
- Prayer and financial support for missionary chaplaincy work

## Notes

### Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John Moe has a long history with Midland Baptist Church; his family attended while his children were in the youth group.
- He spent most of his career in business, owning a company in Lansing now run by his son.
- In 1989 he scored well on the Michigan State Police civil service exam but pursued a civilian career instead; he reflects that God needed to mature him first.
- In 2010 he met a township lieutenant through business, began civilian ride-alongs, and accumulated approximately 300 hours in patrol cars by 2013.
- In 2013 he became a sworn reserve officer; in 2014 he became a police chaplain after initially declining the role.
- Timeline of key milestones:
  - 2014: Appointed chaplain; began chaplain academy training
  - 2016: House fire caused a one-year pause in academy
  - 2017: Appointed to county SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster
  - 2022: Completed basic SWAT school; became a basic SWAT-certified operator
  - 2023: Began serving as chaplain for the county sheriff's office
  - 2025: Attended state Marine Patrol training academy; became a fully deputized deputy

### Scope of Ministry
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team
- Also regularly called upon by three or four other county police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 dispatch center
- Estimates over 350 people across organizations he has open doors to minister to
- Serves as coordinator of the sheriff's office Peer Assistance Team
- Certified debriefer for critical incident stress management through ICISF (International Critical Incident Stress Foundation)

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- A ceremonial chaplain is often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has little day-to-day relationship with the team.
- An integrated chaplain is embedded from the inside — sworn, trained, present, and trusted — able to minister organically within the law enforcement community.
- John's sworn reserve status, deputy role, SWAT membership, and peer assistance coordination give him a trusted seat at the table.

### Sue Moe's Ministry
- Sue serves as a volunteer on the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team (VST).
- Her involvement began four and a half years ago after her nephew died of a fentanyl/heroin overdose in Daytona Beach, prompting her concern for the gap between law enforcement notification and family support.
- She responds to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents — sometimes in the middle of the night — spending three to four hours with families and deputies.
- She is able to build relationships with deputies' spouses, opening doors for deeper ministry connections.
- At one critical incident involving a grandfather and five-year-old who fell through ice (both perished), Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family — in prayer at the family's request.

### Ministry Stories and Gospel Encounters
- **Deputy's Salvation:** A deputy John had been mentoring called him while driving, said he had pulled over and accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism the morning of this service.
- **Law Enforcement Bible Study (January–March 2026):** John conducted an eight-week Bible study for the county with eight deputies attending fairly regularly — the first such study in the county's history. Participants came from Catholic, Church of God, Nazarene/Wesleyan, and other backgrounds. John used the King James Bible and built an opening session on the authority of Scripture. The final session included a clear gospel presentation. One deputy expressed he had never heard that justification is entirely Christ's work, not human effort. Another deputy challenged the idea that his grandparents' murderer could be forgiven, prompting a frank conversation about God's grace and justice. Four deputies from the Bible study attended a friend day at Solid Rock Baptist Church the Sunday before this service.
- **Veteran's Funeral:** A firefighter's son (a veteran) died by suicide. John was asked by the family to conduct the funeral, which was attended by approximately 150 people. Despite a very unchurched crowd and unconventional musical choices, John delivered a clear gospel presentation, framing it as his responsibility as the chaplain ordering the service.
- **SWAT Operation Prayer:** Before a coordinated multi-agency operation to apprehend suspects in a cold-case hunter murder, John's SWAT commander asked him for "a word." John led approximately 75 officers from various local, state, and federal agencies in prayer for safety and justice.
- **MSU Active Shooter Debrief:** After John's tactical team was first on the ground at the Michigan State University active shooter event, he conducted a critical incident stress debrief for the team the following week.
- **Plane Crash Response:** A plane crashed near Bath Township (close to John's property), killing three. John responded to the scene and conducted a diffusal with law enforcement and firefighters.
- **Drunk Driving Mass Casualty:** A family of 16 was struck by a drunk driver; two were killed instantly. John responded, assisted with evidence collection for six hours, and prayed with deputies, a lieutenant, and firefighters on scene. Follow-up continued for months.
- **Marital Counseling:** Two young officers with new babies independently reached out to John for marital advice; he discussed the five love languages and offered practical, caring guidance — reflecting the depth of trust built through his integrated presence.

### Romans 13 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- Romans 13:1-6 establishes human government as ordained by God.
- Verse 4 identifies the one who bears the sword as "a minister of God" — both to promote good and to execute wrath on evildoers.
- Verse 6's phrase "attending continually" describes the unrelenting vigilance of first responders: no holidays, no weather exemptions, no pause for personal hardship.
- Statistic shared: Only 0.69% of the US population serves as first responders (firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers), yet they attend continually to the worst moments of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Needs and Prayer Requests
- **Financial support:** John receives partial hourly compensation from the township (~15 hrs/week in a community relations/chaplaincy hybrid role) and part-time compensation as a Marine Deputy; there are no benefits or retirement. He turned down a full-time law enforcement role with benefits in order to preserve the autonomy his calling requires.
- **Prayer:** The primary need expressed is prayer support.
- **Additional chaplain:** John cannot meet the full demand — a neighboring township has already approached him. He urgently requested prayer for God to raise up another person called to law enforcement chaplaincy.
- **Presentation/website note:** His Facebook page, presentation, and website required approval from a police chief, SWAT commander, and county sheriff due to the use of agency patches.

### Closing Prayer Themes
- Gratitude for Midland Baptist's partnership and support
- Prayer for young people to surrender their lives to serve God
- Call for the congregation to "attend continually" to right doctrine, holy living, and prayer
