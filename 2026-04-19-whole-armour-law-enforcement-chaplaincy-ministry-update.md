## Summary
Missionary John Moe, a law enforcement chaplain serving in the Clinton County, Michigan area, presented a ministry update to Midland Baptist Church on the evening of April 19, 2026. He described his calling and training as an integrated law enforcement chaplain, the day-to-day realities of ministering to police officers, deputies, firefighters, dispatchers, and their families during critical incidents, and the gospel fruit resulting from years of trust-building relationships. His wife Sue also shared her role as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Team, showing how their two ministries complement each other on the scene of tragedies.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John Moe used this passage to ground the divine calling of law enforcement, highlighting verse 4 (officers as "ministers of God" bearing the sword against evil) and verse 6 (the phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting, sacrificial vigilance of first responders).

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of relational, inside-out ministry
- Trust as the foundation for gospel witness among law enforcement personnel
- The weight of "attending continually" borne by first responders
- Gospel proclamation — justification by Christ alone, not by works
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership in crisis response
- Prayer and financial support needs for chaplaincy ministry

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family grew up in the church before moving to Lansing in 2004.
- Owns a business in Lansing, now largely run by his son; stepped away from corporate work two years prior to this message due to a clear sense of God's call to full-time chaplaincy.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 and scored well but pursued an agricultural business career instead; God used those years to mature his character and faith.
- In 2010, met a township police lieutenant through business; began civilian ride-alongs, accumulating roughly 300 hours in a patrol car by 2013.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- Asked to serve as chaplain in 2013 by a sergeant who observed his consistent Christian witness; initially declined but sensed clear divine confirmation and accepted in 2014.
- Completed a public services chaplain academy (2014–2017, interrupted by a house fire in 2016).
- Appointed to County SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster in 2017; completed Basic SWAT School in 2022.
- Began serving as chaplain for the sheriff's office in 2023.
- Attended the State Training Academy for Marine Patrol in 2025; became a fully deputized deputy.
- Runs the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office.
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Also called upon regularly by three or four additional county police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the County 911 dispatch center — over 350 people across those organizations.

### Sue Moe's Ministry — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Sue Moe serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team.
- Activated for critical incidents such as completed suicides and accidental deaths; responds to scenes for three to four hours alongside deputies.
- Her call to VST was sparked by the fentanyl overdose death of a nephew in Daytona Beach and the lack of compassionate family notification she witnessed.
- Being a first-grade teacher with no prior grief/trauma training, she applied and was accepted four years before this message.
- Builds relationships with deputies' wives, opening doors for social connection and gospel witness.
- On scene at a drowning tragedy involving a grandfather and a five-year-old, Sue prayed openly with the grieving family — including the county sheriff — demonstrating the power of her ministry presence.
- County sheriff is a born-again believer, creating a supportive environment for faith-based ministry.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- A *ceremonial chaplain* is often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has little ongoing relationship with the team.
- An *integrated chaplain* is embedded from the inside: sworn reserve status, deputy credentials, SWAT team membership, and peer assistance coordination give John organic access and earned trust.
- Key principle: "A ministry of presence" — being available, visible, and trusted so that when officers hit a crisis, there is a safe, Bible-believing resource already close at hand.

### Gospel Witness and Bible Study
- Conducted an eight-week Bible study in January–March (year of message) for county personnel — the first ever in Clinton County.
- Eight deputies attended fairly regularly; represented a wide range of backgrounds (Catholic, Church of God, Nazarene, modern evangelical, and unchurched).
- Used a King James Bible; was able to demonstrate differences between translations in a gentle, non-combative way.
- Week 1 established the authority of Scripture as a foundation for subsequent weeks.
- Final session included a clear gospel presentation tracing from the Garden of Eden through the New Testament.
- Key conversation: one deputy who had attended church his whole life realized for the first time that justification is entirely the work of Christ at the cross, not human effort — sanctification is where personal effort applies.
- Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled with whether God could forgive a murderer; John answered honestly and pastorally.
- Four of the eight Bible study deputies attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church the Sunday before this message; two are unsaved.
- Earlier testimony: a deputy named Ryan called John immediately after pulling over on the side of the road and trusting Christ; John and Sue attended Ryan's baptism the morning of this very Sunday.

### Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISM)
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- Conducted a CISM debrief after the Michigan State University active shooter event; the Clinton County SWAT team was first on the ground and exposed to deceased students.
- Other debriefs conducted after: a drowning of a grandfather and five-year-old child; a drunk-driver collision that killed two members of a family of sixteen; a plane crash near Bath Township (three fatalities, the wreckage landing within seconds of flight time from John's property).
- Three officer-involved shootings since he joined the county; responds to support officers post-incident.
- Conducted a SWAT pre-operation prayer with approximately 75 officers from multiple local, state, and federal agencies before a coordinated arrest operation related to a five-year-old murder case.
- Conducted a veteran's funeral for a firefighter's son who died by suicide — a standing-room crowd of approximately 150 people; gave a clear gospel presentation despite the unchurched nature of the family.

### Romans 13 and the Calling of Law Enforcement
- Romans 13:1-6 describes governmental authority as ordained by God; officers are described as "ministers of God" (vv. 4, 6).
- The phrase "attending continually" (v. 6) captures the unrelenting weight of first-responder service: no day off for weather, holidays, anniversaries, illness, or national crises.
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first-responder role (full-time and volunteer firefighters, law enforcement at all levels, EMS, dispatchers).
- That fraction of a percent is continually attending to the worst moments of the remaining 99.3%.
- John's mission statement: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs
- Partial hourly compensation from Bath Township (community relations officer role, ~15 hrs/week); no health benefits or retirement.
- Partial compensation from the sheriff's office for Marine Deputy role (seasonal, shared $5,000 state funding with another part-time deputy).
- Turned down a full-time law enforcement community relations position with benefits in order to maintain the autonomy required for chaplaincy.
- Turned down a vice president of business development position from a former colleague.
- Prayer needs: (1) financial support for the ministry; (2) the Lord to raise up additional chaplains — finding called, suited people for law enforcement chaplaincy is extremely difficult; (3) fire departments and other agencies requesting chaplains that he currently cannot adequately serve.
- Mentioned exploring the *Heroic Deeds* organization (Ohio-based) as a more gospel-forward alternative to the International Conference of Police Chaplains (ICPC), which discourages chaplains from sharing their specific faith.
- Fresh prayer cards available at the visitors desk.
