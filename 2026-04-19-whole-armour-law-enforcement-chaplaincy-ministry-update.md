## Summary
Missionary John Moe and his wife Sue gave a ministry update on their work with Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy, serving officers, deputies, and first responders in Clinton County, Michigan. John described his journey from civilian ride-alongs and reserve officer status to becoming a fully integrated chaplain, SWAT-certified operator, and Marine Deputy, explaining how this deep integration builds the trust necessary to minister the gospel to law enforcement personnel. Sue shared her parallel role on the County Victim Support Team, which brings her alongside families at scenes of tragedy and opens relational doors with officers' spouses.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John used this passage to establish the biblical basis for law enforcement as a divinely ordained calling, focusing especially on verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting weight of vigilance carried by first responders, and connecting officers to the God who called them to that work.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling of law enforcement as described in Romans 13
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being present from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry in law enforcement
- The "attending continually" weight borne by the less than 1% who serve as first responders
- Connecting the called to the one who called them
- The gospel of grace — justification by Christ's work at the cross, not by human effort
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership in crisis care

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family grew up in the church before relocating to Lansing in 2004.
- Spent most of his career in business (agricultural and coatings industries); owns a company now run by his son.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued a civilian career instead; God used those intervening years to mature his character.
- In 2010, through a business contact, began civilian ride-alongs with a township police agency — approximately 300 hours over three years.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- In 2013 a sergeant recognized his Christian witness and asked him to become the department's chaplain; John initially declined but sensed a clear call from God and accepted in 2014.
- Timeline of training and appointments:
  - 2014: Became police chaplain; began chaplain academy training.
  - 2016: House fire caused a one-year withdrawal from academy.
  - 2017: Appointed to County SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster.
  - 2022: Completed Basic SWAT School; certified as a SWAT operator.
  - 2023: Began serving as chaplain for the sheriff's office.
  - 2025: Completed state Marine Patrol academy; became a fully deputized deputy.

### Scope of Ministry
- Primary responsibilities: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team.
- Also regularly called upon by three or four other police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and County 911 dispatch.
- Estimated ministry reach: over 350 individuals across these organizations.
- Runs the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office — a sergeant and three deputies who monitor team wellbeing after difficult calls.

### Sue Moe's Ministry — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Began volunteering with the County Victim Support Advocates Team approximately four years ago, motivated by the loss of her nephew to a fentanyl overdose at age 32.
- Responds to scenes involving completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents; spends three to four hours on scene with deputies and family members.
- Her relationships with officers' families — particularly spouses — complement and extend John's chaplaincy relationships.
- Noted example: At the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old who broke through pond ice (both fatally), Sue prayed aloud with the grieving family, the sheriff, and deputies present in the room.
- The county VST is not an explicitly Christian organization, but the county sheriff is a born-again believer who actively encourages officers to engage with chaplaincy resources.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- A ceremonial chaplain is often a local pastor whose name appears on an organizational chart but who has limited day-to-day presence and relationship with the team.
- An integrated chaplain is embedded from the inside — sworn status, SWAT certification, peer assistance role — which generates organic trust rather than requiring an external entry point.
- John's frustration with the International Conference of Police Chaplains (ICPC): their guidance discourages chaplains from being specific about their faith. John's conviction is that if Christ is not the goal, the ministry lacks purpose.
- He has identified an Ohio-based organization called Heroic Deeds that takes a more explicitly Christ-centered approach to law enforcement chaplaincy; he plans to attend one of their meetings in August.

### Gospel Opportunities
- John conducted a county Bible study from January through mid-March with approximately eight deputies attending regularly — the first Bible study ever held in Clinton County for law enforcement.
  - Participants represented a wide range of religious backgrounds: Catholic, Church of God, Nazarene/Wesleyan, modern evangelical, and unchurched.
  - He used the King James Bible and gently highlighted textual differences when participants noticed them, without making Bible version the focus.
  - Session one established the authority of Scripture as a platform for the remaining weeks.
  - Topics included a study of Gideon, the divine calling of law enforcement (Romans 13), and a final session with a clear gospel presentation tracing redemption from Genesis through the New Testament.
  - One deputy responded: "I've been in church my whole life — I never knew it was all about what Christ did, not what I did."
  - Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled openly with the idea of grace extending even to murderers; John affirmed his human reaction while pointing him to God's sovereignty and grace.
  - Four of the eight Bible study participants attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church; two of those are unsaved.
- A deputy named Ryan called John to report he had pulled his car over and trusted Christ; John and Sue attended his baptism that morning (the day of this service).
- John conducted a funeral for a firefighter's son — a veteran who died by suicide. The family was not churchy, but John delivered a clear gospel presentation to approximately 150 attendees, including rough characters, framing it as his responsibility as the one ordering the service.

### Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM)
- John is a certified debriefer with the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- Conducts structured critical incident stress debriefs after traumatic events — a closed, confidential group process for those present at a specific incident, designed to help the brain heal.
- Notable debriefs conducted:
  - Michigan State University active shooter event: Clinton County SWAT was first tactical team on the ground; debrief conducted the following week.
  - Grandfather and five-year-old lost through pond ice: responded to scene, conducted diffusels with deputies, followed up over subsequent days.
  - Family of 16 struck by a drunk driver: two killed instantly, nearly all others hospitalized; six hours on scene, prayed with deputies, lieutenant, and firefighters; months of follow-up.
  - Plane crash in Bath Township (three fatalities, near John's property): diffusel with all law enforcement and fire personnel on scene.
  - Three officer-involved shootings since he joined the county; responded to the most recent one, escorted officers away from scene to decompress.
  - Coordinated SWAT operation to arrest suspect in a multi-year Bath Township murder investigation: John led the assembled multi-agency team (approximately 75 officers, including federal agents) in prayer before deployment.

### Romans 13 — The Weight of "Attending Continually"
- John read Romans 13:1-6, emphasizing verse 6: "for they are God's ministers, attending continually upon this very thing."
- "Attending continually" means waiting on or taking care of without pause — regardless of weather, holidays, anniversaries, birthdays, personal illness, national disasters, or societal unrest.
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first responder role (full-time and volunteer firefighters, local/state/federal law enforcement, EMTs, paramedics, dispatchers).
- That less-than-1% attends continually to the worst day of the other 99.3% of the population.
- John's chaplaincy goal: connect each individual officer to the God who called and wired them for that role.

### Ministry Needs
- Financial support: John receives limited hourly compensation from the township (approximately 15 hours/week in a community relations role) and partial compensation as a part-time Marine Deputy (sharing $5,000 in state funding with another part-time deputy). There are no health benefits or retirement attached to these roles. He turned down a corporate VP offer and a full-time law enforcement role with benefits in order to maintain the autonomy his calling requires.
- Prayer: The most urgent need. Every phone call John and Sue receive signals that a tragedy has occurred.
- Personnel: It is extremely difficult to find individuals called to law enforcement chaplaincy. John is being asked to take on additional agencies he does not have capacity to serve. Prayer for God to raise up additional chaplains is requested.
- Prayer cards are available at the visitors' desk.
