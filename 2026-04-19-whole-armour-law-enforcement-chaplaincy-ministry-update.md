## Summary
Missionary John Moe, a law enforcement chaplain serving in Clinton County, Michigan, presented a ministry update on his integrated chaplaincy work with local police, sheriff's office, and SWAT team. He described how he builds deep trust with officers over time in order to share the gospel with them and minister to them during critical incidents and personal crises. Drawing from Romans 13, he cast a vision for law enforcement as a divine calling and challenged the congregation to pray for first responders who "attend continually" to the worst moments of others' lives.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on governing authorities as God's ordained ministers, with John Moe focusing particularly on verse 6 and the phrase "attending continually" as a window into the unrelenting burden carried by law enforcement officers.

## Key Themes
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy: being present from the inside rather than as an outsider
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel ministry in law enforcement
- Romans 13 and the divine calling of law enforcement officers
- The physical and emotional toll of "attending continually" on first responders
- Evangelism and clear gospel presentations in unexpected settings
- The role of Sue Moe and the Victim Support Team (VST) in complementary ministry
- Critical incident stress debriefing as pastoral care

## Notes

### Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John Moe has a long history with Midland Baptist Church; moved to Lansing in 2004 and started a business there.
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued a civilian career in agricultural business instead.
- In 2010, began civilian ride-alongs with a township police agency; accumulated approximately 300 hours by 2013.
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013; recognized by a sergeant for his Christian character and asked to become chaplain.
- Initially declined the chaplain role, feeling unworthy of the title, but sensed clear direction from the Lord to accept.
- Became chaplain in 2014 and began intensive training.

### Roles and Responsibilities
- Chaplain, Bath Township Police Department
- Chaplain and Marine Deputy, Clinton County Sheriff's Office
- Chaplain and Quartermaster, Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team — completed Basic SWAT School in 2022
- Peer Assistance Team Coordinator for the sheriff's office
- Certified Critical Incident Stress Management (CISM) Debriefer through ICISF (International Critical Incident Stress Foundation)
- Called upon by three to four additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and County 911 dispatch — over 350 people across these organizations

### Sue Moe — Victim Support Team (VST)
- Sue Moe serves as a volunteer for the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates Team.
- Motivated by the loss of her nephew to a fentanyl overdose; recognized the gap between law enforcement notification and family pastoral care.
- Responds to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents.
- Her presence at scenes has opened doors with officers' families and enabled prayer ministry in otherwise secular settings.
- Example: At the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old lost through ice on a pond, Sue prayed with the grieving mother and the entire room — including the sheriff and deputies.

### Integrated Chaplaincy Philosophy
- Distinguishes between "ceremonial" chaplains (name on an org chart, rarely involved) and "integrated" chaplains who are organically embedded in the agency.
- His sworn status, SWAT certification, and peer assistance role give him a "seat at the table" and build the reservoir of trust necessary for effective ministry.
- Describes chaplaincy as a "ministry of presence" — simply being there and being available.

### Gospel Ministry Highlights
- Conducted the first-ever Bible study for Clinton County Sheriff's Office, January–March of the presentation year; eight deputies attended regularly across eight weeks.
  - Week one: established the authority of Scripture.
  - Final week: clear gospel presentation from Genesis through the New Testament.
  - One deputy, a lifelong churchgoer, had never understood that justification is entirely Christ's work at the cross, not human effort.
  - Another deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled openly with whether God could forgive their killer — a conversation John engaged honestly and pastorally.
  - Four of the Bible study deputies attended Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church; two of them are not yet saved.
- A deputy named Ryan called John the night he accepted Christ while pulled over on the side of the road; John and Sue were present at his baptism the same morning as the ministry update.
- Conducted a funeral for the son of a county firefighter — a veteran who died by suicide — and delivered a clear gospel presentation to a standing-room-only crowd of approximately 150 people.
- Has counseled young officers on marriage and family challenges (e.g., the Five Love Languages), reflecting the breadth of pastoral trust he has earned.

### Critical Incident Response
- Led critical incident stress debriefs following the Michigan State University active shooter event (Clinton County SWAT was first tactical team on scene).
- Responded to a plane crash in Bath Township — the crash site was within seconds of his own property.
- Involved in response to three officer-involved shootings in the county.
- Responded to a drunk driving crash involving a family of 16; two killed instantly, nearly all others hospitalized. Prayed with all deputies, the lieutenant, and firefighters on scene; months of follow-up ensued.
- Frequently leads prayer before SWAT operations; example given of praying with approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies before a coordinated arrest operation.

### Romans 13 — The Calling of Law Enforcement
- Romans 13:1: All governing powers are ordained of God.
- Romans 13:4: The officer/ruler is a "minister of God" — bearing the sword not in vain, a revenger to execute wrath on evil.
- Romans 13:6: "Attending continually upon this very thing" — John emphasizes this phrase as describing the unrelenting, around-the-clock nature of law enforcement service regardless of weather, holidays, personal circumstances, or national crises.
- Statistic: Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in some first responder capacity (firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers).
- Core calling as chaplain: "Connect the called to the one who called them."

### Ministry Needs
- Financial support: John receives limited hourly compensation from the township (~15 hrs/week) and part-time compensation as a Marine Deputy; no benefits or retirement. The ministry depends on church support partnerships.
- Turned down a full-time community relations position with benefits and a corporate VP role in order to maintain the flexibility his chaplaincy requires.
- Prayer for additional chaplains: the demand far exceeds his capacity; other agencies are requesting his services and he cannot meet the need alone.
- Ongoing prayer for deputies, officers, and their families navigating trauma, faith questions, and the weight of their calling.
