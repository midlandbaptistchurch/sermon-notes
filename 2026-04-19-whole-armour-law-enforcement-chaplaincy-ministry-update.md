## Summary
John Moe, a former longtime member of Midland Baptist Church, shares an update on his Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy ministry based in Clinton County, Michigan. He traces his journey from a business career and civilian ride-alongs to becoming a sworn reserve officer, county deputy, SWAT-certified chaplain, and peer assistance team coordinator who ministers to over 350 first responders across multiple agencies. Drawing on Romans 13:1-6, John challenges the congregation to appreciate the divine calling of law enforcement and to pray for those who "attend continually" to the worst days of others.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focuses especially on verse 6, which describes civil authorities as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using this phrase to frame the unrelenting weight of vigilance carried by law enforcement and first responders.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and biblical mandate of law enforcement (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — building trust from the inside out
- Gospel evangelism in a law enforcement context (Bible study with deputies, funeral ministry)
- The husband-and-wife ministry partnership of John and Sue Moe
- Critical incident stress debriefing and caring for officers' mental and spiritual health
- The need for prayer and financial support for law enforcement chaplaincy
- Less than 0.7% of the U.S. population serves as first responders for the other 99.3%

## Notes

### John's Background and Call to Chaplaincy
- John and Sue are former longtime members of Midland Baptist who relocated to Lansing in 2004 to run a family business.
- John wrote the civil service exam for the Michigan State Police in 1989 but pursued an ag-business career instead; he credits God with preparing his character over the intervening decades.
- Beginning in 2010, John did approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs with a local police department. In 2013 he was invited to become a sworn reserve officer, and in 2014 he became the department's chaplain.
- Timeline of credentials: sworn reserve officer (2013), chaplain (2014), police academy attendance (2014–2016), county SWAT chaplain/quartermaster (2017), basic SWAT certification (2022), Clinton County Sheriff's Office chaplain (2023), fully deputized Marine Patrol deputy (2025).
- He currently serves Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, and the Clinton County Special Operations Team as primary assignments, with open doors to several additional police agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the county 911 Dispatch Center — over 350 people total.

### Sue's Role — Victim Support Team
- Sue serves as a volunteer with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates team, responding to critical incidents (suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support families on scene.
- Her presence alongside John creates a synergy: she builds relationships with officers' spouses, and her compassionate, prayerful ministry at scenes — including leading a room containing the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members in prayer — complements John's integrated chaplaincy role.

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- John distinguishes between "ceremonial" chaplains (often local pastors listed on an org chart with little relational involvement) and "integrated" chaplains who are embedded within the agencies they serve.
- His sworn status, deputization, and SWAT membership give him a seat at the table and allow trust to be built organically over time.
- Key principle: "By being present and integrated, I'm building a reservoir of trust. So when the tough cop demeanor finally hits something that affects them, there's a safe and trusted resource close at hand."

### Gospel Ministry Examples
- **Deputy's salvation and baptism:** A deputy John ministered to pulled over one day, called John, and accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism the very morning of this presentation.
- **Bible study with deputies:** January–March 2026, John led an eight-week Bible study for eight deputies — the first of its kind in the county. He navigated a mixed audience (Catholic, Wesleyan, various Bible versions) by first establishing the authority of Scripture, then building toward a clear gospel presentation. One deputy wrestled with the question of whether the murderer of his grandparents could be forgiven; John gave an honest, compassionate answer rooted in the sufficiency of Christ's atonement. Four deputies from the study attended a friend day at Solid Rock Baptist Church.
- **Veteran's funeral:** John conducted a funeral for a firefighter's son — a veteran who died by suicide — giving a clear gospel presentation to a rough, non-churched crowd, even after the service opened with a profanity-laced song the family had chosen.
- **SWAT callout prayer:** Before a coordinated multi-agency arrest operation involving ~75 officers (local, state, federal), John's SWAT commander asked him for "a word," and John led the assembled law enforcement in prayer for safety and justice.
- **Three-hour gospel conversation:** During a recent SWAT callout, John spent three hours in a gear truck sharing the Lord with a fellow operator who was not on the active element.

### Critical Incident Stress Debriefing
- John is a certified debriefer through the International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF).
- He conducted a debrief after his TAC team was first on the ground at the Michigan State University active shooter event, where team members were exposed to deceased students.
- Other debriefs: a grandfather and five-year-old lost through pond ice; a family of 16 struck by a drunk driver; officer-involved shootings; a plane crash in Bath Township.
- He also provides ongoing pastoral care for officers dealing with marriage stress, infertility, injuries, and personal loss.

### Romans 13 and the Weight of Continuous Service
- Romans 13:4 describes civil authorities as "minister[s] of God" who bear "the sword" as "revenger[s] to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil."
- Romans 13:6 — "they are God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing" — is the key verse. John interprets "attending continually" as waiting on or caring for without pause, regardless of weather, holidays, illness, or personal circumstances.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population (full-time and volunteer firefighters, all levels of law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers) serves as first responders attending to the worst moments of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: "The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."

### Ministry Needs and Prayer Requests
- Financial support: John receives partial hourly compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township; the Marine Deputy role is funded by a shared $5,000 state allocation split between two part-time deputies. He has turned down a VP-level corporate role and a full-time department position to preserve ministry flexibility.
- Prayer for additional Bible-believing chaplains — John is being asked to expand to neighboring counties and agencies but lacks capacity.
- Prayer for the ongoing ministry to deputies, fire, EMS, and dispatch personnel in Clinton County and beyond.
- Prayer cards are available at the visitor's desk at Midland Baptist.
