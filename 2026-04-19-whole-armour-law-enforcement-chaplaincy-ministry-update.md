## Summary
Missionary John Moe, formerly of Midland Baptist Church and now based in Lansing, presented an update on his integrated law enforcement chaplaincy ministry, Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy. He shared how God called him — through years of ride-alongs, reserve officer service, and SWAT training — into a deeply embedded role serving officers, deputies, and first responders across Clinton County and beyond. Drawing on Romans 13:1-6, John challenged the congregation to recognize law enforcement as a God-ordained calling and to pray faithfully for those who "attend continually" on others' worst days.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John read and expounded this passage, highlighting verse 6's phrase "attending continually" as a description of the unrelenting, God-ordained duty of law enforcement officers, and connecting their role to divine authority and calling.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and authority of law enforcement (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — the importance of being embedded within law enforcement agencies to build trust
- Ministry of presence — chaplaincy as being available, trusted, and consistently present
- The gospel shared in unique mission fields — Bible studies, funerals, critical incidents, and one-on-one conversations
- The weight of "attending continually" — the physical and emotional toll on first responders
- Sue Moe's complementary ministry through the County Victim Support Advocates Team
- The need for prayer and financial support for law enforcement chaplaincy

## Notes

### John Moe's Background and Call
- Long history with Midland Baptist Church; family raised here before moving to Lansing in 2004
- Owns a business in Lansing now run by his son; previously traveled extensively in corporate sales roles
- Wrote the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued business instead; God used those years to mature his character
- In 2010, began civilian ride-alongs with a township police lieutenant — approximately 300 hours over three years
- Became a sworn reserve officer in 2013
- A sergeant recognized his Christian witness and asked him to serve as police chaplain; he initially declined, then sensed clear leading from the Lord
- Became chaplain in 2014; underwent intensive training including a public services chaplain academy
- 2016: withdrew from academy for a year due to a house fire
- 2017: appointed to county SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster
- 2022: completed basic SWAT school; now a basic SWAT certified operator-chaplain-quartermaster
- 2023: began serving as chaplain for the county sheriff's office
- 2025: completed state training academy for Marine Patrol; became a fully deputized deputy
- Also serves as coordinator of the Peer Assistance Team for the sheriff's office
- Serves Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations Team, and is regularly called upon by 3-4 additional agencies, 2 fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and county 911 dispatch — over 350 people across these organizations

### Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy
- Ceremonial chaplains are often local pastors whose name appears on an org chart but who have little ongoing relationship with officers
- Integrated chaplaincy means being organically embedded — sworn status, SWAT membership, peer assistance role — ministry flows from the inside out
- Trust is earned one relationship at a time; that trust opens doors to minister at critical moments

### Sue Moe — Victim Support Team Ministry
- Motivated by the fentanyl overdose death of her nephew four years ago; recognized the gap between law enforcement and grieving families
- Applied to and was accepted onto Clinton County's Victim Support Advocates Team
- Responds to scenes of completed suicides, accidental deaths, and other critical incidents — sometimes through the night
- Builds relationships with deputies' wives, opening doors for deeper family ministry
- At a scene involving a grandfather and five-year-old who broke through ice (both lives lost), Sue prayed with the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members
- Her VST role and John's chaplaincy create a powerful, complementary ministry partnership

### Gospel Opportunities
- Conducted an eight-week Bible study for county personnel (January–mid-March 2026); eight deputies attended fairly regularly — the first Bible study of its kind in the county
- Participants brought various Bible versions; John used differences in translation to point gently toward the King James Bible's clarity
- Final session included a clear gospel presentation from Genesis through the New Testament
  - One deputy realized for the first time that salvation is by Christ's work alone, not by human merit
  - Another deputy wrestled with whether God could forgive his grandparents' unidentified murderer — a deeply personal and ongoing conversation
- Four of the Bible study deputies attended Solid Rock Baptist Church's Friend Day; two are unsaved
- A deputy called John after getting saved — pulling over on the side of the road to trust Christ — and John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning (April 19, 2026)
- Conducted a funeral for a firefighter's son (a veteran who died by suicide): standing room only (~150 people), non-churched family; John gave a clear gospel presentation despite an unconventional service
- Regularly counsels young officers on marriage and family matters, reflecting deep personal trust

### Critical Incident Ministry
- Certified debriefer through ICISF (International Critical Incident Stress Foundation)
- Conducted critical incident stress debrief for Clinton County SWAT team after the Michigan State University active shooter event
- Responded to scene of grandfather and child lost through ice; conducted diffusels with deputies
- Responded to Bath Township plane crash (three fatalities, near his property); conducted diffusels with law enforcement and firefighters on scene
- Three officer-involved shootings since becoming county chaplain; responded to most recent one, escorted officers from scene to decompress
- Responded to scene where a family of 16 was struck by a drunk driver (two killed, most hospitalized); prayed with deputies, lieutenant, and firefighters; months of follow-up
- Coordinated prayer before a multi-agency, multi-location SWAT operation to apprehend suspects in a long-unsolved Bath Township murder
- Regularly conducts three-hour conversations with individual officers during call-outs

### Romans 13 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement
- Romans 13:1: All governing authority is ordained by God
- Romans 13:4: The officer/ruler is a "minister of God" — bearing the sword not in vain, executing wrath on evildoers
- Romans 13:6: "Attending continually upon this very thing" — John emphasized the unrelenting, 24/7 nature of law enforcement service regardless of weather, holidays, personal circumstances, or national crises
- Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves in a first responder capacity (full-time and volunteer firefighters, law enforcement, EMS, dispatchers)
- That less-than-three-quarters of a percent attends continually to the worst days of the remaining 99.3%
- John's mission: connect the called officer to the One who called them — God, the author of all authority
