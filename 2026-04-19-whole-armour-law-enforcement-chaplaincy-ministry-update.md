## Summary
John Moe, a longtime former member of Midland Baptist Church, shares an update on his Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry serving Clinton County, Michigan. He describes how God called him from a business career into integrated law enforcement chaplaincy — serving as a sworn reserve officer, county deputy, SWAT team member, peer assistance coordinator, and certified critical incident stress debriefer. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, John reflects on the divine calling of law enforcement and his mission to connect officers to the God who called them.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — Paul's teaching on God-ordained governmental authority, with particular focus on verse 6's description of civil servants as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing" — a phrase John applies to the unrelenting, around-the-clock burden carried by law enforcement officers and first responders.

## Key Themes
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy: the importance of being embedded within law enforcement rather than merely on an organizational chart
- Building trust with officers through presence, availability, and genuine relationship
- The divine calling of law enforcement as established in Romans 13
- Connecting officers to the God who called them as the core mission of chaplaincy
- Evangelism in a unique mission field: Bible studies, gospel presentations at funerals, and one-on-one conversations
- Sue Moe's complementary ministry through the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates (VST)
- The weight of "attending continually" — the unrelenting vigilance required of the 0.69% of the U.S. population who serve as first responders

## Notes

**Background and Call**
- John and Sue Moe were longtime members of Midland Baptist before relocating to Lansing in 2004 to start a business.
- John wrote the civil service exam for the Michigan State Police in 1989 but pursued agriculture instead; he describes God needing to mature his character before placing him in law enforcement.
- Beginning in 2010, John did approximately 300 hours of civilian ride-alongs with a local police department, becoming a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- In 2014, a sergeant recognized John's witness and asked him to become the department's chaplain.

**Roles and Credentials**
- Chaplain: Bath Township Police Department, Clinton County Sheriff's Office
- Member and Quartermaster: Clinton County Special Operations (SWAT) Team; Basic SWAT certified (2022)
- Peer Assistance Team Coordinator for the county
- Fully deputized Marine Patrol Deputy, Clinton County Sheriff's Office (2025)
- Certified Debriefer, International Critical Incident Stress Foundation (ICISF)
- Ministry reach: over 350 personnel across multiple police agencies, fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance, and 911 Dispatch

**Sue Moe's Ministry**
- Sue serves as a volunteer Victim Support Advocate (VST) for Clinton County, responding to scenes of critical incidents (suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support surviving family members.
- Her presence at scenes has opened doors to relationships with deputies' spouses and allowed her to pray with families, including a moving example of leading an entire room — including the sheriff and deputies — in prayer at the scene of a drowning involving a grandfather and five-year-old child.

**Key Ministry Stories Shared**
- *Deputy's salvation and baptism:* A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to say he had just accepted Christ. John and Sue were present at his baptism the morning of this presentation.
- *Bible study with deputies:* John led an eight-week Bible study for eight deputies (January–March 2026), concluding with a clear gospel presentation. Deputies came from various religious backgrounds; one deputy wrestled openly with whether the murderer of his grandparents could be forgiven — a conversation John described as ongoing.
- *Veteran's funeral:* John was asked to conduct the funeral of a firefighter's son (a veteran who died by suicide). Despite a non-church-going family and an unconventional service, John delivered a clear gospel presentation to a mixed crowd.
- *Michigan State University active shooter (2023):* Clinton County's TAC team was first tactical boots on the ground; John conducted a critical incident stress debrief the following week.
- *Drowning incident:* John responded to a scene where a grandfather and five-year-old child died after going through ice on a pond; Sue prayed with the family and law enforcement present.
- *Family struck by drunk driver:* John prayed with deputies, a lieutenant, and firefighters after a family of 16 was hit; one deputy required months of follow-up support.
- *SWAT raid prayer:* With approximately 75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies assembled for simultaneous arrests in a cold-case murder, John's SWAT commander asked him to lead the group in prayer before deployment.
- *Three-hour gospel conversation:* During a recent SWAT callout, John spent three hours talking about the Lord with a non-operational team member while waiting in a gear truck.

**Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement**
- John walked through the passage, emphasizing that governmental authority is ordained by God (v. 1-2), that rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil (v. 3), and that the officer bearing a sword is "the minister of God, a revenger to execute wrath upon him that doeth evil" (v. 4).
- He focused on verse 6 — "they are God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing" — as a description of the unrelenting nature of law enforcement service: no holidays, no weather exceptions, no pause regardless of personal circumstances.
- Only 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders (law enforcement, fire, EMS, dispatch), yet they attend continually to the worst days of the other 99.3%.
- **Key statement:** *"The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."*

**Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy**
- John contrasted "ceremonial" chaplains (often local pastors listed on an org chart with little relational presence) with "integrated" chaplains who are embedded within the agency through sworn status, shared training, and daily relationship.
- He expressed frustration with the International Conference of Police Chaplains' reluctance to emphasize Christ-centered ministry, and noted his interest in *Heroic Deeds*, an Ohio-based organization with a more explicitly gospel-focused approach.

**Ministry Needs and Prayer Requests**
- Financial support: John receives partial hourly compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township in a community relations role; as a Marine Deputy, he shares $5,000 in state funding between two part-time deputies. He turned down a full-time salaried position and a VP business development offer to maintain ministry flexibility.
- Prayer for a Bible-believing chaplain to help meet the growing demand; John is being asked to expand into additional counties and agencies he does not have capacity to serve.
- Prayer for John and Sue as every call they receive represents someone's tragedy or crisis.
- Midland Baptist is one of the ministry's supporting churches; prayer cards are available at the visitors' desk.
