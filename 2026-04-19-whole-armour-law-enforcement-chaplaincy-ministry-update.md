## Summary
John Moe, a former member of Midland Baptist Church and founder of the Whole Armour Law Enforcement Chaplaincy Ministry, shares an update on his integrated chaplaincy work with law enforcement agencies in Clinton County, Michigan. He recounts specific ministry moments — including a deputy's salvation and baptism, a Bible study with eight officers, critical incident debriefs, and a veteran's funeral — to illustrate how trust is built from the inside out. Drawing from Romans 13:1-6, he calls the church to recognize the divine calling on first responders and to pray for those who attend continually upon the needs of others.

## Scripture
Romans 13:1-6 — John focuses especially on verse 6, which describes civil servants as "God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing," using it to highlight the unrelenting weight borne by law enforcement officers and the chaplain's role in connecting the called to the one who called them.

## Key Themes
- The divine calling and authority of law enforcement (Romans 13)
- Integrated vs. ceremonial chaplaincy — ministry from the inside out
- Building trust as the foundation for gospel witness
- The importance of a clear gospel presentation in every ministry context
- Husband-and-wife ministry partnership (John's chaplaincy and Sue's Victim Support Team role)
- The emotional and psychological toll on first responders
- Prayer and financial support needs for frontline ministry

## Notes
**John Moe's Background and Call**
- Long-time former member of Midland Baptist; moved to Lansing in 2004 to start a business.
- Had an early interest in law enforcement — scored high on the Michigan State Police civil service exam in 1989 but pursued agriculture business instead.
- Through civilian ride-alongs beginning in 2010 (~300 hours by 2013), was invited to become a sworn reserve officer in 2013.
- Became chaplain for Bath Township Police Department in 2014 after his Christian witness was noticed by a sergeant.
- 2017: appointed to Clinton County SWAT team as chaplain and quartermaster.
- 2022: completed basic SWAT school; certified SWAT operator chaplain.
- 2023: began serving as chaplain for the Clinton County Sheriff's Office.
- 2025: attended state Marine Patrol training academy; became a fully deputized deputy.
- Currently has open doors to minister to 350+ people across Bath Township PD, Clinton County Sheriff's Office, Clinton County Special Operations Team, multiple other agencies, two fire departments, Clinton County Area Ambulance Service, and the County 911 Dispatch Center.

**Integrated vs. Ceremonial Chaplaincy**
- A ceremonial chaplain is often a local pastor whose name appears on an org chart but who has no deep relationship with officers.
- Integrated chaplaincy means being sworn in, participating in operations, and earning trust from the inside — making the chaplain a natural, trusted resource when officers face crisis.
- John's sworn reserve and deputy status gives him "a seat at the table" that purely civilian chaplains do not have.

**Sue Moe's Role — Victim Support Team (VST)**
- Sue volunteers with the Clinton County Victim Support Advocates team.
- Responds to critical incident scenes (suicides, accidental deaths, etc.) to support families.
- Her presence at scenes has built relationships with deputies' spouses, opening doors for couple-level ministry.
- A vivid example: at the scene of a grandfather and five-year-old who fell through ice, Sue led the entire room — including the sheriff, a sergeant, a deputy, and grieving family members — in prayer.

**Key Ministry Moments Shared**
- *Deputy Ryan's salvation and baptism:* A deputy called John while pulled over on the side of the road to say he had accepted Christ. John and Sue attended his baptism that very morning before the evening service.
- *Bible study with eight deputies (January–March 2026):* First ever county-wide law enforcement Bible study. Participants came from Catholic, Nazarene, Wesleyan, and other backgrounds. John used differences between Bible versions to open conversation, built a case for the authority of Scripture in week one, and gave a clear gospel presentation in the final session. One deputy, whose grandparents were murdered, wrestled openly with whether God could forgive the unresolved killer — John handled the conversation with honesty and grace. Four of the eight deputies attended the Friend Day at Solid Rock Baptist Church the following Sunday.
- *MSU active shooter response (TAC team was first on ground):* John conducted a critical incident stress debrief the following week for team members who had to clear the student union and were exposed to deceased students.
- *Grandfather and five-year-old drowning through ice:* John responded to support deputies; Sue led the grieving family and officers in prayer on scene.
- *Firefighter's son's suicide:* John spent two hours with the grieving father, then was asked to conduct the funeral. Despite no pastoral background, he prepared a full order of service and delivered a clear gospel presentation to a mixed, unchurched crowd.
- *SWAT coordinated arrest of murder suspect (Bath Township hunter case):* With ~75 officers from local, state, and federal agencies assembled, John's SWAT commander asked him to pray before the operation.
- *Officer-involved shootings:* Three since John became involved in the county; he responds to scenes, escorts officers away for debriefing, and conducts structured critical incident stress debriefs.
- *Family of 16 hit by drunk driver:* Six-hour scene response; prayed with deputies, lieutenant, and firefighters. One deputy on scene required months of follow-up support.
- *SWAT call-out two weeks prior:* Spent three hours in the gear truck talking about the Lord with a non-operational team member.

**Romans 13:1-6 — The Divine Calling of Law Enforcement**
- Verse 1: All governmental authority is ordained by God.
- Verse 2: Resisting that authority is resisting God's ordinance and invites judgment.
- Verse 3: Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil.
- Verse 4: The ruler/officer is explicitly called "a minister of God" — one who "beareth not the sword in vain" but executes wrath on evildoers.
- Verse 6: "They are God's ministers attending continually upon this very thing." John emphasized the phrase *attending continually* — describing service without pause, regardless of weather, holidays, personal circumstances, or national crises.
- Less than 0.69% of the U.S. population serves as first responders, yet they attend continually to the worst moments of the other 99.3%.
- John's stated mission: *"The job of a cop chaplain is to connect the called to the one who did the calling."*

**Ministry Needs**
- Receives partial compensation (~15 hrs/week) from Bath Township for community relations work; Marine Deputy role shares $5,000 in state funding between two part-time deputies.
- Turned down a VP of Business Development offer and a full-time police department role with benefits to maintain ministry autonomy.
- Needs: financial/deputation support, and especially prayer.
- Urgently seeking a Bible-believing co-chaplain; cannot keep up with the growing number of agencies requesting his services.
- A neighboring county agency contacted him two weeks prior requesting chaplaincy coverage.
- Prayer cards available at the visitor's desk at Midland Baptist.
