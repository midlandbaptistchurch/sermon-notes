## Summary
Pastor Simpson explores Ephesians 5:18, emphasizing that being filled with the Spirit is not a one-time experience at salvation but an ongoing choice we make daily. By comparing drunkenness with Spirit-filling, he reveals three key similarities: choice, control, and change. The sermon clarifies that Spirit-filling is about yielding ourselves to God and living righteously, not a supernatural phenomenon that happens to us.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:18 — "Be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit."

## Key Themes
- The difference between the indwelling of the Spirit (at salvation) and the filling of the Spirit (sanctification)
- Spirit-filling as a daily choice, not a prayer request
- Three similarities between drunkenness and Spirit-filling: choice, control, and change
- Biblical authority and taking Scripture at face value
- Holiness and yielding our members to righteousness

## Notes

### The Indwelling vs. the Filling
Pastor Simpson establishes a crucial distinction: when you get saved, you are indwelt by the Spirit of God and sealed. However, being filled with the Spirit is something that takes place after salvation as part of our sanctification and walk with God. These are not the same thing.

### Wine in Scripture
The word "wine" appears over 200 times in the Bible. While the Bible speaks positively of wine in some contexts, the pastor emphasizes that we should let Scripture interpret Scripture and not add our own interpretations. He personally sees no benefit in drinking alcohol under grace.

### The Pentecostal Experience vs. Spirit-Filling
The sermon clarifies that Ephesians 5:18 is not referring to the Pentecostal experience of Acts 2, where God gave understanding of languages. Spirit-filling under grace is something different and is available to every believer.

### Three Key Similarities
1. **Choice** — Both drunkenness and Spirit-filling involve a deliberate choice
2. **Control** — In both cases, you relinquish control of your thoughts, words, and actions to an outside force
3. **Change** — Both result in a visible change in behavior and demeanor

### The Role of Decision and Yielding
Being filled with the Spirit is not something that happens to some people and not others. It is a decision every believer is supposed to make. Paul's prayers focus on people gaining wisdom and understanding because they need to know what they're doing. Spirit-filling is based on the decisions we make to yield our members as instruments of righteousness unto God (Romans 6).

### Drunkenness as a Picture (Proverbs)
Proverbs 23 provides vivid descriptions of drunkenness that serve as a parallel to understand Spirit-filling. The drunk person has lost control, experiences confusion, and is unable to function properly. In contrast, the Spirit-filled person yields control to God and functions according to His direction.

### A Warning About Misunderstandings
The pastor cautions against confusion that arises when we don't understand biblical distinctions. For example, people sometimes conflate the Rapture and the Second Coming because they have similarities, leading to confusion about end-times doctrine. Similarly, not understanding the difference between indwelling and filling can lead to spiritual confusion.

### Holiness and Spiritual Degeneration
The pastor reflects on his background in a Pentecostal holiness church and notes how the emphasis has shifted over time. What began as a focus on holy living and obedience has devolved into a focus on miraculous signs and emotional experiences. True Spirit-filling requires yielding to the things of God and living a holy life.
