## Summary
In this third installment of the Survey of Colossians, Pastor Jim Payne continues a New Testament survey by examining the nature and purpose of Colossians within Paul's epistolary sequence. He focuses on the supremacy and all-sufficiency of Jesus Christ as the head of the body of Christ, warning that the Colossian believers were being beguiled into adding to what Christ had already fully accomplished. He also draws out dispensational distinctions between the hope of Israel and the hope of the body of Christ, and between the Gospel of the Kingdom and Paul's Gospel.

## Scripture
Colossians 1–2 — The primary text, with extensive cross-references including Ephesians 2:8-10, Romans 6:4, Romans 8:20-24, 2 Corinthians 5, Hebrews 6:4-6, Matthew 24:14, Jude 14, Deuteronomy 33:2, Philemon 1, and Colossians 4:12-17.

## Key Themes
- The supremacy and all-sufficiency of Jesus Christ as head of the body
- Completeness in Christ — nothing need be added
- The structure of Paul's epistles: doctrine, reproof, correction
- Dispensational distinctions: the Gospel of the Kingdom vs. Paul's Gospel; Israel's hope vs. the body of Christ's heavenly hope
- Fruit-bearing in this dispensation (grace) vs. the kingdom program
- The Godhead and the fullness dwelling bodily in Christ
- The danger of being spoiled or beguiled away from Christ's headship

## Notes

### Paul's Epistolary Structure
- Pastor Payne continued his New Testament survey, situating Colossians within Paul's ordered sequence of letters.
- The pattern identified: **Romans** (doctrine — justification), **1 & 2 Corinthians** (reproof — ungodly behavior), **Galatians** (correction — mixing law and grace), **Ephesians** (doctrine — the church, the body of Christ), **Philippians** (correction — failure to maintain unity in the body), **Colossians** (correction — failure to hold Christ as supreme head).

### Background: The Church at Colossae
- Unlike other Pauline churches, Colossae was not founded by Paul directly.
- During Paul's three-year ministry at Ephesus (third missionary journey), a man named **Epaphras** was led to Christ by Paul, instructed in the truth, and returned to his hometown of Colossae to plant a church — in the house of **Philemon**.
- Epaphras later traveled to Rome where Paul was imprisoned and was himself imprisoned alongside Paul (Philemon 23).
- Epaphras reported on both the encouraging fruit and the concerning threats facing the Colossian church, prompting Paul to write.
- **Archippus**, son of Philemon, became the pastor of the church at Colossae after Epaphras departed (Colossians 4:17; Philemon 1-2).
- Colossae was situated approximately 100 miles east of Ephesus, in a tri-city area with Hierapolis and Laodicea.

### Opening Salutation and Thanksgiving (Colossians 1:1-8)
- Paul addresses "the saints and faithful brethren in Christ which are at Colossae" — noting two aspects: being **in Christ** (salvation) and being **at a place with believers** (sanctification and service).
- He gives thanks for their **faith in Christ Jesus**, **love toward all the saints**, and **hope laid up in heaven** — mirroring the faith-hope-love triad of 1 Corinthians 13:13 and 1 Thessalonians 1.
- He commends that fruit was manifested in the Colossian believers **from the very day they heard the Gospel**.

### Fruit-Bearing: Grace vs. Kingdom Program
- In this dispensation (grace), fruit-bearing is the intended outcome of salvation but not the condition of it: Ephesians 2:8-10 — saved by grace through faith **unto** good works; Romans 6:4 — we should walk in newness of life.
- In the kingdom program, fruit was **required** under threat of judgment: Matthew 3 (John the Baptist — "bring forth fruit meet for repentance" or be cut down and burned); John 15:6 (cast forth as a branch and burned); Hebrews 6:4-8 (those who fall away face cursing and burning).
- Pastor Payne emphasized the necessity of rightly dividing these programs to avoid misapplication.

### Dispensational Gospel Distinctions
- Colossians 1:6 states Paul's Gospel has gone out "in all the world."
- Matthew 24:14 says "this Gospel of the Kingdom shall be preached in all the world and then shall the end come" — but the end did not come when Paul wrote.
- Resolution: these are **two different Gospels** for two different programs. The Gospel of the Kingdom (Matthew 24:14) belongs to Israel's prophetic program, to be preached by the 144,000, the two witnesses, and the nation of Israel during the Tribulation. Paul's Gospel of grace belongs to the mystery program of the body of Christ.
- Similarly, Mark 16:15 and Matthew 28:19-20 (with baptism) belong to Israel's commission, not the body of Christ's. Paul states he was sent "not to baptize but to preach the Gospel" (1 Corinthians 1).

### The Believer's Heavenly Hope
- Colossians 1:5 — "the hope which is laid up for you in heaven."
- The body of Christ's hope is a **heavenly** hope: a new, glorified body (2 Corinthians 5; Romans 8:20-24 — "the redemption of the body").
- Israel's hope is earthly: resurrection into the millennial kingdom on earth.
- The saints returning with Christ at the Second Coming (Jude 14 — "ten thousands of his saints") are **angels**, not the body of Christ. Evidence: Deuteronomy 33:2 uses identical language of the Lord coming with "ten thousands of saints" at Sinai (when angels accompanied the giving of the Law); Daniel records angels calling one another "saints."

### The Holy Spirit in Colossians vs. Ephesians
- In Ephesians (dealing with the body of Christ), the Holy Spirit is mentioned throughout all six chapters — because entry into the body is by the baptism of the Spirit.
- In Colossians, the Spirit is mentioned **only once** (Colossians 1:8). The focus of Colossians is the **headship of Christ**, not the indwelling Spirit.
- The name of Christ appears **26 times in 95 verses** in Colossians.

### Christ's Supremacy and the Believers' Completeness (Colossians 2)
- The key danger at Colossae: believers were being pulled away from Christ's all-sufficiency by false teachers, adding human philosophies, asceticism, angel worship, and dietary/calendar observances to Christ.
- Key warning words in Colossians 2: **"spoil"** (beware lest any man spoil/plunder you — v.8), **"beguile"** (v.4, 18), **"judge"** (v.16).
- Colossians 2:9 — "In him dwelleth all the fullness of the Godhead bodily." The fullness leaves no room for additions.
- Colossians 2:10 — "Ye are complete in him."
- Christ is not only head of the church/body but **head of all principality and power** (Colossians 2:10; cf. Ephesians 1 — ascended far above all principalities, powers, might, and dominion).
- Colossians 2:19 — the problem at Colossae was "not holding the head" — failing to embrace Christ in his supremacy and sufficiency.
- Pastor Payne noted four specific errors addressed in Colossians 2 that he will cover in a future session.

### The Godhead
- The term "Godhead" appears three times in Scripture (Acts 17:29; Romans 1:20; Colossians 2:9).
- The Godhead consists of three subsistent, distinct individuals: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
- Creation itself testifies to the Godhead: a three-dimensional world (breadth, length, height); radiation with alpha, beta, and gamma rays.
- Christ received the Spirit without measure at his baptism (John 3:34), possessing the full fullness of the Godhead bodily.
