## Summary
Bill Baughman leads a Sunday School lesson exploring Romans 5–8, focusing on the distinction between individual sins and the sin nature inherited from Adam, and the believer's deliverance from the law through Christ. The lesson emphasizes the indwelling Holy Spirit as the believer's constant guide and empowerment for sanctification, and encourages listeners to release guilt, repent, and walk daily in the Spirit.

## Scripture
Romans 5–8 — Paul's extended treatment of sanctification: the struggle between flesh and Spirit in the believer's life, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, and the assurance of no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

## Key Themes
- The distinction between sins (acts) and the sin nature (root)
- Deliverance from the law through Christ
- The indwelling Holy Spirit and Spirit-led decision making
- The ongoing internal struggle between flesh and Spirit
- Grace that justifies, sustains, and sanctifies
- Freedom from guilt through repentance and walking in the Spirit

## Notes

### Structure of Romans
Baughman offered an overview of Romans as a whole before focusing on chapters 5–8:
- **Chapters 1–2:** The need for righteousness
- **Chapters 3–5:11:** Justification — righteousness imputed
- **Chapters 5:12–8:** Sanctification — righteousness imparted
- **Chapter 8 onward:** Glorification — the hope of the believer
- **Chapters 9–11:** God's purpose
- **Chapters 12–16:** Practical application

### Sins vs. the Sin Nature
A key distinction is drawn between *sins* (the fruit — specific acts, cf. Galatians 5:19–21) and *the sin nature* (the root — inherited from Adam, Romans 5:12). Romans 1–5:11 largely addresses sins; Romans 5:12–8:39 addresses the sin nature itself.

### Adam and Christ (Romans 5:13–17)
Death entered through Adam and passed to all. But the free gift of grace through Christ abounds to many — justification countering condemnation. The believer stands between these two representative heads.

### Deliverance from the Law (Romans 7:6)
The theme verse for the lesson: *"But now we are delivered from the law, that being dead wherein we were held, that we should serve in newness of spirit, and not in the oldness of the letter."* Believers are no longer under the law as a means of justification or condemnation. Notably, Paul teaches that the law can stir up sinful desire in the flesh (Romans 7:8–9).

### The Indwelling Holy Spirit (Romans 8:11; 1 Corinthians 3:16; 6:19–20; Galatians 2:20; Ephesians 3:17–19)
Baughman emphasized that the same Holy Spirit who raised Christ from the dead dwells in every believer. The Spirit resides in the believer's inner person — mind, will, affections, and conscience. Key point: the Holy Spirit is the closest presence to the believer at all times, fully aware of every thought, decision, and action.

### Paul's Internal Struggle (Romans 7:14–24)
Paul's candid confession — doing what he hates and failing to do what he wills — illustrates the real, ongoing conflict every believer faces between flesh and Spirit. Baughman cited Paul's zeal, humility, and perseverance under suffering as evidence that even the greatest Christians wrestle with the flesh.

### No Condemnation — Lose the Guilt (Romans 8:1–2)
*"There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit."* Baughman exhorted the congregation to release guilt over fleshly failures, repent, and move forward rather than dwelling in shame.

### Grace Abounding Over Sin (Romans 5:20–21; 6:14; Titus 2:11–12)
Grace not only saves but teaches and enables godly living. The same grace that justifies also sanctifies. Believers are not under law but under grace — a grace that instructs us to deny ungodliness and live soberly in this present world.

### Walking in the Spirit (Galatians 5:16–18, 24–25; Philippians 2:12–13; Romans 8:26)
The practical call of the lesson: bring every decision — large and small — into conversation with the indwelling Spirit. The flesh cannot be satisfied; only Spirit-led choices lead to life. God works in believers both to will and to do His good pleasure (Philippians 2:13), and the Spirit intercedes for us even when we do not know how to pray.

### Summary Statement (from the lesson)
*"The struggle is real, but God does not leave us alone in it. His grace through the finished work of Christ and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit both justifies us with no condemnation and empowers us to walk in the newness of life. The same grace that saves us is the grace that sustains and sanctifies us."
