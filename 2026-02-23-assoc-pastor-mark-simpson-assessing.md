## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson teaches on the necessity of assessing proper value to oneself under grace. Unlike law-based motivation driven by fear of judgment, grace requires believers to understand their significance, relevance, and importance to God's work. He emphasizes that each believer must see their personal value not through worldly self-esteem but through their identity as the body of Christ, the temple of God, and the building of God. Using illustrations from marriage and physical fitness, he demonstrates how understanding our importance to God motivates faithful living.

## Scripture
**Galatians 6:1** — Instructs believers to restore those overtaken in fault with meekness while considering themselves lest they also be tempted.

**Ephesians 4:16** — Describes the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplies, with every part contributing to the body's growth and edification in love.

## Key Themes
- **Grace Motivation vs. Law Motivation**: Grace doesn't force compliance through fear of judgment; believers must be self-motivated by understanding their value to God.
- **Three Critical Assessments**: Believers must see their significance (having meaning), relevance (being connected to God's work), and importance (mattering to God personally).
- **Personal Responsibility**: Each believer has equal opportunity under grace to make their life count regardless of ministry position or visibility.
- **Stewardship of Self**: Taking care of oneself spiritually, physically, and emotionally matters because we belong to God and are important to Him.

## Notes

### The Challenge of Grace Motivation
- Grace doesn't make you do anything—there's very little judgment until the end
- Under law, there was motivation through fear of consequences and judgment
- A person can get saved and live however they want with few immediate repercussions
- Must have "grace motivation"—understanding grace is one of the greatest motivators
- In order to be motivated by grace, you have to assess value to certain things

### The Need to Assess Personal Value
- We are described as the body of Christ, the temple of God, and the building of God
- Easy to see value in what Christ paid for our salvation
- Harder to see our individual value while living on earth after salvation
- Tendency to compare ourselves to others with "greater" ministries (Paul, local soul winners, pastors)
- Question arises: "Does it really matter if I pray, study my Bible, am faithful to church?"
- If you can't assess your own value, you won't invest much in spiritual things
- What you prioritize reveals what you value—what has your heart, mind, possessions, thoughts

### Three Words for Assessing Value

**1. Significance**
- Definition: having meaning
- You are significant to the work of God, to the age of grace, to what God is trying to do
- God is trying to have His body established
- Ephesians 4:16 describes every joint and every part working together
- No body part is dispensable—all are needed
- If you make the Word of God your passion, how significant is that to God?
- When you study the Bible, you think things most people never think about
- You can tell God things you noticed that others don't see—this matters to Him
- Example: Recognizing God as Creator and stating belief in creation vs. evolution
- Your faithfulness and choices to live holy are significant to God personally
- Choosing to deny flesh and come to church when you don't feel like it matters
- Being a blessing to others without seeking credit is significant
- You don't know when something you do will be the only one who did it

**2. Relevance**
- Definition: to have bearing upon or to be connected with the matter at hand; to carry weight
- How connected do you see yourself to the work of the Lord and what He's doing?
- Must feel connected enough to stay in the Word to see what God wants and how He wants it done
- When you understand your relevance to the body of Christ, the time period you live in, the God who died for you, and the heaven to come, you feel connected
- Example: Feeling ownership and responsibility for the church building and ministries
- "It's mine because it's His and I'm His, so thus this is mine"
- If you don't see your connection and importance, you won't see the importance of doing your part
- Under grace, if you don't assess value, you won't be what you're supposed to be

**3. Importance**
- Are you important to what God is doing?
- Question each individual must ask themselves personally
- Example given of teaching two and three-year-olds—a difficult but important ministry
- Easier to feel significance when doing visible ministry
- Harder when it's just you and God in personal Bible study and prayer
- When you feel that connection, you won't just go through the motions
- Not operating under law but under grace—supposed to bear fruit and get something from Bible reading
- Pastor's personal discipline: stops and restarts when mind wanders during reading; writes something down even in difficult Old Testament passages
- Comparison to marriage: giving wife attention because she's significant, relevant, and important
- If relationship with wife is greater than relationship with God, indicates a problem
- God could have taken us immediately to heaven but left us here in sin-cursed bodies
- We get to choose to deny flesh and prove to God how important He is to us
- Our choices mean more because we have to choose against our flesh

### Understanding God's Pride in His People
- Job example: "Have you noted my servant Job?"
- God was proud enough of Job to point him out
- Our sufferings and how we handle them are ultimately significant to the Lord
- We will be rewarded in heaven for sufferings

### Practical Self-Talk and Motivation
- Pastor's personal question: "What else you gonna do? What you gonna do that's gonna count?"
- Why allow sin to reign in your body when you'll answer for it?
- Do what you're supposed to do, what you've studied and know is right
- Gives the Lord credit and glory, gives you credit, and benefits everyone around you

### The Necessity of Self-Care (Consider Thyself)
- Galatians 6:1 says when restoring others, "consider thyself"
- Must make sure you have spirit of meekness, strength, that you're right
- Must take care of yourself to minister to others
- Example of grandfather not taking care of himself physically
- Pastor's 15th anniversary cruise story: started working out and maintained it for over 15 years
- Wife's response: physical change was good, but more important was living healthier for longer life and her security
- Understanding what matters to loved ones motivates continued faithfulness
- Same principle applies to walk with God—see the importance and continue

### Living Under Grace
- There's less than you think of people who think this way—most people don't
- Bible teaches humility and meekness, but also to see our value and importance
- If you don't see yourself as significant, relevant, and important, you'll just do things as you feel like it
- When you know something is dependent on you and affects someone you love, it means even more when that person is the Lord
- Seeing your value strengthens your relationship with God
- God left us here and created us as individuals—made you different from everyone else
- None of us are the same—that's how God looks at us
- What you do toward Him means something to Him

### Critical Mindset for Grace Living
- Easy to forget, get down on ourselves, talk ourselves out of significance
- Can make mistakes, mess up, not feel like doing right, wonder if it matters
- Can get wrong idea that God is disappointed again
- Need to be sober, clear-minded, logical
- Look to the Word of God
- God has saved and forgiven us—means we're always going to be with Him
- He won't leave or forsake us
- When we mess up, we deal with it, but God always wants us to come back and do right
- We're always going to be significant, important, and relevant to God
- Must strengthen this mindset to see our significance, importance, and relevance
- Really see our value and live according to it
