## Summary
Bill Baughman explores what Jesus said and did to demonstrate he is God, not merely a good teacher. He examines how various groups—past humanity, Jewish leadership, the disciples, and people today—have failed to recognize Jesus's divinity, and contrasts Christ's unique resurrection with other biblical resuscitations to show his eternal power and authority.

## Scripture
Luke 24:1-12 — The resurrection account of Jesus, where women discover the empty tomb and angels proclaim his resurrection.

## Key Themes
- Jesus's claims to divinity through his words and actions
- The "organically outrageous" nature of Jesus's teaching and miracles
- Different responses to Jesus across history: past humanity, Jewish leaders, disciples, and modern people
- The uniqueness of Christ's resurrection compared to other biblical resuscitations
- Practical discipleship: being authentically and visibly Christian in daily life

## Notes

### Whom Do Men Say That I Am?
Jesus asked his disciples what people claimed about his identity. The responses have been consistent across time:
- **Past humanity**: Some said John the Baptist, Elijah, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets
- **Jewish leadership**: The Pharisees believed he was the son of David, but failed to understand his divine nature when confronted with Psalm 110:1
- **The disciples**: Peter correctly identified him as "the Christ, the Son of the living God," but later rebuked Jesus for speaking of his suffering, prompting Jesus to call him Satan
- **Modern people**: Surveys show approximately 52% of U.S. adults believe Jesus was a good moral teacher but not God; even among evangelicals, about 30% hold this view

### The Outrageous Words of Jesus
Jesus's teachings deny the possibility that he was merely a good or wise teacher:
- He commanded cutting off body parts that cause sin (Matthew 5:30)
- He claimed authority to forgive sins
- He identified himself as the Son of Man—a title meaning God
- These statements are shocking, extreme, and wildly unconventional

### The Outrageous Actions of Jesus
Jesus's deeds demonstrate divine power and authority:
- **Cleansing the temple**: He made a scourge, drove out merchants, and overturned tables, declaring his Father's house should not be a marketplace
- **Predicting resurrection**: He said, "Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up," referring to his body
- **The withering fig tree**: He caused a barren fig tree to wither completely, demonstrating divine judgment
- **Commanding demons**: He granted demons permission to enter a herd of swine, which then ran into the sea and perished
- **Healing the blind**: He healed blindness through miraculous power, using methods like spittle and clay

### Christ's Unique Resurrection
There were ten instances of resurrection or resuscitation in Scripture, but Jesus's resurrection stands apart:

**Other resuscitations** (temporary):
- Widow of Zarephath's son (raised by Elijah)
- Shunnamite woman's son (raised by Elisha)
- Unnamed man (revived by touching Elisha's bones)
- Widow of Nain's son (raised by Jesus)
- Jairus's daughter (raised by Jesus)
- Lazarus (raised by Jesus)
- Many saints in Jerusalem (appeared after Jesus's resurrection)
- Tabitha/Dorcas (raised by Peter)
- Eutychus (raised by Paul)

All these people died again later and were resuscitated to temporary physical life.

**Jesus's resurrection** (permanent):
- **Nature**: Permanent transformation to eternal life
- **Outcome**: Never dies; lives forever
- **Body type**: Glorified, immortal, spiritual body (per 1 Corinthians 15:42-45)
- **Power source**: Self-resurrection through divine authority; the stone was already rolled away when women arrived
- **Abilities after rising**: Could eat, be touched, appear and disappear, pass through solid objects
- **Purpose**: Defeated death and sin permanently as the first fruit for believers

### Right Division and Understanding
The sermon emphasizes the importance of rightly dividing Scripture (2 Timothy 2:15) and recognizing Jesus's claims about himself. The world's characterization of Jesus as a "good teacher" contradicts everything he said and did. To deny his divinity is to ignore his direct statements about himself.

### Application: Organically Outrageous Living
Bill challenges believers to live with "organically outrageous" authenticity:
- **Outrageous** = shocking, extreme, unconventional, arising genuinely from a situation or person
- **Organic** = honest, authentic, not manufactured or scripted

Practical examples:
- Visibly identifying as a believer through appearance and behavior (wearing a suit and carrying a Bible to show you're going to church)
- Praying publicly with conviction and a projecting voice
- Having a visible presence that shows you believe in Christ's resurrection and active work today
- Being honest and authentic without being weird or stupid
- Showing through deportment and conduct that you are a believer

The goal is not to perform miracles like Jesus did, but to live in a way that makes your faith unmistakably visible and genuine to those around you.
