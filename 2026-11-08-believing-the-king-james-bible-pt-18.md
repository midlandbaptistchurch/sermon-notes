## Summary
This lesson continues examining the producers of the Greek New Testament that formed the foundation for the King James Bible. The study focuses on Theodore Beza and the Elzevir family, showing how God used individual scholars to refine and preserve His Word through successive editions of the Textus Receptus. The lesson emphasizes that while Erasmus did the preponderance of the work, subsequent scholars like Stephanus, Beza, and Elzevir made minor refinements that purified the text, culminating in the received text used by the King James translators.

## Scripture
Psalm 12:6-7 — "The words of the LORD are pure words: as silver tried in a furnace of earth, purified seven times. Thou shalt keep them, O LORD, thou shalt preserve them from this generation for ever."

## Key Themes
- **God's Preservation of His Word**: God promised to preserve His pure words through successive refinements and purifications in both Greek and English.
- **Individual Scholars as God's Instruments**: God used individual men, often coming from Catholic backgrounds, who had to counter their upbringing to produce reliable Greek texts.
- **The Textus Receptus Foundation**: The work of Erasmus, Stephanus, Beza, and Elzevir provided the solid Greek foundation that the King James translators used.
- **The Cost of Bible Preservation**: These scholars lived under threat of persecution and often had to flee their homelands to continue their work.

## Notes

### Theodore Beza (Teddy B)
- French Protestant theologian and scholar who played an important role in the Reformation
- Spent most of his life in Geneva, Switzerland
- Known as John Calvin's disciple and successor
- Produced 10 editions of the Greek New Testament (9 during lifetime, 1 posthumously)
- Most important edition: Novum Testamentum 4th Folio edition published in Geneva in 1598
- Based his text on Stephanus's 1551 edition
- Made less than 100 minor changes to Stephanus's text with little or no effect on subsequent English translations
- His changes were considered generally improvements upon the text
- Best recognized for his annotations and marginal notes rather than textual changes
- His Calvinistic doctrine influenced his marginal notes and textual annotations
- The New Herzog Encyclopedia of Religious Knowledge noted: "it is to be regretted that the author's view of the doctrine of predestination exercised upon the interpretation of Scripture to preponderating an influence"
- However, he did not alter the text itself—only his comments reflected his doctrinal viewpoint
- His 1598 text was most often used and followed by the translators of the King James Version

### The Elzevir Family
- Dutch family of booksellers, printers, and publishers in the 17th and 18th centuries
- Known as the House of Elzevir
- The father founded the House of Elzevir and had five to seven sons
- Bonaventura Elzevir (1583-1652) was the most famous
- Joined in partnership with his nephew Abraham (son of his brother)
- The partnership of Bonaventura and Abraham produced the most notable work
- Bonaventura was the leading proponent and senior partner
- Published three editions of the Greek New Testament: 1624, 1633, and 1641
- All three editions published after the King James Bible (1611), so not used by KJV translators
- The Elzevir text was practically a reprint of Beza's 1565 text with about 50 minor differences

### The Term "Textus Receptus"
- First appeared in the preface of Elzevir's second edition (1633)
- The Latin phrase in the preface stated: "Textum ab omnibus receptum" (the text now received by all)
- Full statement: "You have therefore the text now received by all, in which we give nothing changed or corrupt"
- This statement established the term "Textus Receptus" (received text) that has stuck in history
- While partly marketing language, in God's providence it was a true statement
- The King James translators had called it the "received text" in English before Elzevir coined the Latin term

### The Textus Receptus Reign
- Was truly the text received by all at that time
- Reigned as the supreme, authoritative original language text for the New Testament
- Continued unbroken for 200 years
- In England, Stephanus's form was generally preferred and used by King James translators
- On the European continent, Elzevir's second edition was embraced
- King James translators used editions of Erasmus, Stephanus, and Beza to cross-check and verify

### Modern Versions vs. Textus Receptus
- Modern Bible versions are translated from completely different sources
- Use critical text edited by Nestle-Aland (now in 28th edition)
- Use United Bible Societies Greek text (6th edition published recently in 21st century)
- Based on corrupt manuscripts like Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus
- Based on recent Egyptian papyri discoveries
- Not coincidental that Egypt is the source of corrupt manuscripts underlying modern perversions

### The Six Bibles Leading to King James
- All translated from the Textus Receptus
- All from the correct Greek textual tradition
- All consistent with each other and with the King James Bible
- Based on the same sound manuscripts and reliable Word of God
- Modern versions are dramatically different from all seven of these Bibles
- Accepting modern versions means rejecting not only King James but all six Bibles before it
- The King James is the culmination of almost 100 years of scholarship and sacrifice
- The six earlier translations eventually went out of ordinary print
- The King James became the standard English Bible used by virtually all Christians until modern corruptions
- The six Bibles before King James were good Bibles—the best English speakers had at the time
- They were like silver being purified
- King James Bible represents the seventh purification

### Personal Application Points
- The teacher is not a scholar but a student studying the works of scholars
- These were real people who lived under threat of persecution
- Many had to flee their homeland to maintain their life and continue ministry
- Most came from Roman Catholic backgrounds and had to counter their upbringing
- Their personal sacrifice makes their contribution even more significant
- We have the Bible in a relatively safe, free, non-threatening environment
- We should never take for granted what we've been blessed with
- Those raised in a dispensational King James background should be especially grateful
- The cost in blood, sweat, and tears to produce our Bible should not be forgotten

### The Process of Purification
- Individual efforts by Erasmus, Stephanus, Beza built upon each other
- Each scholar refined and purified the previous work
- Like quality control and quality assurance—each adding improvements
- God used individual efforts that became a team result
- The King James translation itself was a committee effort with multiple eyes
- Multiple reviews help catch errors that individual work might miss
- Layer upon layer, line upon line, precept upon precept refinement
