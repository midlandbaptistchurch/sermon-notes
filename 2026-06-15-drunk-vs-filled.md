## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson explores the contrast between being drunk with wine and being filled with the Spirit from Ephesians 5:18. He emphasizes that being filled with the Spirit is an active choice requiring believers to yield control to God, study Scripture richly, and work proactively at spiritual growth rather than passively waiting.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:18 — Paul contrasts the excess of wine with the filling of the Spirit as a choice believers must make.

## Key Themes
- Being filled with the Spirit requires active choice and control
- Letting Scripture dwell richly within us
- Yielding ourselves to God's control
- The importance of biblical authority over personal preference

## Notes

### Biblical Authority and Perspective
- Scripture must remain our authority even when we cannot fully understand it
- We must not make the Bible say what we want it to say
- Different study methods (detailed vs. overview) provide valuable perspectives
- Rightly dividing Scripture requires proper perspective

### Comparison: Drunkenness vs. Spirit-Filling
Three main comparisons between being drunk and being filled with the Spirit:
1. **Control and Choice**: Both involve relinquishing control, but being filled with the Spirit is a conscious choice to yield to God
2. **Change**: Both produce change, but Spirit-filling produces positive transformation in thinking, heart, and desires
3. **Effects**: 
   - Spirit-filled: in control, produces joy, spiritually sober, tames fleshly desires, brings knowledge and wisdom
   - Drunkenness: out of control, produces sorrow, impairs thinking, inflames fleshly desires, causes loss of faculties

### Yielding Control (Romans 6:13, 16, 19)
- You can only yield to God what you have control over
- Many people say they'll give their life to the Lord but lack control over basic areas (spending, anger, time management)
- Must first gain control before you can yield that control to God
- The goal is to graduate from Romans 6 (doing right) to Romans 8 (walking in the Spirit)

### Understanding "Filled"
**Negative examples of being "filled":**
- Filled with sorrow (John 16:6)
- Filled with wrath (Luke 4:28)
- Filled with envy (Acts 13:45)

**Positive examples of being filled:**
- Filled with fruits of righteousness (Philippians 1:11)
- Filled with all the fullness of God (Ephesians 3:19)
- Filled with knowledge of His will (Colossians 1:9)

### Practical Steps to Being Filled
- **Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly** (Colossians 3:16)
- "Richly" means saturation - enough Scripture in mind and heart that it becomes wisdom
- Must actively work at being filled - it won't happen passively
- Need to sometimes empty ourselves to make space
- Must take control of areas of life so we can yield them to God

### Key Principle
Being filled with the Spirit is not about waiting for God to "fall upon us" (as in Acts), but about making daily choices to acknowledge the Holy Spirit's presence and yield our controlled areas to His guidance.
