## Summary
Hugh Taylor preaches on God's ongoing search — first seen in the Garden of Eden and throughout Scripture — for men and women willing to stand in the gap. Drawing from Ezekiel 22, Jeremiah 5, Philippians 2, and 2 Timothy, he outlines five qualities God seeks in a person: seeking and living by truth, faithfulness, preparedness to preach the gospel, caring for fellow Christians, and standing with other servants of God.

## Scripture
Ezekiel 22:30 — God declares He sought for a man to stand in the gap and make up the hedge for the land, but found none; the sermon uses this as a launching point to explore what kind of person God is looking for across multiple passages of Scripture.

## Key Themes
- Seeking truth and living by it
- Faithfulness in everyday commitments
- Preparedness and willingness to share the gospel
- Caring for fellow believers
- Standing with and supporting other servants of God

## Notes
**1. Seeks the Truth and Lives by It**
- Jeremiah 5:1 — God searches for even one person who executes judgment and seeks truth.
- John 8:31-32 — Continuing in God's Word brings knowledge of the truth and genuine freedom.
- 2 Timothy 2:15 — Believers are called to study to show themselves approved unto God, rightly dividing the Word of truth; failing to do so risks shame at the Judgment Seat of Christ.
- 1 Timothy 2:4 — God desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth.

**2. Faithful**
- Proverbs 20:6 — Most men proclaim their own goodness, but a truly faithful man is rare.
- 2 Timothy 2:1-2 — Paul charges Timothy to commit what he has learned to faithful men who can teach others.
- Faithfulness is defined as simply and continually doing what we are supposed to do; it is accessible to everyone regardless of talent or ability.
- Key exhortation: "Don't let what you can't do for God keep you from doing what you can do for God."

**3. Prepared and Positioned to Preach**
- Acts 8 — Philip was prepared and obedient; God repositioned him to meet the Ethiopian eunuch's need for someone to explain Isaiah and the gospel.
- Ephesians 4:25 — Believers are to speak truth to their neighbors.
- Romans 10 — Salvation comes through calling on the Lord, but people cannot hear without a preacher; "how beautiful are the feet" of those who bring glad tidings.
- Application question: Am I willing to go but preparing to stay, or willing to stay but preparing to go?

**4. Cares for Other Christians**
- Philippians 2:19-21 — Paul could find no one like-minded as Timothy who would genuinely care for the state of the Philippian believers; most sought their own interests rather than the things of Christ.
- 2 Timothy 3:10 — Paul ministered both doctrinally and socially, on purpose.
- Galatians 6 — Believers are to bear one another's burdens.
- People may not recognize sound doctrine, but nearly everyone recognizes friendliness and genuine care. "People don't care how much you know until they know how much you care."

**5. Stands with Other Men and Women of God**
- 2 Timothy 4:9-16 — Paul writes from prison that at his first defense no one stood with him; Demas had forsaken him, loving the present world.
- The call is not only hypothetical (e.g., standing with a pastor if persecuted), but practical and immediate: attending services faithfully, participating in the Lord's Supper, showing up on Wednesday nights, and supporting the ministry now.
- Personal illustration: While helping plant Grace Baptist Church in Shalimar, FL in the late 1980s, a new convert named Mr. Bill began driving his car across the street to park at the church — not because he needed to, but so passing drivers would see more cars and be curious enough to visit.

**Conclusion**
Every believer should be able to check off all five qualities. God is not looking for a crowd but for individual men and women willing to stand in the gap. The sermon closes with a call to self-examination and an invitation for anyone unsure of their salvation to speak with the speaker.
