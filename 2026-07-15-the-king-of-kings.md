## Summary
Tim Bish traces the title "King of Kings" across roughly 3,000 years of human history — from the first Assyrian rulers through Nebuchadnezzar, the Medo-Persian Empire, Alexander the Great, and into the modern era — showing how history consistently confirms rather than contradicts the Bible. He then connects this ancient title to Jesus Christ, arguing through Matthew 1's genealogy that Jesus was born a crown prince of Israel and that, according to 1 Timothy 6:15 and Revelation 19:16, he will one day claim the title King of Kings in its fullest and final sense. The sermon calls listeners to trust that God has had his plan worked out far longer than any human kingdom has existed.

## Scripture
- **Daniel 2:36–45** — Daniel interprets Nebuchadnezzar's statue dream, identifying the Babylonian king as the first biblical holder of the "King of Kings" title and outlining the succession of world empires that follows.
- **Matthew 1:1–17** — The genealogy of Jesus Christ, establishing his royal lineage through David and his status as crown prince of Israel.
- **Isaiah 9:6** — "For unto us a child is born… the Prince of Peace" — referenced to show Jesus currently holds the title of Prince, not yet King of Kings.
- **1 Timothy 6:15** — "…the blessed and only Potentate, the King of kings and Lord of lords."
- **Revelation 17:14; 19:16** — The Lamb overcomes as Lord of lords and King of kings; his vesture bears the name written at his return.
- **Mark 6:3** — Jesus' brothers named, supporting the discussion of Joseph's royal lineage and its disappearance from history.

## Key Themes
- The title "King of Kings" is one of the oldest in recorded human history and has been held by rulers across dozens of civilizations over three millennia
- History and archaeology repeatedly confirm rather than disprove the Bible (e.g., the Hittites, Daniel's name in Babylonian records, Nebuchadnezzar's title)
- God revealed future world empires to Nebuchadnezzar centuries before they arose, including Medo-Persia, Greece (Alexander the Great), and Rome
- Jesus was born a crown prince through the royal line of David; Joseph was the crown prince of Israel
- Jesus is currently the Prince of Peace; he has not yet assumed the title King of Kings — that occurs at his return
- The "stone cut without hands" in Daniel 2 represents Christ's future kingdom that will shatter and replace all earthly kingdoms
- God's sovereignty and foreknowledge extend over all of human history

## Notes

### The Antiquity of the Title
- "King of Kings" is documented as far back as Tukulti-Ninurta I of Assyria (approx. 1233–1197 BC), roughly 3,200 years ago.
- The title passed through Assyrian kings and was not common in Babylon until God gave it specifically to Nebuchadnezzar (Daniel 2:37).
- After Nebuchadnezzar, every Babylonian king and then the Medo-Persian rulers (Cyrus, Darius) assumed the title.
- Alexander the Great (the "brass" kingdom of Daniel 2) also held it; his empire spanned three continents.
- The last living ruler to hold the title was Mohammad Reza Pahlavi of Iran, deposed in 1979.
- In Ethiopia, the Solomonic dynasty held a version of the title, claiming descent from Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.

### History Validates Scripture
- Critics used science and history to disprove the Bible; repeatedly they have been proven wrong (e.g., the Hittites, confirmed archaeologically in 1927, ruled a kingdom roughly half the size of Texas).
- Daniel's name appears over 800 times in Babylonian historical records — making him one of the most historically documented biblical figures.
- The King James Bible is noted as one of the few translations that records the origin of the Babylonian "King of Kings" title accurately in Daniel 2.

### Nebuchadnezzar's Dream (Daniel 2)
- The statue: head of gold (Babylon), breast and arms of silver (Medo-Persia), belly and thighs of brass (Greece/Alexander), legs of iron and feet of iron and clay (Rome and its divisions).
- God called these empires hundreds of years before they existed.
- Nebuchadnezzar's pride led directly to building the golden image in Daniel 3 — a very human response to being called "the greatest."

### Jesus as Crown Prince
- Matthew 1:1–17 establishes an unbroken royal genealogy from Abraham through David to Joseph, Jesus' legal (step)father.
- Joseph was the crown prince of Israel — the heir to David's throne.
- Jesus was therefore born a prince, in a stable, partly because of the social stigma of Mary's pregnancy before marriage and the Roman taxation census requiring travel to Bethlehem (fulfilling prophecy about his birthplace).
- The wise men coming to Jerusalem asking for the "king of the Jews" alarmed Herod precisely because this genealogical claim was real and documented.
- Significantly, the genealogies of Jesus' four brothers (James, Joseph, Judah, Simon — Mark 6:3) have essentially vanished from Jewish historical record, which the speaker suggests may be providential — preventing rival claimants to David's throne.

### Jesus Has Not Yet Taken the Title
- Isaiah 9:6 calls him "Prince of Peace" — he is currently the Prince, not yet the King of Kings.
- 1 Timothy 6:15 speaks of the title being revealed "in his times" — future tense.
- Revelation 17:14 and 19:16 show Christ receiving and bearing that title at his return.
- The "stone cut without hands" (Daniel 2:34–35) will crush every prior earthly kingdom so completely that gold becomes like fine dust carried away on the wind — representing the most dramatic physical transformation of the earth since Noah's flood.
- That title has been vacant since 1979; the sermon suggests its ultimate claimant is coming.
