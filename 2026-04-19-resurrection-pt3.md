## Summary
Bill Baughman continues the Sunday School Resurrection series by examining extra-biblical evidence for Jesus and the Resurrection, then contrasts Jesus's "organically outrageous" claims and teachings with the idea that he was merely a good teacher. He argues that Jesus's words and actions force us to conclude he is either a liar, a lunatic, or the Lord—there is no middle ground.

## Scripture
Mark 16:1-18 — The Resurrection account from the Gospel of Mark, including Jesus's appearances after rising.

Matthew 16:13 — Jesus asks his disciples whom men say that he is.

John 20:9 — Reference to the disciples not yet understanding the resurrection from Scripture.

## Key Themes
- Extra-biblical evidence for Jesus and the Resurrection
- The limits of the scientific method in evaluating historical events
- Legal and historical frameworks for establishing certainty about past events
- Jesus's claims that transcend the category of "good teacher"
- The cost of discipleship and following Jesus
- Division and division in families over faith in Jesus
- The "organically outrageous" nature of Jesus's words and deeds
- Living boldly as a Christian witness

## Notes

### Opening: The Resurrection is Central to Our Faith
- Every day is a resurrection day for believers
- The sermon reviews Mark 16 account of the Resurrection

### Scientific Method Cannot Address the Resurrection
- Science can only address natural, observable, repeatable processes
- The Resurrection lies outside these parameters, so science is silent on it
- This doesn't mean the Resurrection is false—it means science has no tools to investigate it
- We must use legal and historical evidence instead, similar to how we prove other unique historical events (e.g., a stolen car)

### Extra-Biblical Evidence for Jesus
Historians outside the Bible confirm key facts about Jesus:

1. **Tacitus** (Roman historian, ~116 AD) — A hostile source who reports Jesus was crucified under Tiberius
2. **Josephus** (Jewish historian, ~93-94 AD) — Records that Jesus was called "the Christ," was crucified, and "appeared to them alive again the third day"
3. **Babylonian Talmud** (2nd-4th centuries) — Confirms Jesus was executed on the eve of Passover
4. **Lucian Samosata** (125-175 AD) — A mocker who references followers of "their crucified sophist"

These sources are valuable because they come from opposition or hostile sources—people who had no reason to invent Christian claims.

### Matthew 16:13 — Who Do Men Say I Am?
- Jesus asks his disciples what people are saying about him
- The common answer then (and now): "a good and wise teacher"
- But Jesus's own sayings and actions deny this possibility

### Jesus's "Organically Outrageous" Claims and Teachings
Jesus made claims that arise naturally from genuine conviction but seem extreme or shocking:

- **Hate your family to follow me** (Luke 14:26) — He demands absolute allegiance
- **I came not to send peace, but a sword** (Matthew 10:34) — His message causes division, not harmony
- **Division in families over me** (Luke 12:51-53) — Following Jesus will divide households
- **Unless you eat my flesh and drink my blood, you have no life** (John 6:53-54) — A shocking statement that caused many disciples to leave
- **Let the dead bury their dead** (Luke 9:60) — Seeming coldness about a sacred family obligation
- **Cut off your hand if it causes you to stumble** (Matthew 5:29-30) — Hyperbolic but clear about the cost
- **I am** statements — Jesus speaks as God ("before Abraham was, I am")

### The Trilemma: Liar, Lunatic, or Lord
- If Jesus is not God, his claims make him either a deliberate deceiver (liar) or mentally unstable (lunatic)
- But he cannot be a "good and wise teacher" if he made false or crazy claims
- His sayings and actions are organically outrageous—they can only make sense if he truly is the Lord

### Living Out the Resurrection
- We have received multiple spiritual gifts through Christ: circumcision without hands, eternal life, new species in Christ, part of the new man
- We are called to live boldly and "organically outrageous" in our faith
- The challenge: Be as willing to take a bold stand for Christ as he was
