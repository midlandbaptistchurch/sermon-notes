## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson continues a series on Biblical Sobriety, focusing on Titus 2:12 and the call to live soberly, righteously, and godly in the present world. He explores a word study methodology for understanding Scripture and applies the qualities listed in Titus 2:1-2 — sober, grave, temperate, and sound — particularly to aged men as examples and pillars of the church. The sermon emphasizes that genuine sobriety involves being rational, real, rooted, and responsible in one's Christian walk.

## Scripture
Titus 2:1-2, 12 — Paul instructs Titus on how various members of the church should conduct themselves, calling aged men to be sober, grave, temperate, and sound in faith, charity, and patience; and reminding all believers that grace teaches us to live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present world.

## Key Themes
- Biblical sobriety as a call to rational, genuine, grounded, and responsible living
- The inseparable relationship between living righteously (right decisions) and living godly (set apart to God)
- The danger of pursuing one without the other — righteousness without godliness leads to hypocrisy
- The role of aged men as foundational examples in the local church
- Word study methodology: context, usage, lists, and contrast
- Personal ownership and accountability as marks of a sober believer

## Notes
- **Series context:** This is Part 3 of an ongoing series on Biblical Sobriety anchored in Titus 2:12.
- **Word study approach:** Pastor Simpson reviewed four methods for studying biblical words: (1) context, (2) usage in other passages, (3) appearance in lists alongside other words, and (4) contrast with opposites (e.g., 2 Corinthians 5 — "beside ourselves" vs. "sober").
- **Soberly, righteously, godly:** These three adverbs in Titus 2:12 are treated as distinct but inseparable aspects of the Christian life. "Righteously" relates to judgment and decision-making; "godly" relates to being set apart to God, reflecting relationship rather than mere religion.
- **Living righteously without godliness** produces hypocrisy. Living godly without righteousness produces inconsistency in conduct. Biblical sobriety unites both.
- **Titus 2:1-2 — Aged Men:** The sermon moves to the specific application of sobriety to aged men, who are described as the foundation of the church (contrasted with young people as the future). Their example is critical for the next generation to see what righteous and godly living looks like in practice.
- **Four R's of Sobriety** (from the qualities sober, grave, temperate, sound):
  - **Rational** — knowing why you do what you do; exercising discernment
  - **Real** — being genuine, sincere, and transparent
  - **Rooted** — having a strong spiritual foundation and true spiritual strength
  - **Responsible** — taking personal ownership and accountability in the body of Christ
- **Illustrations used:** The mantle of Elijah passed to Elisha (2 Kings 2) as a picture of sobriety and the weight of responsibility; personal anecdotes about church maintenance and the faithfulness of church members (Fred) as illustrations of responsible ownership.
- **Closing references:** 1 Corinthians 15; 1 Thessalonians 4 mentioned in the context of sobriety and eternal perspective.
- Note: Pastor Simpson noted he was shortening the message and did not reach the full treatment of "grave" and "temperate" — those appear to be reserved for a future part of the series.
- The service included a baby dedication following the sermon.
