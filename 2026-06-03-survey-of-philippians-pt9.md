## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues the Survey of Philippians series by revisiting Paul's prayer in Philippians 1:8–11, focusing on the command that Christian love abound in knowledge and discernment. He emphasizes that genuine love must be grounded in biblical knowledge and sound judgment rather than mere emotion, and that believers have a responsibility to approve excellent things — particularly the more excellent way of grace over law — in view of accountability at the coming Day of Christ.

## Scripture
Philippians 1:8–11 — Paul's prayer for the Philippian believers, that their love would abound in knowledge and all judgment, enabling them to approve excellent things, remain sincere and without offense until the Day of Christ, and bear the fruits of righteousness.

## Key Themes
- Love grounded in knowledge and judgment, not emotion
- The biblical concept of divine love and hate as expressions of choice and supremacy
- Approving excellent things: grace and the dispensation of grace over law
- Sincerity and being without offense in doctrine and practice
- The Day of Christ: the judgment seat of Christ and future glory
- Believer accountability and the fruits of righteousness

## Notes

### Paul's Deep Love for the Philippians (Philippians 1:8)
- Paul loved the Philippians "in the bowels of Jesus Christ" — a deep, Christ-centered love that goes beyond carnal or fleshly feeling.
- This type of love prevents believers from quickly casting off or giving up on one another.
- God's grace and mercy toward us in all our failings is the model for how we love others.

### Love Must Abound in Knowledge and Judgment (Philippians 1:9)
- The prayer is not merely Paul's but, by inspiration of the Holy Spirit, reflects what God Himself desires for His people.
- Love is not to be superficial or purely emotional; it must abound "in knowledge and in all judgment."
- Biblical love necessarily involves discernment — including understanding what God hates:
  - Proverbs 6:16–19: Six things God hates (haughty eyes, lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart devising wicked plans, feet swift to mischief, a false witness, one who sows discord among brethren).
  - Psalm 5:5: "Thou hatest all workers of iniquity."
- Luke 14:26 — "Hating" father, mother, and one's own life in order to follow Christ refers to supremacy of choice, not emotional hatred.
- Malachi 1:2–3 — "Jacob I loved, Esau I hated" illustrates God making a sovereign choice, not an emotional reaction.
- A reprobate mind lacks the knowledge needed to exercise judgment.

### Approving Excellent Things (Philippians 1:10)
- Believers have a responsibility to sit under preaching, study the Bible, and exercise judgment to approve what is more excellent.
- 1 Corinthians 12:31–13:13 — Paul points to "a more excellent way" (charity/love) that surpasses even spiritual gifts.
- Examples of approving more excellent things in the dispensation of grace:
  - Total forgiveness of sins according to the riches of His grace (Ephesians 1:7) is more excellent than the conditional forgiveness extended to Israel (forgive as you forgive others; 70 times 7).
  - The ministry of reconciliation (2 Corinthians 5) is more excellent than the Great Commission of Matthew 28:19–20, which was tied to Israel's law-keeping context.
  - Baptism is not required for salvation today; Paul states Christ sent him not to baptize but to preach the gospel (1 Corinthians 1:17).
- Teaching 1 John 1:9 as the means of ongoing forgiveness for church-age believers, or requiring baptism for salvation, constitutes an offense to God and to the body of Christ.

### Sincerity and Without Offense (Philippians 1:10)
- "Sincere" carries the idea of purity of motive — like pottery without hidden cracks filled with wax, or honey without wax adulterant.
- Titus 2:7 — Ministers are to show uncorruptness in doctrine and sound speech that cannot be condemned.
- Believers are to speak and teach with sincerity and uncorruptness about what they believe from Scripture.

### Till the Day of Christ (Philippians 1:10–11)
- The Day of Christ is distinct from the rapture; it follows the rapture and involves the judgment seat of Christ.
- At the rapture: the dead in Christ rise first; their souls reunite with their resurrected bodies; living believers are changed in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye.
- At the judgment seat of Christ: the Lord Jesus presents believers to God the Father; works are evaluated — gold, silver, precious stones versus wood, hay, stubble — resulting in reward or loss of reward.
- In the middle of the Tribulation period, the heavens will open (Revelation 6:14, the sixth seal), and Christ will be seen on His throne; believers appear with Him in glory.
- The goal of Paul's prayer: that believers would be filled with "the fruits of righteousness, which are by Jesus Christ, unto the glory and praise of God" (Philippians 1:11).
- God praising the believer (cf. 1 Corinthians 4:5, "then shall every man have praise of God") is the ultimate reward and motivation for faithful living.

### Practical Application
- Believers must pursue knowledge of the Word — attendance, study, and application all matter in view of future accountability.
- Knowledge → prayer and application → judgment/discernment → approval of excellent things.
- Those who are absent from teaching opportunities are losing the chance to build on a foundation of gold, silver, and precious stones.
