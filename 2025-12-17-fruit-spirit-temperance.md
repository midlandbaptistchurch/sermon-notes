## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson concludes the Fruit of the Spirit series by examining temperance, the final fruit listed in Galatians 5:22-23. He distinguishes temperance from mere self-control, showing how it encompasses discipline, precision, completion, and separation for holiness—qualities that strengthen believers for ministry and conformity to Christ's image.

## Scripture
Galatians 5:22-23 — Paul lists the nine fruits of the Spirit that should characterize believers walking in the Spirit rather than fulfilling the lusts of the flesh.

## Key Themes
- Temperance as more than self-control
- The three categories of spiritual fruit: upward (toward God), outward (toward others), inward (for strength)
- Old Testament foundation of temperance as precise, disciplined mixing and consecration
- Temperance as spiritual strength for endurance and conformity to Christ

## Notes

### Context of Galatians 5
- Paul addresses the battle between law and liberty, warning against the Judaizers promoting circumcision
- Emphasizes love as the foundation: "thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself"
- Transitions from doctrinal correction to practical Christian living

### The Works of the Flesh (vv. 19-21)
Categorized into:
- Sexual sins: adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness
- Religious sins: idolatry, witchcraft
- Relational sins: hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies, envying, murders
- Substance abuse: drunkenness, revelings

### The Fruit of the Spirit (vv. 22-23)
Three categories:
1. **Upward (toward God)**: Love, joy, peace - what we receive from God
2. **Outward (toward others)**: Long-suffering, goodness, gentleness - what we give to others
3. **Inward (for strength)**: Faith, meekness, temperance - characteristics that strengthen us for ministry

### Bearing Upward Fruit
- **Love**: Acknowledge and recognize God's love; be constrained by His love
- **Joy**: Delight in God's good works past, present, and future; gladness of heart
- **Peace**: Security in eternal salvation, life's purpose, acceptance in Christ, God's understanding

### Understanding Temperance
- Modern versions often replace "temperance" with "self-control," but temperance has deeper meaning
- Old Testament examples show temperance as precise mixing, discipline, and consecration (Exodus 29:2, 30:34-38, Ezekiel 46:13-14)
- Applied to the body of Christ in 1 Corinthians 12:24 - members tempered together in unity

### Two Meanings of "Temper"
1. **Losing temper**: Losing strength, control, and holiness; becoming temporarily insane with anger
2. **Tempering steel**: Controlled heating process that hardens, strengthens, and makes steel conformable without breaking

### Practical Application
- Temperance includes discipline, precision, completion, and separation for holiness
- Enables believers to be "steadfast, unmovable" while remaining conformable to Christ's image
- Provides strength to endure trials and resist the works of the flesh
- Placed last in the list intentionally as the culmination requiring all other fruits
