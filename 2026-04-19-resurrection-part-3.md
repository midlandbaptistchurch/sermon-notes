## Summary
Bill Baughman presents extra-biblical historical evidence for the resurrection of Jesus, including accounts from Roman historian Tacitus, Jewish historian Josephus, the Babylonian Talmud, and Greek satirist Lucian. He explores how Jesus's radical teachings and claims—about hate, division, sacrifice, and his own deity—reveal that he cannot be merely a good teacher, but must be either a liar, lunatic, or Lord. The sermon challenges believers to live with "organic outrageousness" in their faith.

## Scripture
Acts 1:3 — "by many infallible proofs, being seen of them 40 days, speaking of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God."

## Key Themes
- Extra-biblical evidence for the resurrection
- Historical certainty and the law of authenticity
- Jesus's "organically outrageous" claims and teachings
- The logical categories: Liar, Lunatic, or Lord
- Radical cost of discipleship
- Living boldly as a Christian in culture

## Notes

### Extra-Biblical Evidence
- **Tacitus** (Roman historian, 116 AD): Hostile witness who documented Christians and Jesus's crucifixion
- **Josephus** (Jewish historian, 93-94 AD): Recorded that Jesus appeared alive again the third day after crucifixion
- **Babylonian Talmud** (2nd-4th centuries): Confirms Jesus's execution, adding credibility through opposition
- **Lucian** (Greek satirist, 125-175 AD): Mocked Christians but documented their devotion to Christ

### Scientific Method and the Resurrection
- Science operates within natural, observable, repeatable processes
- The resurrection lies outside those parameters, so science cannot validate or refute it—science must remain silent
- Legal/historical methods (law of authenticity) apply instead, similar to proving a stolen car case
- Historical certainty comes through exposure to facts that force a conclusion

### Jesus's "Organically Outrageous" Teachings
Organic (genuine, not forced) + Outrageous (arising from shocking extremity):

- **Hate your family**: "If any man come to me, and hate not his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brethren, and sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple" (Luke 14:26)
- **Division, not peace**: "Think not that I am come to send peace on earth: I came not to send peace, but a sword" (Matthew 10:34)
- **Consume my flesh and blood**: "Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of Man, and drink his blood, ye have no life in you" (John 6:53)—shocking to Jewish law
- **Let the dead bury their dead**: When a disciple asked to first bury his father, Jesus responded with urgency about the kingdom
- **Extreme self-sacrifice**: "And if thy right hand offend thee, cut it off...for it is profitable for thee that one of thy members should perish, and not that thy whole body should be cast into hell" (Matthew 5:30)

### The Liar, Lunatic, or Lord Argument
Jesus's claims and actions deny the possibility that he was simply a good or wise teacher. If he is not Lord, he must be either:
- A **liar** (intentionally deceiving)
- A **lunatic** (delusional)
- The **Lord** (truly God)

### Application
Believers are called to live with "organic outrageousness" in their faith:
- Show boldness in prayer and witness
- Demonstrate faith differently in everyday interactions
- Be willing to stand out in culture, even if neighbors find it strange
