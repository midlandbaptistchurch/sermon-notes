## Summary
Returning from a break, Pastor Jim Payne continues the ongoing series on the foundational structure of Scripture, focusing on God's literal physical throne in heaven and its character as a seat of righteousness and judgment. The sermon then turns to Ezekiel 28 and Isaiah 14 to examine Lucifer's original glory, his role as the anointed covering cherub, and the pride and violence that drove his rebellion against God's throne. Pastor Payne closes by previewing Lucifer's five "I will" statements from Isaiah 14, which will be examined in the following message.

## Scripture
Isaiah 14; Ezekiel 28 — Primary texts used to reconstruct Lucifer's original created glory, his position of worship leadership, and the nature of his insurrection against God. Supporting passages drawn throughout from Genesis 1–2, 2 Peter 3, Psalm 47, Psalm 97, Revelation 4, Revelation 12, and Ecclesiastes 11–12.

## Key Themes
- The literal, physical throne of God in the third heaven as a seat of righteousness and judgment
- The three heavens and the distinction between the physical creation and the "worlds" of Scripture
- Lucifer's original beauty, wisdom, musical ability, and priestly/worship leadership role
- Pride and rebellion as the first sins in God's creative order
- Lucifer's merchandising of rebellion among a third of the angels
- The present age as a time of faith rather than supernatural manifestation
- God's sovereignty over judgment — past, present, and future

## Notes
- **Series context:** Part 10 of an ongoing series; the series is described as likely not even halfway complete. Previous weeks established the physical creation account, the gap between Genesis 1:1 and 1:3, and the concept of distinct "worlds" in Scripture (the world that then was, the heavens and earth which are now, and the world to come).
- **God's throne (Isaiah 66:1; Revelation 4:1–2; Psalm 47:2; Psalm 97:2; Jeremiah 17:12):** Pastor Payne argues God's throne is a literal, physical location above the second heaven and above a frozen body of water ("the deep"). It is described as surrounded by clouds and characterized by righteousness and judgment.
- **The three heavens:** (1) First heaven — atmosphere/clouds; (2) Second heaven — sun, moon, and stars; (3) Third heaven — God's throne. The "heavens and earth which are now" (2 Peter 3:7) correspond to the finished creation of Genesis 2:1.
- **Lucifer in Ezekiel 28:12–18:**
  - Described as "sealing up the sum" — the pinnacle of wisdom and beauty among created beings.
  - Adorned with nine precious stones and gold, connecting him to the high priestly breastplate and indicating a role of religious/worship leadership.
  - Name "Lucifer" means "light bearer"; he radiated light through his gemstone covering.
  - Possessed "tabrets and pipes" — a built-in musical capacity, used in worship leadership.
  - Described as "the anointed cherub that covereth," placed on the holy mountain of God.
  - Was perfect from the day of his creation until iniquity was found in him.
- **Lucifer's rebellion:**
  - His wisdom became corrupted through pride in his own beauty and brightness (Ezekiel 28:17).
  - His "merchandise" or "traffic" involved going throughout the angelic world and promoting insurrection — selling his five "I will" declarations (Isaiah 14:13–14, to be examined next week).
  - The rebellion was a literal, physical assault against the throne of God.
  - A third of the angels followed him (Revelation 12:7).
  - The three named great angels — Lucifer, Michael, and Gabriel — are suggested to have each led one third of the angelic host, corresponding to the three persons of the Trinity.
  - God answered Lucifer's five "I wills" with His own five "I wills" (Ezekiel 28:16–18) and created the lake of fire as prepared judgment for the devil and his angels (Matthew 25:41).
- **The present age:** Pastor Payne emphasizes that the current age is not characterized by miraculous manifestation; God speaks today through Scripture and the inner witness of the Holy Spirit. Charismatic claims of tongues, healing, and miracles are rejected as a failure to rightly divide Scripture.
- **Ecclesiastes 11:9–12:1:** Used to illustrate that God will bring all into judgment, and that youth should remember their Creator before the difficulties of age and the final accounting arrive.
- **Preview for next week:** The five "I will" statements of Lucifer from Isaiah 14:13–14, and the significance of the light created on Day One of Genesis.
