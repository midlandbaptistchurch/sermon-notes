## Summary
Pastor Simpson teaches on the seven gifts to the body of Christ listed in Romans 12, emphasizing that these are not just gifts to be given but also to be received. The sermon distinguishes between the manifestation gifts of the Spirit in 1 Corinthians 12 (which were supernatural and transitional) and the gifts given to the body in Romans 12 (which believers exercise through study and application of God's Word). The message challenges believers to purposefully practice these gifts within the local church body for God's glory and the strengthening of other members.

## Scripture
Romans 12:3-8 — Paul instructs believers not to think more highly of themselves than they ought, but to think soberly according to the measure of faith God has given. He explains that the body has many members with different offices, and lists seven gifts: prophecy, ministry, teaching, exhortation, giving, ruling, and showing mercy, each with brief instructions on their proper exercise.

## Key Themes
- **Distinction Between Gift Lists**: The gifts in Romans 12 differ from those in 1 Corinthians 12, which were manifestations of the Spirit requiring supernatural empowerment during the transitional period.
- **Giving and Receiving**: The cycle of both giving and receiving these gifts is critical for the body of Christ to function properly and bring glory to God.
- **Sober Self-Assessment**: Believers should think rationally and responsibly about their role in the body, according to the measure of faith (the doctrine given to Paul for this age).
- **Purposeful Practice**: These gifts require intentional effort and planning to exercise effectively within the local church.

## Notes

### Introduction and Context
- This is described as a "maintenance message" on a familiar passage that needs regular review
- Danger of complacency with familiar Scripture passages
- Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12, and Ephesians 4 are the main chapters teaching on the body of Christ

### Distinguishing the Gift Lists (1 Corinthians 12 vs. Romans 12)
- 1 Corinthians 12:7 emphasizes "manifestation of the Spirit"
- Only prophecy appears in both lists; all other gifts differ between the two passages
- 1 Corinthians 12 gifts were supernatural manifestations requiring the Spirit to come upon believers
- These included: word of wisdom, word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healing, working of miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, interpretation of tongues
- Example: Peter knowing about Ananias and Sapphira's deception was supernatural knowledge
- These manifestation gifts pointed to Christ as Messiah and previewed kingdom conditions
- Acts 2: Peter's message was given supernaturally, not through prepared study
- Romans 12 gifts are exercised through study, application, and walking by grace

### The Measure of Faith (Romans 12:3-5)
- Verse 3: "not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think, but to think soberly according as God hath dealt to every man the measure of faith"
- "The measure of faith" (with the definite article) refers to the doctrine given to Paul for this dispensation
- References to "measure," "proportion," and "rule" indicate boundaries and limits
- Context is the body of Christ and practical sacrificial living
- Verse 4-5: Many members in one body, not all having the same office, but all members one of another
- "According to" is a key phrase in Paul's writings, always showing connection and foundation

### The Seven Gifts to the Body of Christ

#### 1. Prophecy (Verse 6)
- "Having then gifts differing according to the grace that is given to us, whether prophecy, let us prophesy according to the proportion of faith"
- During the transitional period: prophets identified what was Scripture as the canon was being completed
- Paul wrote more letters than what is in our Bible; not all were inspired
- Today: prophecy is God revealing His Word to His people
- The trail: Revelation → Inspiration → Preservation → Translation → Illumination and Interpretation
- Includes teaching, preaching, and living out the Word so others can see it
- Not everyone must teach from a pulpit, but everyone has responsibility to communicate God's Word
- Can be one-on-one ministry, small group Bible study, family devotions, or conversations among believers

#### 2. Ministry/Service (Verse 7)
- "or ministry, let us wait on our ministry"
- The word "wait" connects to the role of a waiter/server in a restaurant
- A good waiter serves but also waits—there is balance between service and timing
- Illustration: waiters must discern when to approach, when to ask questions, when to serve, when to step back
- Ministry requires discernment about what needs to be met, when, and how much
- Grace is not just rules but principles requiring the mind of Christ and discernment
- Requires knowing people closely enough to understand their needs
- Not just going through motions or meeting quotas
- Must be willing to both minister to others and be ministered to
- Sometimes means distributing tasks, sometimes doing them yourself, sometimes taking things off others' plates

#### 3. Teaching (Verse 7)
- "or he that teacheth, on teaching"
- Before a teacher is a teacher, they must be a student
- Teachers must remain teachable and correctable
- Example: Apollos was eloquent and effective but received correction from tentmakers Aquila and Priscilla
- He was not prideful but received their teaching and incorporated it
- 2 Timothy 2:2: "the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also"
- Good teaching requires preparation and study
- Best insights often come toward the end of study, not the beginning
- Teaching is not just talking but putting in labor and work
- Can teach through booklets, pamphlets, books, videos, audio ministry
- Sometimes teaching means bringing someone to a teacher or getting them to listen to teaching

#### 4. Exhortation (Verse 8)
- "Or he that exhorteth, on exhortation"
- To exhort: encourage, embolden, cheer, advise, stimulate, motivate
- Helping people be excited about the things of God and the work of God
- Cannot excite others about something you're not excited about yourself
- Must be done on purpose—requires intentional planning
- Example: deciding to exhort someone today, then planning how to do it (text, call, in person)
- The three deacons (Frank, Pat, Hugh) are good exhorters
- Being around people who exhort strengthens you
- Challenge: Can you look back and prove you exhorted someone this week?

#### 5. Giving (Verse 8)
- "he that giveth, let him do it with simplicity"
- Give without show or fanfare
- Important to know what you're giving to
- Give according to what you have, not what you don't have
- Not just finances—can give teaching, exhortation, encouragement, comfort
- Must purpose to give and be able to look back and verify you did
- Challenge: Look back over the last hour, 12 hours, 24 hours, week—did you give?

#### 6. Ruling (Verse 8)
- "he that ruleth with diligence"
- Leadership, asserting authority, coming under authority
- First authority is the Word of God
- Includes roles: husband, wife, parent, child, employee, employer, church member, pastor
- May involve putting yourself under the authority of a more mature, wise Christian
- Concept of accountability partner fits here

#### 7. Showing Mercy (Verse 8)
- "he that sheweth mercy with cheerfulness"
- Pastor Simpson identifies this as his personal struggle among the gifts
- Must show mercy, not just justice or judgment
- Easier to show mercy to someone going through something they didn't cause
- Harder to show mercy to someone who made a mistake or did something stupid
- Galatians 6: restore such a one in the spirit of meekness
- Romans 12 (end): "Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord"—do good to those who don't do good to you
- Sometimes requires doing what doesn't come naturally or what you're not comfortable with
- May need to learn from someone who is good at showing mercy
- Seeing others deal with difficulties you don't face can change your perspective

### Application and Challenge
- These seven gifts strengthen the church and bring glory to God
- We will be held accountable for these at the Judgment Seat of Christ
- The cycle of giving and receiving must function for the body to be strong
- Some build walls and refuse to receive, but we have obligation to both give and receive
- Don't want to fail any of these gifts at the judgment seat
- Practical challenge: Pick one gift this week to purposefully exercise
- Put a note in your phone, set an alarm mid-week to check if you've done it
- Keep a record, however you choose to do it
- Start with one and build from there
- Make a plan to ensure it gets done
- Look for specific opportunities to minister to members of your local church
