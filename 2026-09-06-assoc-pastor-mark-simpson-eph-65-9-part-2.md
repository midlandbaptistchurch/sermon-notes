## Summary
Pastor Simpson continues teaching through Ephesians 6:5-9 on the servant-master relationship, emphasizing the importance of understanding context before making application. He stresses that study must come before service, and demonstrates how the cultural reality of servanthood in Paul's day teaches believers to serve temporal masters "as unto Christ." The sermon explores how both servants and free people are called to proper humility—servants are "the Lord's free men" while free people are "Christ's servants."

## Scripture
Ephesians 6:5-9 — Paul instructs servants to obey their masters with fear and trembling, in singleness of heart, as unto Christ, not with eye-service as men-pleasers, but doing the will of God from the heart, knowing that whatever good anyone does, they will receive the same from the Lord.

## Key Themes
- **Study Before Service**: Understanding Scripture in context must come before making practical application, as emphasized by the priority of study over service in the Christian life.
- **Servanthood as Unto Christ**: Believers are called to serve their earthly authorities with the same dedication they would serve Christ directly, transforming temporal relationships into spiritual service.
- **The Paradox of Freedom and Service**: Servants in Christ are "the Lord's free men" while those who are free are "Christ's servants," teaching proper humility regardless of earthly position.
- **Humility in the Body of Christ**: Members of Christ's body must not think more highly of themselves than they ought, recognizing they don't know when it's their time to serve or be served.

### Introduction to Study Method
- People tend to immediately make Scripture practical and apply it
- More emphasis today on service and works than on study
- Pastor Simpson notes that in his years of Bible college, no one put study before service
- Study should come in priority before service so believers know why they do what they do
- Can be busy doing work but accomplish nothing without understanding

### Christ as Head - The Foundation
- 1 Corinthians 11:3 - The head of every man is Christ, the head of woman is man, the head of Christ is God
- During this age, Christ deliberately maintains a submissive position to God the Father
- Christ not only humbled Himself to come to earth (Philippians 2) but continues in submission
- This submissive position is temporary—when Christ returns and sets up His kingdom, He will be head over everything
- Christ's example of submission teaches believers how to live under His headship

### The Unfamiliar Relationship
- Husband-wife and parent-child relationships are familiar to modern believers
- Servant-master relationship is the least familiar to contemporary culture
- We don't live where people are servants because they were born to servant families
- In that culture, servants owned nothing and had only what was provided
- America is "the land of opportunity" but lands with masters and kings were not
- No land available for servants to own, no independent work opportunities

### Understanding True Ownership
- 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 - Believers are bought with a price, not their own
- Christ paid for believers with His own blood and life
- No problem with Christ owning us, but would have problem with someone else owning us
- If we belong to Christ and are His body, we must serve the body
- Sometimes must take position of serving others within the body

### The Teaching to Servants (Ephesians 6:5-8)
- Servants commanded to obey masters according to the flesh
- With fear and trembling and singleness of heart
- "As unto Christ" - this is the key to the teaching
- Not with eye-service as men-pleasers
- As servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart
- With good will, doing service as to the Lord and not to men
- Whatever good thing anyone does, they will receive the same from the Lord
- Whether bond or free—applies to all

### The Issue of Pride in the Body
- Romans 12:3 - Don't think of yourself more highly than you ought to think
- Think soberly according to the measure of faith God has given
- Follows with teaching about being members of the body with different gifts
- Must have humility because you don't know when it's your time to serve or be served
- Young Christians who were servants might struggle with this
- Satan would use their position to cause strife and division
- They might ask: if we're members of the body, shouldn't we be equal?

### The Opportunity in Servanthood
- Servants had opportunity to show love and dedication to Christ
- Work and serve temporal master the same way they would serve Christ
- Must humble themselves and not think more highly than they should
- Not serving just because master bought them under cultural system
- Doing it "as unto the Lord and not just unto men"

### Practical Application to Employment
- May work where there are lost people
- May have a lost boss in difficult situations
- Regardless, work as unto the Lord and not unto men
- Character defined as how you act when nobody is looking
- Men-pleasers work differently when being watched versus when alone
- True character means doing the job right because it needs to be done

### 1 Timothy 6:1-2 - The Continuing Issue
- "Let as many servants as are under the yoke count their own masters worthy of all honor"
- Purpose: "that the name of God and his doctrine be not blasphemed"
- Those with believing masters should not despise them because they are brethren
- Rather do them service because they are faithful and beloved
- "These things teach and exhort" - Timothy commanded to teach this in churches

### 1 Corinthians 7:21-23 - The Paradox
- If called being a servant, "care not for it" - don't worry about it
- But if you can be made free, use that opportunity
- "He that is called in the Lord being a servant is the Lord's free man"
- Even in earthly bondage of servitude, if saved, you are the Lord's free man
- "He that is called being free is Christ's servant"
- Both sides need the right mindset regardless of temporal position
- Just a servant? No, you're the Lord's free man
- Free on earth? Yes, but you're Christ's servant
- Both bring a person back to the right mindset

### Context of 1 Corinthians 7
- Paul dealing with transitional period
- Short time to go to the Jew first and also to the Greek
- Before signs, miracles, and wonders would be cut out
- Middle wall of partition coming down
- Jews would no longer be preferred but saved same way as everyone else
- Paul's burden for his own people affects this teaching
- Better to act as if you don't have a spouse (transitional teaching)
- Contradicts Ephesians 5 if not understood as transitional
- Paul wrote this as a free man but would write other books from prison

### 1 Timothy 6:3-5 - Warning Against False Teaching
- If any man teaches otherwise and doesn't consent to wholesome words of Jesus Christ
- And the doctrine according to godliness
- He is proud, knowing nothing
- Doting about questions and strife of words
- Envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings result
- Perverse disputings of men of corrupt minds
- Destitute of the truth
- "Supposing that gain is godliness"
- Command: "from such withdraw thyself"
- This teaching connected to the servant-master issue
- Servant had no hope of gain in that system
- Explains why Paul uses such strong language against those teaching "gain is godliness"

### Christ's Example of Service
- John's Gospel - Jesus took towel and water to wash disciples' feet
- Fishermen, men's men who had left all to follow Him
- Peter's response: "You are not washing my feet"
- Christ rebuked him; Peter then asked for head and hands washed
- Peter lacked understanding at that point in his life
- Christ humbled Himself to do job that servants would do
- In some houses, servants would wash feet of visitors
- Requires great humility to serve in this way
- Shows how much Christ values humility in believers' lives
