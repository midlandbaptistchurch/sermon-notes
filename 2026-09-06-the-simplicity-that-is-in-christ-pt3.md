## Summary
In this third installment of his series on the simplicity that is in Christ, Pastor Jim Payne turns from salvation to sanctification — what it means for God to set believers apart and what believers are called to do in response. Drawing heavily from Paul's epistles, he outlines both the positional reality of sanctification (God's work) and the practical responsibility of the believer, offering a straightforward six-step approach to handling sin and six practical daily habits for holy living.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 11:3 — Paul's warning that believers' minds should not be corrupted from "the simplicity that is in Christ," which frames the entire sermon series.

## Key Themes
- Sanctification as both God's positional work and the believer's practical responsibility
- The battle between the flesh and the spirit in the believer's daily life
- Total forgiveness in Christ by grace, not by repeated confession for re-forgiveness
- Practical steps for responding to sin and walking in holiness
- The role of thanksgiving, prayer, and glorifying God in everyday decisions

## Notes

### Context and Series Overview
- This is Part 3 of an ongoing series. Previous parts covered the simplicity of salvation.
- The five facets of life in Christ outlined in the series: Salvation, Sanctification, Study, Service, and Soul-winning.
- Paul's ministry at Ephesus (Acts 20:20–31) is used as an opening illustration of a pastor's responsibility to declare "all the counsel of God" — both deep and simple things.

### What Sanctification Is
- Sanctification defined as the work of God whereby, through justification, He claims the believer — to serve Him, represent Him, and one day be with Him.
- Distinguished from justification (being declared free from the penalty of sin) and propitiation (God's wrath satisfied by Christ's death).
- Two sides of sanctification:
  - **Positional** (God's side): God sets the believer apart at the moment of salvation (1 Corinthians 1:2, 1:30; 1 Corinthians 6:11).
  - **Practical** (believer's side): The believer is called to live out that holiness (2 Corinthians 6:17; 7:1; 1 Peter 1:16; 2 Timothy 2:19).
- Key verses: 1 Corinthians 1:2 ("sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints"); 1 Corinthians 1:30 (Christ is made unto us wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and redemption); 1 Corinthians 6:11 ("such were some of you, but ye are washed, sanctified, justified").
- Old Testament parallels: Leviticus 11:44; Leviticus 20 (God severing Israel from the nations); Joshua 3:5 ("Sanctify yourselves, for tomorrow the Lord will do wonders among you").

### The Battle with Sin (Romans 7:15–25)
- Paul's honest description of the believer's inner conflict — wanting to do good but doing what he hates.
- The "law of sin" is written in the members (the body/flesh); the "law of God" is in the mind.
- Illustrated as two dogs fighting — the one you feed wins.
- Resolution: "Thanks be unto God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."

### Forgiveness Is Already Given
- Believers in this dispensation of grace already have total forgiveness in Christ (Colossians 2:13; Ephesians 1:7; Titus 2:14).
- Confession is not to obtain forgiveness but to acknowledge sin and commune with God about it.
- Sin retroactively compounded Christ's suffering on the cross — a motivation for godly sorrow.

### Six Steps for When You Sin
1. **Stop** — cease the sinful thought, word, or action.
2. **Think** — examine and acknowledge what you are doing wrong.
3. **Confess** — bring it before God; take His side against your sin (Psalm 51:3).
4. **Claim** — receive the forgiveness already granted in Christ (Colossians 2:13; Ephesians 1:7).
5. **Repent** — turn away from the sin with godly sorrow (2 Corinthians 7:10).
6. **Forget** — do not dwell in guilt; press forward (Philippians 3:13–14).

### Six Practical Daily Habits for Holy Living
1. **Expressing** — give thanks in everything (1 Thessalonians 5:18; Ephesians 5:20).
2. **Entreating** — pray without ceasing (1 Thessalonians 5:17).
3. **Exalting** — do all to the glory of God (1 Corinthians 10:31).
4. **Edifying** — build yourself and others up; not all lawful things are edifying (1 Corinthians 10:23).
5. **Expeditioning** — ask whether your actions help you along your journey in Christ; avoid besetting sins (1 Corinthians 10:23).
6. **Enslaving** — do not be brought under the power of anything other than God (1 Corinthians 6:12).

### Closing Exhortation
- Believers are not to live under guilt or bondage; Christ is made unto us wisdom, righteousness, sanctification, and glorification.
- Sanctification is simple — not easy, but not needlessly complicated. God knows we will fail; the key is not to despair but to follow the steps and move forward.
