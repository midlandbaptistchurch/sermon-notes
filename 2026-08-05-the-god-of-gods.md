## Summary
Tim Bish challenges the modern Christian tendency to dismiss the gods of the Bible as mere statues, arguing from Scripture that they represent real spiritual beings under the dominion of Satan, the "God of this world." He surveys dozens of biblical references to show that God repeatedly identifies Himself as the God of gods and Lord of lords — not because other gods are His equals, but because He rules over every spiritual power that exists. The sermon closes with a call to take spiritual warfare seriously, resting in the truth that there is ultimately one blessed and only Potentate, Jesus Christ.

## Scripture
1 Timothy 6:15 — Paul's declaration that Jesus is "the blessed and only potentate, the King of kings and the Lord of lords," used as the sermon's anchor text and closing focus.

## Key Themes
- The biblical title "God of gods" and what it reveals about false gods as real spiritual beings
- Satan as the "God of this world" and "Prince of the Power of the Air"
- The reality and seriousness of spiritual warfare (Ephesians 6:12)
- God's supremacy and judgment over all other spiritual powers
- The uniqueness of Christianity: our God paid the sacrifice rather than demanding one

## Notes

### "God of gods" and "Lord of lords" in Scripture
- Deuteronomy 10:17 — "The Lord your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, a great God, a mighty and terrible."
- Psalm 136:2–3 — Back-to-back verses giving thanks to the "God of gods" and "Lord of lords," treating the titles as parallel.
- Joshua 22 (referenced) — "The Lord God of gods, he knoweth."
- Daniel 2:47 — Nebuchadnezzar confesses, "Of a truth your God is a God of gods and a Lord of kings," acknowledging Israel's God above Babylon's 16+ deities.
- The phrase "God of gods" appears only in the Old Testament; in the New Testament the title shifts to "Lord of lords" (Revelation 17:14; 19:16). Tim suggests this reflects how modern readers no longer recognize false gods as real spiritual entities.

### False gods are not merely statues
- Baal (meaning "Lord") is the most-named false god in the Bible — over 90 occurrences in at least seven spellings. In the New Testament he appears as Beelzebub.
- The Egyptian magicians Jannes and Jambres (named in 2 Timothy 3:8) performed genuine miracles: turning rods to serpents, water to blood, and bringing forth frogs — before failing at the plague of lice (Exodus 7–8).
- Exodus 12:12 — God executed judgments "upon all the gods of Egypt," implying real spiritual beings, not inert idols.
- Leviticus 17:7 — Israel warned not to offer sacrifices "unto devils."
- Deuteronomy 32:17 — They "sacrificed unto devils... to new gods that came newly up," suggesting waves of new spiritual entities entering history.
- 1 Corinthians 10:20 — Paul confirms the Gentiles "sacrifice to devils and not to God" — separated by ~2,700 years from Deuteronomy 32 yet the same reality.

### Satan's titles and authority
- 2 Corinthians 4:4 — Satan is called "the God of this world" who blinds unbelievers.
- John 12:31; 14:30; 16:11 — Jesus calls him "the prince of this world" — a ruler, but one who can be judged, distinguishing him from the uncreated God.
- Ephesians 2:2 — "The prince of the power of the air."
- Ephesians 6:12 — We wrestle against "principalities, powers, rulers of the darkness of this world, spiritual wickedness in high places."
- Isaiah 14:12 — Lucifer called "son of the morning" (morning star, sun); Tim notes the parallel to sun-god worship (Baal, Ra).
- Lucifer was the most powerful created being, recruited a third of the angels, and invented deception ("Satan invented marketing").

### Gods named in the Bible (partial catalog read by Tim)
Adrammelech, Anammelech, Artemis, Asherah, Ashima, Ashtoreth/Astaroth, Baal (multiple forms), Castor and Pollux, Chemosh, Chiun, Dagon, Hadad, Jupiter, Mercurius, Milcom, Molech/Moloch, Nebo, Nehushtan, Nibhaz, Nisroch, the Queen of Heaven (Jeremiah 7 & 44), Rimmon, Succoth-benoth, Tammuz, Tartak.
- Many of these gods persist in culture today (days of the week, Norse/Roman mythology, Marvel films).
- The Queen of Heaven is still invoked in Roman Catholicism (Mary) and, Tim argues, in Islam (Allah).

### One God above all
- 1 Corinthians 8:5–6 — "For though there be that are called gods, whether in heaven or in earth... unto us there is but one God, the Father... and one Lord Jesus Christ."
- 1 Timothy 6:15 — Jesus is the "only potentate" — the single, ultimate Ruler over every king, lord, and god.
- Unlike every other religion, Christianity's God paid the sacrifice on behalf of His people rather than requiring sacrifice from them.

### Application
- 21st-century Christians have fictionalized the supernatural and lost a healthy fear of real spiritual warfare.
- The armor of God (Ephesians 6) is not a platitude — it is protection for believers and those who follow them, especially children.
- Satan's strategy has always been to draw people away from God through deception; his methods change (ancient sacrifice → social media → AI), but his goal does not.
