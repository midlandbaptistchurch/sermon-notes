## Summary
Pastor Payne examines Paul's prayer for the Philippians in Philippians 1:8-10, emphasizing the Christian's responsibility to grow in knowledge and judgment. He challenges believers to develop spiritual discernment and understand the difference between excellent and inferior teachings, warning that many Christians today have a reprobate mind due to lack of foundational biblical knowledge.

## Scripture
Philippians 1:8-10 — Paul's powerful prayer for the Philippian church and his desire for believers to abound in love with knowledge and judgment, approving things that are excellent until the day of Christ.

## Key Themes
- The necessity of biblical knowledge for spiritual maturity
- Judgment and discernment as Christian responsibilities
- Love defined by commitment, faithfulness, and truth rather than emotion alone
- The reprobate mind: spiritual blindness resulting from rejection of truth
- Accountability to Christ at the judgment seat
- Distinguishing between sound doctrine and false teaching
- The role of reproof and exhortation in pastoral ministry
- Understanding the mystery revealed to Paul versus the kingdom message

## Notes

### Paul's Prayer and Its Importance
- This prayer ranks among Paul's greatest prayers, found in the prison epistles (Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians)
- The prayer takes believers to a high level of living and understanding God's will
- Christians have personal responsibility and accountability for understanding and applying this passage

### Love Abounding in Knowledge and Judgment (1:9)
- True Christian love is not merely emotional but rooted in knowledge and discernment
- Love requires making judgments and decisions based on biblical truth
- The modern liberal mindset wrongly divorces love from discernment, leading to uncritical acceptance of error
- Christians must exercise judgment to distinguish excellent things from lesser things

### The Reprobate Mind
- Approximately 95% of professing Christians lack the mechanism to attain to this passage's standard
- A reprobate mind results from rejecting God's knowledge (Romans 1:28)
- The reprobate mind is characterized by: ever learning but never coming to knowledge of truth (2 Timothy 3:7)
- People may have a form of godliness but deny its power (2 Timothy 3:5)
- Even believers can have a reprobate mind if they refuse sound doctrine

### Approving Things That Are Excellent (1:10)
- "Excellent" means superior by comparison—excelling other things
- Christians must examine and prove doctrine, then approve it, then move toward it in practice
- Example: We have complete forgiveness in Christ (Ephesians 1:7, Colossians 1:14) rather than needing repeated confessions
- Misunderstanding God's complete forgiveness work offends God

### The Connection to the Day of Christ
- Paul repeatedly mentions "the day of Christ" to emphasize future accountability
- The day of Christ is when Christ will establish His kingdom in heavenly places after casting Satan out (Revelation 12)
- Three and a half years after that, Christ will come to establish His earthly kingdom
- Believers will appear with Him in glory, with degrees of glory based on obedience and belief

### Timothy's Charge and Reproof (from 2 Timothy 4)
When preaching, one must:
- **Mystery**: Know the gospel of grace committed to Paul (not the kingdom gospel)
- **Man**: Consider Paul's apostolic teaching and Christ's resurrection according to Paul's gospel
- **Material**: Know sound doctrine from Scripture
- **Method**: Rightly divide the word of truth (2 Timothy 2:15)
- **Motivation**: Understand all Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for doctrine, reproof, and correction

### Sound Words and Keeping Truth
- Sound words are committed through Paul and must be kept by the Holy Spirit's confirmation
- Faith and love in Christ Jesus are central to holding fast truth
- The difference between Peter's gospel (kingdom) and Paul's gospel (grace) is fundamental but widely misunderstood
- The major division of Scripture: what was kept secret from the foundation of the world vs. what was spoken by the prophets

### Sincere and Without Offense (1:10)
- Sincere means pure, as when honey is checked for purity or pottery is held to light for cracks
- God desires believers to be sincere in their faith and understanding
- Understanding is possible for all believers: "Consider what I say and the Lord give the understanding in all things" (2 Timothy 2:7)
- Reproof must be given repeatedly to orient believers correctly toward truth
