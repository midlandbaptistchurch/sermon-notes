## Summary
This lesson concludes the study of Desiderius Erasmus and introduces Robert Stephanus (Estienne) as key contributors to the Textus Receptus. Hugh Taylor emphasizes how Erasmus spent his entire life collecting Greek manuscripts and writings of early church fathers to produce the first printed Greek New Testament in 1516, publishing five editions over twenty years. Robert Stephanus continued this work, publishing four editions of the Greek New Testament (1546-1551), with his 1551 edition being the first to divide the New Testament into chapters and verses—the system still used today. The lesson emphasizes how God providentially used these men to preserve His Word and challenges believers to recognize their own sphere of influence and ministry responsibility.

## Scripture
2 Timothy 2:15 — Referenced as the believer's responsibility to study and show themselves approved unto God, made possible because we have God's Word in our own language.

## Key Themes
- **Erasmus's Lifelong Preparation**: Erasmus spent approximately 15 years in intensive study and his entire life collecting Greek manuscripts and writings of early church fathers to verify the correct Greek text
- **Manuscript Evidence**: Erasmus had access to every variety of manuscript known today and chose the majority text, rejecting manuscripts that aligned with the Catholic Vulgate and modern corrupt versions
- **The Textus Receptus Foundation**: Erasmus laid the cornerstone for the Textus Receptus by publishing five editions of the Greek New Testament (1516, 1519, 1522, 1527, 1535)
- **Stephanus's Contribution**: Robert Stephanus published four editions (1546, 1549, 1550, 1551), with the 1551 edition establishing the chapter and verse divisions still used today
- **Personal Ministry and Influence**: Every believer has a God-given sphere of influence and body of work—their legacy with family, church, and community—that requires faithfulness to God's Word

## Notes

### Conclusion of Erasmus Study
- Erasmus moved to Basel, Switzerland in 1514, weighed down with books and copious notes on the New Testament
- His materials were confiscated by customs; he said they had "stolen the labors of his life"
- The materials were eventually released and he continued his work

### Erasmus's Manuscript Access
- Critics falsely assert Erasmus didn't have access to manuscripts we have today
- According to Frederick Nolan, "it is indisputable that he was acquainted with every variety which is known to us"
- Erasmus published an edition corresponding with the text discovered to prevail in the great body of Greek manuscripts (the majority text)
- He understood there was a body of manuscripts with consistent majority agreement and rejected those that did not align

### Use of Early Church Fathers
- Erasmus spent the last 15 years in concentrated study of Christian writers from the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th centuries
- He collected their Scripture quotations and used them to verify the correct Greek text
- These quotations pre-date by several hundred years the Vaticanus and Sinaiticus manuscripts used by modern translations
- The early church fathers' writings contradicted the Alexandrian manuscripts and supported the majority text

### Erasmus's Published Editions
- First edition: 1516 - "Novum Instrumentum Omne" (the very first printed Greek New Testament)
- Second edition: 1519
- Third edition: 1522
- Fourth edition: 1527
- Fifth edition: 1535
- Each edition included his own Latin translation in parallel with the Greek
- He corrected errors found in the Latin Vulgate

### Erasmus's Legacy
- Martin Luther used Erasmus's second edition in 1534 to produce Germany's first complete Bible
- This inspired the Reformation adage: "Erasmus laid the egg and Luther hatched it"
- Erasmus died in Basel on July 12, 1536, among Protestant friends
- He died without relations with the Roman Catholic Church, having forsaken tradition for truth
- His life laid the cornerstone for the Textus Receptus

### Robert Stephanus (Robertus Stephanus/Robert Estienne)
- Also known in Latin as Robertus Stephanus or in English as Robert Stevens
- Born in 1503 in Paris, France
- Son of Henri Estienne, an established prominent printer
- Educated early in ancient languages: Hebrew, Greek, and Latin
- Followed his father into the printing business

### Stephanus's Publishing Work
- Published a Latin New Testament in 1532
- Published a Hebrew Bible twice: first in 13 volumes (1539-1543), second in 10 volumes (1544-1546)
- Had quite a reputation for printing theological and educational materials
- France's royal topographer and printer in Greek to the king

### Stephanus's Greek New Testament Editions
- First edition: 1546 (30 years after Erasmus's first edition)
- Second edition: 1549
- Third edition: 1550
- Fourth edition: 1551 - same Greek text as third edition but first to divide New Testament into chapters and verses, the system still used today

### Persecution and Move to Geneva
- His printing and promotion of the Greek New Testament aroused opposition from the Roman Catholic Church
- Despite being France's royal topographer and printer in Greek to the king, the Catholics were relentless
- In 1550, he was compelled to leave Paris and settle in Geneva
- The Roman Catholics forced him into the Reformation movement, becoming officially Protestant
- He embraced the Reformed faith and died in Geneva in September 1559

### Application: Personal Ministry and Sphere of Influence
- Most people don't produce a large-scale body of work like Erasmus or Stephanus
- Every believer has a personally customized sphere of influence: spouse, siblings, children, grandchildren
- The home is a learning laboratory for life
- Each person's sphere of influence is their ministry responsibility
- Sphere of influence increases with marriage of children and birth of grandchildren
- Believers are called to "make full proof of thy ministry" (2 Timothy 2:15)
- The King James Bible is the guiding light, true north, and authority for life's work
- God has providentially provided His Word in English so believers can fulfill their calling
- We are not casual observers but active participants in God's will and plan
