## Summary
In Part 3 of this Sunday School series on believing the King James Bible, Hugh Taylor presents the fifth and final precursor to the main study: the element of faith required to believe God's Word. He contrasts the Apostle Paul's direct, face-to-face encounters with Christ with the faith-to-faith relationship available to believers today through Scripture, and then offers both internal and external validations of the Bible's reliability, including its internal consistency, its alignment with scientific observations, its historical accuracy, and its transformative power in the lives of believers.

## Scripture
- **2 Timothy 2:15** — The call to study and rightly divide the word of truth, used to establish the discipline required to properly understand Scripture.
- **2 Timothy 3:16–17** — All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness.
- **Leviticus 17:11** — "The life of the flesh is in the blood" — cited as an example of Scripture's external validation through medical science.
- **John 5:2** — The pool of Bethesda, once considered mythical, was archaeologically confirmed in 1888, illustrating Scripture's historical reliability.
- **Hebrews 11:1–3** — The definition of faith as the substance of things hoped for and the evidence of things not seen.

## Key Themes
- The role of faith in believing the Bible
- Paul's face-to-face faith versus the believer's faith-to-faith relationship with God through Scripture
- Internal validation of Scripture through its consistency and logic
- External validation of Scripture through science, history, and archaeology
- The Bible as the supreme authority over all human authorities
- The transformative, generational impact of living by God's Word

## Notes
**Review of Precursors 1–4:**
Taylor briefly recaps the first four facets already covered in the series: (1) Satan works to corrupt and complicate the Bible issue; (2) God wants us to understand His Word and will; (3) the men, manuscripts, and message are all tied to the issue (to be addressed in Part 2 of the series); (4) the pursuit of truth between God and man.

**Fifth Precursor — Faith:**
Believing the King James Bible, or any Bible, or even God Himself, requires an element of faith. Taylor commends those who hold a firm, committed position on Scripture over those who prefer vagueness.

**Face-to-Face Faith vs. Faith-to-Faith:**
Paul received direct, personal revelations from Christ — what Taylor calls "face-to-face faith" — which equipped him to endure extreme suffering and fulfill his apostolic ministry. Believers today have the documented result of that partnership (Paul's epistles and Luke's historical writings) and exercise "faith-to-faith": faith given by God, grounded in the faith Paul worked out before us.

**Avoiding Circular Reasoning:**
Taylor acknowledges the charge of circular reasoning ("the Bible is true because it says it is") and distinguishes it from a reasoned, faith-based position supported by evidence outside the text itself — personal transformation, historical confirmation, and archaeological discovery.

**Internal Validation of Scripture:**
- *2 Timothy 2:15* — Scripture validates itself through its internally consistent system of logic, interpretation, and application; believers are called to study and rightly divide it dispensationally.
- *2 Timothy 3:16–17* — Scripture is complete and sufficient to thoroughly furnish the believer for every good work.

**External Validation of Scripture:**
- *Leviticus 17:11* — "The life of the flesh is in the blood" anticipated modern medical understanding, contrasted with harmful historical practices such as bloodletting.
- *John 5:2* — The pool of Bethesda, long dismissed by critics as mythical, was excavated in 1888 near the Church of St. Anne in Jerusalem, with all five porches intact as described by John.
- Archaeology has confirmed biblical cities, rulers, and events across two centuries of discovery; Scripture roots its message in verifiable history and invites scrutiny.

**The Bible as Supreme Authority:**
All human authorities — pastors, fathers, presidents, kings — are under the authority of Scripture. Taylor illustrates this with a personal story of sharing the gospel with a military commander by explaining that his submission to authority was rooted in biblical obedience.

**The Bible's Transformational Power:**
Taylor closes with the example of an 88-year-old man he was to eulogize: saved as a child through a bus ministry, he raised six children in the faith and left behind 21 grandchildren and 32 great-grandchildren. Taylor held this up as a living testimony to what believing and living by the Bible can produce across generations.

**Closing Prayer:**
Taylor prayed for continued clarity and conviction as the class moves deeper into the core issue of the King James Bible in the English language, asking God to help the congregation live the book as they ought.
