## Summary
Tim Bish explores artificial intelligence from a biblical perspective, emphasizing that while AI is a powerful tool, it requires wisdom and discernment to use well. He addresses both the legitimate benefits of AI and the serious dangers—including misinformation, emotional dependency, and misuse—while calling believers to teach the next generation to think critically and ground truth in God's Word.

## Scripture
Proverbs 4:7 — "And with all thy getting, get understanding."

## Key Themes
- Wisdom vs. knowledge in the age of technology
- Stewardship of tools and technology
- AI as morally neutral but requiring biblical discernment
- Dangers: misinformation, scams, and emotional dependency
- Parental responsibility in protecting children from AI misuse
- Teaching the next generation to think and know truth
- The importance of Scripture as the final authority

## Notes

### Understanding AI Basics
- AI is "really fancy math"—not the Antichrist or something supernatural
- Current AI (like ChatGPT 5.2) operates at roughly a 12-year-old intelligence level
- AI has been trained on 15+ trillion tokens of information—equivalent to 40 billion pages of text stacked between Earth and the moon three times
- Every tool requires wisdom to use it well; stewardship applies to technology

### The Dangers of AI
- **Misinformation**: AI can confidently present false information and will "lie right to your face"
- **Scams**: AI-generated voice and video scams are increasing, especially targeting elderly people and those with emotional connections (grandparent scams)
- **Emotional Dependency**: Teenagers are developing unhealthy dependencies on AI chatbots; there are open lawsuits regarding teen suicides linked to extended AI use
- **Privacy Concerns**: Free versions of AI tools use your data to train future models; paid versions may still store information
- **Medical/Theological Misuse**: AI mixed with contradictory information from 40+ billion pages of text can give dangerous medical or theological advice

### How to Spot AI-Generated Content
- **Images**: Check hands (still often have wrong number of fingers), eyes (look "creepy" or misaligned), skin (often missing pores), lighting (doesn't match background)
- **Video**: Blinking at odd times, head movement when blinking, inconsistent lighting between face and background
- **Text**: Excessive use of M-dashes (long dashes), overly long words and sentences (AI is verbose), unnatural word choices
- **General**: The "uncanny valley"—if something looks or feels wrong, it probably is

### Parenting in the AI Age
- Invade your kids' privacy: "If they live in your house and you pay all their bills, invade their privacy"
- Limit access and monitor their AI usage carefully
- Don't share full names, social security numbers, passwords, or sensitive information with any AI tool
- Know which AI tools are dangerous: avoid Character AI (designed for dependency) and Grok (uncensored, has generated millions of CSAM images)
- Be aware of Meta AI's privacy violations through Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp
- Teach kids that they make the final decisions, not the algorithm

### Proper Use of AI
- Use AI for legitimate productivity: translation, research, image generation for legitimate purposes, language learning
- Never substitute AI for prayer, Bible study, or the Holy Spirit's guidance
- Always fact-check AI output, especially for health or theological matters
- If AI contradicts Scripture, AI is wrong
- Use AI to look up context (e.g., "Ur of the Chaldees") but always return to God's Word as the authority

### Teaching the Next Generation
- Gen Z is the first generation in 150 years to be "dumber than the generation before it" due to over-reliance on technology
- Parents must teach kids to **think** rather than outsource thinking to AI
- Teach them where truth comes from: ground everything in Scripture
- Truth is no longer objective in culture ("my truth" mentality), but Christians must teach biblical truth
- Parents don't stop their responsibility when kids move out; ongoing discipleship matters

### The Bigger Picture
- AI is the fastest-growing tool in human history and isn't going away
- Some Christians have historically resisted technology (like livestreaming before COVID), but we can't ignore AI
- Every job, including skilled trades, will increasingly involve AI
- Christians have an opportunity and responsibility to train the next generation how to use AI safely and wisely
- The younger generation (Gen Alpha) will be "AI native" but needs teaching to think independently
