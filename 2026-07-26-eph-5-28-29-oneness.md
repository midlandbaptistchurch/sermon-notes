## Summary
This sermon examines Ephesians 5:28-29 with emphasis on the concept of "oneness" both in marriage and in the believer's relationship with Christ. Pastor Simpson teaches that just as husbands are to love and care for their wives as their own bodies (something done instinctively), believers are members of Christ's body—of His flesh and bones. This oneness requires lowliness, meekness, long-suffering, forbearing, and endeavoring, but reflects the eternal relationship believers have with Christ that transcends temporal earthly relationships.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:28-30 — "So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself. For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church: For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones."

## Key Themes
- **Oneness in Marriage and with Christ**: The fundamental concept is that marriage relationships should mirror the oneness believers have with Christ as members of His body
- **Natural Self-Care as Object Lesson**: Men naturally nourish and cherish their own sin-cursed flesh without being commanded; this illustrates how they should care for their wives
- **Members of His Body**: Believers are not merely servants or stewards but are described as being of Christ's flesh and bones, indicating profound unity
- **Prisoner of the Lord**: Paul's self-description as a prisoner indicates willing choice and commitment, not bondage—a model for both marriage and relationship with Christ
- **Requirements for Oneness**: Achieving oneness requires lowliness, meekness, long-suffering, forbearing one another in love, and endeavoring to keep unity

## Notes

### Context of the Passage
- Paul addresses three relationships in Ephesians 5:22-33 that picture the body of Christ
- The husband-wife relationship receives the most attention
- This teaching emphasizes the doctrinal aspect of relationship with Christ, not just marriage counseling
- The passage should be studied with balance, not overemphasizing one aspect while under-emphasizing others

### Previous Study Progress
- Multiple weeks spent on headship: Christ as head, not just Savior, King, or Lord
- Studied the headship chain: God the Father is head of Christ, Christ is head of man, man is head of woman
- Covered Ephesians 5:25: husbands loving wives as Christ loved the church
- Examined verses 26-27 regarding presentation before God—being holy and unblameable
- Discussed glorification and what the rapture will mean for the Trinity (relief that believers are no longer sin-cursed)

### The Concept of Oneness (verse 28)
- "So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies"
- Oneness is not commonly contemplated in marriage but is critical
- Reference back to Ephesians 4:4-6: seven "ones" (one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father)
- God desires oneness in families, local church bodies, and the universal church
- Foundational oneness begins with doctrine, truth, and teaching—not personality or preferences
- Scripture states: "we can do nothing against the truth, but for the truth"

### Attributes Required for Oneness (Ephesians 4:1-3)
- Paul identifies himself as "prisoner of the Lord"—a willing choice, not bondage
- He chose to be prisoner because his service to the Lord was worth it
- Required attributes for oneness:
  - Lowliness
  - Meekness
  - Long-suffering
  - Forbearing one another in love
  - Endeavoring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace
- These are not "vacation words"—oneness requires effort and sacrifice
- Oneness is a place of vulnerability where you relinquish control and allow another to meet your needs

### The Object Lesson (verse 29)
- "For no man ever yet hated his own flesh, but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church"
- No man hates his own sin-cursed flesh—we naturally care for ourselves
- This care is instinctive: feeding, bathing, resting, clothing ourselves
- Lost and saved people alike take care of themselves without needing biblical instruction
- God uses this natural self-care as the pattern for how husbands should care for wives
- The comparison: "even as the Lord the church"—Christ cares for us as we naturally care for ourselves
- Christ is not sin-cursed; He is all about us like we are all about ourselves
- This realization should motivate believers to have the mind of Christ and see others as He does

### Members of His Body (verse 30)
- "For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones"
- This is a profound statement about believers' relationship with Christ
- Christ had a flesh and bone body on earth and during His resurrection appearances
- Now believers individually and collectively become His body, bones, and flesh
- The Lord wants husbands to nourish and cherish wives as much as they naturally do themselves
- Beyond that, God wants the marriage relationship to have the same oneness believers should have with Him
- One relationship should mirror the other

### Levels of Understanding
- Most saved people are members of Christ's body (baptized into the body per 1 Corinthians 12)
- However, many don't recognize it or see it
- Comparison to marriages where people share a name and house but have no oneness or relationship
- Similarly, people may claim to be Christian but lack true relationship and oneness with Christ
- Accepting Christ as head and desiring oneness allows believers to see themselves as bone of His bone and flesh of His flesh

### Marriage as Temporal Picture of Eternal Reality
- Verse 31 quotes Genesis: "For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh"
- The oneness in marriage is temporal, much smaller than eternal oneness with Christ
- All relationships described in this passage (parent-child, master-servant, husband-wife) are temporal
- In heaven, believers will be as angels—no longer married, no longer parents
- These temporal relationships should be conducted in light of and within the cycle of Christ's headship
- The eternal purpose is to fulfill God's will, not maintain earthly relationships

### Glory God Receives
- Certain things done in life bring glory to God that wouldn't be received otherwise
- If a person doesn't study to show themselves approved, witness, remain faithful, or maintain proper relationships, God doesn't receive that specific glory
- This is why there's emphasis on being perfect (complete/mature)
- Understanding oneness brings a glory to God that He doesn't get very often
- These are "deeper things of God" that require work to see and understand

### Application
- People can have good marriages by applying biblical principles without being spiritual
- But they cannot bring glory to God without understanding why they do what they do
- Lost people can have good relationships but cannot bring glory to God
- What brings glory is not just actions but understanding and knowing the purpose
- Believers should desire that oneness with Christ and see themselves clearly as flesh and bones of their Savior and head
- This oneness should then be reflected in the marriage relationship
