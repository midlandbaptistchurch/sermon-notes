## Summary
In Part 4 of his ongoing Sunday School series on the King James Bible, Hugh Taylor continues reviewing the precursors of the Bible issue and introduces the second major sub-point: the interactive process between God and man in producing Scripture. Drawing on passages from 2 Timothy, 2 Peter, Psalms, Deuteronomy, and Ephesians, he emphasizes that God sovereignly worked through sinful yet willing men to inspire and preserve His pure Word, and that believers today carry an equally important responsibility to rightly use that Word in their lives.

## Scripture
- **2 Timothy 3:16** — The foundational statement that all Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness.
- **2 Peter 1:21** — Holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Ghost, establishing the principle of divine authorship through human instruments.
- **Psalm 12:6–7** — God's promise to preserve His pure words from this generation forever.
- **Deuteronomy 30:19** — God sets before man life and death, blessing and cursing, and urges mankind to choose life.
- **Joshua 24:15** — Joshua challenges Israel to choose whom they will serve, declaring for himself and his house they will serve the Lord.
- **Romans 6:16** — To whom you yield yourself servants to obey, his servants you are.
- **1 Corinthians 10:23** — All things are lawful, but not all things are expedient or edifying.
- **1 Corinthians 1:27** — God has chosen the foolish and weak things of the world to confound the wise and mighty.
- **Ephesians 2:10** — We are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works which God foreordained.

## Key Themes
- The Bible is the product of an interactive process between God and man
- God is the author; holy men were the human instruments moved by the Holy Spirit
- God's sovereignty in preserving His Word despite the corruptive efforts of Satan and sinful men
- The reality and significance of human free will and personal choice
- The strategic will of God (for all creation) versus the tactical will of God (for each individual)
- Choices carry real consequences — life and death, blessing and cursing
- Believers today bear a responsibility to use the right Bible, just as those holy men bore a responsibility to record it
- Integrity is central to the men, manuscripts, and message of the King James Bible

## Notes
**Review of Previous Sessions**
- The study is structured in two parts: (1) Precursors of the King James Bible issue and (2) the men, manuscripts, and message of the KJB.
- Under the precursors, five things were previously covered regarding the backdrop of the Bible issue:
  1. Satan wants to complicate, corrupt, and confuse so believers lose confidence in having God's Word.
  2. God wants to make it simple and straightforward — the simplicity that is in Christ.
  3. The three main reasons for believing the KJB (men, manuscripts, message) all relate to integrity.
  4. The Bible issue is fundamentally a pursuit of truth between God and man.
  5. Faith is required to believe and understand the King James Bible issue.

**The Interactive Process Between God and Man (New Sub-Point)**
- The Bible was authored by God and written by men of His choosing (2 Timothy 3:16; 2 Peter 1:21).
- Any undertaking of God that involves men is, from a human perspective, a risky proposition — yet God knows the outcome and chooses to use His creation.
- God operates on two levels of will:
  - *Strategic will* — His overarching plan for all creation, angels and mankind, designed to bring glory to Him throughout the ages.
  - *Tactical will* — His personal, individual plan for each believer within the larger strategy.
- God has chosen to indwell believers by His Spirit, empowering obedience, yet never removing free will.

**Free Will and the Significance of Choices**
- God gave mankind, as He gave Lucifer and the angels, a free will.
- Our choices are a matter of life and death (Deuteronomy 30:19).
- God consistently encourages people to choose life — to choose the right path, the genuine article, not a distorted facsimile.
- Joshua 24:15 illustrates the power of individual and household choice: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord."
- Under grace (1 Corinthians 10:23), all things may be lawful but not all things are expedient — believers are still responsible to make wise, God-honoring choices.
- Romans 6:16 — we become servants of whatever we yield ourselves to obey, whether sin or righteousness.

**God's Sovereignty Over Sinful Men**
- God reserves the right to assert His sovereignty and superintend even the works of sinful men when He deems it necessary (2 Peter 1:21).
- He promised to preserve His pure words forever (Psalm 12:6–7) — a promise that stands despite Satan's and man's corruptive efforts.
- Satan's strategy has always been to twist and distort God's words (as with Eve in Genesis 3), producing perverted versions — this principle applies directly to Bible versions.
- God chose the foolish and weak things to confound the wise (1 Corinthians 1:27).

**Our Responsibility to Use God's Word**
- Ephesians 2:10 — we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works foreordained by God.
- Just as God worked interactively with holy men to produce Scripture, He works interactively with believers today to employ it.
- Using the right Bible matters: a believer must have the right sword to do what they are supposed to do — a wooden or nerf sword will not cut.
- Holding forth the Word of truth to the world is just as important as the original recording of it — making it and not using it defeats the purpose.
