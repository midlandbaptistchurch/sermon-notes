## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson continues the Guidelines for Grace series by examining biblical perfection, emphasizing that perfection in Scripture means completeness and maturity rather than sinlessness. Using 2 Corinthians 13:11 as the anchor text, he explains that being perfect involves being of good comfort, having one mind with other believers, and living in peace. The sermon stresses that understanding God's perfect will requires proving what is good and acceptable through Scripture, and that believers should seek wisdom and maturity in their Christian walk.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 13:11 — "Finally, brethren, farewell. Be perfect. Be of good comfort. Be of one mind. Live in peace. And the God of love and peace shall be with you."

## Key Themes
- **Biblical Perfection Means Completeness**: Perfection in Scripture refers to maturity, completeness, and understanding, not sinless perfection in a sin-cursed body.
- **Comfort Brings Security**: To be of good comfort means to provide security, support, and assurance to others while also receiving it from the body of Christ.
- **Unity in Doctrine**: Being of one mind refers to doctrinal unity and grace doctrine, not uniformity in personality or preferences.
- **Proving God's Will**: God's perfect will is proven through evaluating whether choices are good, acceptable, and complete according to Scripture, with liberty in many decisions.
- **Speaking Wisdom Among the Perfect**: Maturity allows believers to receive and understand deeper wisdom beyond spiritual milk.

## Notes

### Understanding Biblical Perfection (2 Corinthians 13:11)
- The word "perfect" must be understood in its biblical context, not modern cultural definitions
- Requires retraining the mind to accept what the Bible means by "be perfect"
- Perfection under grace is attainable when properly understood
- The passage lists several components of perfection: being perfect, being of good comfort, being of one mind, living in peace

### Be of Good Comfort
- Comfort relates to security, support, and assurance
- 2 Timothy 2:17 connects comfort with being established
- 1 Thessalonians 5:11: "Wherefore comfort yourselves together and edify one another, even as also you do"
- Requires both giving comfort to others and receiving it from others
- Ministers can get out of balance by only giving comfort without receiving it
- The cycle of ministry includes both giving and receiving within the body of Christ

### Be of One Mind
- Does not require same personality, preferences, or rooting for the same team
- Does not require same gifts and abilities
- Primarily deals with doctrine and grace doctrine
- Means fellowship in the mystery
- Involves understanding forgiveness under grace versus law
- Example given: Teaching youth about forgiveness using Colossians 2:13 (forgiven all trespasses) versus 1 John 1:9 (confessing sins in tribulation context)
- Matthew 24:13 ("he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved") applies to the tribulation period, not losing salvation

### Live in Peace
- Peace within ourselves and within the body of Christ
- Distinction between the peace of God and the God of peace (referenced from Philippians)
- Want complete package: peace with others, inward peace, comfort that brings peace from God
- Outward circumstances should not have greater influence than the peace of God
- Eternal perspective: bad things in this life won't last long and will be worked out
- Regarding difficult saved people: "They gonna get glorified one day and they won't be stupid no more"

### Salvation is Complete (CRIBS/BRICS Acrostic)
- **C - Circumcision** made without hands (Colossians 2:11): God separated our sin-cursed body from our soul and spirit
- **R - Regeneration** (Titus 3:5): "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy, he saved us by the washing of regeneration and the renewing of the Holy Ghost"
- **I - Indwelling** of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19-20): "Your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost which is in you"
- **B - Baptism** (spiritual) (1 Corinthians 12:13): "For by one spirit, we are all baptized into one body"
- **S - Sealing** of the Spirit (Ephesians 1:13): Sealed until the day of redemption
  - The Holy Spirit stays with the body until the rapture
  - Illustration: Even if someone fell off a boat, was eaten by a shark, and spread throughout the ocean, the Holy Spirit knows where every particle is and will bring them back together at the rapture
  - Ephesians 4:30: "Grieve not the Holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption"

### The Body of Christ vs. Universal Church Debate
- In Bible college, there was argument against the term "universal church" because of association with Catholicism
- Strong emphasis on local church led some to reject the concept of the body of Christ
- Pastor Mark's observation: 1 Corinthians 12 clearly teaches that everyone saved is part of the body of Christ
- Local churches are necessary for practical reasons (can't attend a church in Africa if living in Midland)
- Both the body of Christ and local church are biblical concepts

### Proving the Perfect Will of God (Romans 12:2)
- "Be not conformed to this world but be transformed by the renewing of your mind that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God"
- Traditional teaching: perfect will of God vs. permissive will of God (one specific plan you might miss)
- Under grace: there is liberty in God's perfect will
- Not just about location or one exact decision
- Should not sit and do nothing while "waiting for God to show exactly what to do"
- Advice from old country preacher: "Son, just bloom where you planted"
- Proving involves three aspects: good, acceptable, and perfect
  - Good: Does it align with Scripture?
  - Acceptable: Will it stand up at the judgment seat?
  - Perfect: Are we being complete, mature, and wise in our decisions?
- Example: Church maintenance (fixing monitor, removing dead mouse) vs. teaching Bible study—both can be God's will, but some things are better than others (gold, silver, precious stones vs. wood, hay, stubble)

### Perfection and Unity (1 Corinthians 1:10)
- "Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment"
- Perfectly joined together in the same mind and judgment
- Refers to biblical doctrinal things, not preferences or personality
- Liberty exists, but oneness under Christ and under the mystery is required
- This verse from the beginning of 1 Corinthians connects to 2 Corinthians 13:11 conclusion

### Speaking Wisdom Among the Perfect (1 Corinthians 2:5-7)
- "That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God. Howbeit we speak wisdom among them that are perfect, yet not the wisdom of this world, nor of the princes of this world that come to nought; but we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, even the hidden wisdom which God ordained before the world unto our glory"
- Perfect = those with understanding and maturity
- Contrasted with babes in Christ and carnal Christians (1 Corinthians 3:1-2)
- "I, brethren, could not speak unto you as unto spiritual, but as unto carnal, even as unto babes in Christ. I have fed you with milk, and not with meat"
- Some believers never desire anything deeper than milk
- Pastor Payne gives out deeper truth to move people beyond being babes
- Example: Teaching youth group required backing up to basics (Romans road, simple gospel) because of visitors and varied spiritual maturity
- Distinction between teaching those who can receive wisdom versus those needing basics
- Challenge to ask questions rather than assuming everyone else knows the answers
- Asking questions helps achieve perfection

### Practical Examples from Teaching Ministry
- Teaching teenagers about sin and forgiveness after salvation
- Showed Colossians 2:13: "having forgiven you all trespasses"
- Question: If forgiven, do you have to ask forgiveness?
- Proper response to sin: repentance and godly sorrow (Romans 6, 2 Corinthians)
- Confessing sin in 1 John 1:9 is for tribulation period, not church age believers
- Young man returned with questions, showing engagement with the teaching
- One young lady got saved; one young man (Catholic background) asked many questions over 20 minutes, processing the gospel
- Importance of watching people's reactions to gauge understanding
- The work of bringing people to completeness/perfection in understanding
