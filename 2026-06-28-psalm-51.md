## Summary
Pat McCloskey teaches through Psalm 51, examining David's cry for mercy after his sin with Bathsheba and murder of Uriah. Using David's life as both positive example (killing bears, lions, and Goliath) and negative example (adultery, deception, murder, poor husband and father), McCloskey emphasizes that true repentance requires personal responsibility without blaming others. David's prayer shows he wanted complete cleansing and restoration of joy, not just forgiveness of consequences.

## Scripture
Psalm 51:1-12 — David's prayer for mercy and cleansing after Nathan confronted him about his sin with Bathsheba. David pleads for God to have mercy, wash him thoroughly, create a clean heart, and restore the joy of his salvation.

## Key Themes
- **Personal Responsibility for Sin**: David didn't blame circumstances or others but confessed "against thee, and thee only, have I sinned" and took full ownership of his wrongdoing
- **God's Mercy Over Justice**: David appealed to God's loving kindness and tender mercies rather than claiming he deserved forgiveness based on his previous good deeds
- **True Cleansing Beyond Appearance**: God wants truth in the inner parts, not just outward religion or rituals—integrity must be internal, not just external appearances
- **Restoration Not Loss of Salvation**: Verse 12 says "restore unto me the joy of my salvation," indicating David hadn't lost his salvation but had lost the joy of it

## Notes

### David's Life: Positive and Negative
- As a shepherd boy, David was fearless—killed bears and lions to protect his sheep, trusting God rather than his own abilities
- Killed Goliath when others were intimidated; told Goliath "this day shall the Lord deliver thee into my hand"
- Was an awesome leader who helped unite the 12 tribes of Israel and led victories over Moabites and others
- However, David was a lousy husband with multiple wives and a woman problem
- Was a terrible father—Solomon took after his problems, Amnon violated Tamar, Absalom was prideful and tried to kill David
- Committed adultery with Bathsheba, then became deceptive trying to cover it up
- Became a murderer when he sent Uriah to the front lines to be killed
- Sin caused David to lose his ability to reason—each deception led deeper into more sin

### The Confrontation and Confession
- Nathan the prophet confronted David about his sin (2 Samuel 11-12)
- David had been living in denial, with no one approaching him about his wrongdoing for a time
- Psalm 51 shows David's broken response after being confronted
- David didn't ask for justice but for mercy

### David's Prayer in Psalm 51
- **Verse 1**: "Have mercy upon me, O God, according to thy loving kindness...blot out my transgressions"
  - David wanted sin not just forgiven but completely gone/blotted out
  - He didn't blame Bathsheba, Uriah, or circumstances—took full responsibility
  - His appeal wasn't based on his own worth but on God's character
- **Verse 2**: "Wash me thoroughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin"
  - David didn't say "if it wasn't for others, I wouldn't be in this position"
  - He owned his sin completely
- **Verse 3**: "For I acknowledge my transgressions: and my sin is ever before me"
  - True confession—didn't say "I made a mistake" but "I have sinned"
- **Verse 4**: "Against thee, and thee only, have I sinned"
  - Though David sinned against Uriah, Bathsheba, Nathan, his children and others, his primary concern was his offense against God
  - God sees everything—David thought God couldn't see but He saw it all
- **Verse 7**: "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean: wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow"
  - Hyssop was a plant with long stem and small leaves used in Middle Eastern cleansing rituals
  - Could be dipped and sprinkled—symbolic of ceremonial cleansing
- **Verse 10**: "Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me"
  - David wanted internal transformation, not just external religion
- **Verse 11**: "Cast me not away from thy presence; and take not thy holy spirit from me"
  - David feared losing the presence of God
- **Verse 12**: "Restore unto me the joy of my salvation"
  - This verse is significant because it shows David didn't lose his salvation, only the joy of it
  - Eternal life in Jesus Christ remains eternal—it cannot become non-eternal
  - We live for God not out of fear of losing salvation but out of love and desire for good testimony

### The Principle of Sin Leading to More Sin
- Sin causes people to lose their ability to reason
- Each act of deception leads deeper—adultery led to more deception, which led to murder
- People justify sin to themselves until they believe their own justifications
- Sometimes people say "I'm sorry" for the situation rather than truly being sorry for what they did

### Application: Drawing Near to God
- Don't blame government, circumstances, or other people for your sin
- Need to confess specifically to God without making excuses
- Young people need to trust their parents who have "been around the block"
- Find people with integrity to associate with—not just people who look good outwardly
- McCloskey's personal testimony: felt distance from God while serving as Christian school administrator, woke up in middle of night feeling far from God, got on his knees and confessed, experienced refreshing when he lost himself so Christ could be more predominant

### The Importance of Internal vs. External Religion
- God wants more than outward religion—He wants truth within a sincere heart
- Matters more than rituals or appearances
- McCloskey's observation about Midland Baptist: people actually love being there, love Jesus, realize they're sinners saved by grace
- Joy comes from knowing we're God's workmanship created in Christ Jesus—the work is ongoing
- Not just an outward thing but something internal where people realize they're nobody apart from Christ

### Warning About Works-Based Salvation
- Story of family member who believed good works contribute to salvation
- Said "God surely sees the good things I do" and those contribute to being with Jesus
- McCloskey responded: "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works" (Ephesians 2:8-9)
- You can't earn a gift
- True repentance begins when we come to God empty-handed, trusting only in His grace
- We can't clean ourselves up—God washes away the sin

### Daily Practice
- McCloskey's testimony: Every night before bed says "God, I've been wrong. Have mercy on me. Thank you for your mercies. Forgive me of my sin"
- Need to come to God regularly, confess, walk with Him, stand before Him
- Can walk away with a clean heart when we confess
