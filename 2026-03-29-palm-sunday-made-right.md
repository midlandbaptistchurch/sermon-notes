## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne explores the historical and spiritual significance of Palm Sunday, examining why Jesus entered Jerusalem on a donkey rather than a white horse as many expected. The sermon reveals how the people's misplaced expectations—looking for political salvation rather than spiritual redemption—led to rejection of Christ. Payne highlights how Palm Sunday will ultimately be fulfilled when believers gather around God's throne in heaven.

## Scripture
Luke 19:28-43 — Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem, with attention to the contrast between the people's expectations and Jesus's true mission.

## Key Themes
- Messianic prophecy and Old Testament fulfillment
- Unmet expectations and faith tested
- The difference between political and spiritual salvation
- God's agenda versus human expectations
- Christ as the Passover Lamb
- God's grief over a people who reject Him
- True worship and praise of Christ

## Notes

### The Antiquity of Palm Sunday
- Jesus rode a young donkey (colt) into Jerusalem, fulfilling Zechariah 9:9: "Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion; shout, O daughter of Jerusalem. Behold, your King comes to you, lowly and riding on a donkey"
- The people lined the streets, laying their coats and palm branches on the road—a "red carpet treatment" for their expected Messiah
- For 2,000 years the church has called this day Palm Sunday, marking the beginning of Christ's final week

### The People's Expectations
- The crowd expected Christ to be a political deliverer, freeing them from Roman oppression
- They cried "Hosanna! Hosanna!" meaning "Save us now!" but they meant political salvation, not spiritual
- Their understanding was limited to what they could see: miracles, signs, and wonders
- The apostles and people misunderstood Christ's primary agenda—to come as a sacrificial lamb, not a military king

### Christ's True Mission
- Jesus came to free people from the oppression of sin, not from political government
- His first coming was characterized by humility, meekness, and sacrifice (lowly, riding a donkey)
- He presented Himself to the Father as a Passover Lamb worthy to be slain for humanity's sins
- His second coming will be different—He will return on a white horse as Judge and King (Revelation 19:11)

### The Consequences of Rejection
- Jesus wept over Jerusalem, grieving that they did not understand "the time of their visitation"
- He prophesied judgment: "The days will come upon you when your enemies will build an embankment about you and surround you on all sides...and dash you and your children within you to the ground"
- This prophecy was fulfilled in 70 A.D. when Roman forces destroyed Jerusalem, killing over one million Jews and leaving not one stone upon another

### Five Lessons for Believers Today
1. **God uses His people in service.** The Lord was pleased to use disciples to retrieve the donkey. He invites us to participate in advancing His kingdom.
2. **Christ is worthy of praise regardless of circumstances.** We should praise Him not only when He answers prayers as expected, but also when things don't go our way.
3. **Don't be deterred by opposition.** The Pharisees opposed Jesus's praise, but opposition from others should never deter our faith and witness.
4. **God weeps for the lost.** Jesus wept for those who rejected Him. Believers should share His compassion for the unsaved.
5. **The day of visitation is critical.** When God reveals Himself and Christ to us, our response determines our eternity. Rejection brings judgment.

### The Achievement: Palm Sunday Fulfilled
- In Revelation 7, John sees a great multitude holding palm branches, praising God around His throne in heaven
- The true fulfillment of Palm Sunday will occur when all of God's redeemed people gather in heaven, giving Christ the worship and honor rightfully due to Him
- What the earthly Jerusalem rejected, heaven will celebrate eternally
