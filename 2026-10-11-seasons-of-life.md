## Summary
Pastor Andrew Watt encourages believers not to grow weary in doing right despite difficult seasons of life. Using the principle of reaping and sowing from Galatians 6, he reminds the church that God is not mocked—we will reap what we sow. Though we all experience different seasons in soul winning, marriage, parenting, and work, we must remain faithful because "in due season we shall reap if we faint not." The sermon illustrates how different people experience the same life stages differently, yet God remains constant and the ultimate harvest is worth the present struggle.

## Scripture
Galatians 6:7-9 — "Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting. And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."

## Key Themes
- **Reaping and Sowing**: The biblical principle that we reap what we sow applies to every area of life—spiritual, relational, and practical
- **Seasons of Life**: Just as there are physical seasons, we experience different seasons in soul winning, marriage, parenting, work, and ministry
- **Perseverance in Well-Doing**: The encouragement not to grow tired of doing the right thing, even when it seems exhausting or unfruitful
- **God's Unchanging Nature**: Though seasons change, God remains constant—He is "the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever"
- **The Worth of Due Season**: The ultimate harvest, whether temporal or eternal, makes present suffering worthwhile

## Notes

### Introduction
- Speaking on the Sunday night before missions revival
- Wanted to keep message simple and practical rather than missions-focused
- Encouraged church to attend missions revival—asking people to go from comfortable to comfortable is not too much when missionaries go from comfortable to uncomfortable

### The Principle of Reaping and Sowing (Galatians 6:7-9)
- God is not mocked—whatsoever a man sows, he will reap
- Sowing to the flesh reaps corruption; sowing to the Spirit reaps life everlasting
- "Let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not"
- Context: Paul addressing the Galatian church about restoring those overtaken in a fault
- The question: Do you ever get tired of doing the right thing?
- Sometimes it seems others are getting ahead by doing wrong while you struggle doing right

### Why We Grow Weary (2 Thessalonians 3:11-13)
- Paul addresses people in the church who are disorderly, not working, being busybodies
- Commands them to work quietly and eat their own bread
- But to the faithful: "Be not weary in well doing"
- We grow weary when we see others around us not doing right
- America's strength comes from biblical principles; weakness comes from standing against God's Word
- Government tries to stand against reaping/sowing principle (welfare, criminal justice reform)

### God's Role in Seasons (Daniel 2:20-21)
- Daniel blessed God who "changeth the times and the seasons"
- God removes and sets up kings, gives wisdom to the wise
- The same God who makes physical seasons hears our prayers
- We are not alone in our difficult seasons

### God's Unchanging Nature
- Hebrews 13:8: "Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever"
- Malachi 3:6: "I am the LORD, I change not"
- Though seasons change and God deals differently in different dispensations, God Himself does not change
- God is righteous, just, and not mocked

### Seasons in Soul Winning (1 Corinthians 3:6-8)
- Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase
- Neither planter nor waterer is anything—God gives the increase
- Each receives reward according to his own labor
- Different seasons: tilling (preparing hard hearts), planting, watering, harvesting
- Young people today often have hardened hearts (rejecting God) or numb hearts (going through motions with parents)
- We cannot predict what season someone's heart is in
- Don't grow weary in any season of gospel work—you don't know when someone is ready for harvest
- Illustration: Sometimes first door knocked is ready to hear gospel; other times many doors slammed before harvest

### Seasons of Marriage
- Five seasons identified:
  1. **Honeymoon phase** - Vacation-like, not juggling all responsibilities yet
  2. **Newlywed phase** - Learning to live together, manage finances, adjust to each other's habits
  3. **Established phase** - Perceived stability, career, home ownership, children
  4. **Empty nester** - Children grown and gone, adapting to life together again
  5. **Aged/Grandparents** - Blessed with grandchildren, investing in next generations
- Colossians 3:19: "Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them"
- This verse wouldn't be needed on honeymoon—Paul is addressing a different season
- Pastor's two biggest difficulties: first getting married, then having children
- Different people experience same seasons completely differently
- Importance of investing in marriage even when raising children, because children will eventually leave
- "For better or for worse, till death do you part"

### Seasons of Parenting
- Proverbs 22:6: "Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it"
- Proverbs 19:18: "Chasten thy son while there is hope, and let not thy soul spare for his crying"
- There is a season when there is more hope to win a child's heart
- Six seasons identified:
  1. **Newborn season** - Sleep deprivation, can't communicate except screaming (Pastor's least favorite)
  2. **Toddler season** - Adventures, unpredictable, exhausting but fun (Pastor's favorite); includes potty training
  3. **Kid/school age** - Critical thinking developing, more responsibility
  4. **Teenage season** - Can be season of great growth and opportunity, not necessarily rebellion
  5. **College age** - Old enough to vote but still need parental advice
  6. **Adult children** - Fully grown offspring
- Encouragement from older parents: "They won't go to college in diapers"
- Sleep training example: wife trained all three children so she could sleep
- Different seasons: rearing, instilling responsibility, establishing relationship
- Teenagers don't have to be "sin-bound lost cause"—can be "season of great opportunity"
- Read "Age of Opportunity" before dismissing all teens as difficult

### Seasons of Work
- First job (age 14-18)
- College/part-time work
- Post-college job with credentials
- Advancement/job security
- Retirement
- Pastor's favorite job: bank teller (liked counting money, meeting people, days went fast)
- Different seasons have different benefits (work-life balance vs. salary)

### Other Seasons Mentioned
- Health seasons
- Ministry seasons (easy days and difficult days as youth pastor)
- Friendships and relationships

### The Challenge of Different Experiences
- Two people can be in same season geographically and physically but completely different seasons spiritually/emotionally
- Same age, married same day, had kids same time—still different seasons
- Makes getting counsel difficult when others had completely different experiences
- Example: parents whose children all slept well vs. parents with children who won't sleep
- Can feel lonely when your season is drastically different from others'

### The Worth of Due Season

#### Philippians 1:23
- Paul: "Having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better"
- Easier to want heaven during seasons of struggle
- No teenager wants rapture day before 16th birthday/driver's license
- No bride wants rapture night before wedding
- But in pain, suffering, heartache—we look to the clouds

#### Romans 8:18-19
- "For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us"
- We don't know what due season will fully look like
- Best we can imagine in our mortal, sin-cursed minds cannot compare
- Due season is well worth the struggle

### The Uncertainty of Harvest Timing
- We don't know when due season will come
- May not be on earth—may be when "corruptible puts on incorruptible"
- May be face-to-face with Jesus Christ
- Illustration: Uncle (a pastor) bought shoes for boy at camp whose shoes stunk badly (2009)
- Boy returned 17 years later (2026) to ask uncle to perform his wedding
- Said he never forgot the kindness of buying him shoes so he wouldn't be bullied
- We want instant gratification—plant seed, expect immediate harvest
- God gives increase in His timing, sometimes decades later
- We don't know hardship others endure between seed and harvest

### Application
- Be faithful and not weary in well-doing
- Look forward to due season when we see the Lord
- Give our life and work to Him for His glory
- Do not faint or quit in difficult seasons
