## Summary
Brother Mark discusses the need for establishing foundational teaching on right division for newer members who have been attending for 6 months to several years without formal grounding in dispensational Bible study. He explores the difficulty of determining what core doctrines to include and how to structure such a class. The discussion leads into examining headship in 1 Corinthians 11, the concept of ordinances versus memorials, and the unique position of Christ's submission to God the Father as an example for the body of Christ.

## Scripture
**1 Corinthians 11:2-16** — Paul addresses headship, explaining that the head of every man is Christ, the head of woman is man, and the head of Christ is God, using the context of head coverings and customs to teach about authority structures that picture the relationship between Christ and the body.

**Matthew 22:23-30** — Jesus answers the Sadducees' question about marriage in the resurrection, explaining that those resurrected will be like the angels in heaven, neither marrying nor being given in marriage.

**Ephesians 5:22-33** — The husband-wife relationship pictures Christ and the church, with husbands called to love their wives as Christ loved the church, and wives called to submit as the church submits to Christ.

## Key Themes
- **Foundational Teaching Need**: Many people attending for extended periods have never received formal grounding in right division and dispensational Bible study
- **Ordinances as Memorials**: The word "ordinance" in Scripture refers to something ordained as a memorial (Exodus 12:14, 13:9-10), not necessarily a church requirement for today
- **Headship Structure**: The head of every man is Christ, the head of woman is man, and the head of Christ is God—establishing a pattern of authority and submission
- **Christ's Ongoing Submission**: Christ remains seated at the right hand of the Father (not on the throne) as an example of submission to headship throughout the church age
- **Angels as Spectators**: Angels learn about the mystery of the body of Christ by watching believers fulfill their roles, including women demonstrating submission
- **Transitional Teaching**: First Corinthians 11 contains both transitional customs from the law and permanent doctrinal truths about headship
- **Marriage in Heaven**: There will be no marriage in the resurrection, as believers will be like the angels who neither marry nor are given in marriage

### Understanding "Ordinance" in Scripture
- Most Baptists claim two "church ordinances": baptism and communion/Lord's Supper
- Searching Scripture reveals "ordinance" appears 44 times in Old Testament, only 9 times in New Testament
- **Exodus 12:14**: "And this day shall be unto you for a memorial; and ye shall keep it a feast to the Lord throughout your generations; ye shall keep it a feast by an ordinance for ever"
- **Exodus 13:9-10**: Memorial and ordinance used together—ordinance means something ordained as a memorial
- **1 Corinthians 11:2**: "Now I praise you, brethren, that ye remember me in all things, and keep the ordinances, as I delivered them to you"
- An ordinance is something ordained to serve as a memorial, not necessarily a permanent church requirement

### Headship in 1 Corinthians 11
- **Verse 3**: "But I would have you know, that the head of every man is Christ; and the head of the woman is the man; and the head of Christ is God"
- This passage is difficult but centers on the doctrine of headship
- The first teaching after mentioning ordinances is about headship and different roles

### Marriage as Picture of Christ and Church (Ephesians 5)
- Wives in pink highlights, husbands in blue, references to church/body in gold
- The husband-wife relationship exists to picture Christ and the body
- Man as head, wife as submissive pictures Christ as head of body and members in subjection to Him
- **Privilege for women**: Taking on the submissive role allows a woman to portray something about the body of Christ she could never show before marriage
- **Privilege for men**: "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it" (Ephesians 5:25)
- Men can learn from watching a biblically submissive wife and see things to give back to the Lord that they might not see from a male perspective
- Wife's submission is tougher because she submits to an imperfect, sin-cursed husband, yet still honors that role
- When she does this despite husband's imperfections, she brings glory to God

### Christ's Example of Submission
- Christ took position of humility in Philippians 2—became man, servant, obedient unto death
- God the Father remained on the throne; turned it dark for three hours because He couldn't look upon sin
- After resurrection, Christ is seated at the **right hand** of the Father, not on the throne
- He remains in submissive position to God the Father even now throughout the church age
- This is Christ's ongoing example to us of submission to headship
- Eventually Christ will rise to come get us, then to clean house on earth, then His throne will be established on earth
- Christ has kept Himself in this submissive position for 2,000 years as an example

### Head Coverings and Customs (1 Corinthians 11:4-16)
- **Verse 4**: Every man praying or prophesying with head covered dishonors his head
- **Verse 5**: Every woman praying or prophesying with head uncovered dishonors her head
- Discussion of hair length, covering, shaving
- **Verse 10**: "For this cause ought the woman to have power [authority] on her head because of the angels"
- **Verse 14-15**: Nature teaches that long hair on a man is a shame, but on a woman it is her glory and given as a covering
- **Verse 16**: "But if any man seem to be contentious, we have no such custom, neither the churches of God"
- These were customs related to transition from law to grace, similar to Romans 14 disputes about food
- Main permanent teaching is about headship, not the specific customs

### Angels as Spectators
- Angels are spectators in the New Testament and Paul's writings
- We are made a spectacle unto them
- We are going to judge angels
- Angels learn about the mystery by watching us—it was never revealed to them before
- **1 Corinthians 6**: One day the body of Christ will judge angels
- God established the body of Christ to rule and reign in heavenly places
- Just as Jews will reign on earth, we will reign in angelic kingdoms
- Angels have been there a long time; we will arrive, go through Judgment Seat of Christ, receive assignments, and some angels will be under our authority
- Women fulfilling the submissive role may be teaching angels how authority will work in the kingdom to come

### Marriage and Angels in Heaven
- **Matthew 22:23-30**: Sadducees ask about woman married to seven brothers (following Mosaic law)
- Under the law, if a man died without children, his brother was required to marry his wife and raise up seed
- **Verse 30**: "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven"
- No marriage or family structure as we know it in heaven
- No reproduction in heaven
- Angels in heaven do not marry; angels on earth in physical form could have sexual relations (Genesis 6)
- We will be like the angels in our glorified bodies
- Unclear what distinction between male and female will exist in heaven

### Charity (Love)
- Discussion of "above all" passages in Scripture (used 14 times in New Testament)
- "Above all these things put on charity" (Colossians 3:14)
- "Above all taking the shield of faith" (Ephesians 6:16)
- **1 Corinthians 13**: Faith, hope, charity—the greatest is charity
- Most modern versions replace "charity" with "love"
- Charity is similar to love but has more to do with actions and performance of love
- Charity involves doing things with the right spirit, because you love the Lord, for the right reasons
- It's the spiritual aspect of love in action
- Difficult to fully grasp; requires continual study
- Living without charity makes our actions not count (1 Corinthians 13)
- Need to emphasize charity more in preaching and teaching

- At banquet, 500-700 people recited the Lord's Prayer together—Nathan had never experienced this
- He called his parents confused because he didn't know the prayer by memory

### Objections to Right Division
- Some call these teachings heretical, blasphemy, or claim you don't believe your Bible
- Many believe there's only one gospel and everyone in the Old Testament was looking forward to the cross
- They believe those in Abraham's bosom had to believe in Christ's death, burial, and resurrection (like the thief on the cross)
- Concept of multiple ways of salvation is controversial: tribulation saints must "endure to the end to be saved"
- Growing up, Brother Mark was taught "endure to the end" meant maintaining salvation by living right or you could lose it
- Other churches strongly oppose the idea that the twelve apostles are not our apostles
- Fundamental Baptists and charismatics divide over speaking in tongues but miss middle ground on when signs and wonders operated
- **Romans 1:16**: "For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek"
- Some missionaries focus on reaching Jews today based on "to the Jew first," but that phrase applied to a specific time period

### Hyper Dispensationalism
- Main reason someone is called "hyper dispensationalist": they don't believe in the two "church ordinances"
- Traditional claim: baptism and communion are church ordinances
- Brother Mark's wife asked where Scripture says baptism is an ordinance of the church—he couldn't find it
- Good that some didn't know the answer about "two church ordinances" because it's not true, though widely taught

### Body of Christ vs. Local Church
- Midland Baptist Church emphasizes the local church and membership
- Bible doesn't explicitly command "thou shalt be a member of a local church"
- However, Bible teaches accountability, responsibility, and commitment
- Membership follows biblical principles even without explicit command
- Catholic Church emphasizes "universal church" (body of Christ as a whole)
- Baptists separate from Catholic terminology and de-emphasize "body of Christ" language
- They talk about "the church" but avoid "body" terminology
- This is problematic because "body" is emphasized in Paul's writings
- The body of Christ is taught where? In Paul's writings
- Many don't emphasize Paul's writings because it's harder than teaching stories from Matthew-John or Old Testament accounts

### Why Matthew-John Is Easier to Teach
- Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John contain parables, stories, and accounts
- Easier to prepare messages from narratives about Elijah, Elisha, Daniel, David, Gideon
- Can create pictures and tell stories, especially for children's Sunday school
- Romans through Philemon contains mostly doctrine and teaching—few stories or parables
- Paul's letters are harder to understand and teach
- When people try to relate Paul's writings to Matthew-John without right division, contradictions appear
- They don't know what to do with these contradictions without understanding dispensational divisions

### Reconciling Scripture
- Previous studies covered scriptures that show contradictions unless properly reconciled
- Reconciliation means putting things where they belong: "this belongs here, this belongs here"
- Example from church accounting: categorizing expenses in QuickBooks (fertilizer under "building maintenance" with note "yard")
- Ministry of reconciliation: we were separated from God, believed, now reconciled back into the body of Christ where God wants us
- Properly reconciling Scripture means identifying who passages are written to and their context
