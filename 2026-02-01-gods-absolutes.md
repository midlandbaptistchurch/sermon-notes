## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six absolute certainties about God drawn from Scripture, moving from God's jealousy and coming wrath to His boundless grace, steadfast love, the promise of the rapture, and His ultimate victory over sin and death. The message is framed as an encouragement to the congregation during a difficult, dark season, reminding believers of the eternal guarantees they can hold onto. The sermon closes with a personal challenge to examine whether listeners are living in light of these eternal realities.

## Scripture
- **Exodus 20:5** — God declares Himself a jealous God in the context of the commandment against idolatry.
- **Exodus 34:14** — God's very name is revealed as "Jealous."
- **Proverbs 6:34** — Jealousy over the wrong things is described as the rage of a man.
- **Isaiah 13:6-9** — A vivid description of the coming Day of the Lord and God's fierce wrath.
- **Revelation 14:17-20** — The great winepress of the wrath of God at the end of the age.
- **Romans 1:18-23** — The wrath of God revealed against those who suppress the truth.
- **Romans 5:15** — The free gift of grace through Christ contrasted with the offense of Adam.
- **Ephesians 2:8** — Salvation by grace through faith as the gift of God.
- **Romans 8:35-39** — Nothing can separate believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:13-17** — The rapture: the dead in Christ rise first, then the living are caught up.
- **Revelation 4:1** — A preview of the trumpet voice calling "Come up hither."
- **Revelation 20:11-14** — The Great White Throne Judgment; death and hell cast into the lake of fire.
- **Revelation 21:4** — In the new world, God wipes away all tears, pain, and death.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real, righteous, and will be fully executed
- God's grace is the only and sufficient escape from His wrath
- Nothing can separate believers from God's love
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately and completely win the war over sin, death, and the devil

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" carries a positive connotation in Scripture, closely tied to valuing and protecting a relationship.
- Notably, God's very *name* is "Jealous" (Exodus 34:14) — not just an attribute but a defining characteristic.
- Application: Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as gods; God's jealousy is a righteous response to this idolatry.
- Jealousy over wrong things (Proverbs 6:34) is destructive, but God's jealousy is righteous.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- God's wrath is real and has been building throughout history — illustrated by Elijah calling down fire and the judgments of Revelation.
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces as flames, sun and moon darkened — not a mere eclipse, but God withdrawing what He set in place.
- Revelation 14: the harvest of the earth is cast into "the great winepress of the wrath of God," producing blood up to the horses' bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 12:19 (referenced as Romans 13:19 in sermon notes): "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord."
- Romans 1:18-23: Wrath is revealed against those who hold the truth in unrighteousness — applicable to today's culture of self-taught, Bible-rejecting knowledge.
- Encouragement: God's wrath is just, and believers have been spared from it.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — Grace through faith, not of ourselves; it is the gift of God.
- Seeing God's wrath clearly makes grace all the more magnificent — grace is the only exit from that wrath.
- Romans 5:15 — As one man (Adam) brought sin and death to many, so one man (Jesus Christ) brought boundless grace to many.
- Challenge: Believers too easily lose sight of this immeasurable gift, distracted by mortgages, politics, and daily frustrations.
- Grace is sufficient to carry believers through every trial — even the "dark days of February."

### Point 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39; John 3:16)
- Romans 8:35-39: Paul identifies two fronts of attack trying to separate believers from God's love — earthly/physical things (tribulation, famine, sword) attacking our relationship with Christ, and spiritual things (principalities, powers, height, depth) attacking our relationship with God.
- Neither front succeeds: believers are "more than conquerors" through Christ.
- What Adam lost as king of the earth, God's love restores — and more.
- God's love, understood as His choice and preference for His people (chosen before the foundation of the world), should fill believers with joy.
- Even death does not separate — it only brings the believer closer to God.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- The rapture is imminent — no prophetic sign is required before it occurs.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17: The qualifier is belief that Jesus died and rose again. The dead in Christ rise first; the living are caught up together with them in the clouds.
- Revelation 4:1 offers a preview: a door opened in heaven, a trumpet-like voice saying "Come up hither" — possibly a foreshadowing of the rapture call.
- The rapture will involve the trumpet, the archangel's voice, and God's own voice calling believers upward.
- Speaker's personal reflection: he is motivated by not wanting the rapture to come before he has lived more fully for God; simultaneously he longs to see loved ones and heroes of the faith (Paul, Tyndale, others) — though he feels unworthy.
- The church's rapture is distinct from the raptures described later in Revelation; believers are not appointed to go through the Tribulation.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-14; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-14: The Great White Throne Judgment — earth and heaven flee from God's face; the dead are judged from the books; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are personified as entities that are ultimately destroyed.
- Revelation 21:4: In the new world, God wipes away all tears; no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain.
- The new heaven and new earth will have no darkness — Jesus Himself is the light.
- The devil, sin, and all that corrupts relationships and causes suffering will be cast away permanently.

### Closing Challenge
- Are you prepared to be among those who will be there?
- Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones?
- Are you ready to put on immortality?
