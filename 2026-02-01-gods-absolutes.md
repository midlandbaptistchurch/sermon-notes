## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six theological certainties — "God's Absolutes" — drawn from across Scripture, moving from the sobering reality of God's jealousy and coming wrath to the comforting truths of his grace, love, the rapture, and his ultimate victory over sin and death. The sermon aims to encourage believers during a difficult season by anchoring them to guarantees found only in God's Word. Vasold closes with a challenge to examine whether listeners are truly living in light of these eternal realities.

## Scripture
Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14; Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23; Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15; Romans 8:35-39; 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1; Revelation 20:11-14; Revelation 21:4 — These passages are drawn together to establish six attributes or actions of God that are absolute and unchanging.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fully executed
- God's grace is the only sufficient answer to his wrath, and it is a free gift
- God's love is inseparable from believers — nothing in heaven or earth can sever it
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately win the war: death, hell, and sin will be cast away, and a new creation will begin

## Notes

### Absolute 1: God Is a Jealous God
- Drawn from Exodus 20:5 and Exodus 34:14, where God calls jealousy one of his very names.
- Jealousy in the biblical sense reflects God's rightful claim on his people's exclusive devotion — analogous to a spouse valuing their relationship.
- Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as gods; God's jealousy is his righteous response to that kind of idolatry.
- Proverbs 6:34 is referenced to show that jealousy exercised wrongly is destructive rage — underlining why only God's jealousy is righteous.

### Absolute 2: God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord as a time of fierce, inescapable divine judgment — hearts melting, faces like flames, sun and moon darkened.
- Revelation 14:17-20 provides the vivid image of the great winepress of God's wrath, with blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth of God that is plainly seen in creation and exchanges it for idolatry.
- Application: believers should be sobered and grateful — God's response to sin has not changed, but his grace has spared us from this judgment.
- "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord" (referenced from Romans 12:19) — God's justice is certain.

### Absolute 3: God's Grace Saves You
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."
- Grace is only truly appreciated when seen against the backdrop of God's wrath; the enormity of what we are saved from reveals the boundlessness of grace.
- Romans 5:15 — just as one man (Adam) brought sin and death to many, one man (Jesus Christ) brought grace abounding to many.
- Challenge to the congregation: we are prone to lose sight of this immeasurable gift, replacing it with worry over mortgages, politics, and daily frustrations.
- Grace is sufficient — sufficient to carry believers through every trial, including the metaphorical "dark days of February."

### Absolute 4: God Loves You
- Romans 8:35-39 — Paul lists earthly threats (tribulation, persecution, famine, sword) and spiritual threats (angels, principalities, powers, height, depth) that attempt to separate believers from the love of Christ and the love of God.
- None of them can succeed — believers are "more than conquerors" through Christ.
- The two-pronged attack of the enemy (physical and spiritual) is no match for the love of God.
- The loss of Adam is more than recovered in Christ — we gain more than what was lost in the Fall.
- God's love, understood as his choice and preference for his people from before the foundation of the world, should fill believers with joy.

### Absolute 5: God Will Rapture You
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — Paul comforts the Thessalonians regarding those who have died in Christ, promising that the dead in Christ will rise first, then living believers will be caught up together with them to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 — John's experience of a voice like a trumpet saying "Come up hither" is offered as a preview of what the rapture call will sound like.
- The rapture is imminent — no prophecy must precede it — and this should motivate holy living.
- Vasold expresses personal longing for the rapture and the reunion with loved ones and faithful saints of history, while also expressing the desire to have lived more fully for God before it occurs.
- The church's rapture is distinguished from other rapture-like events in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000); believers are not appointed to go through the tribulation.

### Absolute 6: God Will Win the War
- Revelation 20:11-14 — The Great White Throne Judgment: all the dead, small and great, stand before God; the books are opened; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new creation, God wipes away all tears; there is no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain.
- The devil, sin, death, and hell are permanently removed; what remains is fellowship with God, reunion with loved ones, and a new heaven and new earth with Jesus as its light.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those who will be there? Have you exchanged temporal things for the eternal?
