## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six "absolutes" — guaranteed, unchanging truths about God — drawn from across Scripture. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath to the comforting promises of grace, love, the rapture, and God's ultimate victory, the sermon aims to encourage believers to hold fast to these certainties through the trials of everyday life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Proverbs, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used together to build a panoramic picture of who God is and what he has guaranteed to his people.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real, righteous, and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only — and fully sufficient — escape from that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from believers, by both Christ and the Father
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will win the final war; death, hell, and the devil will be cast away

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14; Proverbs 6:34)
- God explicitly calls himself a "jealous God" in the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:5).
- In Exodus 34:14 God's very *name* is given as "Jealous" — a detail Vasold highlights as often overlooked.
- Biblical jealousy is closely tied to rightly valuing what belongs to you (e.g., a spouse); the world has corrupted the word into a purely negative trait.
- Modern idolatry of athletes and celebrities parallels the idol worship of ancient Rome and the Old Testament world.
- Proverbs 6:34 warns that misdirected jealousy becomes destructive rage — contrasting with God's holy jealousy.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- God's wrath is difficult to conceptualize in the age of grace, but the Old and New Testaments are clear that it is real and stored up.
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces as flames, sun and moon darkened — not a natural event but God's direct action.
- Revelation 14:17-20 pictures the great winepress of God's wrath producing a flood of blood 1,600 furlongs wide.
- Romans 1:18-23 reveals the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth about God that is plainly visible in creation, becoming vain in its imaginations.
- The speaker notes this should not discourage believers but should fill them with gratitude that grace has spared them from this judgment.
- "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord" (referenced from Romans/Deuteronomy) assures believers that God's justice will be executed on those who reject him.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."
- Grace is boundless precisely because God's wrath is so vast; the two must be compared together to appreciate either fully.
- Romans 5:15 — as one man (Adam) brought death, one man (Christ) brings abounding grace to many.
- Vasold challenges the congregation: everyday anxieties (mortgages, car troubles, workplace conflict, politics) crowd out appreciation for this immeasurable gift.
- Grace is unmerited, from God alone, and cannot be taken away.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35-39 lists two categories of threats to our sense of God's love:
  - *Earthly/physical* things (tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword) that can erode our relationship with Christ.
  - *Spiritual* things (death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, depth) that attempt to separate us from the love of God.
- Neither category succeeds — believers are "more than conquerors" and inseparable from God's love.
- The speaker connects this to the biblical concept of love/hate as preference and choice: God chose believers before the foundation of the world.
- Even physical death only draws believers *closer* to God rather than separating them.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — believers are not to grieve as those without hope concerning the dead in Christ.
- The qualifier for the rapture: belief that Jesus died and rose again (v. 14).
- 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 — the Lord descends with a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trump of God; the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are caught up together.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" is offered as a preview of the voice/trumpet call of the rapture.
- Vasold distinguishes the church's pre-tribulation rapture from other rapture events in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000), warning against teaching that believers will endure the tribulation.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the rapture is that it might occur before he has lived more fully for God.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-15; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-15 — the Great White Throne Judgment: the dead stand before God, books are opened, and death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain; the former things are passed away.
- The universe itself — described as dark and cold — will be melted away and replaced with a new heaven and new earth lit by Jesus himself.
- No more devil, no more sin, no more separation from loved ones.

### Closing Challenge
Vasold closed with three questions for self-examination:
1. Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be there?
2. Have you given up temporal things in exchange for eternal ones?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
