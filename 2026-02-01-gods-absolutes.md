## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six foundational, unchanging truths — "absolutes" — about God's character and promises. Moving from God's jealousy and coming wrath to His saving grace, inseparable love, the rapture, and His ultimate victory over death and sin, the sermon aims to encourage believers during a difficult season with the certainty of what God has guaranteed.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used together to build a portrait of God's character and His promises to believers.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only sufficient answer to His wrath
- God's love is inseparable from the believer
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the Church
- God will ultimately win the war over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" in Scripture is not the negative human emotion we typically associate with it; it is closely tied to valuing and protecting what is rightly one's own.
- Notably, Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Applied today: modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as idols, echoing the idol worship of Roman and biblical times.
- Proverbs 6:34 warns that jealousy directed wrongly becomes destructive rage — a contrast to God's righteous jealousy.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- God's wrath is difficult to fully grasp in the age of grace, but Scripture makes it vivid and certain.
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord as a day of cruel wrath and fierce anger — hearts melting, faces like flames, the sun and moon darkened.
- Revelation 14:17-20 pictures God's wrath as a great winepress, with blood flowing as high as a horse's bridle for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 identifies the cause of wrath: men who suppress the truth, refuse to glorify God, and exchange His glory for created things — a pattern visible in contemporary culture.
- The point is not discouragement but gratitude: believers have been spared this wrath through grace.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — *"For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."*
- Grace is truly understood only when seen alongside the weight of God's wrath; the greater the wrath, the more boundless grace appears.
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: just as one man (Adam) brought death to many, so the grace of one man (Jesus Christ) abounds to many.
- The speaker challenged the congregation not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid daily concerns — mortgages, car troubles, workplace conflicts, political disagreements.
- God's grace is sufficient, eternal, and cannot be taken away.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35-39 lists the things that might seem to threaten our connection to God's love — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword — and declares that none of them can succeed.
- Believers are described as *"more than conquerors"* — surpassing even what Adam was given as king of creation before the Fall.
- Two categories of attack are identified: earthly/physical things that threaten our personal relationship with Christ (v. 35), and spiritual forces that attempt to separate us from the love of God (v. 38-39).
- Neither succeeds. The love of God in Christ Jesus is inseparable from the believer — even death draws us closer to God rather than away from Him.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — Paul did not want believers to be ignorant or sorrowful concerning those who have died, because the rapture means we will see them again.
- The qualifier for the rapture (v. 14): believing that Jesus died and rose again.
- The sequence: the trump of God sounds, the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 offers a preview: John hears a voice like a trumpet saying *"Come up hither"* — the speaker suggested this mirrors what believers will hear at the rapture.
- The rapture is imminent with no preceding prophecy required — it could occur at any moment.
- The speaker noted the pre-tribulation rapture position: God's grace is not merely sufficient to get believers *through* the tribulation — it removes them from it entirely.
- Personal reflection: the speaker expressed not fear of the rapture itself, but a desire to have lived more fully for God before it comes.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-15; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-15 — the Great White Throne Judgment: the dead, small and great, stand before God; the books are opened; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are personified as two entities that are ultimately defeated and removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world, there will be no more pain, sorrow, tears, or death; no more devil or sin; Jesus Himself will be the light.
- The dark, cold, corruptible universe will be replaced by a new heaven and new earth.

### Closing Challenge
The speaker closed with three questions for self-examination:
1. Are you prepared to be among those who will be there?
2. Have you given up the temporal things of this world for the eternal?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
