## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six theological certainties — "absolutes" — that Christians can hold onto as unchanging guarantees from God. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath to the encouraging promises of grace, love, the Rapture, and God's ultimate victory, the sermon aims to lift the spirits of the congregation through the dark days of February and beyond.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used to establish each of the six absolutes in turn.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- Salvation is entirely by God's grace, not human merit
- Nothing can separate believers from the love of God
- The Rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately win over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### 1. God Is a Jealous God
- Exodus 20:5 — God identifies himself as "a jealous God" in the second commandment.
- Exodus 34:14 — Remarkably, "Jealous" is given as one of God's very names: *"the LORD, whose name is Jealous."`*
- Biblical jealousy is not the sinful kind; it relates to properly valuing and guarding what belongs to you.
- Application: modern celebrity and athlete worship mirrors ancient idol worship and provokes God's jealousy.
- Proverbs 6:34 — "For jealousy is the rage of a man" — jealousy over wrong things is destructive; God's jealousy is righteous.

### 2. God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- God's wrath is a consistent biblical reality from the Old Testament (Elijah calling down fire) through the book of Revelation.
- Isaiah 13:6-9 — Vivid description of the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces like flames, sun and moon darkened.
- Revelation 14:17-20 — The great winepress of the wrath of God; blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 12:19 (referenced as Romans 13:19) — "Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord."
- Romans 1:18-23 — Wrath is revealed against those who suppress the truth; creation leaves all people without excuse.
- Purpose of this point: not to discourage but to magnify the wonder of grace and assure believers that God's justice will prevail.

### 3. God's Grace Saves You
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God."
- Grace is the only escape from God's righteous wrath; understanding the wrath makes the grace more precious.
- Romans 5:15 — As one man (Adam) brought death, so one man (Jesus Christ) brings the free gift of grace to many.
- Challenge: believers can lose sight of this boundless gift when consumed by mortgages, politics, and everyday frustrations.
- Grace is sufficient, unmerited, and cannot be taken away.

### 4. God Loves You
- Romans 8:35-39 — Nothing — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, death, angels, principalities, height, nor depth — can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- Two-pronged attack by the enemy: earthly/physical things try to separate us from Christ's love; spiritual things try to separate us from God's love — both fail.
- We are "more than conquerors" — recovering and exceeding what Adam lost.
- God's love is described as choice/preference (election): God chose believers before the foundation of the world.
- John 3:16 cited as the foundational statement of God's love.

### 5. God Will Rapture You
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — The classic Rapture passage: the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are caught up together in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- The qualifier: belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- Revelation 4:1 — John's experience of "come up hither" is a preview of the voice/trumpet that will call the church home.
- The Rapture is imminent — no prophecy marks the exact moment.
- Speaker distinguishes the church's Rapture from events in Revelation, affirming a pre-tribulation position.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the Rapture is that it would come before he has lived more fully for God.

### 6. God Will Win the War
- Revelation 20:11-12 — The Great White Throne Judgment: the dead stand before God; books are opened; all judged according to their works.
- Revelation 20 — Death and hell are cast into the lake of fire — the final defeat of every enemy.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new world there will be no more pain, sorrow, death, or tears.
- The new heaven and new earth: no darkness, no devil, no sin; Jesus himself is the light.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those who will be there? Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones?
