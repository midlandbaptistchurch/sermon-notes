## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six absolute truths about God drawn from across Scripture, framed as encouragements for believers to hold onto during difficult times. Moving from the sobering reality of God's jealousy and coming wrath, he lifts the congregation toward the comforting certainties of God's grace, love, the rapture, and God's ultimate victory over sin and death.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used together to build a comprehensive portrait of God's unchanging character and promises.

## Key Themes
- God's jealousy as a righteous attribute reflecting his exclusive claim on worship
- The certainty of God's coming wrath against sin and ungodliness
- Salvation by grace through faith as the only escape from that wrath
- The inseparable love of God for believers
- The imminent hope of the rapture
- God's final and total victory over death, hell, and the devil

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God
- Text: Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14
- God identifies himself by the name "Jealous" (Exodus 34:14), reflecting his righteous demand for exclusive worship.
- Jealousy in Scripture is closely tied to valuing and protecting a covenant relationship, not the petty jealousy the world associates with the word.
- Contemporary idols — celebrity athletes, cultural figures — mirror the ancient pattern of glorifying the creature over the Creator.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that jealousy misdirected becomes destructive rage.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Texts: Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 12:19; Romans 1:18-23
- God's wrath is real, stored up, and will be poured out — illustrated vividly in Isaiah's description of the Day of the Lord and Revelation's image of the great winepress of God's wrath.
- Romans 1 explains why: humanity suppresses the truth of God clearly seen in creation and exchanges his glory for idols.
- God's wrath is presented not to discourage believers but to magnify the wonder of grace and assure that justice will be done.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You
- Texts: Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15
- Grace is the only sufficient answer to God's wrath — it is a free, unearned gift, not of human effort.
- Romans 5:15 draws the contrast: one man (Adam) brought death; one man (Jesus Christ) brings abounding grace to many.
- The speaker challenges believers not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid the everyday concerns of life.

### Point 4 — God Loves You
- Text: Romans 8:35-39
- Nothing — tribulation, persecution, famine, spiritual powers, height, nor depth — can separate the believer from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- The speaker notes a two-pronged attack: earthly trials try to separate us from Christ's love; spiritual forces try to separate us from God's love. Neither succeeds.
- God's love, understood in its biblical sense as sovereign choice and preference, means he chose believers before the foundation of the world.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You
- Texts: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1
- The rapture is presented as an imminent, prophetically unscheduled event — a timeless hope for a church living through troubled times.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17: the Lord will descend with a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trump of God; the dead in Christ rise first, then the living are caught up together with them.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" — is offered as a preview of the words believers will hear at the rapture.
- The speaker distinguishes the church's rapture from events described later in Revelation, affirming a pre-tribulation position.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the rapture is that it might come before he has lived more fully for God.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War
- Texts: Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Revelation 20) sees death and hell themselves cast into the lake of fire — ultimate defeat of all evil.
- Revelation 21:4 promises no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain in the new creation.
- The universe itself — dark and finite — will be replaced by a new heaven and a new earth lit by Jesus himself.

### Conclusion and Challenge
The speaker closes with three personal challenges:
1. Are you prepared to be among those who will be there?
2. Have you exchanged temporal things for the eternal?
3. Are you ready to cast off mortality for immortality?
