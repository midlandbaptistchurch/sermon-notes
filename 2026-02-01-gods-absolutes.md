## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six theological certainties — "God's Absolutes" — as an encouragement to the congregation during a dark winter season. Moving through Scripture from Exodus to Revelation, he draws on God's jealousy, coming wrath, saving grace, unfailing love, the promise of the Rapture, and God's ultimate victory as unshakeable guarantees believers can hold onto in the trials of life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning both Testaments, anchored in Exodus 20:5, Exodus 34:14, Isaiah 13:6–9, Revelation 14:17–20, Romans 1:18–23, Ephesians 2:8, Romans 5:15, Romans 8:35–39, 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17, Revelation 4:1, and Revelation 20:11–12; 21:4 — together tracing God's character and redemptive plan from creation to the new heaven and new earth.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only sufficient answer to his wrath
- God's love is inseparable from his people
- The Rapture is a certain, imminent hope for believers
- God will ultimately win the war over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" is not merely a negative human emotion; in Scripture it is closely tied to rightly valuing what belongs to God.
- Remarkably, Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the Lord, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Modern culture creates new gods — especially celebrity athletes — echoing ancient idol worship. God's jealousy is a righteous response to anything placed before him.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that jealousy in the wrong direction is destructive; God's jealousy, by contrast, is righteous and holy.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6–9; Revelation 14:17–20; Romans 1:18–23)
- God's wrath is not abstract; it is described in visceral, physical terms throughout Scripture.
- Isaiah 13:6–9 describes the Day of the Lord: melting hearts, faint hands, darkness covering sun, moon, and stars.
- Revelation 14:17–20 pictures the great winepress of God's wrath, with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs — imagery of total, righteous judgment.
- Romans 1:18–23 establishes the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth about God that is plainly visible in creation, becoming futile in reasoning and worshipping created things.
- The note of comfort: believers have been spared this wrath through grace. God's justice should also comfort the believer — those who reject and persecute Christ will face his repayment.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — *"For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."* Grace is the only escape from God's wrath.
- Romans 5:15 — Just as one man (Adam) introduced sin and death, one man (Jesus Christ) brings grace that abounds to many.
- Vasold challenges the congregation not to let daily anxieties — mortgages, politics, workplace conflicts — crowd out wonder at this immeasurable gift.
- Grace is boundless because it must cover the full extent of God's wrath; nothing believers could add to their sin exceeds the grace available to them.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35–39)
- Romans 8:35–39 lists two categories of things that attempt to separate believers from God's love:
  - *Earthly/physical things* (tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword) — these threaten the love of Christ.
  - *Spiritual things* (death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, depth) — these attempt to sever the love of God.
- Paul's conclusion: none of these are sufficient. Believers are "more than conquerors" — recovering even more than Adam lost.
- God's love, understood through the lens of biblical choice and preference, means God chose his people before the foundation of the world.
- Even physical death does not separate us from God's love; it draws us nearer to him.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 — Paul's revelation to the Thessalonians: believers who have died will rise first, then living believers will be caught up together to meet the Lord in the air.
- The qualifier for the Rapture: believing that Jesus died and rose again (v. 14).
- Vasold connects Revelation 4:1 — *"Come up hither"* — as a preview of what the trumpet and voice of the archangel will say at the Rapture.
- The Rapture is pre-tribulational; the speaker notes that the Rapture of the Church is distinct from other rapture events described in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000).
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the Rapture is that it would come before he has lived more fully for God — yet it remains a comfort amid world troubles.
- Believers will be reunited with those who "sleep in Jesus."

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11–12; 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11–12 — The Great White Throne Judgment: the dead are judged according to the books; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are personified as defeated enemies, cast as far away as possible from God's people.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new heaven and new earth, there is no more pain, sorrow, death, or night; Jesus himself is the light.
- The new world ends the reign of the devil, sin, and the darkness of this present age.

### Closing Challenge
Vasold closed with three pointed questions for self-examination:
1. Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be there?
2. Have you given up the temporal things of this world in exchange for eternal things?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
