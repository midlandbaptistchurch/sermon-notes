## Summary
Riley Vasold preaches through six unchanging certainties — "absolutes" — that God has given his people as anchors of faith. Beginning with God's jealous nature and the reality of his coming wrath, the message moves to the boundless grace and love of God before closing with the promises of the rapture and God's ultimate victory over sin, death, and hell. The sermon is both a doctrinal survey and a pastoral encouragement to hold fast to these guarantees amid the trials of everyday life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used together to trace six attributes or promises of God from creation through the new heaven and new earth.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fully executed
- Salvation is entirely by God's grace through faith — a free gift
- Nothing can separate believers from the love of God
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately and completely win the war over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" is not merely a negative human emotion; in Scripture it is closely tied to God's rightful claim on the exclusive devotion of his people.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"The LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Application: Modern culture has many functional gods (celebrity athletes, wealth, politics). God's jealousy is a righteous response to having his glory displaced.
- Proverbs 6:34 illustrates that jealousy executed in the wrong direction is deeply destructive — underscoring why God's jealousy must be taken seriously.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- In the age of grace it is easy to lose sight of God's wrath, but the Old and New Testaments are consistent: God's wrath against sin is real and accumulating.
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord — hearts melting, faces like flames, sun and moon darkened — as a portrait of inescapable divine judgment.
- Revelation 14:17-20 pictures the "great winepress of the wrath of God," with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs — a staggering image of the scope of final judgment.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth about God that is plainly visible in creation, exchanging the glory of God for created things.
- Key pastoral point: Understanding God's wrath makes grace all the more precious. Believers should be grateful, not complacent.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — *"For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."* Grace is the only exit from the wrath described in Absolute 2.
- Romans 5:15 draws the comparison: one man (Adam) brought sin and death; one man (Jesus Christ) brings grace that abounds to many. The gift far surpasses the offense.
- Grace is boundless precisely because it must cover the full weight of God's wrath — if it covers all of that, nothing can exceed it.
- Challenge: We too easily set aside this immeasurable gift to worry about mortgages, car trouble, workplace conflicts, and politics. Grace is sufficient for all of it.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35-39 lists two categories of threats to our assurance of God's love:
  - **Physical/earthly**: tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword.
  - **Spiritual**: death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, depth.
- Neither category is sufficient to separate a believer from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- "More than conquerors" (v.37) — Adam was placed on earth as a king; sin seemed to forfeit that. But God's love restores more than was lost. Adam's loss becomes our gain.
- God's love is not passive sentiment; it is an active choice and preference — he chose us before the foundation of the world (see Ephesians 1). Even death draws us closer to God rather than farther away.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — Paul does not want believers to be ignorant about those who have died: they will not miss the rapture. The dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together with them in the clouds.
- The qualifier: *"If we believe that Jesus died and rose again"* (v.14) — the rapture is tied directly to faith in the resurrection.
- Revelation 4:1 — John hears a voice like a trumpet saying *"Come up hither"* — a preview of the rapture call. The trumpet is not merely a sound but a voice and a word: *"Come up."*
- The rapture is pre-tribulational; God's grace is not merely sufficient to sustain believers through the tribulation — it removes them from it entirely.
- Personal note from the speaker: the rapture motivates him to live more fully for God *before* it happens, not just to escape difficulty.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-12 — The Great White Throne Judgment: even the earth and heaven flee from God's face. The dead are judged from the books; the Book of Life is opened.
- Revelation 20:14 — Death and hell are cast into the lake of fire. Two great enemies of humanity are permanently removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new heaven and new earth there is no more pain, sorrow, crying, or death. The former things are passed away.
- The new world will be lit by the presence of Jesus himself — no night, no darkness, no devil, no sin pulling people apart from one another or from God.

### Closing Challenge
- Are you prepared to be among those who will be there?
- Have you exchanged the temporal things of this world for the eternal?
- Are you ready to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
