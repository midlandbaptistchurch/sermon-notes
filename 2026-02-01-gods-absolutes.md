## Summary
In this Sunday evening message, Riley Vasold presents six unchanging attributes and promises of God — called "God's Absolutes" — drawing from across both Testaments. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath to the comforting promises of grace, love, the rapture, and ultimate victory, Vasold encourages believers to hold firmly to these guarantees as anchors through the trials of life.

## Scripture
Exodus 20:5; 34:14 — God declares himself a jealous God and states that "Jealous" is his very name. Isaiah 13:6-9 — A vivid description of the coming Day of the Lord and God's fierce wrath. Revelation 14:17-20 — The harvest of the earth cast into the great wine press of God's wrath. Romans 1:18-23 — God's wrath revealed against those who suppress the truth. Ephesians 2:8 — Salvation by grace through faith as the gift of God. Romans 5:15 — The free gift of grace through Christ abounding over the offense of Adam. Romans 8:35-39 — Nothing in all creation can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus. 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — Paul's revelation of the rapture and the catching up of believers. Revelation 4:1 — A preview of the trumpet voice saying "Come up hither." Revelation 20:11-15; 21:4 — The Great White Throne Judgment, the defeat of death and hell, and the promise of no more pain in the new world.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals or idols
- God's wrath against sin is real, righteous, and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is boundless and is the only sufficient answer to his wrath
- God's love is inseparable from believers — nothing can sever it
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately win the war; death, hell, and sin will be cast away forever

## Notes
**Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; 34:14)**
- God calls himself a jealous God in the Ten Commandments, and in Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of his names.
- Vasold notes that modern culture has corrupted the word "jealous" with negative connotations, but in Scripture it is closely tied to the idea of rightly valuing what belongs to you.
- Application: Contemporary culture creates modern idols — especially celebrity athletes — and God's jealousy calls believers to keep him first.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that misdirected jealousy is destructive, contrasting with God's righteous jealousy.

**Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13; Revelation 14; Romans 1)**
- God's wrath, though stored up in this age of grace, will be fully executed.
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord as one of destruction, melting hearts, and darkened heavens.
- Revelation 14:17-20 pictures a harvest of the earth crushed in the great wine press of God's wrath, with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity has seen God clearly in creation but suppressed the truth and exchanged his glory for idols.
- Vasold emphasizes this should drive gratitude: believers have been spared this wrath through grace.

**Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)**
- Ephesians 2:8 — grace through faith, not of ourselves, is the gift of God.
- Romans 5:15 — as Adam's one offense brought death to many, Christ's one act of grace abounds to many more.
- Vasold argues that seeing God's wrath makes the boundlessness of grace make sense: grace must be limitless to cover limitless wrath.
- Challenge: Believers too easily lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid the ordinary anxieties of life (mortgages, car trouble, workplace conflicts, politics).

**Point 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)**
- Romans 8:35-39 lists both physical threats (tribulation, persecution, famine, sword) and spiritual forces (principalities, powers, height, depth) that attempt to separate believers from God's love — and declares all of them insufficient.
- Vasold notes a two-pronged attack: earthly things try to separate us from the love of Christ; spiritual things try to separate us from the love of God.
- Because of God's love, believers are "more than conquerors" — exceeding even what Adam lost in the fall.
- God's love, understood in biblical terms as his chosen preference for his people, was set before the foundation of the world.

**Point 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)**
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — Paul corrects ignorance about those who have died in Christ, promising they will rise first, followed by living believers caught up to meet the Lord in the air.
- The rapture is imminent — no prophecy must precede it.
- Revelation 4:1 is offered as a preview: the trumpet voice saying "Come up hither" is the kind of call believers will one day hear.
- Vasold expresses personal longing for the rapture while also expressing humility about whether he has lived a life worthy of those he hopes to see there (Paul, Tyndale, George Washington).
- He affirms a pre-tribulation rapture, noting that God's grace is sufficient to spare believers from the coming wrath entirely.

**Point 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-15; 21:4)**
- Revelation 20:11-15 — the Great White Throne Judgment: the dead stand before God, books are opened, and death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world, God wipes away all tears; there is no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain.
- The devil, sin, and all darkness are permanently removed; believers will dwell in a world lit by Christ himself.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those who will be there? Have you exchanged the temporal for the eternal?
