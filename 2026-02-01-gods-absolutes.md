## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six certain, unchanging attributes and acts of God — called "God's Absolutes" — drawing from both the Old and New Testaments as well as the book of Revelation. The sermon moves from sobering truths about God's jealousy and coming wrath to deeply encouraging promises of grace, love, the rapture, and God's ultimate victory. Vasold challenges listeners to hold fast to these guarantees as anchors through the trials of life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — together tracing the full arc of God's character and redemptive plan, from his holy jealousy to the new heaven and new earth.

## Key Themes
- God's jealousy as a holy attribute, not a flaw
- The certainty and righteousness of God's coming wrath
- Salvation by grace alone through faith alone
- The inseparability of God's love from believers
- The pre-tribulation rapture as a living hope
- God's final and total victory over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God
- Text: Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14
- Jealousy in Scripture is tied to God's rightful claim on his people's devotion, not to a negative human emotion.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names.
- Application: Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as god-like figures; God alone is worthy of worship.
- Proverbs 6:34 — jealousy misdirected becomes destructive rage.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Texts: Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 12:19; Romans 1:18-23
- God's wrath is not abstract — it is portrayed vividly in Scripture as a coming reality for the unrepentant.
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces as flames, sun and moon darkened.
- Revelation 14 pictures the great winepress of God's wrath, with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the basis of wrath: humanity suppressing the truth about God despite clear evidence in creation.
- God's wrath should move believers to gratitude that grace has spared them from it.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You
- Texts: Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15
- Grace is the only sufficient answer to God's wrath.
- Ephesians 2:8 — salvation is entirely a gift, not of human effort.
- Romans 5:15 — just as one man (Adam) brought sin, one man (Christ) brought grace abounding to many.
- Vasold challenges listeners not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid daily worries (finances, politics, relationships).
- Grace is boundless because it must cover the fullness of God's wrath — and it does.

### Point 4 — God Loves You
- Text: Romans 8:35-39
- Nothing in the physical realm (tribulation, famine, peril, sword) or the spiritual realm (principalities, powers, height, depth) can separate believers from God's love.
- Paul — who had been caught up to heaven — is personally persuaded of this.
- "More than conquerors" (v.37): what Adam lost in the fall, God's love restores and surpasses.
- God's love is not merely an emotion but a sovereign choice made before the foundation of the world.
- Even death draws a believer closer to God rather than away from his love.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You
- Texts: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1
- The rapture is imminent — no prophecy must be fulfilled before it occurs.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:14 — the qualifier: belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- The dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together in the clouds.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" offers a preview of what the trumpet-voice of God will say at the rapture.
- Vasold distinguishes the Church's rapture from the raptures described later in Revelation, affirming a pre-tribulation position.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only hesitation about the rapture is that it might come before he has lived more fully for God.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War
- Texts: Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4
- The Great White Throne Judgment: all the dead, small and great, stand before God; books are opened.
- Death and hell are cast into the lake of fire — permanently removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world: no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain; all former things passed away.
- The new heaven and new earth will be filled with light (Jesus himself as the light), free of sin, darkness, and the devil.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those who will be there? Have you exchanged the temporal for the eternal?
