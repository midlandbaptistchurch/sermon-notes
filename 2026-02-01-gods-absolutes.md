## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six foundational certainties — "absolutes" — about God's character and promises, drawn from across Scripture. Moving from God's jealousy and coming wrath, through His saving grace and unfailing love, to the rapture and His ultimate victory, the message aims to anchor believers with eternal hope during difficult times. The sermon challenges listeners to consider whether they are living in light of these eternal realities rather than temporal concerns.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across both Testaments, anchored in Exodus 20:5; Romans 1, 5, and 8; Ephesians 2:8; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; and Revelation 4, 14, 20–21 — together tracing God's character from His jealous holiness to His final triumph over death and hell.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace through Christ is the only rescue from that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from believers — nothing can sever it
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will win the final war; death, hell, and sin will be cast away

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- "Jealous" is not merely a negative trait; in Scripture it is closely tied to rightly valuing one's relationship — here, God's exclusive claim on His people's worship.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Modern culture creates substitute gods (celebrity athletes, fame, self) in patterns that echo ancient idol worship.
- Proverbs 6:34 warns that misplaced jealousy becomes destructive rage — a contrast to the righteous jealousy of God.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23; Romans 12:19)
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord as a time of cosmic darkness and overwhelming dread — hearts melting, faces burning.
- Revelation 14 pictures God's wrath as a great winepress from which blood flows for 1,600 furlongs — a staggering image of judgment.
- Romans 1:18–23 explains the root cause: humanity suppresses the truth of God evident in creation, exchanges the glory of God for created things, and becomes willfully foolish.
- "Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord" (Romans 12:19) — justice belongs to God alone.
- This point is meant not to discourage but to magnify the wonder of grace and assure believers that God's justice is certain.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — *"For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God."*
- Grace is only fully appreciated when held alongside the reality of God's wrath; the boundlessness of grace corresponds to the severity of what it covers.
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: one man (Adam) brought death; one man (Jesus Christ) brings grace abounding to many.
- Vasold challenges the congregation not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid daily worries — mortgages, broken cars, workplace conflict, political anxiety.
- Grace is an absolute: unmerited, God-given, and cannot be taken away.

### Point 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39; John 3:16)
- Romans 8:35–39 lists two categories of threats to God's love: earthly/physical (tribulation, famine, sword) and spiritual (principalities, powers, height, depth) — and declares neither category can succeed.
- Believers are "more than conquerors" — recovering more through Christ's love than Adam lost through sin.
- God's love is not merely emotional affection; in biblical terms it includes God's choice and preference for His people before the foundation of the world.
- Even death does not separate — it only draws believers closer to God.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — believers are not to sorrow as those without hope concerning the dead in Christ.
- The qualifier (v. 14): belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- The sequence: the Lord descends with a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trump of God; the dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 offers a preview of the rapture call — *"Come up hither"* — the first audible words spoken to John from heaven.
- Vasold holds a pre-tribulation rapture position, distinguishing the church's rapture from later events in Revelation.
- Personal reflection: his only concern about the rapture is that it might come before he has lived a fuller life for God; yet it remains a certain comfort and hope.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11–12 — the Great White Throne Judgment: heaven and earth flee from God's face; the dead, small and great, stand before Him; the books are opened alongside the Book of Life.
- Death and hell themselves are cast into the lake of fire — the final enemies destroyed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new heaven and new earth there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain; the former things are passed away.
- No more night, no more devil, no more sin causing division — Jesus Himself is the light of that world.
- The universe as we know it — dark, cold, finite — gives way to an eternal new creation.

### Conclusion and Challenge
Vasold closes with three questions for self-examination:
1. Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be there?
2. Have you given up temporal things in exchange for eternal ones?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality for immortality?

The message closes in prayer, thanking God for the certain defeat of death and hell, for the promise of reunion with loved ones, and for grace given to sin-cursed creatures.
