## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six unchanging attributes and promises of God — called "God's Absolutes" — to encourage the congregation during a dark and cold February. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath, through the comforting truths of His grace, love, and the Rapture, the message culminates in the assurance that God will ultimately win the war over sin, death, and hell.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — used together to paint a comprehensive picture of who God is and what He has guaranteed His people.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fully executed
- God's grace is the only and sufficient escape from that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from His people
- The Rapture is a certain, imminent hope for believers
- God will ultimately and finally win the war over evil

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14; Proverbs 6:34)
- The word "jealous" is not merely negative; in Scripture it is closely tied to a rightful guarding of what one values.
- Exodus 20:5 — God identifies Himself as a jealous God who will not share worship with idols.
- Exodus 34:14 — Remarkably, "Jealous" is listed as one of God's actual names: "the LORD, whose name is Jealous."
- Contemporary idolatry is visible in the cult of celebrity athletes and the glorification of individuals, echoing the idol worship of Roman times.
- Proverbs 6:34 — Jealousy over the wrong things is destructive; God's jealousy, however, is righteous.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- Isaiah 13:6-9 — A vivid description of the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces burning, the sun and moon darkened. This is not merely an eclipse — it is the Creator withdrawing the light He placed there.
- Revelation 14:17-20 — The imagery of the great winepress of God's wrath: the earth's harvest cast in and blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 — God's wrath is revealed against those who suppress the truth. Creation itself leaves mankind without excuse; those who reject God and manufacture their own gods invite His judgment.
- God's wrath is not irrational but is the just response to persistent, willful sin. The Christian should be moved to gratitude that grace has spared them from it.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God." Grace is the only way out from under the wrath described above.
- Romans 5:15 — Just as one man (Adam) brought death to many, so one man (Jesus Christ) brought abounding grace to many. The symmetry is intentional and beautiful.
- Vasold challenges the congregation not to lose the wonder of grace by becoming preoccupied with mortgages, politics, or daily frustrations. Grace is boundless precisely because God's wrath is so great — and it covers all of it.
- Grace is unmerited, given by God, and cannot be taken away.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35-37 — Nothing in the physical realm — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, or sword — can separate us from the love of Christ. In all these things we are "more than conquerors."
- Romans 8:38-39 — Nothing in the spiritual realm — death, life, angels, principalities, powers, things present or to come, height or depth — can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- The enemy wages a two-pronged attack: physical trials against our relationship with Christ, and spiritual forces against our relationship with God — but both fail.
- God's love was expressed definitively at the cross (John 3:16) and was set upon His people before the foundation of the world.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 — Paul writes so that believers will not grieve without hope. The qualifier for the Rapture is belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 — The sequence: the Lord descends with a shout, the voice of the archangel, and the trump of God; the dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together with them in the clouds.
- Revelation 4:1 — The first voice John hears in heaven is like a trumpet saying, "Come up hither." Vasold sees this as a preview of what believers will hear at the Rapture.
- The Rapture is imminent — no prophecy must be fulfilled before it occurs. It is distinct from the rapture events described later in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000).
- Vasold's personal reflection: his only concern about the Rapture is that it might come before he has lived a fuller life for God — yet the hope of seeing loved ones and the Lord still encourages.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-15; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-14 — The Great White Throne Judgment: the dead, small and great, stand before God; the books are opened alongside the Book of Life; death and hell deliver up their dead and are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are themselves finally defeated and removed — cast as far away as possible.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new heaven and new earth there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain. The former things are passed away.
- No more devil, no more sin, no more darkness. Jesus Himself is the light of that new world.

### Closing Challenge
Vasold closed with three personal questions for the congregation:
1. Are you prepared to be among those who will be in that eternal company?
2. Have you given up the temporal things of this world in exchange for what is eternal?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
