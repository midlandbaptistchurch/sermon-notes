## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six unchanging certainties — "absolutes" — about God drawn from Scripture, covering God's jealousy, his coming wrath, the saving power of his grace, his inseparable love, the promise of the rapture, and his ultimate victory over sin and death. The message is framed as an encouragement for believers to hold fast to these guarantees during difficult times. Vasold closes with a challenge to examine whether listeners are living in light of these eternal realities.

## Scripture
- **Exodus 20:5** — God declares himself a jealous God in the context of the commandment against idols.
- **Exodus 34:14** — God's very name is revealed as "Jealous."
- **Proverbs 6:34** — Jealousy described as the rage of a man.
- **Isaiah 13:6-9** — A vivid description of the coming Day of the Lord and God's fierce wrath.
- **Revelation 14:17-20** — The great winepress of the wrath of God.
- **Romans 1:18-23** — The wrath of God revealed against those who suppress the truth.
- **Romans 12:19** — "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord."
- **Ephesians 2:8** — Salvation by grace through faith, not of works.
- **Romans 5:15** — The free gift of grace through Jesus Christ contrasted with the offense of Adam.
- **Romans 8:35-39** — Nothing can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:13-17** — The rapture: the dead in Christ rise first, then the living are caught up.
- **Revelation 4:1** — A foreshadowing of the rapture call: "Come up hither."
- **Revelation 20:11-12** — The Great White Throne Judgment; death and hell cast into the lake of fire.
- **Revelation 21:4** — No more pain, sorrow, or death in the new world.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is certain and righteous
- God's grace is the only and sufficient answer to his wrath
- God's love is inseparable from the believer
- The rapture is a sure and imminent hope for the church
- God wins — sin, death, and hell are ultimately defeated
- The eternal state brings full restoration beyond what Adam lost

## Notes

### 1. God Is a Jealous God
- Drawn from Exodus 20:5 and Exodus 34:14, where "Jealous" is revealed as one of God's actual names.
- Vasold distinguishes biblical jealousy from the negative connotation the word carries in modern culture, connecting it to rightful ownership and valuing one's relationship.
- Applied to contemporary idol worship, particularly the glorification of athletes in American culture, drawing a parallel to ancient Roman hero worship.
- Proverbs 6:34 illustrates that jealousy misdirected becomes destructive rage.

### 2. God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- God's wrath is a reality both in the Old Testament (Elijah calling down fire, the Flood) and in the prophetic future (Revelation's judgments).
- Isaiah 13:6-9 describes the Day of the Lord with imagery of hearts melting, darkness covering the heavens, and the land laid desolate.
- Revelation 14:17-20 presents the great winepress of God's wrath, with blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 grounds wrath in humanity's willful suppression and rejection of the knowledge of God.
- Romans 12:19 affirms that vengeance belongs to God — a point of both warning and comfort.
- Grace for believers does not mean God's response to sin has softened; rather, believers have been spared from a wrath that is entirely just.

### 3. God's Grace Saves You
- Ephesians 2:8 — salvation is by grace through faith, entirely a gift, not earned.
- Romans 5:15 — just as one man (Adam) brought sin and death, one man (Jesus Christ) brought grace abounding to many.
- Vasold emphasizes that the magnitude of grace only becomes clear when seen alongside the magnitude of God's wrath.
- He challenges believers not to lose sight of grace amid everyday concerns — mortgages, car troubles, workplace conflicts, political tensions.
- Grace is described as boundless and unable to be exceeded by any sin, since it is sufficient to cover all that God's wrath would require.

### 4. God Loves You
- Romans 8:35-39 — Paul lists tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword, death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, and depth — none of these can separate believers from God's love.
- Vasold notes a two-pronged structure: earthly/physical things try to separate us from the love of Christ; spiritual forces try to separate us from the love of God.
- Being "more than conquerors" (v.37) exceeds even what Adam possessed before the Fall.
- God's love is framed in terms of divine choice — God chose believers before the foundation of the world.
- The loss of love is one of life's deepest pains; therefore, the guarantee of God's inseparable love should fill believers with joy.

### 5. God Will Rapture You
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — the foundational rapture passage: the dead in Christ rise first, then the living are caught up in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- The qualifier for participation: belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" is presented as a preview of the rapture call, the first thing believers will hear God audibly say.
- Vasold expresses personal longing for the rapture while also a desire to have lived more fully for God before it occurs.
- He distinguishes the church's rapture from the raptures described later in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000), arguing against a post-tribulation rapture view.
- The rapture is offered as comfort amid wars, tribulations, and the deaths of loved ones.

### 6. God Will Win the War
- Revelation 20:11-12 — the Great White Throne Judgment: the dead stand before God, books are opened, and the book of life is consulted.
- Death and hell are cast into the lake of fire — two distinct entities personified and permanently removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world there will be no more pain, sorrow, tears, or death.
- The new heaven and new earth are described as a world without night, with Jesus as its light.
- The devil, sin, and all that causes division and grief will be permanently absent.
- Vasold closes with a challenge: Are you living in a way that reflects readiness for eternity? Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones?

### Closing Challenge
Vasold challenged the congregation with three questions:
1. Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be in eternity with God?
2. Have you given up temporal things in exchange for eternal ones?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
