## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six unchanging certainties — "absolutes" — about God drawn from across Scripture, intended to encourage the congregation through a dark and difficult season. Beginning with God's jealousy and wrath, he moves through grace, love, the rapture, and God's ultimate victory, building a case that every believer has profound, unshakeable reasons for hope. The sermon closes with a personal challenge to live worthily of these eternal guarantees.

## Scripture
This sermon draws from a wide range of passages across both Testaments, including Exodus 20 and 34, Isaiah 13, Romans 1, 5, and 8, Ephesians 2, 1 Thessalonians 4, and Revelation 4, 14, 20, and 21. Together these texts paint a picture of God's character — his jealousy, justice, grace, love, and sovereign victory over sin and death.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only — and fully sufficient — answer to that wrath
- Nothing can separate believers from the love of God
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will win the final war; death, hell, and sin will be cast away forever

## Notes

### Absolute 1: God Is a Jealous God
- Exodus 20:5 — God identifies himself as a jealous God in the Ten Commandments.
- Exodus 34:14 — Uniquely, "Jealous" is given as an actual *name* of God, not merely a descriptor.
- Vasold argues that jealousy in the biblical sense is not a negative trait but reflects God's rightful claim on the devotion of his people, comparable to a spouse's claim on a partner's loyalty.
- Applied to contemporary culture: the idolization of athletes and celebrities mirrors ancient Roman glorification of Olympians.
- Proverbs 6:34 cited to show that misplaced jealousy — jealousy over the wrong things — is destructive.

### Absolute 2: God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Isaiah 13:6-9 — A description of the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces like flames, sun and moon darkened. Vasold emphasizes this is not merely an eclipse but a sovereign act of the God who set those lights in place.
- Revelation 14:17-20 — The great winepress of God's wrath; blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 — Wrath is revealed against those who suppress the truth. Vasold applies this to a modern "podcast knowledge culture" where people seek out teachers who confirm their own views rather than submitting to Scripture.
- Romans 12:19 (referenced as Romans 13:19 in the sermon) — "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord."
- Purpose of this point: not to discourage but to magnify the grace that spares believers from this wrath, and to assure that God is just — those who reject him will face consequences.

### Absolute 3: God's Grace Saves You
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."
- Romans 5:15 — As one man (Adam) brought death to many, so the free gift of grace through one man (Jesus Christ) has abounded to many. Vasold calls this the elegant symmetry of the gospel.
- Key insight: grace can only be fully appreciated when set against the backdrop of God's wrath. Holding wrath in one hand and grace in the other, the boundlessness of grace becomes clear.
- Pastoral application: believers lose perspective when they focus on mortgages, broken cars, workplace conflicts, or politics rather than the immeasurable gift of grace.

### Absolute 4: God Loves You
- Romans 8:35-39 — Paul lists earthly threats (tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword) that try to separate us from the love of *Christ*, and spiritual threats (death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, depth) that try to separate us from the love of *God*. Neither succeeds.
- Vasold notes a two-pronged attack by the enemy — physical and spiritual — and that Paul's assurance covers both fronts.
- "More than conquerors" — Adam was created as a king to conquer the earth; sin seemed to forfeit that. God's love restores something greater than what was lost.
- John 3:16 referenced as the foundational statement of God's love.
- Even death does not separate us from God's love — it only brings us closer to him.

### Absolute 5: God Will Rapture You
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 — Paul's revelation to the Thessalonian church: those who sleep in Jesus will rise first; the living will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- Vasold emphasizes the rapture is imminent — no prophecy must be fulfilled before it occurs.
- Revelation 4:1 — John hears a trumpet-like voice say "Come up hither," which Vasold presents as a preview of what believers will hear at the rapture: a combination of the trumpet, the archangel's voice, and God's own words.
- Personal reflection: Vasold expresses awe and a measure of unworthiness at the prospect of standing alongside Paul, William Tyndale, and the founders of the faith.
- Vasold firmly holds a pre-tribulation rapture position, warning that teaching Christians will endure the tribulation undermines the sufficiency of grace.

### Absolute 6: God Will Win the War
- Revelation 20:11-14 — The Great White Throne Judgment: death and hell deliver up their dead and are themselves cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — In the new world there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain; the former things are passed away.
- The new heaven and new earth will have no night; Jesus himself is its light.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those present? Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones? Are you ready to put on immortality?
