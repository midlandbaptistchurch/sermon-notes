## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six "absolutes" — guaranteed, unchanging truths about God — drawn from across Scripture. Beginning with God's jealousy and wrath, the sermon moves through grace, love, the rapture, and God's ultimate victory, offering the congregation firm anchors of hope during difficult times. The closing challenge calls listeners to examine whether they are living in light of these eternal realities.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus through Revelation, tracing God's character from his jealousy over idolatry (Exodus 20:5; 34:14) and his coming wrath (Isaiah 13; Revelation 14) through his saving grace (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15), inseparable love (Romans 8:35-39), the promise of the rapture (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1), and final victory over death and hell (Revelation 20-21).

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only — and fully sufficient — answer to that wrath
- Nothing can separate believers from the love of God
- The rapture is an imminent, certain hope for the church
- God will ultimately and completely win the war against sin, death, and hell

## Notes
**Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; 34:14)**
- The word "jealous" carries a positive, covenantal meaning in Scripture — closely tied to valuing and protecting a relationship
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is itself one of God's names
- Misplaced jealousy (e.g., glorifying athletes or celebrities) reflects the ancient human tendency to manufacture gods; God's jealousy is a righteous response to that idolatry
- Proverbs 6:34 illustrates how destructive jealousy in the wrong direction can be

**Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)**
- Isaiah 13 depicts the Day of the Lord as a day of fierce anger, cosmic darkness, and heart-melting dread
- Revelation 14 presents the stunning image of the great winepress of God's wrath, with blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth about God that creation plainly reveals, becoming vain and foolish in its own wisdom
- God's wrath is righteous; believers should be moved to gratitude that grace has spared them from it

**Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)**
- Grace is the only exit from God's wrath; it is entirely a gift, not earned
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: one man (Adam) brought death, one man (Jesus Christ) brought abounding grace — and the gift far exceeds the offense
- The speaker challenged the congregation not to let daily anxieties (finances, politics, relationships) crowd out wonder at this immeasurable gift
- Grace sufficient to cover all of God's wrath cannot be exhausted by any further sin

**Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)**
- Romans 8:35-37 lists earthly trials (tribulation, persecution, famine, sword) that try to sever the believer's relationship with Christ — none can succeed
- Romans 8:38-39 lists spiritual forces (death, life, angels, principalities, height, depth) that try to separate us from the love of God — none can succeed either
- The enemy wages a two-pronged attack — physical and spiritual — but both prongs fail
- God's love, understood in biblical terms as his sovereign choice and preference, was set on believers before the foundation of the world

**Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)**
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 is Paul's direct revelation to the Thessalonian church: the dead in Christ rise first, then the living are caught up together with them in the clouds
- The qualifier is faith: "if we believe that Jesus died and rose again" (v. 14)
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" — offers a preview of the voice and trumpet that will call the church home
- The church's rapture is distinct from and prior to the events of the Tribulation; believers are not appointed to endure God's wrath
- The speaker expressed personal longing to have lived more fully for God before the rapture comes

**Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-12; 21:4)**
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Revelation 20:11-12): all the dead stand before God; the books are opened; death and hell themselves are cast into the lake of fire
- Revelation 21:4: in the new heaven and new earth there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain — the former things are passed away
- The devil, sin, and all darkness will be permanently removed; Jesus himself will be the light of the new creation

**Closing Challenge**
- Are you prepared to stand among those who will be there?
- Have you exchanged the temporal things of this world for the eternal?
- Are you ready to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
