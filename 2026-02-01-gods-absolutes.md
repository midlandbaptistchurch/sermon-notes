## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six guaranteed truths — "absolutes" — about God drawn from Scripture, ranging from God's jealousy and coming wrath to his boundless grace, unfailing love, the promise of the Rapture, and his ultimate victory over sin and death. The message is framed as an encouragement to the congregation during a dark, cold season of the year and of the world, calling listeners to hold fast to these eternal certainties.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — together tracing God's character and his redemptive plan from creation through the new heaven and earth.

## Key Themes
- The jealousy of God and the danger of false gods
- God's righteous wrath against sin and its future fulfillment
- Salvation by grace through faith as the only escape from that wrath
- The inseparable love of God in Christ Jesus
- The hope and imminence of the Rapture
- God's final and total victory over death, hell, and the devil

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God
- Text: Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14
- God names himself "Jealous" (Exodus 34:14) — a unique and significant divine title.
- Biblical jealousy is tied to rightly valuing what belongs to you, not the petty envy the culture associates with the word.
- Modern idolatry (e.g., the glorification of athletes) echoes ancient patterns condemned in Scripture.
- Proverbs 6:34 illustrates how jealousy over wrong things becomes destructive rage.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Texts: Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 12:19; Romans 1:18-23
- God's wrath is real, stored up, and will be poured out — vividly described in Isaiah and Revelation (the great winepress of God's wrath, blood to the horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs).
- Vengeance belongs to God; he will repay (Romans 12:19).
- Romans 1:18-23 grounds the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth, exchanges the glory of God for idols, and becomes vain in its reasoning.
- This truth should not discourage believers but remind them of the magnitude of grace that has spared them.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You
- Texts: Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15
- Grace is the only sufficient response to God's wrath — unmerited, a gift, and impossible to earn.
- Romans 5:15 contrasts one man's offense (Adam) bringing death with one man's gift (Christ) abounding grace to many.
- Speaker challenged the congregation not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid everyday anxieties (mortgages, car troubles, politics).
- Grace is boundless precisely because it must cover the full weight of God's wrath.

### Point 4 — God Loves You
- Text: Romans 8:35-39
- Nothing — tribulation, persecution, famine, death, spiritual powers, height or depth — can separate believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- The passage presents a two-pronged attack by the enemy: earthly/physical things trying to sever relationship with Christ; spiritual forces trying to separate from the love of God. Both fail.
- Adam was made to be a king and conqueror; through God's love believers become "more than conquerors" — recovering more than Adam lost.
- God's love, understood as his sovereign choice and preference for his people, was set before the foundation of the world.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You
- Texts: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1
- The Rapture is imminent — no prophecy pinpoints the moment, so it could occur at any time.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17: the dead in Christ rise first; living believers are caught up to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 offers a preview: John hears a trumpet-like voice say "Come up hither" — a foreshadowing of what believers will hear.
- Speaker expressed personal longing to have lived more fully for God before the Rapture, while affirming it as the believer's great hope amid a troubled world.
- Noted that the church-age Rapture is distinct from rapture events described within the Tribulation period in Revelation.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War
- Texts: Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Revelation 20:11-12): the dead judged by their works; death and hell cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4: in the new heaven and new earth there will be no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain.
- The devil, sin, and all darkness are permanently removed; Jesus himself is the light of that world.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those who will be there? Have you exchanged the temporal for the eternal?
