## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six "absolutes" — guaranteed, unchanging truths about God — drawn from across Scripture. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath, through the comforting promises of grace and love, to the future hope of the rapture and God's ultimate victory, the sermon aims to encourage believers to hold fast to these certainties through the trials of everyday life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus through Revelation, anchoring each of the six absolutes in direct biblical text. Key texts include Exodus 20:5 (God is jealous), Romans 1:18 (God's wrath), Ephesians 2:8 (grace through faith), Romans 8:35–39 (nothing separates us from God's love), 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 (the rapture), and Revelation 20–21 (the great white throne and new creation).

## Key Themes
- The jealousy of God as a righteous, protective attribute
- God's wrath as certain, righteous, and coming
- Salvation by grace alone as the only escape from that wrath
- The inseparable love of God in Christ Jesus
- The pre-tribulation rapture as a living hope
- God's final and total victory over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" is not merely a negative trait; in Scripture it is closely tied to God's rightful claim on worship and his valuing of the relationship with his people.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names.
- Modern culture creates alternative "gods" (e.g., celebrity athletes); God's command to have no other gods before him remains absolute.
- Proverbs 6:34 illustrates that misdirected jealousy is destructive — by contrast, God's jealousy is righteous.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- God's wrath is difficult to conceptualize in the age of grace, but the whole of Scripture — Old Testament judgments, the book of Revelation — makes it plain.
- Isaiah 13:6–9 describes the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, the sun and moon darkened, the land made desolate.
- Revelation 14:17–20 pictures the harvest of the earth cast into "the great wine press of the wrath of God," with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 12:19 (referenced as Romans 13:19 in the sermon) — "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord."
- Romans 1:18–23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth, exchanges God's glory for created things, and becomes willfully foolish.
- God's response to sin has not changed; grace does not eliminate his hatred of sin.
- This truth should produce gratitude in believers who have been spared.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."
- Placing God's wrath and God's grace side by side magnifies the wonder of grace: because wrath is so severe, grace must be boundless to cover it.
- Romans 5:15 — as one man (Adam) brought sin and death, so one man (Jesus Christ) brings grace abounding to many.
- The danger is losing sight of this immeasurable gift by being consumed with earthly concerns (finances, politics, daily frustrations).
- Grace is unmerited, from God alone, and cannot be taken away.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35–39 lists two categories of things that attempt to separate believers from God's love:
  - **Physical/earthly** (tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword) — trying to separate us from the love of *Christ*.
  - **Spiritual** (death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, depth) — trying to separate us from the love of *God*.
- In both cases, Paul is persuaded that none of these things can succeed — believers are inseparable from God's love.
- "More than conquerors" (v. 37) — Adam was meant to be a king; through God's love we receive more than what Adam lost.
- God's love, understood as his choice and preference for us from before the foundation of the world, should fill believers with joy.
- Even death does not separate — it only draws us closer to God.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — Paul did not want believers to be ignorant or to grieve without hope concerning those who have died in Christ.
- Qualifier for the rapture (v. 14): belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- The dead in Christ rise first; then the living are caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" offered as a preview of the voice/trumpet of the rapture.
- The rapture is presented as pre-tribulational; the speaker distinguishes it from other rapture-like events in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000).
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only anxiety about the rapture is that it might come before he has lived more fully for God; yet it remains a source of comfort amid world troubles.
- The hope of the rapture includes reunion with deceased believers and standing in the presence of Christ.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-14; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11–14 — the great white throne judgment: all the dead stand before God, the books are opened, and death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are personified as entities that are ultimately defeated and removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new world, there will be no more pain, sorrow, death, or tears.
- The new heaven and new earth will be a world of light (Jesus as the light), without night, without sin, without the devil.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be there? Have you exchanged temporal things for the eternal?

### Closing Prayer Themes
- Gratitude for the defeat of death and hell.
- Hope of reunion with loved ones.
- Acknowledgment of grace given to a "sin-cursed creature."
- Prayer that Scripture study would draw the congregation closer to God so that what is read in the Bible is confirmed as ultimate reality.
