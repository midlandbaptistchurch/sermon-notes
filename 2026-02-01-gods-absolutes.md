## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six foundational, unchanging truths — "absolutes" — about God's character and promises. Moving from God's jealousy and righteous wrath through to his saving grace, his love, the rapture, and his ultimate victory over sin and death, the sermon aims to encourage the congregation during a difficult season by anchoring them in the certainties of Scripture.

## Scripture
Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14; Isaiah 13:6–9; Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15; Romans 8:35–39; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; Revelation 4:1; Revelation 14:17–20; Revelation 20:11–12; Revelation 21:4 — These passages together trace the arc of God's character from holy jealousy and fearsome wrath to boundless grace, inseparable love, the catching-away of believers, and the final defeat of death and hell.

## Key Themes
- The jealousy of God as a holy, rightful attribute
- God's wrath against sin — certain and fully justified
- Salvation by grace through faith alone (Ephesians 2:8)
- The inseparable love of God in Christ (Romans 8)
- The pre-tribulation rapture as a living hope for believers
- God's ultimate and final victory: the Great White Throne, the lake of fire, and the new creation

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; 34:14)
- The word "jealous" is not merely a negative trait; in Scripture it reflects God's rightful claim on his people, closely linked to a husband's devotion to a spouse.
- Notably, Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the LORD, whose name is Jealous."*
- Application: Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as functional gods; God's jealousy is a reminder that he alone is worthy of worship.
- Proverbs 6:34 — misplaced or sinful jealousy destroys; God's jealousy is righteous.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6–9; Romans 1:18–23; Romans 12:19; Revelation 14:17–20)
- God's wrath, though often overlooked in the age of grace, is real, stored up, and certain.
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord: hearts melting, faces aflame, sun and moon darkened — a sovereign act by the same God who set them in place.
- Revelation 14 pictures the harvest of the earth cast into "the great wine press of the wrath of God," with blood rising to horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18–23 — humanity is without excuse; creation itself testifies to God. Rejecting that testimony and exchanging the glory of God for created things is the root cause of wrath.
- Romans 12:19 — "Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord."
- Encouragement: knowing God's wrath is certain should deepen our gratitude for grace and assure us that injustice will not go unanswered.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — salvation is by grace through faith, entirely a gift of God, not of works.
- Romans 5:15 — just as one man (Adam) introduced sin and death to many, so one man (Jesus Christ) brings grace abounding to many. Adam's loss becomes our gain.
- Grace is boundless precisely because it must cover the full weight of God's wrath. The two must be held together to appreciate either.
- Challenge: believers can lose sight of this immeasurable gift when consumed by mortgages, politics, and daily frustrations.

### Point 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35–39; John 3:16)
- Romans 8:35–39 — nothing in creation — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, death, spiritual powers, height or depth — can separate the believer from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- The enemy mounts a two-pronged attack: earthly, physical trials threaten the believer's relationship with Christ; spiritual powers attack the believer's sense of God's love. Neither succeeds.
- "More than conquerors" (v. 37) — Adam was created to reign; through Christ's love, believers receive something greater than what Adam forfeited.
- God's love, understood in the biblical sense of sovereign choice and preference, means he chose believers before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1).

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 — believers are not to grieve as those without hope. The qualifier: *"if we believe that Jesus died and rose again."* The dead in Christ rise first; living believers are then caught up together with them in the clouds.
- Revelation 4:1 — John's experience of "Come up hither" at the voice of a trumpet is seen as a picture of the rapture call.
- The rapture is pre-tribulational; God's grace is sufficient to spare believers from his wrath, not merely to sustain them through it.
- Personal note from the speaker: his greatest concern about the rapture is that it might occur before he has done more for God — a motivation to live urgently for Christ.
- The rapture is a comfort amid wars, persecutions, and the loss of loved ones.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11–12; 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11–12 — the Great White Throne Judgment: earth and heaven flee from God's face; the dead are judged from the books; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Death and hell are personified as distinct entities that are ultimately defeated and removed.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new creation there is no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain. The former things have passed away.
- The new heaven and new earth will have no darkness; Jesus himself is its light.
- Final encouragement: all that remains is the goodness of God, reunion with loved ones, and face-to-face communion with the Savior.

### Conclusion and Challenge
- Are you prepared to be among those present at these events?
- Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones?
- Are you ready to "cast off mortality and put on immortality" (cf. 1 Corinthians 15)?
