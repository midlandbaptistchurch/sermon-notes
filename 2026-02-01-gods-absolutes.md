## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six unchanging truths — "absolutes" — about God drawn from Scripture, intended to encourage the congregation through a dark season. Moving from God's jealousy and coming wrath to His grace, love, the rapture, and ultimate victory, the sermon calls believers to hold fast to these guarantees as anchors for their faith.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — collectively tracing the character of God from his righteous jealousy through his wrath, his saving grace, his inseparable love, the promise of the rapture, and his final triumph over death and hell.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real, stored up, and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only — and entirely sufficient — escape from that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from the believer, both physically and spiritually
- The rapture is an imminent, certain hope for the Church
- God wins the war: death, hell, and the devil are cast away, and a new creation begins

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- Jealousy is not inherently negative; in Scripture it is closely tied to rightly valuing what belongs to you.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names.
- Modern culture creates substitute gods — particularly celebrity athletes — echoing the idol worship of ancient Rome and the warnings of the second commandment.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that misdirected jealousy is destructive; God's jealousy, by contrast, is righteous.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 12:19; Romans 1:18-23)
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord: melting hearts, fading light, fierce anger laying the land desolate.
- Revelation 14 presents the harvest of the earth cast into "the great wine press of the wrath of God," with blood rising to horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 12:19 — "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord" — confirms God's exclusive right to execute judgment.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses evident truth about God, becomes vain in its imaginations, and exchanges the glory of God for created things.
- The speaker notes this should not discourage believers but rather deepen gratitude that God's grace has spared them from this judgment.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — salvation is by grace through faith, entirely a gift, not of human effort.
- Romans 5:15 contrasts the offense of one man (Adam) bringing death with the gift of grace through one man (Jesus Christ) abounding to many.
- Grace is described as boundless precisely because it must be sufficient to cover the full weight of God's wrath.
- The speaker challenges the congregation not to let earthly concerns — finances, politics, workplace conflict — crowd out the wonder of this immeasurable gift.

### Point 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39; John 3:16)
- Romans 8:35-37: tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, and sword cannot separate believers from the love of Christ — in all these things we are "more than conquerors."
- Romans 8:38-39: on the spiritual side, neither death, life, angels, principalities, powers, height, nor depth can separate believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- The speaker identifies a two-pronged attack by the enemy — physical circumstances attacking our relationship with Christ, and spiritual forces attacking our sense of God's love — and declares both prongs defeated.
- God's love is described in terms of his sovereign choice (election), having chosen believers before the foundation of the world.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13 — believers are not to sorrow as those without hope regarding the dead in Christ.
- The qualifier for the rapture (v. 14): belief that Jesus died and rose again.
- The dead in Christ rise first; then living believers are caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 is offered as a preview of what the trumpet will say: "Come up hither."
- The speaker distinguishes the pre-tribulation rapture of the Church from other rapture events in Revelation, rejecting the view that the Church goes through the tribulation.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the rapture is that it might come before he has done more for God.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4)
- Revelation 20:11-12 — the Great White Throne Judgment: earth and heaven flee from God's face; the dead are judged from the books; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — in the new heaven and new earth there is no more pain, sorrow, death, or tears.
- The devil, sin, and all darkness are cast away permanently; Jesus himself is the light of the new world.
- The universe as we know it is remade entirely.

### Conclusion and Challenge
- Are you prepared to be among those who will be there?
- Have you exchanged the temporal things of this world for the eternal?
- Are you ready to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
