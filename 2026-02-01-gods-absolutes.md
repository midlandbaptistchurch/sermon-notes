## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six guaranteed truths — "absolutes" — about God drawn from Scripture, spanning God's character, his coming judgment, and his promises to believers. The sermon moves from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and wrath to the deeply encouraging promises of his grace, love, the rapture, and his ultimate victory over death and evil. The goal is to lift the spirits of the congregation and anchor them in unchanging biblical certainties.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across Old and New Testaments, anchored in Exodus 20, Romans 1 and 8, Ephesians 2, 1 Thessalonians 4, and Revelation 4, 14, 20–21.

## Key Themes
- God's jealousy as a righteous attribute, not a negative trait
- The certainty of God's coming wrath upon sin and unrighteousness
- Salvation by grace through faith as the only escape from that wrath
- The inseparable love of God toward believers
- The pre-tribulation rapture as a living hope for the church
- God's final and total victory over death, hell, and the devil

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God
- Text: Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14
- The word "jealous" has been corrupted by culture to seem purely negative; in Scripture it is closely tied to rightly valuing what belongs to you.
- Notably, Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's *names*.
- God's jealousy is a warning against placing other things — athletes, celebrities, cultural idols — before him, especially relevant in an age of celebrity worship.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that misplaced jealousy in humans is destructive, contrasting with God's righteous jealousy.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Texts: Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23; Romans 12:19 ("Vengeance is mine, saith the Lord")
- God's wrath is real, stored up, and will be poured out — illustrated vividly by the wine press of God's wrath in Revelation 14.
- The imagery of Isaiah 13 — hearts melting, faces as flames, darkened sun and moon — points to an inescapable divine judgment.
- Romans 1 identifies the root cause of wrath: mankind suppressing the truth of God they already know, becoming vain in their imaginations.
- This point is framed not to discourage but to make God's grace all the more wondrous by contrast.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You
- Texts: Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15
- Salvation is entirely a gift of God — "by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves."
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: one man (Adam) brought death; one man (Jesus Christ) brought grace abounding to many.
- Vasold challenges the congregation not to lose sight of this immeasurable gift amid the everyday concerns of mortgages, politics, and inconveniences.
- Grace is described as boundless precisely because it must be sufficient to cover the full weight of God's wrath.

### Point 4 — God Loves You
- Text: Romans 8:35-39
- Nothing — tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword, angels, principalities, powers, height, or depth — can separate believers from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- Vasold notes a two-pronged attack: earthly things try to separate us from the love of *Christ*; spiritual forces try to separate us from the love of *God*. Both fail.
- God's love is connected to his choosing of believers before the foundation of the world.
- The loss of love is one of life's deepest heartaches — yet God's love is permanent and grows even closer at death.

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You
- Texts: 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1
- The rapture is described as a pre-tribulation event; Vasold explicitly rejects the view that the church will go through the tribulation.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 is read in full: the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are caught up in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" — is offered as a preview of the voice and trumpet that will summon the church.
- Vasold expresses both eagerness for the rapture and a personal desire to have lived more fully for God before it occurs.
- The rapture is presented as a source of comfort amid the world's trials and as the moment when believers will be reunited with loved ones.

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War
- Texts: Revelation 20:11-14; Revelation 21:4
- The Great White Throne Judgment: the dead, small and great, stand before God; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain — is held out as the ultimate destination for believers.
- The new heaven and new earth will be a world without night, with Jesus himself as its light.
- Death, hell, the devil, and sin will be permanently removed, and believers will commune face to face with their Savior.

### Conclusion and Challenge
Vasold closes with a personal challenge to the congregation:
- Are you prepared to be among those present in eternity?
- Have you exchanged the temporal things of this world for the eternal?
- Are you ready to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
