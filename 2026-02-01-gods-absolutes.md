## Summary
Riley Vasold presents six "absolutes" — guaranteed, unchanging truths about God — drawn from across Scripture. Moving from God's jealousy and coming wrath to his saving grace, inseparable love, the promise of the rapture, and his ultimate victory over death and hell, the sermon aims to anchor believers with confidence and joy, especially in discouraging times.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across Exodus, Isaiah, Romans, Ephesians, 1 Thessalonians, and Revelation — each selected to illustrate one of six absolute attributes or acts of God.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only sufficient answer to that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from believers
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately and completely win over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### Absolute 1 — God Is a Jealous God (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- The word "jealous" carries a positive biblical meaning tied to rightly valuing what belongs to you, particularly in a covenant relationship.
- Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the Lord, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as substitute gods, echoing the idolatry warned against in the Old Testament.
- Jealousy over the wrong things is destructive (Proverbs 6:34), but God's jealousy is righteous.

### Absolute 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Revelation 14:17-20; Romans 1:18-23)
- Isaiah 13 describes the Day of the Lord as a time of destruction, hearts melting, and the sun and moon going dark — a direct act of the God who placed them.
- Revelation 14 pictures God's wrath as a great winepress: the earth's harvest cast in and trampled, with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 grounds the reason for wrath: humanity suppressed the truth of God plainly visible in creation, became vain in imagination, and exchanged the glory of God for corruptible images.
- God's wrath is not a cause for despair among believers but a reminder of what grace has spared them from and a guarantee of justice for those who reject Christ.

### Absolute 3 — God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- Ephesians 2:8 — *"For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God."* Grace is the only exit from God's wrath.
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: one man (Adam) brought death, and one man (Jesus Christ) brought abounding grace to many.
- Vasold challenges the congregation not to let daily concerns — mortgages, politics, workplace frustrations — crowd out appreciation for this immeasurable gift.
- Grace is boundless precisely because the wrath it covers is so severe.

### Absolute 4 — God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39)
- Romans 8:35-39 lists two categories of threats to believers' connection with God: earthly/physical things (tribulation, famine, sword) that would separate from the love of *Christ*, and spiritual things (principalities, powers, height, depth) that would separate from the love of *God*.
- Neither category succeeds — believers are declared "more than conquerors."
- God's love predates creation; he chose believers before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1 concept referenced).
- Even death cannot sever this love — it only draws believers closer to God.

### Absolute 5 — God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17 reveals the rapture: the Lord descends with a shout and the trump of God; the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are caught up together to meet the Lord in the air.
- The qualifier for participation: believing that Jesus died and rose again (v. 14).
- Revelation 4:1 is offered as a preview of the rapture call — a trumpet voice saying *"Come up hither."*
- Vasold expressed personal anticipation of reunion with departed saints and a desire to have lived faithfully before that moment arrives.
- The rapture is presented as pre-tribulational — distinct from other rapture-like events in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000).
- No prophecy sets a date; it is imminent and could happen at any moment.

### Absolute 6 — God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-14; Revelation 21:4)
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Revelation 20:11-14): the dead, small and great, stand before God; the books and the Book of Life are opened; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 promises the new world: no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain — all former things passed away.
- The devil, sin, darkness, and the present universe are done away with; a new heaven and new earth remain, lit by Jesus himself.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among those present? Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones? Are you ready to put on immortality?
