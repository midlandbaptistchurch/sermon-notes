## Summary
Speaking on a Sunday evening in February, Riley Vasold presents six "absolutes" — unchanging guarantees about God — drawn from across Scripture. Beginning with God's jealousy and wrath, he moves through God's saving grace and unfailing love, the promise of the rapture, and God's ultimate victory over sin and death. The sermon is intended to encourage and comfort believers through the difficulties of everyday life by anchoring them to eternal certainties.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus through Revelation, with particular emphasis on Exodus 20:5; Romans 1, 5, and 8; Ephesians 2:8; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; and Revelation 14, 20, and 21.

## Key Themes
- God is a jealous God who tolerates no rivals
- God's wrath against sin is real and will be fulfilled
- God's grace is the only and sufficient answer to that wrath
- God's love is inseparable from believers
- The rapture is a certain, imminent hope for the church
- God will ultimately win the war over sin, death, and hell

## Notes

### 1. God Is Jealous (Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14)
- God identifies himself by the very name "Jealous" (Exodus 34:14).
- Unlike worldly jealousy, God's jealousy reflects his rightful claim on his people's devotion.
- Modern culture elevates athletes and celebrities as idols, echoing the idol worship condemned in Scripture.
- Proverbs 6:34 shows how jealousy over the wrong things is destructive; God's jealousy is righteous.

### 2. God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled (Isaiah 13:6-9; Romans 1:18-23; Revelation 14:17-20)
- God's wrath is not abstract — Isaiah 13 describes hearts melting and faces flushed at the coming Day of the Lord.
- Revelation 14 presents the vivid image of the "great wine press of the wrath of God," with blood flowing for 1,600 furlongs.
- Romans 1:18-23 explains the reason for wrath: humanity suppresses the truth of God despite clear evidence in creation.
- "Vengeance is mine, I will repay, saith the Lord" (Romans 12:19, referenced as Romans 13 in the sermon).
- This wrath should cause believers to be grateful for their redemption, not fearful.

### 3. God's Grace Saves You (Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15)
- "For by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God" (Ephesians 2:8).
- Romans 5:15 contrasts Adam's offense with the free gift of grace through Jesus Christ — one man brought sin, one man brought redemption.
- Grace is boundless precisely because it must cover the full extent of God's wrath.
- The speaker challenges believers not to let everyday anxieties (finances, politics, relationships) crowd out the wonder of grace.

### 4. God Loves You (Romans 8:35-39; John 3:16)
- Romans 8:35-39 lists both earthly trials (tribulation, famine, sword) and spiritual forces (principalities, powers) that attempt to separate believers from God's love — and declares all of them insufficient.
- Believers are "more than conquerors" through Christ, recovering more than Adam lost.
- Paul's personal persuasion: nothing in all creation can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- God's love, understood as his chosen preference for his people from before the foundation of the world, should be a source of deep joy.

### 5. God Will Rapture You (1 Thessalonians 4:13-17; Revelation 4:1)
- The rapture is imminent — no prophecy must be fulfilled before it occurs.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:14 qualifies participants: those who believe that Jesus died and rose again.
- Believers will be reunited with loved ones who have died in Christ.
- Revelation 4:1 — "Come up hither" — offers a preview of the voice that will call the church home.
- The speaker affirms a pre-tribulation rapture, distinguishing the church's rapture from events in Revelation.
- Personal reflection: the speaker's only concern about the rapture is that it might come before he has done more for God.

### 6. God Will Win the War (Revelation 20:11-12; Revelation 21:4)
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Revelation 20:11-12) depicts every person judged according to the books, with death and hell themselves cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 promises no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain in the new creation.
- The dark, finite, corruptible universe will give way to a new heaven and new earth, lit by Jesus himself.
- The enemy, sin, and all that separates believers from God will be permanently removed.

### Conclusion
Vasold closes with three questions as a personal challenge:
1. Are you prepared to be among those present in eternity?
2. Have you given up temporal things in exchange for eternal ones?
3. Are you prepared to cast off mortality and put on immortality?
