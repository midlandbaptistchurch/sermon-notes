## Summary
Speaker Riley Vasold presents six guaranteed truths — "absolutes" — about God's character and plan, drawing from both Old and New Testaments. Moving from the sobering realities of God's jealousy and coming wrath to the comforting promises of grace, love, the rapture, and ultimate victory, the sermon aims to encourage believers during a spiritually and seasonally dark time. The message calls listeners to hold fast to these eternal certainties rather than the temporary concerns of everyday life.

## Scripture
Multiple passages spanning Exodus through Revelation, anchoring each of the six absolutes in direct biblical text. Key verses include Exodus 20:5 (God's jealousy), Isaiah 13:6–9 (the Day of the Lord), Ephesians 2:8 (grace through faith), Romans 8:35–39 (inseparable love of God), 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 (the rapture), and Revelation 20:11–12 (the Great White Throne judgment).

## Key Themes
- God's jealousy as a righteous attribute, not a flaw
- The certainty and justice of God's coming wrath
- Salvation by grace alone as the only escape from that wrath
- The inseparable, unconditional love of God toward believers
- The imminent rapture as a living hope for the church
- God's final, total victory over death, hell, and the devil

## Notes

### Point 1 — God Is a Jealous God
- Text: Exodus 20:5; Exodus 34:14
- The word "jealous" is reclaimed from its negative cultural connotation; in Scripture it reflects God's rightful claim on His people, much like a spouse's rightful claim on their partner.
- Notably, Exodus 34:14 reveals that "Jealous" is actually one of God's names: *"the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God."*
- Application: Modern idolatry — particularly the glorification of athletes and celebrities — mirrors ancient Roman hero-worship and provokes God's jealousy.
- Proverbs 6:34 is cited to show that misplaced jealousy can be a destructive, wrathful force — contrasting with God's holy jealousy.

### Point 2 — God's Wrath Will Be Fulfilled
- Texts: Isaiah 13:6–9; Revelation 14:17–20; Romans 1:18–23; Romans 12:19 ("Vengeance is mine, I will repay")
- God's stored-up wrath is compared to human frustration that builds until it finally erupts — but God's wrath is righteous and just, not sinful.
- The vivid image of the "great winepress of the wrath of God" (Rev. 14:19–20) — blood flowing to the height of horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs — is used to convey the magnitude of coming judgment.
- Romans 1:18–23 grounds the reason for wrath: humanity has suppressed the truth of God that is evident in creation, becoming vain in its imaginations.
- Intended not to discourage, but to magnify the wonder of grace and to assure believers that God is perfectly just.

### Point 3 — God's Grace Saves You
- Texts: Ephesians 2:8; Romans 5:15
- Grace is the only sufficient answer to God's wrath; the two must be understood together.
- Ephesians 2:8 — "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God."
- Romans 5:15 draws the parallel: one man (Adam) brought death; one man (Jesus Christ) brought abounding grace to many.
- The speaker challenges believers not to lose sight of this "immeasurable, boundless gift" amid daily worries — mortgages, car troubles, workplace conflicts, politics.
- Grace is described as limitless precisely because God's wrath, though enormous, is finite; grace is sufficient to cover it entirely.

### Point 4 — God Loves You
- Text: Romans 8:35–39
- Two categories of things attempt to separate believers from God's love:
  - *Earthly/physical* things (tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, sword) try to separate us from the love of *Christ*.
  - *Spiritual* things (principalities, powers, height, depth) try to separate us from the love of *God*.
- Paul's conclusion: neither category succeeds. Believers are inseparable from the love of God in Christ Jesus.
- Believers are described as "more than conquerors" — exceeding even what Adam was created to be before the Fall.
- God's love is also framed in terms of His eternal choice: He chose believers before the foundation of the world (referencing election).

### Point 5 — God Will Rapture You
- Texts: 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17; Revelation 4:1
- The rapture is presented as an imminent, unprophesied event — no specific sign precedes it, so it could occur at any moment.
- 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 outlines the sequence: the dead in Christ rise first, then living believers are "caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air."
- Revelation 4:1 — John's vision of the open door in heaven and the trumpet voice saying "Come up hither" is offered as a preview of what the church will hear at the rapture.
- The speaker expresses personal longing to see departed loved ones and biblical/historical heroes of the faith (Paul, Tyndale, George Washington), coupled with a holy humility about his own worthiness.
- A pre-tribulation rapture position is affirmed; the idea that believers must endure the tribulation is described as a corruption of Scripture.
- Distinction is made between the church's rapture and other "raptures" described later in Revelation (e.g., the 144,000).

### Point 6 — God Will Win the War
- Texts: Revelation 20:11–12; Revelation 21:4
- The Great White Throne Judgment (Rev. 20:11–12): the dead, small and great, stand before God; books are opened; death and hell are cast into the lake of fire.
- Revelation 21:4 — the new heaven and new earth: no more death, sorrow, crying, or pain; the former things passed away.
- God's final victory means the total removal of death, hell, the devil, and sin — cast as far away as possible.
- The new world will be lit by Jesus Himself; darkness and the cold, finite universe will be gone.
- Closing challenge: Are you prepared to be among the company of those who will be there? Have you exchanged temporal things for eternal ones? Are you ready to "cast off mortality to put on immortality"?
