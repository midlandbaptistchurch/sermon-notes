## Summary
Jay McCrum discusses the essential pillars of a Christian foundation: the King James Bible, rightly dividing the Word of God through 2 Timothy 2:15, and salvation by grace through faith. He explains how to maintain and protect this foundation through spiritual discipline, and provides practical approaches for engaging with Christians who lack a solid biblical foundation.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 3:10-11 — Paul describes himself as a wise master builder who laid a foundation in Jesus Christ, upon which all other building must be based.

## Key Themes
- The King James Bible as the authoritative Word of God
- Rightly dividing Scripture to understand Paul's distinct ministry
- Salvation by grace through faith, not works
- Spiritual disciplines needed to maintain a strong foundation
- Identifying and addressing contradictions that arise from improper biblical interpretation
- The importance of doctrine in Christian growth

## Notes

### First Pillar: The King James Bible
- The KJV preserves the ability to define biblical words correctly (e.g., "perfect" means "complete")
- Non-KJV translations can obscure eternal truths (e.g., Micah 5:2 on Christ's eternality; 1 Corinthians 1:18 on eternal security)
- Only the King James Bible maintains consistency without contradictions
- Satan uses subtlety to confuse Christians through multiple Bible versions
- Many men died translating the KJV; none died for modern versions
- A solid, non-contradicting foundation is essential for effective Bible study and ministry

### Second Pillar: Rightly Dividing the Word (2 Timothy 2:15)
- Paul is our distinct apostle and minister for today
- The mystery—the Body of Christ and our sealing in Christ—belongs to Paul's doctrine
- Doctrine is foundational; it appears first in Paul's lists (2 Timothy 3:10)
- All Scripture is given by inspiration and profitable for doctrine (2 Timothy 3:16)
- Understanding Paul's unique ministry prevents confusion about salvation requirements

### Third Pillar: Salvation by Grace Through Faith
- Salvation is "not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8-9)
- The gospel is: Christ died, was buried, and rose the third day (1 Corinthians 15:1-4)
- Believers are sealed by the Holy Spirit as a down payment (2 Corinthians 1:22; Ephesians 1:13)
- We are "once saved, always saved" because of Christ's work alone
- The world pressures churches to replace doctrine with emotional comfort (2 Timothy 4:3-4)

### Maintaining Your Foundation
- **Work**: Good works are evidence of salvation but not the means of it (Ephesians 2:10)
- **Walk in the Spirit**: Daily submission to God's Spirit (Galatians 5:16)
- **Study**: Gaining knowledge, understanding, and wisdom through Scripture
- **Enlightenment**: Ask God for wisdom and revelation to understand His ways (Ephesians 1:16-19)
- **Cleansing**: Constantly purifying yourself from spiritual filthiness (2 Corinthians 7:1)
- **Self-examination**: Examine yourself daily to see if you're walking worthy (2 Corinthians 13:5)
- **Consistency**: Prove yourself through daily obedience and transformed behavior (Romans 12:1-2)
- **Avoid leaven**: Even small sins accumulate and corrupt the whole foundation (Galatians 5:9)
- **Prayer and boldness**: Continue praying for boldness even after years of service (Ephesians 6:18)

### Engaging Christians Without a Solid Foundation
When discussing Scripture with those lacking a proper foundation, ask clarifying questions:

- "Do you believe your Bible literally, or do you rely on your preacher to spiritualize verses?"
- "Why Paul?" — Show from Ephesians 3:1 and other passages that the gospel of grace was committed to Paul
- "How many gospels are there?" — Compare Christ's earthly gospel (for the circumcision) with Paul's gospel (for the Gentiles) using Romans 15:8 and 2 Corinthians 12:2
- Point out biblical contradictions that only make sense when properly divided:
  - Matthew 19:21 (sell all) vs. 1 Timothy 5:8 (provide for family)
  - Matthew 28:19 (teach all things) vs. our gospel of grace
  - James 5:1 (woe to the rich in tribulation) vs. 1 Timothy 6:17-18 (richness in good works)

Note: These questions are designed not to convince immediately but to plant seeds of doubt about unrightly divided theology, encouraging deeper personal study.
