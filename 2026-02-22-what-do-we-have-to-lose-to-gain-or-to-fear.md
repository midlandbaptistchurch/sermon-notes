## Summary
Pastor Simpson challenges believers to move beyond mere knowledge of biblical truth to genuine perception and understanding of eternity. He explores how our view of the coming heavenly realm should transform our priorities and values in this life, emphasizing that while salvation cannot be lost, our inheritance and eternal reward can be forfeited through disobedience and neglect of sanctification.

## Scripture
Romans 8:1; 1 Corinthians 6:2-3; Colossians 3:23-25; Ephesians 5:5-6; Galatians 5:19-21; Matthew 25:14-30

## Key Themes
- The difference between knowing truth and perceiving truth
- Saints judging the world and angels in eternity
- Loss of inheritance through disobedience
- The seriousness of works at the judgment seat of Christ
- Walking in the Spirit versus walking in the flesh
- The wrath of God upon children of disobedience
- The smallness of earthly concerns compared to eternity
- Personal accountability before God for our stewardship

## Notes

### The Levels of Spiritual Understanding
Pastor Simpson emphasizes a progression in understanding Scripture: from knowledge (gaining information) to perception (seeing and understanding) to reception (getting it personally) to giving (sharing it with others). He references Proverbs 1:2-4 to illustrate these levels.

### Saints Will Judge the World and Angels
From 1 Corinthians 6:2-3, believers are told they will judge both the world and angels in eternity. This is a staggering reality that should affect how we view our earthly existence. The pastor speculates that the judgment seat in the clouds will allow believers to see both the heavenly kingdoms and earth from a distance, putting earthly concerns in proper perspective.

### The Distinction Between Salvation and Sanctification
While Romans 8:1 promises no condemnation to those in Christ Jesus, this must be understood in context: those who walk after the Spirit, not the flesh. There is condemnation for those saved but walking in the flesh. This is not about salvation but about service and sanctification.

### Inheritance Can Be Lost
The pastor teaches that while salvation cannot be lost, inheritance can. Colossians 3:24-25 and Ephesians 5:5-6 warn of the loss of inheritance for those engaged in idolatry, covetousness, and disobedience. This reflects the wrath of God coming upon "children of disobedience"—those who know better but choose to disobey anyway.

### The Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25:14-30)
The servant who buried his talent is called "wicked and slothful" and cast into outer darkness. Though this passage refers to the tribulation period, it demonstrates God's serious attitude toward stewardship and faithfulness.

### Works and the Judgment Seat
The pastor emphasizes that our works matter in eternity. Passages speak of suffering loss, of the wrath of God, of inheritance being withheld—all addressed to believers. God takes seriously what we do with the time, talents, and truth He gives us.

### Personal Illustrations
Pastor Simpson uses personal examples with his children (Eli and Zach) to illustrate the concept of obedience and disobedience, showing how even small acts of stewardship matter and affect rewards.
