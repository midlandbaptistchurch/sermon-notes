## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson explores Paul's declaration of confidence in the Corinthian church, examining what actions and attitudes led Paul to make this bold statement. Through a study of 2 Corinthians 7, he highlights the importance of godly sorrow, genuine repentance, and the transformative power of God's Word in producing spiritual maturity and revival in believers' lives.

## Scripture
2 Corinthians 7:1-16 — Paul writes to the church at Corinth following reports from Titus about their response to a corrective letter, ultimately expressing his confidence in them.

## Key Themes
- Confidence built through genuine repentance and spiritual growth
- Godly sorrow versus worldly sorrow
- Personal responsibility for cleansing ourselves from filthiness after salvation
- The power of God's Word to transform hearts and produce change
- The importance of spiritual discernment and mourning over sin
- The role of bold, loving correction in church leadership

## Notes

### Cleansing and Personal Responsibility (v. 1)
- After salvation, believers have a responsibility to cleanse themselves from filthiness of flesh and spirit
- This is distinct from justification—we are forgiven completely in Christ, but must walk in holiness
- Repentance (turning from sin) differs from mere confession (acknowledgment of sin)
- True cleansing involves replacing wrong actions with right ones, not just stopping wrong behavior

### What Titus Reported (v. 7)
Three key indicators of the Corinthians' spiritual health that brought Paul comfort:

1. **Earnest Desire** — An invested, committed desire (like an earnest payment or down payment) showing they had "bought into" the truth of God's Word
2. **Mourning** — Spiritual concern and grief over sin and spiritual condition, not just surface sorrow
3. **Fervent Mind** — A zealous, fiery devotion to God's truth and Paul's teachings

### Godly Sorrow vs. Worldly Sorrow (v. 9-10)
- **Worldly sorrow**: Sorrow over being caught, embarrassment, or consequences—leads to death spiritually
- **Godly sorrow**: Sorrow that comes from seeing sin as God sees it, understanding its cost (Christ's death), leading to genuine repentance and spiritual revival

### The Process of Repentance (v. 11)
Seven aspects of true repentance:

1. **Carefulness** — Haste and eagerness to make things right
2. **Clearing of yourselves** — Taking responsibility with vigor
3. **Indignation** — Developing anger toward sin itself, not just its consequences
4. **Fear** — Holy fear of returning to sin and living in a sin-cursed body
5. **Vehement desire** — A powerful, overwhelming desire to turn from wrong to right
6. **Zeal** — Active, passionate pursuit of righteousness
7. **Revenge** — Redirecting that desire for justice against one's own disobedience through obedience

### The Role of God's Word
- The Corinthians were moved to repentance by reading Paul's letter—God's written Word
- Believers today have access to the complete written Word and should treat it with similar reverence
- True change comes from allowing Scripture to saturate our hearts and reshape our thinking
- We must achieve both saturation in God's Word and separation from worldly influence

### Paul's Confidence (v. 16)
Paul's final statement—"I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things"—came because:
- The church genuinely repented and pursued holiness
- Their repentance was motivated by Scripture, not external pressure
- They demonstrated spiritual maturity through their response to correction
- Their actions brought comfort and encouragement to both Titus and Paul

### Application for Believers
- Mourning over sin is a spiritual discipline that should characterize the Christian life
- We should regularly examine ourselves through God's perspective, not our own comfort
- True biblical repentance requires more than confession—it requires aggressive pursuit of holiness
- We should value God's Word as highly as the Corinthians valued Paul's letter
- Bold, loving correction that points us to Scripture is a gift and should be received as such
