## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson continues a series on the Biblical Timeline, walking through key pre-Genesis events, the elevation of the Jewish people beginning in Genesis 11–12, the age of grace as a parenthetical mystery, and the end-times sequence including the tribulation, millennium, and great white throne judgment. The session also briefly explores Romans 12:3 and the concept of "thinking soberly" in the context of functioning as members of the body of Christ.

## Scripture
Multiple passages referenced throughout, including Genesis 1–2, Genesis 11–12, Job 38, Jeremiah 31, Matthew 28, Mark 16, Ephesians 2, Romans 12:1–3, and Revelation 18. No single primary text was designated.

## Key Themes
- The Biblical Timeline as a teaching and reference tool
- Pre-Genesis events: creation of Lucifer, the angels, the earth, the rebellion, and the creation of hell
- Genesis 11–12 as the pivotal transition to God's program through the Jews
- The age of grace as a parenthetical mystery hidden in Old Testament prophecy
- The tribulation period as focused on God's program for the Jewish people
- The Great Commission in Matthew 28 and Mark 16 as commissions for the tribulation period and millennial kingdom
- The new covenant (Jeremiah 31) fulfilled in the millennial kingdom
- End-times sequence: rapture, judgment seat of Christ, seven-year tribulation, second coming, millennium, battle of Gog and Magog, great white throne judgment
- Romans 12:3 — thinking soberly in the context of the body of Christ

## Notes
- Pastor Simpson references a specific reference book (unnamed in transcript) that he credits with establishing his foundational understanding of the Biblical Timeline. He recommends the "expanded edition" Kindle version and cautions against the "prophetic edition."
- **Pre-Genesis Timeline (before Genesis 1:2):** Drawing from Job 38:4–7, Simpson outlines the sequence: creation of Lucifer → creation of angels → creation of the earth → angelic rejoicing → angelic habitation on earth → rebellion → creation of hell → divine judgment → earth left without form and void (Genesis 1:2).
- **Genesis 11–12 Transition:** God elevates the Jewish people through Abraham (Abram). From this point, Gentiles could only reach God by being proselytized into Judaism. This elevation is represented on the timeline as an upward-slanting line.
- **The Age of Grace:** Depicted in parentheses on the timeline because it was a mystery hidden from Old Testament prophecy and not revealed until Paul's writings (Romans through Philemon are addressed to the church in this age).
- **The Four Gospels:** Matthew presents Christ as King, Mark as Servant, Luke as Man, John as God/Deity.
- **The Great Commission:** Matthew 28 commission is understood as given for those who will enter the millennial kingdom under the new covenant, going out to reach the nations. Mark 16's commission (including signs such as taking up serpents) is understood as applying to tribulation-period believers.
- **The Millennium:** Christ reigns on earth; the new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31–34) is fulfilled with the house of Israel and Judah. Nations come to worship in Jerusalem. The sin curse is lifted. Near the end of the millennium, Satan is loosed, deceives the nations, gathers an army, and the battle of Gog and Magog occurs.
- **Great White Throne Judgment** follows the millennium and is distinct from the Judgment Seat of Christ (which occurs at/after the rapture).
- **Romans 12:3 — Thinking Soberly:** Simpson concludes with an insight from sermon preparation: Paul's instruction not to think of oneself more highly but to "think soberly" is specifically about functioning properly within whatever role one occupies as a member of the body of Christ — whether teacher or student, head or body, husband or wife, parent or child — rather than being a general exhortation against pride.
- The approximate Biblical timeline spans: ~1,700 years from Genesis 1:2 to Genesis 11–12; ~2,000 years from Abraham to Christ; ~2,000 years from Christ to the present; followed by the seven-year tribulation and 1,000-year millennium.
- Pastor Simpson notes the transition books of Matthew, Acts, and Hebrews as bridging different dispensational periods.
