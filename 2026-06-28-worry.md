## Summary
In this Sunday morning message, evangelist Pat McCloskey draws from Matthew 6 to challenge believers not to let worry rob them of their daily joy. Using personal testimony and Scripture, he calls the congregation to seek God's kingdom first and trust that He will provide, rather than allowing anxiety over earthly concerns to crowd out faith and peace.

## Scripture
Matthew 6:22-34 — Jesus teaches about having a single, focused eye, the impossibility of serving two masters, and the command not to take anxious thought for food, clothing, or tomorrow, culminating in the call to "seek ye first the kingdom of God and his righteousness."

## Key Themes
- Worry is one of the greatest thieves of Christian joy
- The devil uses anxiety about earthly needs to rob believers of present happiness
- God calls His people to singular focus — a "single eye" fixed on His light
- Seeking God's kingdom first is the antidote to worry
- Trusting God means getting ourselves out of the way and letting His light in
- God's peace surpasses understanding and comes in His presence

## Notes
- **Worry steals joy:** McCloskey opens with a direct warning that worry — about jobs, houses, bills, food, clothes — is the devil's primary tool to steal a Christian's joy and happiness. The devil does not need your possessions; he wants your joy.
- **Matthew 6:22-34 as the foundation:** The "single eye" passage is used to illustrate the importance of spiritual focus. When we look in multiple directions (toward our worries rather than toward God), we lose focus just as Peter did when he took his eyes off Jesus while walking on water.
- **Seek first the kingdom (v. 33):** McCloskey emphasizes that God's promise is clear — all necessary things will be added when believers prioritize His kingdom. Worry is a symptom of misplaced priorities.
- **We block the light:** Using the analogy of trying to scan a QR code on a phone, McCloskey illustrates how Christians often stand in the way of God's light by insisting on telling God how to handle their problems rather than stepping aside and trusting Him.
- **Personal testimony:** McCloskey shares a detailed account of a season of intense worry — facing the death of a brother-in-law, his father-in-law's stroke, uncertainty about relocating from Indiana to Georgia, and the financial pressures of finding a home in a changed housing market. Waking at 2:30 a.m. overwhelmed with anxiety, he went to his recliner in their motor coach and began to pray and sing. The song "In the Presence of Jehovah" brought him God's peace. Within three months, God provided a house and the path forward became clear.
- **Psalm 118:24 referenced:** "This is the day that the Lord hath made; I will rejoice and be glad in it" — cited as a reminder that each day is a gift from God not to be wasted in worry.
- **Philippians 4 alluded to:** God gives a peace that passes all understanding.
- **Closing appeal:** McCloskey invited the congregation to the altar to surrender their worries to God and to seek His kingdom first; he also extended an invitation to salvation for any who did not know Christ.
