## Summary
Youth Pastor Andrew Watt challenges the congregation to truly remember and honor the sacrifice of Jesus Christ, drawing parallels between how we commemorate human sacrifice and how we should approach the Lord's Supper. He emphasizes that the death of Christ—the blood of God shed for our sins—deserves our deepest reverence and constant remembrance, not mere tradition.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 15:1-4 — Paul declares the gospel: Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures, was buried, and rose again the third day.

Additional passages:
- 1 Corinthians 11:26 — "For as often as you eat this bread and drink this cup you do show the Lord's death till he come."
- Acts 20:28 — The church purchased with God's own blood.
- Ephesians 2:8-14 — Saved by grace through faith; made nigh by the blood of Christ.

## Key Themes
- The importance of remembrance and not forgetting
- The sacrifice and blood of Christ as central to redemption
- Approaching the Lord's Supper with reverence, not tradition
- The balance of God's love, justice, and wrath
- Personal conviction and examination before communion

## Notes

### The Problem of Forgetting
Andrew opens by noting that we are "prone to wander" and forget important things. He contrasts how we seek to remember temporal things—donuts paid for by strangers, organ donors, memorial services—yet often fail to remember the eternal sacrifice of Christ's death.

### The Donut Illustration
A personal story about discovering that someone had anonymously paid for donuts serves as an entry point. If we want to know who paid for a dozen donuts, how much more should we seek to remember and honor the One who paid for our salvation with His blood?

### The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier
Andrew recounts visiting Arlington Cemetery as an eighth grader and laying a wreath at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. He notes:
- 90,000 people came to the first unknown soldier's funeral
- The tomb was protected 24/7 starting in 1937
- We establish memorials so people don't forget the sacrifice made
- This protection and remembrance demonstrates the importance society places on honoring sacrifice

He asks: "How much protection, honor, reverence, attention, and remembrance do we give to the sacrifice of Jesus?"

### The Lord's Supper as Remembrance
The command in 1 Corinthians 11:26 to "show the Lord's death" should be taken seriously. Andrew laments that the Lord's Supper can become mere tradition—"just another thing we do"—rather than a heartfelt act of remembrance.

He shares a personal moment when, while leading a song after communion, he saw church members weeping as they remembered Christ's blood. This convicted him that he had been going through the motions without genuine remembrance.

### The Blood of God
Andrew emphasizes the radical truth that it was "the blood of God" shed for us (Acts 20:28), not merely the blood of a man. This is the highest sacrifice possible—God Himself paying the price for our redemption.

### Personal Sin and Divine Forgiveness
Andrew reflects on his own sins and failures, including unkindness to a girl in his youth group that may have caused her church hurt. While we wish to forget our sins, we should desperately want to remember the cross where those sins were paid for.

### The Balance of God's Attributes
God is simultaneously:
- A God of love (showing grace)
- A God of justice and righteousness
- A God of wrath

Christ's death resolved the tension between these attributes, allowing God to be both just and merciful.

### A Call to Remember, Not Forget
Andrew challenges believers to pray: "God, help me remember the nails, help me remember the blood, help me remember the mockery, help me remember Barabbas, help me remember the crown of thorns, help me remember the prophecies fulfilled."

He contrasts the attention given to Christmas (the virgin birth) and Easter (the resurrection) with the need to remember the cross in between—the death itself.

### The Three-Fold Look at Communion
Quoting 1 Corinthians 11:26-28, the pastor explains communion involves:
1. **Backward look** — Remembering the cross and Christ's death
2. **Upward look** — "Until he comes" (future hope)
3. **Inward look** — Self-examination before partaking
