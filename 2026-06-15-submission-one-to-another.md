## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a verse-by-verse study through Ephesians, focusing on Ephesians 5:21 and the call to submit ourselves to one another in the fear of God. He unpacks the meaning of submission — tracing it from our submission to the Lord, to His Word, to God-ordained roles, and finally to one another within the body of Christ. Drawing on Christ's own example of humble submission in Philippians 2, Simpson encourages believers to remember, obey, and even "salute" (embrace with love) those in authority over them, with the reminder that submission in God's program ultimately leads to exaltation.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:21 — "Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God." This verse closes Paul's list of Spirit-filled principles before he transitions into teaching on specific relational roles (husband/wife, parent/child, master/servant).

## Key Themes
- Submission begins with submitting to the Lord, then to His Word, then to God-ordained roles, then to one another
- The sin-cursed nature resists submission — illustrated by a child's instinctive "no"
- Christ is the supreme example of humble submission (Philippians 2:3–8; 2 Corinthians 5:21)
- Three practical responses to those in authority: Remember them, Obey them, Salute (embrace) them (Hebrews 13:7, 17, 24)
- Submission is tied to allowing the peace of God to rule in our hearts and the Word of Christ to dwell in us richly (Colossians 3:15–16)
- Submission in God's program leads to exaltation (Philippians 2:9–11)

## Notes
- Simpson opens by recapping previous weeks in Ephesians 5: the importance of thankfulness and the need for peace as a foundation for gratitude; Paul's practice of praying for and being thankful for specific individuals.
- **Ephesians 5:21** is identified as the capstone verse of Paul's list of Spirit-filled principles, bridging into the household code that follows.
- **Defining submission:** to rank underneath, to be obedient, to be under authority, to be respectful in action and attitude — not merely outward compliance but a matter of heart and mind.
- **1 Kings 10:6–8 (Queen of Sheba):** The queen marveled not just at Solomon's wealth but at the *happiness* of his servants. This sets a goal: joyful submission, not merely reluctant obedience.
- **Philippians 2:3–8:** Christ's humiliation — taking the form of a servant, humbling himself to death on the cross, submitting to unjust human authority — is the defining model for believers.
- **2 Corinthians 5:21:** The deepest act of Christ's submission was bearing our sin.
- **Colossians 3:15–16 (parallel passage):** Letting the peace of God rule and the Word of Christ dwell richly both require a posture of submission; you cannot receive these without surrendering resistance.
- **Hebrews 13:7, 17, 24 — Three actions toward those in authority:**
  1. *Remember them* — bring to mind, do not take for granted what they have modeled and taught.
  2. *Obey and submit* — the practical, sometimes difficult heart of submission.
  3. *Salute them* — greet, embrace, draw close; love those who speak hard truths and hold authority over you. Illustrated by Jonathan's embrace of David after Goliath (1 Samuel 17–18): Jonathan willingly set aside his own position to draw near to what God had placed on David.
- **James 4:1:** Wars and fighting arise from internal lusts warring in our members — the opposite spirit to submission.
- **Application:** Work on being submissive this week — obey, remember why you do what you do, and go above and beyond by "saluting" (actively loving and drawing close to) those in authority, even when it is difficult.
- **Closing promise — Philippians 2:9–11:** Because Christ submitted, God highly exalted him. Submission in God's program leads to exaltation; whatever the cost here, the return will be greater on the other side.
