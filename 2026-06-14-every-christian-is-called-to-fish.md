## Summary
Speaker Jon Helmick, visiting from Crown College, calls every believer — not just pastors or ministry leaders — to take up the responsibility of soul-winning. Drawing from Matthew 4, he outlines three principles: everything done for others must be preceded by time with God, following Christ and fishing for men is God's stated priority, and as we follow Christ He progressively makes us into fishers of men.

## Scripture
Matthew 4:12-22 — Jesus walks by the Sea of Galilee, calls Simon Peter and Andrew from their nets, and declares, "Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men." He then calls James and John in the same manner.

## Key Themes
- Every Christian is called to soul-winning, not only those in formal ministry
- Time with God must precede all ministry and service
- Following Christ produces a natural burden for the lost
- Obedience to the Holy Spirit's leading in witnessing moments
- The local church as the vehicle through which God does His work
- Soul-consciousness as a lifelong posture, not just an early-Christian enthusiasm

## Notes
**Introduction**
- Helmick opens with an anecdote about the Big Rock Blue Marlin fishing competition in Eastern North Carolina — a 919-pound, 15-foot fish that won a crew $871,000 — as a hook into the biblical theme of fishing.
- He frames the message as an encouragement for the congregation's personal soul-winning lives.
- Central premise: "A true measure of a ministry must always be in our likeness to Christ" — not the size of buildings, bus routes, or attendance numbers.

**Principle 1 — The Principle: Time with God Before Ministry**
- Quotes Hudson Taylor: *"God's work done God's way will never lack God's supply."*
- "Follow me" comes before "I will make you fishers of men" — the order is intentional.
- There are no self-made spiritual men; Christ builds up those He uses.
- Uses an illustration of getting lost navigating Chicago without GPS signal — believers know they should witness but fail to follow the Holy Spirit's leading in the moment.
- Application: Every believer should find a place of service in the local church and get fully committed to it.
- Cites Psalm 37:4 — "Delight thyself also in the Lord and he shall give thee the desires of thine heart."
- Failure can drive us to Christ; the danger is that success causes us to forget Him.

**Principle 2 — The Priority: Following Christ and Fishing for Men**
- Luke 19:10 — "For the Son of man has come to seek and to save that which was lost."
- The disciples were catching fish destined to die; Jesus called them to bring spiritually dead people to life.
- Quotes Dr. Lee Robertson (via Dr. Clarence Sexton): *"Most Christians do more witnessing the first six months that they're saved than they do for the rest of their life"* — because new believers naturally prioritize following Christ and fishing for men.
- Challenge: Recapture the excitement of your own salvation as motivation to reach others.

**Principle 3 — The Progression: Christ Makes Us Fishers of Men**
- Reads from a book by Pastor Clarence Sexton:
  - If we are not fishing for men, it is because He did not make us fishers of men.
  - If He did not make us fishers of men, it is because we are not following Him.
  - If we are following Him, His passions become our passions and we will fish for men.
- Soul-winning is a supernatural work — it cannot be accomplished in one's own strength.
- Personal confession: "I'm not as conscious of souls as I should be, because Jesus Christ isn't holding the place in my life that He should."
- You can pursue the lost without following Christ, but you will see no fruit. You cannot truly follow Christ without going after the lost.
- Notes the parallel call of James and John (Matthew 4:21-22) as a second example of immediate obedience.

**Altar Call**
- Congregation challenged to come to the altar and ask God for a burden for souls, boldness in witnessing, and immediacy in obedience.
- Ties to a challenge given at a Saturday morning prayer breakfast on the same theme of soul-consciousness.
