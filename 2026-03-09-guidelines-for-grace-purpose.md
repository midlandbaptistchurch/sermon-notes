## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson explores the first of seven guidelines for grace: operating according to God's purpose. Using Ephesians 1, he emphasizes that believers are predestined with a divine purpose before the foundation of the world. Our value comes from understanding we are God's servants, stewards, and sons—heirs with royal responsibilities—called to bring glory to God through aligning our goals and desires with His eternal will.

## Scripture
Ephesians 1:1-11 — Paul's teaching on predestination, adoption, and God's purpose for believers before the creation of the world.

## Key Themes
- Operating according to God's purpose rather than worldly goals
- Understanding our threefold identity: servant, steward, and son
- Predestination and the believer's royal responsibilities as heirs
- Bringing pleasure to God through obedience and faithful choices
- God's power manifested through believers living out their divine purpose
- The importance of valuing eternal things over temporal pursuits

## Notes

### Our Identity and Value
- We possess significance, relevance, and importance in God's plan
- Three aspects of our relationship with God: servant (responsibility), steward (trust), and son (relationship with Father)
- As heirs of Christ the King, we inherit royal responsibilities in God's kingdom
- Understanding our value motivates us beyond fear to be grace-driven

### Purpose and Predestination
- God predestined our purpose before we were born or created
- Purpose is about asking: "What is my existence on earth for after salvation?" rather than just "What do I have to do?"
- Under grace, we ask "What do I get to do?" instead of viewing obedience as obligation
- Purpose-driven living aligned with grace means applying talents and goals to eternal things that glorify God

### Our Royal Responsibilities
- Like a king's heir, we must grow into understanding the responsibilities of our inheritance
- Solomon's example: taking 1,000 sacrifices on Mount Gibbon showed his serious commitment to his role as king
- We may have opportunities for royal responsibilities in eternity, but can miss them through complacency
- The spirit of adoption calls us to die to self and walk in the Spirit in this life

### Bringing God Pleasure
- God's pleasure is reflected in Ephesians 1:9 as part of His good will
- Just as a child brings pleasure to a parent through obedience and character, believers bring God pleasure through faithful service
- The example of Job: God found pleasure in Job's righteousness, even speaking of him with pride to Satan
- When believers gather in unity around God's Word, it brings God pleasure

### God's Power Manifested Today
- Under grace, God's power is manifested through believers living according to His purpose, not through supernatural displays
- God's power is shown when His creation yields to His authority and makes choices reflecting His value
- Examples: young people faithfully studying Scripture, believers prioritizing God's Kingdom over comfort, choosing God's will over worldly temptation
- When we choose God over what the world offers, we demonstrate His power is more valuable than temporal pleasures

### The Test of Purpose
- A crucial test: Can we say with Paul that we "would rather be" on God's side than have temporal abundance?
- Spending time in God's Word is essential—it must outweigh all other influences in our lives
- If this world holds more appeal than eternity with God, our purpose is misaligned
- The question becomes: What will carry more weight and influence in our decisions—God or the world?

### Personal Testimony
- Reflecting on 30 years of serving God, Pastor Simpson shares about a woman who led him to the Lord on June 19, 1991
- At age 50, he attended her funeral at age 80, realizing his spiritual birthday and her passing aligned
- The testimony demonstrates the long-term faithfulness of living out God's purpose
- Over three decades, despite struggles and difficulties, consistent service to the Lord has eternal significance
