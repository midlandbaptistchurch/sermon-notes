## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson examines Ephesians 5:19 and Colossians 3:16 to explore the proper role of music in the Christian life, emphasizing that music should be grounded in Scripture and tied to being filled with the Spirit. He warns against letting entertainment, tradition, or theologically mixed content displace the Word of God in the music believers listen to and sing. The sermon calls each believer to personal accountability in evaluating the music they consume in light of the Judgment Seat of Christ.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:19 — Paul's instruction to speak to one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in the heart to the Lord, understood within the broader passage of Ephesians 5:17–21 as a single continuous thought about being filled with the Spirit.

## Key Themes
- Music must be proved and evaluated according to Scripture
- Being filled with the Spirit is tied to the Word of Christ dwelling richly within the believer
- Psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs serve to teach and admonish one another
- Entertainment value in music must not replace theological substance
- Gospel music that mixes the earthly ministry accounts with Paul's grace message can create doctrinal confusion
- Satan has corrupted music, just as he has corrupted Bible texts, making discernment necessary
- Personal preferences in music are acceptable, but must be filtered through scriptural principles
- Believers should actively listen to the words of music, not merely enjoy the style

## Notes
- Ephesians 5:19 and Colossians 3:16 are the primary places Paul addresses music in his writings.
- Being filled with the Spirit is connected to letting the Word of Christ dwell in the believer richly — it is not a Pentecostal experience but a matter of study, yielding, and growth.
- Music is an art, and like preaching it can be entertaining; there is nothing inherently wrong with enjoyment, but when entertainment replaces spiritual substance it becomes a problem.
- Much traditional gospel music draws heavily from Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John rather than Paul's epistles, mirroring sermon patterns in average evangelical churches.
- Songs that mix the kingdom gospel of the earthly ministry accounts with the grace gospel of Paul's letters can blur important doctrinal distinctions (e.g., the woman at the well, the rich young ruler).
- Lucifer is described in Ezekiel 28 as having tabrets and pipes created within him, suggesting he had a role in heavenly music; his goal now is to pervert what was holy.
- The church works to protect two things: the King James Bible text and theologically sound music.
- Contemporary churches that adopt contemporary music tend also to adopt modern Bible translations, reflecting a pattern of accommodating culture over protecting truth.
- Believers should not merely hear music passively but should attend to the words, allowing music to teach, admonish, and stir the soul.
- The sermon closes with a note that next week's message will address the choir and its importance in a conservative church.
- Closing prayer expresses confidence that music and worship will carry over into eternity.
