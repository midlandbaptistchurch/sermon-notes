## Summary
Bill Baughman continues the "AM Crucified" series by examining the reliability of biblical prophecy and reviewing the many Old Testament predictions of Christ's suffering and death. He discusses the statistical improbability of fulfilled messianic prophecies, shares the story of Nabeel's conversion, and explores how Jesus' earthly ministry teachings differ from his heavenly ministry guidance through Paul.

## Scripture
Isaiah 53:1-5 — The suffering servant passage describing the Messiah's rejection, grief, and substitutionary atonement.

## Key Themes
- The reliability and preservation of Scripture
- Messianic prophecies and their fulfillment
- The emotional and spiritual weight of studying the crucifixion
- Statistical probability of prophetic fulfillment
- Distinguishing between Christ's earthly and heavenly ministries
- Taking up the cross as a daily spiritual discipline

## Notes

### The Reliability of Scripture
- Prophecy comes from the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, not the will of man (2 Peter 1:20-21)
- According to 1 Corinthians 13:8-10, prophecy will cease when "that which is perfect" comes—referring to the completed Bible
- God has preserved His word: "The words of the Lord are pure words... O Lord, thou shall preserve them from this generation forever" (Psalm 12:6-7)
- The story of Nabeel and David illustrates how to defend Scripture's reliability: there are over 6,000 Greek manuscripts, more than 10,000 Latin/Coptic/Syrian translations, and over 30,000 quotations from early church fathers—enough to reconstruct the entire New Testament

### Messianic Prophecies Reviewed
- The serpent would bruise the heel of the woman, and Christ would crush the serpent's head (Genesis 3:15)
- Messiah would be cut off but not for himself (Daniel 9:26)
- Betrayed for 30 pieces of silver (Zechariah 11:12)
- Scourging and mocking prophesied and fulfilled
- Garments divided by lots (Psalm 22:18)
- Pierced side (Zechariah 12:10)
- No bones broken
- Buried in a rich man's tomb
- Quoted words of abandonment: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Psalm 22:1)

### Statistical Probability of Prophecy
- Peter Stoner analyzed eight major messianic prophecies and calculated the probability of all being fulfilled by one person: one in 10^17 (1 followed by 17 zeros)
- For 28 prophecies studied: one in 10^50
- J. Barton Payne identified 1,817 total biblical prophecies (1,239 in OT, 578 in NT)
- When calculating probability for all 1,817 prophecies being fulfilled: one in 10^3,861
- These calculations demonstrate the impossibility of such fulfillment without divine intervention

### Jesus' Predictions of His Crucifixion
- First prediction (six months before): Matthew 16:21 — He would suffer, be killed, and rise the third day
- Second prediction: Matthew 17:22-23 — The disciples were exceedingly sorrowful but didn't understand
- Jesus instructed them: "Let these sayings sink down into your ears" (Luke 9:44) because "it was hid from them"
- Third prediction (1-2 weeks before): Matthew 20:18-19 — Detailed account of betrayal, condemnation, and crucifixion
- Fourth prediction (early last week): John 12:23-24 — The grain of wheat dying to bring forth much fruit; a reference to His purpose

### Earthly Ministry vs. Heavenly Ministry
- Jesus' earthly ministry lasted 3.5 years; His heavenly ministry has lasted 1,996 years
- Earthly command: "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me" (Luke 9:23)
- Heavenly command through Paul: "I protest by your rejoicing, which I have in Christ Jesus, our Lord, I die daily" (1 Corinthians 15:31) and "Always bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:10)
- "I beseech you therefore, brother, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice wholly acceptable unto God" (Romans 12:1)
- The doctrines differ: earthly disciples gave up everything materially; heavenly disciples present their bodies as living sacrifices

### Personal Reflection
- Baughman notes the difficulty of studying the crucifixion in depth—the paradox of rejoicing in redemption while grieving the terrible cost
- He shares his own experience joining Midland Baptist Church, needing assurance that the pastor would embrace the doctrine of transformation and fresh starts
