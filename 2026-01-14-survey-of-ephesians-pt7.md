## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne examines Ephesians 3 as "the most important forgotten chapter in the Bible," focusing on God's cosmic plan to gather all things under Christ's authority. The sermon explores Paul's mission to preach the unsearchable riches of Christ to the Gentiles, the three riches believers possess in Christ (redemption, forgiveness, and spiritual circumcision), and the five biblical world systems from the angelic age through eternity.

## Scripture
Ephesians 3 — Paul's revelation of God's will and the mystery of Christ, along with related passages from Ephesians 1, Colossians 1, and other epistles.

## Key Themes
- God's will and eternal purpose
- The mystery of Christ revealed to the Gentiles
- Christ's preeminence over all creation
- The unsearchable riches of Christ
- Spiritual authority and principalities
- The five world systems of Scripture
- Redemption through Christ's blood
- God's grace versus works
- The present evil world versus the world to come

## Notes

### God's Will and the Mystery of Christ
- Ephesians emphasizes understanding and fulfilling God's will
- God's will includes predestination to adoption as His children and redemption of the body
- The mystery of God's will is the revelation of Christ and the body of Christ (the Church)
- God's cosmic purpose is to gather all things—heavenly and earthly—under Christ's authority in the fullness of times

### Cosmic Government and Christ's Authority
- Christ is the image of the invisible God and the creator of all things
- All creation is subject to Christ and exists for His purposes
- Government on earth operates under God's ordained authority through various structures (kingdoms, democracies, theocracies)
- The ideal biblical government is benevolent kingship
- When Christ returns, He will establish His kingdom with the twelve apostles as judges and His government will spread across the earth
- Christ currently does not have preeminence in all heavenly things due to spiritual wickedness in high places, but believers will help restore authority to Christ

### Paul's Mission and the Unsearchable Riches
- Paul describes himself as "less than the least of all saints" despite receiving grace to preach to the Gentiles
- Paul's prior persecution of the Church made him feel deeply unworthy
- The "unsearchable riches of Christ" are infinite spiritual treasures that cannot be fully searched out or comprehended
- These riches were hidden in Old Testament mysteries—the prophets inquired about these mysteries but could not fully understand the body of Christ

### Three Riches in Christ
1. **Redemption through His blood** — The price for sin has been paid by Christ's sacrificial death
2. **Forgiveness of sins** — All sins are forgiven through God's grace, not by works or continued sacrifices
3. **Spiritual circumcision** — At salvation, believers are circumcised spiritually, severed from the flesh nature, dead to sin through union with Christ

### The Five World Systems
Scripture presents five distinct world systems:

1. **The Angelic World** — The original world ruled by Lucifer under God, which was destroyed due to rebellion
2. **The Old World** — Established with Adam and Eve in paradise, continuing until Noah's flood
3. **The Present World** — Established after the flood, divided into nations, currently under Satan's authority and the principalities and powers; called the "present evil world"
4. **The World to Come** — The millennial kingdom when Christ returns and establishes His 1,000-year reign
5. **The World Without End** — Eternity with a new heavens and new earth where God reigns forever

### God's Authority Over Nations
- After the Tower of Babel, God divided the nations and set principalities and powers (spiritual authorities) over them
- These spiritual authorities report to God in His heavenly council
- God rules and judges these authorities, holding them accountable for their governance
- God is preparing to inherit all nations and bring them under Christ's direct rule

### Grace Versus Works in Forgiveness
- Under grace, believers have complete forgiveness—not based on confession alone or repeated sacrifices
- Confession of sin flows from godly sorrow and repentance, but forgiveness is already possessed through Christ
- This contrasts with Old Testament practice requiring ongoing sacrifices and atonement
- Believers should forgive one another based on the fact that God has already forgiven them
