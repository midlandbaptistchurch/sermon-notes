## Summary
Pat Boulton challenges the congregation to view communion as more than mere tradition, emphasizing that Paul received these instructions directly from God. He contrasts his Catholic upbringing with biblical communion, explaining that "unworthily" refers to irreverent and inappropriate participation (like the Corinthians) rather than sinlessness. Through personal illustrations about remembering his father at a gravestone, he demonstrates how communion is a sacred time of remembrance, gratitude, and worship for Christ's death, burial, and resurrection.

## Scripture
**1 Corinthians 11:20-29** — Paul addresses the Corinthian church's improper communion practices where the wealthy arrived early and got drunk while the poor and slaves had nothing. He emphasizes that communion was received directly from the Lord and commands remembrance of Christ's body and blood, warning against partaking unworthily (irreverently/inappropriately).

**Luke 22:14-20** — Jesus instituted communion at the Last Passover, establishing a new memorial to replace the old covenant, commanding "do this in remembrance of me."

**Hebrews 10:4** — The blood of bulls and goats could not take away sins; they were only promissory notes pointing to Christ's final payment.

## Key Themes
- **Direct Divine Instruction**: Paul received communion instructions directly from God, making it more than tradition—it's a gift from God to believers
- **Worthy Participation**: "Unworthily" means irreverent and inappropriate participation, not being sinless, as demonstrated by the Corinthian problems
- **Remembrance and Gratitude**: Communion should cultivate an attitude of gratitude for Christ's work, similar to remembering what a father did for his family
- **Unity of Believers**: Communion should promote unity in the body of Christ, not division or self-centeredness

## Notes

### Background on Communion Practices
- Speaker was raised Catholic, taught that eating the bread meant literally eating Jesus and drinking his physical blood
- Catholics have communion every service, which can make it lose impact through over-familiarity
- Different Baptist practices: open communion (anyone saved), closed communion (only church members), close communion (saved with similar doctrine—Midland's practice)

### Paul's Direct Revelation
- 1 Corinthians 11:23: "For I have received of the Lord"—this instruction came directly from God to Paul
- This communion teaching appears nowhere else in Scripture except Paul's writings
- The word "remembrance" only appears in Paul's writings regarding communion
- This makes communion a special gift directly from God through Paul to the church

### The Corinthian Problem
- Four groups in the Corinthian church created communion problems:
  1. The wealthy—arrived early, got drunk, treated it irreverently
  2. Middle class workers—arrived mid-afternoon with their own food, didn't share
  3. The poor—had only snacks
  4. The slaves—arrived last with nothing
- Nobody was sharing; this violated the unity communion should represent
- This is the context for "unworthily"—irreverent and inappropriate participation

### Understanding "Unworthily"
- Not about being sinless (everyone is a sinner; even unknown sins exist)
- Refers to irreverent, inappropriate participation like the Corinthians
- Examples of unworthy participation: playing on phone, thinking about checkbook, lacking reverence
- Should be a somber, sober time of reverence for what Christ did
- One denomination starts communion preparation two weeks in advance with counseling sessions and confession

### The Remembrance Aspect
- Similar to standing at a father's gravestone and remembering what he did (worked 39 years at Dow Chemical, taught mechanics, had a good name in the community)
- Father once paid for groceries with a piece of torn grocery sack as a promissory note because his name was good
- Communion is remembering Christ's features and benefits: salvation, Holy Ghost, heaven, daily guidance
- Should cultivate daily gratitude, not just during communion services

### Passover Connection (Luke 22)
- The last true Passover—celebrated God's deliverance from Egypt
- Christ instituted a new memorial for a greater deliverance: from sin and the devil
- Moved people from old covenant to new covenant
- Bulls and goats were promissory notes pointing to Christ's final payment (Hebrews 10:4)

### Personal Testimony of God's Presence
- Story of South Carolina camp meeting where Holy Ghost was powerfully present
- Sunday school teacher's accident led to continuous prayer meetings
- Factory worker (Donnie) saved after seeing church lights on every night
- Eight to nine people saved in one service with visible reconciliation and repentance
- Donnie's mother and sister saved that night
- "When God shows up, it's normalcy and you can't work it up. I believe you can pray it down."
- Speaker has only experienced Holy Ghost presence like this three times in 40 years

### Practical Applications
- Before reading Bible, pray for the Holy Ghost to help with understanding and impact
- Consider what might be between you and another believer; get it right before communion
- Don't let communion become mere tradition—it's worship from the heart
- Communion should affect tomorrow, not just tonight
- Most worshipful time for speaker: talking to others about Jesus Christ
- After 42 years of salvation, many wonderful things happened because of Christ

### Call to Worship
- Worship comes from within the heart, not external ritual
- It's a revived heart with attitude of gratitude
- Taking communion in right attitude means being thankful you're in the body of Christ
- Should include remembrance, repentance, and rejoicing
- Communion is more than eating a wafer and drinking juice
