## Summary
Missionary Bryan Girard, drawing from the Parable of the Pounds in Luke 19, challenges believers to faithfully steward the time God has given them — 24 hours a day — just as the servants were commanded to trade with their pounds until their lord returned. Using stories from his decades of missionary work in Papua New Guinea, Girard encourages those who feel unqualified or unable to "trade" on their own to "put their money in the bank" — investing their resources and time in people and ministries that can multiply fruit for the Lord.

## Scripture
Luke 19:11-23 — The Parable of the Pounds, set during the last two weeks of Jesus' earthly ministry as He traveled toward Jerusalem; specifically verse 23: *"Wherefore then gavest not thou my money into the bank, that at my coming I might have required mine own with usury?"*

Supporting passages referenced:
- Titus 2:11-13 — Grace that brings salvation, teaches holy living, and points to the blessed hope
- John 14:1-3 — Christ preparing a place (position) for believers
- Romans 4:21 — Faith defined as being fully persuaded of God's promise

## Key Themes
- Faithful stewardship of time as the universal "pound" given to every believer
- Missions and evangelism as the primary arena for investing that time
- The grace of God saves the spirit, teaches the soul, and anticipates the glorified body
- If you cannot trade effectively yourself, put your resources in the hands of those who can ("the bank")
- God uses unqualified people — the speaker's own testimony of translating Scripture and hymns despite feeling unfit
- Every believer will be called to account for how they traded their pound
- Transformation is possible for anyone, illustrated by the story of Patrick in Papua New Guinea

## Notes
- **Parable context:** Luke 19:11-23, spoken by Jesus in the last two weeks of His life, near Zacchaeus' house; the nobleman represents Christ going to receive a kingdom and returning to judge his servants' faithfulness.
- **The pound identified:** Girard argues the pound common to all servants — regardless of race, gender, or background — is *time*: 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
- **Three responses in the parable:** One servant gained ten pounds, another gained five, and one hid his pound in a napkin. Girard honestly identifies most with the third servant.
- **The bank principle (v. 23):** Rather than trading poorly, the unfaithful servant could have deposited the pound with someone who knew how to multiply it. Girard applies this to supporting missionaries, praying for others, and giving time to those with fruitful ministries.
- **Titus 2:11-13 framework:** Grace saves the spirit (eternal life, faith plus nothing), then teaches the soul to say no to ungodliness and yes to godly living, culminating in the blessed hope (rapture) and glorious appearing (Revelation 19).
- **Future reward:** Believers will receive a *place* (position of authority) at the judgment seat of Christ, proportional to faithful stewardship — ruling and reigning with Christ (cf. John 14:1-3, Isaiah 56:5).
- **Girard's personal testimony:** Called to Papua New Guinea despite feeling unqualified in trading, grammar, music, and navigation — yet God used him to write 278 gospel tracts, translate the King James Bible into Tok Pisin, produce a hymn book (~200 hymns), and create a map book.
- **Patrick's story:** A man who had lived as a woman, suicidal, beaten by his brothers — came to church trembling, heard the gospel, and was saved. He subsequently walked thousands of miles barefoot preaching the gospel and planting churches, saying, *"If God can save someone like me, He can save anybody."*
- **Missionary investment example:** Girard supported a missionary (Hector Castillo) for years without receiving prayer letters; later learned Castillo's son had planted numerous churches — Girard counted this as dividends on his investment.
- **Closing challenge:** Believers are urged to reflect personally on how they spent their time that day and to commit to redeeming the time, whether by plowing, harvesting, or putting their pound in the bank for others to multiply.
