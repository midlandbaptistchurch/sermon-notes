## Summary
This teaching demonstrates how apparent contradictions in Scripture are resolved through right division—recognizing who Scripture is written to and when. Pastor Simpson shows how commands like "eye for eye" under the law, "turn the other cheek" in the kingdom, and "live peaceably" under grace are all literally true but apply to different dispensations. The sermon emphasizes that under grace, believers must figure things out using principles rather than following explicit commands, particularly regarding dealing with offenses and financial giving.

## Scripture
**Exodus 21:23-24** — Under the law, justice required "eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot."

**Matthew 5:38-42** — Jesus taught kingdom principles: "resist not evil, but whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also."

**Romans 12:17-21** — Grace principles: "live peaceably with all men" and "avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath."

**1 Timothy 5:17-18** — Elders who rule well and labor in word and doctrine are worthy of double honor; "the laborer is worthy of his reward."

**2 Corinthians 8-9** — Teaching on cheerful giving, specifically for the poor saints in Jerusalem during the transition period.

## Key Themes
- **Right Division**: Scripture must be read in context of who it's written to—not all Scripture is written to us, though all is for us
- **Dispensational Differences**: Commands differ between law (strict justice), kingdom (turning the other cheek), and grace (living peaceably as much as possible)
- **Grace Requires Discernment**: Under grace, believers must figure things out using principles rather than following explicit commands
- **Accountability vs. Enabling**: There's a difference between bearing one another's burdens and enabling sinful behavior
- **Liberty in Giving**: Tithing was commanded under law and before, but under grace giving is based on cheerful, purposeful stewardship

## Notes

### Reconciling Eye for Eye vs. Turn the Other Cheek

**Under the Law (Exodus 21:23-24)**
- Literal justice: "eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot"
- Cities of refuge established for manslaughter (accidental killing)
- Next of kin responsible to execute justice for intentional murder
- Gathering sticks on Sabbath resulted in death by stoning
- Clear, explicit commands with severe consequences

**In the Kingdom (Matthew 5:38-42)**
- Jesus teaching: "resist not evil...whosoever shall smite thee on thy right cheek, turn to him the other also"
- "Give to him that asketh of thee, and from him that would borrow of thee turn not thou away"
- Why this works: Christ will be on the throne, justice will be swift and perfect
- During the Millennium, the new covenant will be established with law written in hearts
- No need for personal vengeance because the King will ensure all justice

**Under Grace (Romans 12:17-21)**
- "Recompense to no man evil for evil"
- "If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men"
- "Avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord"
- "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good"
- Note the qualifier: "if it be possible" and "as much as lieth in you"—requires discernment

**Principles Under Grace**
- Not exact commands but principles requiring wisdom
- Must consider: providing for own household (1 Timothy 5:8)
- Sometimes must "suffer yourselves to be defrauded" (1 Corinthians 6:7)
- Must evaluate: is the division worth it? Is unity in the body more important?
- Satan is prince of the power of the air—different than kingdom conditions
- Sowing and reaping principles apply

### Taking Care of Your Own vs. Suffering Wrong

**1 Timothy 5:8**
- Context: caring for widows
- "If any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel"
- Provision includes protection, not just financial support
- Responsibility to take care of those in your household

**1 Corinthians 6:1-7**
- Context: believers taking each other to court
- Paul asks: "Is it so that there is not a wise man among you?"
- "Why do ye not rather take wrong? why do ye not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded?"
- Paul could say this having experienced stoning, beating, shipwreck, etc.
- Importance of unity in the body outweighs minor offenses
- Divisions in the body are spiritually damaging

**Balancing the Principles**
- Must discern when to establish boundaries vs. when to let things go
- Consider the long-term view: suffering now not compared to glory to come
- Many offenses won't matter five, ten, twenty years from now
- People often can't even remember what offended them
- Under grace, must put value on the right things

**Examples of Accountability**
- If someone is being abused in the home, they have the right to establish separation
- Holding someone accountable is different from seeking vengeance
- Sometimes people protect the abuser's reputation rather than seeking help
- Illustration: refusing to help unless person comes to church—hardly anyone showed up
- Building project: offered to pay people to help sweep floors, paint doors—only three people came in over a year

### Jews Only vs. Gentiles Included

**Matthew 10:5-6**
- Jesus commissioned the twelve: "Go not into the way of the Gentiles, and into any city of the Samaritans enter ye not"
- "But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel"
- This was a direct command not to go to Gentiles
- Even in Matthew 28 Great Commission, this wasn't changed
- During tribulation, Jews will reach Gentiles as originally commissioned

**Acts 13:46**
- Paul and Barnabas: "It was necessary that the word of God should first have been spoken to you [Jews]"
- "But seeing ye put it from you and judge yourselves unworthy of everlasting life, lo, we turn to the Gentiles"

**Acts 18:6**
- "When they opposed themselves and blasphemed, he shook his raiment and said unto them, Your blood be upon your own heads; I am clean: from henceforth I will go unto the Gentiles"

**Acts 28:28**
- "Be it known therefore unto you that the salvation of God is sent unto the Gentiles, and that they will hear it"
- Each time Paul said this, the transition was progressing closer to full Gentile inclusion
- By end of Acts, the middle wall of partition is coming down
- Jew and Gentile are being placed into one body

### Giving: Tithe vs. Cheerful Giving

**Tithing Under the Law**

**Genesis 14:18-20**
- First mention: Abram gave Melchizedek tithes of all
- This was before the law was given

**Leviticus 27:30-32**
- "All the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land, or of the fruit of the tree, is the LORD's: it is holy unto the LORD"
- "Concerning the tithe of the herd, or of the flock...the tenth shall be holy unto the LORD"
- Tithe = tenth
- Under law, the Levites had no land and no job
- Everything they had came from tithes of the other eleven tribes
- When people didn't tithe, Levites couldn't function properly

**Giving Under Grace**

**2 Corinthians 8-9**
- Context: giving to poor saints in Jerusalem
- These believers had sold everything (Acts 2) expecting to enter the tribulation
- When grace came in, they were destitute
- Great dearth (famine) made situation worse
- This was "transition giving"—one-time need during dispensational change
- Goal: get them on their feet, then let them take care of themselves

**2 Corinthians 9:6-8**
- "He which soweth sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which soweth bountifully shall reap also bountifully"
- "Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver"
- "God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work"

**1 Timothy 5:17-18**
- "Let the elders that rule well be counted worthy of double honour, especially they who labour in the word and doctrine"
- "Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn. And, The labourer is worthy of his reward"
- Comparison to ox: allowed to eat while working in the field
- Pastors who labor in word and doctrine should be supported
- Paul worked a job because the truth he brought was so new and different—didn't want to offend over money

**Principles for Today**
- Bible doesn't command tithing under grace
- Bible doesn't specifically address giving for church buildings (many were house churches)
- Under grace, we must figure out what to give
- Pastor Simpson's personal guideline: 10% as a base, often goes over
- Consider: what do you want financially invested in the Lord's work?
- Finances are just one portion—also giving life and self to Word of God
- Missionaries need prayer, but they also need to eat and put gas in cars
- Churches have real expenses: staff, utilities, facilities, materials, missions
- Illustration: new hardwood flooring, new chairs, maintenance, cleaning

**What God Expects**
- Not about what you "have to do" but what you want to do
- Ask: what do I want to have financially invested in the work of the Lord?
- Give cheerfully, purposefully, not grudgingly
- Consider what God has given you in stewardship
- Under grace, it's about figuring it out with biblical principles

### The Importance of Right Division

**Reading Scripture Literally**
- Goal: take Scripture in its context literally
- Not everything applies directly to you, though all is for you
- Used to try to make everything fit: "square peg in round hole" approach
- Would beat it with a hammer until it seemed to fit

**Romans Through Philemon**
- This is what's written directly to the church, the body of Christ
- Does not include Hebrews (not written by Paul, addresses new covenant for Israel)
- Paul's writings: Jew and Gentile come together, saved by grace through faith, placed into one body

**Timeline Understanding**
- Law period: clear commands, strict obedience required
- Kingdom period: Christ on throne, perfect justice, new covenant written in hearts
- Grace period: transition, principles to figure out, liberty with responsibility
- During grace: Satan is prince of power of the air, different conditions than kingdom
- Under grace: sowing and reaping in effect, must make wise decisions

**The "Your Mail" Principle**
- Not all of the Bible is your mail, though all is for you
- Must identify who passage is written to
- Example: Matthew 10—go to Jews only, not Gentiles (not written to church)
- If you don't rightly divide, you'll try to apply what's not yours
