## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues the survey of Colossians, focusing on the preeminence and all-sufficiency of Christ as Head of the body, the warning against failing to "hold the Head," and Paul's unique role in revealing the mystery of the body of Christ. The sermon draws out the deep doctrinal content of Colossians 1–2, contrasting the kingdom of God with the kingdom of heaven, and calling believers to persevere in the faith they have received.

## Scripture
Colossians 1–2 — Paul's letter of correction to the Colossian church, emphasizing Christ's preeminence as Head and warning believers not to be spoiled or beguiled away from that truth.

## Key Themes
- The preeminence and all-sufficiency of Christ as Head of the body
- Colossians as a book of correction, paralleling Ephesians
- The warning of Colossians 2:19 — not holding the Head
- The distinction between the kingdom of God and the kingdom of heaven
- Paul's prison epistles and the revelation of the mystery
- Perseverance in the faith (Colossians 1:22–23)
- Paul filling up the afflictions of Christ for the church's sake (Colossians 1:24)
- The mystery hidden from ages and generations, now made manifest

## Notes
**Ephesians and Colossians as Companions**
- Ephesians focuses on the body of Christ; Colossians focuses on Christ as the Head.
- The Holy Spirit is mentioned once in Colossians (95 verses) but repeatedly in Ephesians, reflecting each book's emphasis.
- Jesus Christ is named 26 times in Colossians' 95 verses, underscoring His preeminence.

**Colossians as a Correction Book**
- The church at Colossae was founded by Epaphras, not Paul.
- Colossians functions as a corrective to the church drifting from the doctrine of Ephesians, just as Galatians corrects drift from Romans.
- Paul's letters follow a pattern: Romans–Corinthians–Galatians (doctrine, reproof, correction) and Ephesians–Philippians–Colossians (doctrine, reproof, correction).

**Warning: Not Holding the Head (Colossians 2:18–19)**
- Believers risk being beguiled through voluntary humility, angel worship, and fleshly thinking.
- "Holding the Head" means embracing Christ's supremacy and all-sufficiency — believers are complete in Him and lack nothing.
- The body receives nourishment and is knit together by its connection to the Head.

**Deep Doctrine of Colossians 1**
- Colossians 1:13 — believers are delivered from the power of darkness and translated into the kingdom of His dear Son.
- "Kingdom of God" (spiritual rule in the hearts of people) is distinguished from "Kingdom of Heaven" (a literal, physical kingdom promised to Israel, the phrase found only in Matthew).
- Romans 14:17 — the kingdom of God is "righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost," the present fellowship of the body of Christ.
- Colossians 1:15–20 — Christ is the image of the invisible God, firstborn of every creature; all things (thrones, dominions, principalities, powers) were created by Him and for Him; He is the firstborn from the dead and holds preeminence in all things.
- Colossians 1:20 — reconciliation of all things in heaven and earth through the blood of His cross.

**Prison Epistles and Paul's Revelation**
- Ephesians, Philippians, and Colossians are prison epistles, written while Paul was a prisoner — not of Rome, but of the Lord Jesus Christ.
- God used Paul's imprisonment to lift him into the third heaven to reveal the eternal purpose for the body of Christ.
- After these revelations, Paul counted all former religious achievement as dung that he might gain the excellency of the knowledge (epignosis) of Christ.

**Reconciliation and the Call to Persevere (Colossians 1:21–23)**
- Believers were once alienated from God, enemies in mind through wicked works.
- Christ reconciled them in the body of His flesh through death to present them holy, unblameable, and unreprovable.
- Condition stated: "if ye continue in the faith, grounded and settled, and be not moved away from the hope of the gospel."
- Paul warns that some in the latter times will depart from the faith (1 Timothy 4); believers must remain grounded.

**Paul's Suffering and the Mystery (Colossians 1:24–29)**
- Paul rejoiced in his sufferings for the church, filling up the afflictions of Christ in his body — the afflictions Christ would have borne had He remained on earth to carry out this ministry personally.
- Paul was made a minister of the gospel (v. 23) and a minister of the mystery (v. 25) — two distinct ministerial roles highlighted in the same passage.
- The mystery — hidden from ages and generations but now made manifest — is "Christ in you, the hope of glory."
- Paul's goal: to warn and teach every man in all wisdom, to present every man perfect (complete) in Christ Jesus.
- Colossians 1:29 — Paul labored and strove according to Christ's working in him mightily.

**Closing Application**
- Believers have been given much; the responsibility is to be faithful to the truth and continue in the faith.
- The call is to be ministers of the gospel and ministers of the truth, with Paul as the pattern.
