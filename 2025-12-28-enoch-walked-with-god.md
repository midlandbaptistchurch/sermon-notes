## Summary
Nathan Gregory explores what it means to walk with God through the life of Enoch, who "walked with God" and was translated to heaven without seeing death. Gregory emphasizes that walking with God is not a physical journey but a spiritual relationship characterized by intimacy, obedience, transparency, and trust. The sermon challenges listeners to examine their own walk with God as they close out 2025.

## Scripture
Genesis 5:21-24 — Enoch's lineage and his unique testimony of walking with God, making him stand out among the generations listed in this genealogical chapter.

## Key Themes
- **Walking with God as a Spiritual Relationship** — Not merely occasional activity but a willful, continuous personal relationship with the Lord
- **Four Elements of Walking with God** — Intimacy (closeness and communication with God), Obedience (following God's will willfully and joyfully), Transparency (living openly before God), and Trust (relying on God through all circumstances)
- **Faith as Foundation** — True faith is rooted in God's character and His Word, not empty belief
- **Fellowship Through God's Word** — Walking with God requires engagement with Scripture and hearing from God through His Word
- **Generational Fruit** — Our walk with God affects our children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren
- **Life Examples** — Many biblical figures (Abraham, Joseph, Moses, David, Paul, etc.) exemplify what walking with God looks like in various circumstances
- **The Cost and Reward** — Walking with God requires dying to self-interest and worldly pursuits, but provides the greatest joy and partnership possible

## Notes
- Gregory opens by acknowledging this is the last Sunday of 2025 and celebrating that the congregation has chosen to be at church, reflecting their desire to honor God
- He addresses the danger of being physically present in church while spiritually absent, emphasizing that God looks at the heart, not just external position
- The sermon defines "walking with God" through four key words: Intimacy (a love affair with God and His Word), Obedience (willfully saying yes to God's leadership), Transparency (living openly before God who sees all), and Trust (relying on God through life's valleys)
- Gregory stresses that walking with God comes through reading and meditating on God's Word, since God has never spoken to him audibly but has revealed Himself through Scripture
- He illustrates walking with God through Enoch's life as: (1) a life of faith grounded in God's Word, (2) a life of fellowship where one desires the Word of God, and (3) a life bearing fruit through generations
- The sermon notes that while we know little textually about Enoch, his legacy is powerful: "Sometimes it's not the volume of what people say about you. It's the value of what people say about you."
- Gregory emphasizes the importance of biblical authority over extrabiblical texts, referencing the apocryphal Book of Enoch and encouraging reliance on Scripture alone
- The closing challenge asks listeners to decide: "Will you walk with God?" — calling for a decisive commitment as the year closes
- Examples of biblical figures who walked with God are listed (Abraham, Joseph, Moses, David, Samuel, Job, Elijah, Rahab, Joshua, Jeremiah, Shadrach/Meshach/Abednego, Mary, John the Baptist, Paul, Timothy, Titus) to demonstrate that this walk takes various forms across different life circumstances
- The sermon concludes with an altar call and prayer, inviting people to recommit or begin their walk with God
