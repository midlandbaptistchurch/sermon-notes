## Summary
Using Proverbs 23:23 as his text, Associate Pastor Mark Simpson challenges believers to hold fast to truth rather than selling it cheaply through fear, doubt, and compromise. Delivered on the occasion of a pre-wedding shower for a young couple in the church, the sermon weaves together a call to genuine faith and trust in God's sovereignty with practical exhortations about the virtues that sustain a healthy marriage relationship. Central throughout is the idea that truth — in one's walk with God and in one's closest relationships — must be purchased at great personal cost and never traded away.

## Scripture
Proverbs 23:23 — "Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding." The verse serves as the backbone of the message, with Pastor Simpson unpacking both the positive virtues and their opposing enemies as they apply to faith and marriage.

## Key Themes
- Holding fast to truth rather than selling it for comfort or convenience
- The peace of God versus a life dominated by fear and doubt
- Openness, honesty, and transparency in marriage and in Christian living
- Trust and security versus assumptions and insecurity
- Love and respect versus unkindness and disrespect
- Strength and character grounded in a genuine walk with God
- Forgiveness, repentance, and avoiding bitterness
- Accepting God's sovereign will through trials and adversity
- Spirituality and a consistent walk with God as the foundation of marriage

## Notes

### Opening: Peace, Trust, and Accountability Before God
- Pastor Simpson opens by reflecting on a song and the peace of God (Philippians 4:7).
- He challenges the congregation to consider how much they truly trust God amid daily worries and news events.
- He raises the sobering thought that believers will one day give an account for how much they doubted God when He always had things under control.
- "The things of this earth shall grow strangely dim in the light of His glory and grace."

### Proverbs 23:23 — The Text
- The verse commands believers to *buy* truth — not with money, but with time, effort, study, and discipline.
- The word "also" includes wisdom, instruction, and understanding as things worth buying at great cost.
- The message is framed around a pre-wedding shower for a couple in the church, applying the verse to marriage.

### Truth #1: Openness and Honesty vs. Fear and Doubt
- Openness and honesty require transparency, authenticity, and vulnerability.
- Many people prefer to keep certain areas closed rather than deal with the discomfort of being truly known.
- Christians generally accept that God knows everything about them but are more concerned about what other people think — revealing a greater fear of man than fear of God.
- People often "sell the truth" without even realizing it by hiding their flaws from others while performing spirituality publicly.

### Trials, Adversity, and Accepting God's Will
- Pastor Simpson speaks personally about his wife's health limitations preventing her from attending church and his pastor's chronic bronchitis, acknowledging these are hard providences that don't make immediate sense.
- He references Romans 8 (present sufferings not comparable to coming glory) and 2 Corinthians 12:9 (strength made perfect in weakness).
- The call is to say, "Not my will, but Thy will be done," rather than putting truth out in a "yard sale" to get rid of it because it is costly.
- Understanding comes with a price tag — we must by faith trust what we cannot yet comprehend.

### Application to the Couple Attending the Shower
- Pastor Simpson commends a young couple (Keegan and J. Lee) who relocated their lives because of their commitment to biblical truth, even going back through old sermon archives to find it.

### Truth #2: Trust and Security vs. Assumptions and Insecurity
- Trust in marriage means giving your spouse the benefit of the doubt.
- Insecurity flips the presumption — the spouse becomes "guilty until proven innocent."
- Practical counsel: married couples should share passwords and finances; transparency prevents suspicion.
- Oneness in marriage mirrors the oneness Christ desires for the body of Christ (Ephesians 4–5).
- Recommended resource: *Love and Respect* — highlights that love and respect are key, while unkindness and disrespect are their enemies.

### Truth #3: Understanding and Patience vs. Taking Each Other for Granted
- Choose to assume the best until proven otherwise.
- Don't treat people at church better than people at home — those at home know you as you truly are.

### Truth #4: Strength and Character vs. Weakness and Fraudulence
- True strength in marriage flows from a genuine, intimate walk with God.
- Those who consistently meet with God and walk in real relationship with Him are the ones who do not fall into temptation.
- Proverbs 7 is cited as a warning against moral compromise.
- 1 Corinthians 10:13 — there is always a way of escape from temptation.
- Personal illustration: as a young Bible college student, Pastor Simpson sought counsel from a mentor (Brother Schaefer) about a potentially compromising relationship; the mentor's timely teaching on Joseph and Potiphar's wife gave him clarity to walk away.

### Truth #5: Forgiveness and Kindness vs. Unforgiveness and Bitterness
- Ephesians 4:30–32 — grieve not the Holy Spirit; put away bitterness, wrath, anger, malice, and evil speaking; be kind and tenderhearted, forgiving one another.
- Forgiveness does not eliminate accountability; sowing and reaping remains a biblical principle.
- There is a distinction between *forgiveness* (releasing the offense) and *repentance* (genuinely turning from the wrong to the right).
- Many marriages cycle through the same failures because there is confession without true repentance.
- Bitterness is described vividly — it is ugly, undesirable, and corrosive to relationships.
- Marriages deteriorate one step at a time as enemies of truth are allowed to creep in.

### Truth #6: Spirituality and a Walk with God
- Pastor Simpson's wife tells every couple in premarital counseling that what has sustained their marriage is her confidence in her husband's walk with God.
- She says she has no doubt he will ultimately do whatever God tells him to do.
- Pastor Simpson states there is nothing greater a husband can give his wife than the assurance that he will follow the Lord with his whole heart.
- The greatest security in marriage is a spouse who has bought the truth and will not sell it — who may falter, but will get back up, because what they have in Christ is too valuable.
- He closes by directly challenging Keegan and J. Lee with this same standard.
