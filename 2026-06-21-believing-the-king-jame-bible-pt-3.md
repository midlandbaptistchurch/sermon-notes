## Summary
This is the third lesson examining precursors to understanding the King James Bible issue, focusing on the element of faith required in believing the Bible. The sermon establishes that while faith is necessary, the Bible validates itself both internally through its consistent system of logic and externally through science, history, and archaeology. The speaker emphasizes that having certainty about possessing God's Word is foundational to being able to believe and apply what it says.

## Scripture
**2 Timothy 2:15** — Commands believers to study to show themselves approved unto God, being workmen who need not be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth.

**2 Timothy 3:16-17** — All scripture is given by inspiration of God and is profitable for doctrine, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be perfect (complete), thoroughly furnished unto all good works.

**Leviticus 17:11** — States that the life of the flesh is in the blood, demonstrating the Bible's scientific accuracy.

**John 5:2** — Describes the pool of Bethesda with five porches, a detail confirmed by archaeological discovery in 1888.

**Hebrews 11:1-3** — Defines faith as the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen, and explains that through faith we understand the worlds were framed by the word of God.

## Key Themes
- **Faith and Evidence**: Faith is required to believe the Bible, but this faith is not foundationless—it is supported by internal consistency and external validation through science, history, and archaeology.
- **Face-to-Face vs. Faith-to-Faith**: The Apostle Paul had "face-to-face faith" through direct encounters with Christ, while believers today have "faith-to-faith," trusting in the documented results of Paul's experiences and teachings.
- **The Bible as Authority**: The Bible is the ultimate authority that all other authorities (pastors, fathers, presidents, kings) must come under.
- **Study as Work**: Understanding and applying God's Word requires diligent study and effort, not just passive acceptance.

## Notes

### The Element of Faith in Believing the Bible
- There is an element of faith involved in believing the King James Bible or any Bible, and even in believing in the existence of God
- Faith is not circular reasoning—it requires external evidence and personal experience
- Paul had "face-to-face faith" through direct revelations from Christ, including being taken to the third heaven
- God gave Paul what he needed because of the difficult ministry God called him to do
- Believers today have "faith-to-faith"—faith based on the documented results of Paul's face-to-face encounters with God
- Paul's epistles are the product of his face-to-face partnership with God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit

### Internal Validation of Scripture
- The Bible validates itself through its consistency in its own internally-established system of logic, interpretation, and application
- Study is hard work and necessary to be approved unto God as a workman not needing to be ashamed
- Rightly dividing the Word means dispensationally understanding whose mail we're reading
- All scripture is for our learning, but not all is to us for our application
- The speaker won't build an ark like Noah but will preach the gospel to save his family
- All scripture makes the man of God complete and thoroughly furnished unto all good works

### External Validation of Scripture
- **Science**: Leviticus 17:11 states "the life of the flesh is in the blood," contradicting ancient medical practices of bloodletting
- **History and Archaeology**: The pool of Bethesda (John 5:2) was considered mythical until discovered in 1888 near the church of St. Ann in Jerusalem, complete with five porches as described
- The Bible makes historical claims naming real kings, describing real battles, and recording events in specific cities at specific times
- Luke wrote his gospel so readers "might know the certainty of those things" (Luke 1:4)
- Over the past two centuries, archaeology has unearthed thousands of artifacts confirming the Bible's historical reliability
- Places dismissed as fictional have been found; people doubted to exist have been identified in ancient records
- Events questioned by critics have been corroborated by independent evidence

### The Question of Biblical Authority
- If you can't believe you really have the Bible, how can you believe what it says?
- Any variation or deviation from truth is like misalignment when aiming a weapon—you miss the target
- The Bible is a record of God's actions within real human history
- The speaker's testimony: 40+ years of applying biblical principles resulted in a better life and family than the models he came from
- An upward spiral is possible through consistent application of God's Word, contrasting with the world's downward decline
- Personal illustration: The speaker told his military commander he submitted to his authority because God told him to, which opened the door to share the gospel

### Living Out Biblical Faith
- Evidence doesn't definitively prove the Bible in a scientific sense—faith remains essential
- God is not afraid of questions; Christ taught with questions
- Don't just trust one man (even a trusted pastor)—trust the Book
- The Bible is the authority; Pastor Payne is under this authority
- Consistency between profession and practice is essential—if you say you're a Bible believer, you need to be a Bible liver
- Example given of funeral for 88-year-old man saved as a bus kid who lived out his faith, resulting in 6 children, 21 grandchildren, 32 great-grandchildren, and one great-great-grandchild on the way

### The Backdrop of the Bible (Review)
- **Satan's goal**: To corrupt and complicate the issue, making it confusing
- **God's desire**: To keep it simple and straightforward so we can understand His Word and will
- **The integrity issue**: Men, manuscripts, and message (to be examined in part two) are all tied to integrity
- **The pursuit of truth**: Between God and man—Jesus said "I am the truth"
- **The element of faith**: Required to believe the Bible, but supported by evidence
