## Summary
Pastor Mark continues the Guidelines for Grace series by introducing the concept of biblical perfection, which differs from cultural understanding. He explains that believers are commanded to be perfect even in sin-cursed bodies, meaning perfection is about completion and maturity rather than flawlessness. The message emphasizes that perfection involves comfort (security), being of one mind doctrinally, and living in peace, and that these are attainable goals for believers when properly understood.

## Scripture
**2 Corinthians 13:11** — Paul commands believers to "be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace," promising that the God of love and peace shall be with them.

**1 Corinthians 1:10** — Paul beseeches believers to speak the same thing, have no divisions, and be perfectly joined together in the same mind and judgment.

## Key Themes
- **Biblical Perfection**: Perfection in Scripture means completeness and maturity, not sinless flawlessness; it's achievable in sin-cursed bodies
- **Misdirected Zeal**: Zealousness and passion must be directed properly; zeal without knowledge leads believers astray
- **Comfort and Security**: Biblical comfort means being secure, steadfast, and unmovable in faith, not physical ease
- **Unity of Mind**: Being of one mind refers to doctrinal unity with Christ as head, not uniformity of preferences or personalities

### Dangers of Misdirected Zealousness

**Galatians 4:17-18**
- "They zealously affect you, but not well"
- Can be zealous in wrong direction
- "It is good to be zealously affected always in a good thing"

**Romans 10:2**
- "They have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge"
- Good motives must be appreciated but truth cannot be compromised
- There's a time to be patient and a time to draw lines
- Takes discernment and prayer to know when to hold firm

**1 Corinthians 14:1-2, 12**
- Church at Corinth wanted showy spiritual gifts
- Desired gifts out of pride and selfishness
- Paul redirected them: "seek that ye may excel to the edifying of the church"

### The Importance of Desire ("Want To")

**Colossians 1:9**
- Paul's prayer: "that you might be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding"
- Paul desired for them to desire understanding
- Preachers try to convey their passion and implement it in others' lives
- Desire must ultimately be personal, not just borrowed from others

**What to do when you don't have desire:**
- Under grace, there's no immediate punishment for lack of desire
- No snakes biting or earth opening up like under law
- Must take initiative to examine where heart and desire actually are
- Ask: Where do my thoughts go? Where do my finances go? Where does my time go?
- Can lose passion for the Lord even over good things (like engagement, school, work)
- Need to put things in right priority and place

**1 Thessalonians 2:17**
- "Endeavored more abundantly to see your face with great desire"
- Must have desire for people, not just personal devotional life
- Need balance: time with the Lord AND ministry toward others
- Easier to give out what you've prepared than to do the work of preparation

**Philippians 4:17**
- "Not because I desire a gift, but I desire fruit that may abound to your account"
- Desire for others to bear fruit
- If you bear fruit and others bear fruit, equals even more fruit (simple math)
- Want to see others thrive and succeed, not compete
- Paul was glad when things went better for Corinthians than for himself

### Biblical Comfort as Security

**2 Thessalonians 2:17**
- "Comfort your hearts and establish you in every good word and work"
- Comfort and establish go together
- Established = set, secure, not going away
- Can't have peace without security

**Redefinition of "Comfortable Christian":**
- Most say you shouldn't be a comfortable Christian
- Biblical comfort = secure, steadfast, unmovable
- "Always abounding in the work of the Lord" (1 Corinthians 15:58)
- Comfortable = secure in position regardless of circumstances

**Peter's Example:**
- When Jesus asked if disciples would leave: "To whom shall we go? Thou hast the words of eternal life"
- Peter had no job, no income, left family, faced persecution and death threats
- Still followed Christ—that's biblical comfort
- Had sold the boat (literally and figuratively)

**Practical Application:**
- Want to be comfortable in church, Bible, with other believers, in prayer
- Example: Craig Vandenberg's first men's prayer breakfast
- Never prayed out loud with others before
- Given option to just listen or pray after hearing example
- Giving options provided comfort and peace
- Where he was at in life, making decision to come was perfect for that stage
- Now can pray alongside others comfortably

**1 Thessalonians 5:11**
- "Comfort yourselves together and edify one another"
- Comfort and edification always go hand in hand
- When others share how God is working, it edifies the hearer
- Teaching edifies others, their growth edifies back
- Cause and effect relationship in mutual edification

### Biblical Perfection Explained

**2 Corinthians 13:11**
- "Finally brethren, farewell, be perfect"
- Command given casually, like "turn off the lights on your way out"
- Paul confident they could do it despite their past failures
- Written to church at Corinth in second letter (they'd made progress)

**Components of being perfect:**
1. Be perfect
2. Be of good comfort
3. Be of one mind
4. Live in peace
5. Promise: "The God of love and peace shall be with you"

**Conditional promise (like Philippians 4:9):**
- "Those things which you have both learned, received, and heard, and seen and do, and the God of peace shall be with you"
- Not about salvation but access to attributes of God
- Access comes through seeing, doing, understanding certain things

**Key Understanding:**
- Perfection commanded to sin-cursed people
- Takes place within sin-cursed body
- Not about being better than others
- Can be perfect and still get better
- Not talking about being without flaw or error
- Forgiveness is perfect (complete, without error, can't be taken away)
- This perfection involves free will and decisions
- About how we live, see, and understand

### One Mind (Unity)

**Not uniformity of personality:**
- Doesn't mean mind-melding personalities and preferences
- Can still like what you like, be who you are
- One-mindedness about doctrine

**Most Important Unity:**
- Christ is head of body of believers
- Same head, same book, same purpose
- Purpose #1: bring glory to God
- Preference has nothing to do with it

**When believers come together:**
- Understand grace, living by principles
- Passions and zeal mix together
- Two fires create greater fire and heat
- Continues to move forward

**Working toward one-mindedness:**
- Sometimes study alone
- Sometimes learn together in Bible study or services
- As you grow, can assume others who study same book think same way
- Part of perfection of body of Christ
- Coming together as one with Christ as head, bringing Him glory

**1 Corinthians 1:10**
- "That you all speak the same thing, that there be no divisions among you"
- "Be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment"
- Paul's opening statement showing the goal
- Acknowledging they had long way to go
- Much to cover to get there
- By end of 2 Corinthians 13, saying "now do it, go be perfect"
- Confidence they could do it regardless of past
