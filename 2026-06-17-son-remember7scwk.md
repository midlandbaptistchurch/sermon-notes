## Summary
Jim VanBuskirk examines the account of the rich man and Lazarus to explore what we take with us after death—specifically our memories and cognizant recognition. Abraham's words "son, remember" reveal that the rich man retained full memory of his earthly life, his family, and his failures to obey God's law regarding caring for the poor. This reality points forward to the judgment seat of Christ, where believers will give account of their works. The sermon emphasizes the importance of fireproofing our lives now by rightly dividing God's Word and walking worthy of our calling, so we won't face regret when we remember our earthly choices.

## Scripture
Luke 16:19-31 — The account of the rich man and Lazarus, where both men die and retain their memories and cognizant awareness in the afterlife. Abraham tells the rich man "son, remember" your earthly life and choices.

## Key Themes
- **Memory Beyond Death**: The rich man, Lazarus, and Abraham all had cognizant memories and could communicate, proving something goes with us beyond death
- **The Rich Man's Failures**: He violated the law of Moses by failing to care for Lazarus (Leviticus 25:35-36, 19:9-10), showing compassion and providing for the poor at his gate
- **Right Division**: Learning to rightly divide Scripture is essential for understanding what is for us versus what is for other dispensations
- **The Judgment Seat of Christ**: All believers will stand before Christ to give account, and our works will be tried by fire (1 Corinthians 3:13-15, 4:5, Romans 14:12)

## Notes

### The Reality of Memory After Death
- Three men (rich man, Lazarus, Abraham) were having a conversation after death
- The rich man had cognizant recognition of Abraham and Lazarus by name
- He remembered his five brothers and his father's house
- Abraham said "son, remember" - indicating intact memories of earthly life
- We cannot stand before Christ in judgment and give account if we know nothing
- These men had not yet been to their final judgment but retained their memories

### The Rich Man's Violation of the Law
- Leviticus 25:35-36: commanded to relieve brothers who have fallen into poverty
- Leviticus 19:9-10: required leaving corners of fields and gleanings for the poor and stranger
- Leviticus 19:18: commanded to love your neighbor as yourself
- Deuteronomy 15:7-8: commanded to open hand wide to the poor and lend sufficiently for their need
- The rich man saw Lazarus daily at his gate but failed to help him
- Only unclean dogs approached Lazarus, adding to his humiliation
- Abraham reminded him: "they have Moses and the prophets, let them hear them"

### Personal Testimony Context
- Speaker was taught wrong doctrine from youth, not from the Bible itself
- Didn't know until age 35 that Jesus Christ was God
- Could have died lost despite attending church his entire life
- This makes the story of the rich man personally resonant

### Paul and the Dispensation of Grace
- Acts 9:15: Paul chosen as vessel to bear Christ's name to Gentiles
- Romans 11:13: Paul the apostle of the Gentiles
- Romans 16:25: the mystery kept secret since the world began
- Galatians 1:11-12: Paul's gospel received by revelation from Jesus Christ
- We are not under law but under grace
- Paul is our lens for understanding all Scripture
- The Pauline epistles are to us, for us, and about us

### Similar Conduct Requirements Across Dispensations
- 2 Corinthians 5:14: the love of Christ constrains us
- Colossians 3:12-13: put on mercy, kindness, humbleness, meekness, longsuffering, forgiveness
- Romans 13:8-10: love fulfills the law; love works no ill to neighbor
- Though in different dispensation, code of conduct has not changed much
- God wants us to be Christlike in our actions toward others

### The Judgment Seat of Christ
- 1 Corinthians 3:13-15: every man's work tried by fire; some receive reward, some suffer loss but are still saved
- 1 Corinthians 4:5: will bring to light hidden things of darkness and make manifest counsels of the heart
- Romans 14:12: every one of us shall give account of himself to God
- Not a determination of salvation, but judgment of our works
- God will judge not only what we did, but how we did it and our motives
- We will have verbal interaction with Jesus Christ
- Works done for man's approval already have their reward
- Works done with pure motives for Christ's glory will remain

### Practical Application
- Learn to rightly divide the Word of God for protection and understanding
- Help others without being a respecter of persons
- Simple acts (helping elderly couple with appliance) can lead to gospel opportunities
- Don't need to carry a testament and jump on people constantly
- Your conversation (conduct, how you carry yourself) is a testament
- Fireproof your life now by being in the Word and walking worthy of your calling

### Near-Death Experiences (Illustrative)
- Various accounts show people retaining memories and awareness after clinical death
- Some see light and love; others experience darkness and torment
- Many report life flashing before their eyes
- Brain studies show memory centers activated intensely at death
- Scripture is more reliable than experiences, but these accounts corroborate biblical truths
