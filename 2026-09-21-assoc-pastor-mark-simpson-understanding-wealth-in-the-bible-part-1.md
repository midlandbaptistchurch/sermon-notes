## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson explains why the Bible appears to contradict itself regarding wealth—particularly why rich people are condemned in James and the Gospels but not in Paul's epistles. The key is understanding that James through Revelation were written as instructions for the Tribulation period, when believers would need to have nothing (either taking the mark of the beast to be rich, or hoarding goods). Under grace in Paul's epistles, believers have liberty regarding wealth, with contentment being the key whether having much or little. The sermon concludes with practical financial planning illustrations showing how retirement savings can enable greater ministry impact and generational blessing.

## Scripture
**James 1:10-11** — Condemns the rich man who will fade away like grass, written for the Tribulation period when wealth would indicate compromise or hoarding.

**Revelation 13:16-18** — Describes the mark of the beast and absolute economic control during the Tribulation, making it impossible to buy or sell without the mark.

**Acts 2:38-47** — Early believers had all things common and sold possessions because they expected to enter the Tribulation period where God would supernaturally provide (like manna in the wilderness).

**1 Timothy 6:1-19** — Paul addresses servants and masters, condemning the false teaching that gain is godliness, while instructing the rich not to be high-minded but to do good works and distribute freely.

**Philippians 4:10-19** — Paul expresses contentment in both abundance and need, stating "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me" in the context of having much or little financial support.

## Key Themes
- **Rightly Dividing Scripture**: The Bible must be read understanding who is being addressed—tribulation believers in James through Revelation versus grace believers in Paul's epistles
- **Wealth in the Tribulation**: During the Tribulation, being rich means either taking the mark of the beast or hoarding goods, both worthy of condemnation
- **Wealth Under Grace**: Believers today have liberty regarding wealth; it's not the amount but the attitude that matters—avoiding both pride in abundance and discouragement in lack
- **Contentment**: The key principle is learning to be content whether abounding or abased, full or hungry, having all or suffering need
- **Financial Stewardship**: Wise financial planning enables greater ministry impact, supports missionaries, and can bless the church for generations

## Notes

### Understanding Biblical Contradictions About Wealth

- The Tribulation period fits between the Gospels and before Paul's revelation of the mystery
- James through Revelation were written as instructions for those expecting to enter the Tribulation period
- Early believers thought they would enter the Tribulation in their lifetime, then the Millennium, living 1000 years if they did right
- They knew nothing about the age of grace and being part of the body of Christ—that was the mystery

### Condemnation of the Rich in the Tribulation

- **Revelation 13:16-18**: The Antichrist will control the economic system; no one can buy or sell without the mark of the beast (666)
- Being rich during the Tribulation means one of two things:
  1. The individual has taken the mark, allowing him to buy or sell
  2. He is hoarding his goods during these final days, refusing to share with those in dire need
- For this reason, God unconditionally condemns the rich during the Tribulation period
- **James 1:10-11**: "But the rich, in that he is made low: because as the flower of the grass he shall pass away"
- **James 2:6**: "Do not rich men oppress you, and draw you before the judgment seats?"

### Early Church Example (Acts 2)

- **Acts 2:38**: Peter preached "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins"
- This was legitimate for that time—repentance and baptism got them entrance through the Tribulation period into the kingdom and the new covenant
- They did not receive what we get when saved: being sealed by the Spirit, placed into the body, promise of heaven
- **Acts 2:44-45**: "And all that believed were together, and had all things common; And sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every man had need"
- No religion follows this today because it was preparation for the Tribulation
- The seven men in Acts were appointed to distribute to widows from this common fund
- Since nobody was working, the funds would eventually run out—then God would supernaturally provide like manna in the wilderness
- This common fund is a picture of prophecy to come during the Tribulation period

### Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5)

- They sold land and held back part of it
- Today if someone sold something and gave part to the church, we'd say "great, good job"
- But they were immediately killed for holding back part
- Why so extreme? Because they were still heading into the Tribulation period
- The Kingdom was still at hand in that part of Acts
- You couldn't go into the Tribulation period and have anything—it was impossible to be right with holdings

### Jesus Sending Out the Disciples (Matthew 10)

- **Matthew 10:5-10**: Go only to the lost sheep of Israel, heal the sick, cleanse lepers, raise the dead, cast out devils
- "Provide neither gold, nor silver, nor brass in your purses, Nor scrip for your journey, neither two coats, neither shoes, nor yet staves"
- Go out with nothing—this was planned and on purpose
- They were to represent Him doing miracles proving He's the Messiah
- The prophecies about the kingdom—doing away with sickness, meeting needs—He wanted demonstrated
- Peter and the disciples left their jobs and fishing boats
- At one point they got discouraged and went back to fishing—Jesus called them to be fishers of men
- They needed to sell the boat because they were supposed to follow Him and be disciples

### Wealth Under Grace (Paul's Epistles)

- **Philippians 4:11-13**: "I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content. I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound"
- "I know both how to be...full, and to be hungry" (not at the same time—one or the other)
- "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me"
- Context: Regardless of income amount, Paul would still do and be what he was supposed to be
- When Paul ran out of money, he made tents and worked a job
- When in prison, he didn't need money—God put him in different places
- **Philippians 4:15-19**: The Philippian church was the only one that supported Paul financially at first
- Paul didn't teach churches right away about giving because he didn't want them to think he was coming just for money
- "My God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus" (v. 19)

### Instructions to Rich and Poor Under Grace (1 Timothy 6)

- **Verse 1-2**: Servants under the yoke should count masters worthy of honor whether they're believers or not
- Problem: When master and servant both got saved and placed into body of Christ, they were brothers—but next morning one still master, other still servant
- Paul taught: If you're a servant, be a servant; work for them as if working for Paul
- **Verse 5**: False teaching that "gain is godliness"—if you had more than others, you were blessed more by God
- This made servants look at masters as more blessed, but most masters inherited wealth
- **Verse 6-8**: "But godliness with contentment is great gain. For we brought nothing into this world, and it is certain we can carry nothing out. And having food and raiment let us therewith be content"
- **Verse 9**: "But they that will be rich fall into temptation and a snare"—who's he talking to? Servants (verse 1)
- How would servants become rich? They weren't supposed to—wasn't going to work that way
- Paul teaching them: You're trying to get something you're never going to have and not supposed to have, which will lead to temptation
- **Verse 10**: "For the love of money is the root of all evil"
- **Verse 17-19**: "Charge them that are rich in this world, that they be not highminded, nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God, who giveth us richly all things to enjoy"
- Paul didn't tell the rich to sell what they had or that they were evil for having it
- "That they do good, that they be rich in good works, ready to distribute, willing to communicate"
- Balance: If you have wealth, don't be high-minded or trust in it above God, but trust God "who giveth us richly all things to enjoy"

### Application for Today

- Whether you have money or don't doesn't matter—you can be as good a Christian with a lot or with a little
- If you have a lot, don't want it to control you
- If you have a little, don't want it to pull you down
- The important thing: contentment—able to find content whether you have it or not
- "I've lived my life and had very little. I've lived my life and I've had more. But you know what? The book is still the book, the work is still the work"

### Practical Financial Planning

#### Dave Ramsey's Teaching
- Pastor Mark used to listen to Dave Ramsey a lot
- Ramsey's teachings line up with Paul's writings
- But when Ramsey tries to make his teaching fit Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, "it's bad"
- Example: Ramsey's teaching on the rich young ruler—"I was just like, oh my word...because it's like he can't explain it away because it literally was bad"

#### Retirement Planning Under Grace
- Under grace we have liberty—we can make choices
- Probably not good to work so much you have no time for church and serving the Lord
- But following principles of debt reduction and investing is acceptable
- The 4% rule in retirement: If you have $2 million saved, living off 4% = $80,000/year
- This is supposed to be safe and regulate for inflation
- Social Security is additional if you've paid into it

#### Investment Calculator Examples
**Example 1**: Age 20, investing to age 50 (30 years)
- Starting amount: $10,000
- Monthly contribution: $200
- Return rate: 8% annually
- Result at age 50: $382,000
- If monthly contribution increases to $500: $804,000
- If work until 55 (35 years): $1,200,000

**Example 2**: Age 20, investing to age 55 (35 years)
- Monthly contribution based on 15% of $60,000 salary = $750/month
- Return rate: 8% annually
- Result at age 55: approximately $1,700,000

#### Building a Financial Bridge
- Retirement funds can't be accessed penalty-free until age 59½
- Must build a "financial bridge" with liquid funds to get from retirement age to 59½
- Social Security earliest withdrawal: age 62 (but monthly amount increases the longer you wait)
- Medicare: age 65

#### Benefits of Financial Freedom
- At age 55, if you have enough saved and can live off investments, you could stop working
- Financial freedom gives liberty to make different decisions
- When Paul had to make tents, he wasn't preaching, teaching, or doing what he really wanted to do
- With financial freedom: "If you have funds where you don't just give to missions, you go and you see missionaries and you look at the projects and the things that they do"
- "What it gives you when you have more financial freedom is it gives you liberty to make different decisions, to choose to do things"
- "Instead of you going, you give and you do those things"

#### Pastor Mark's Future Plans
- "At some point in my life I will retire financially from the church, but that doesn't mean I'm going to stop working"
- "I'll be around and I'll have all the experience and all the things that I've ever done and I'll be here"
- "I'm going to probably take care of the finances of this church till I die or I become senile...because I like being able to do it"
- "I'll probably fix things as long as I can"
- When retired financially, "the church wouldn't have to pay me, then they can pay somebody else and then there would be more work and more laborers"

### Legacy Giving Strategy

#### The Four-Part $200,000 Gift
Pastor Mark outlined a creative long-term giving strategy:

**Instructions**: Leave $200,000 to the church, divided into four parts of $50,000 each

**Part 1 (Years 1-20)**:
- $50,000 divided over 20 years (240 months) = $208/month to the church
- Actually more like $350/month because money stays invested and grows

**Part 2 (Years 21-40)**:
- First $50,000 has grown to $233,000 at 8% return
- $233,000 divided by 240 months = about $970/month
- With interest while distributing, "a little over a thousand dollars that the church" receives

**Part 3 (Years 41-60)**:
- Second $50,000 has grown for 40 years to over $1,000,000
- $1,086,000 divided by 240 months = $4,500/month to the church
- "And I'm dead"

**Part 4 (Years 61-80)**:
- Third $50,000 has grown for 60 years at 8% to approximately $5,000,000
- $5,000,000 divided by 240 months = almost $21,000/month
- "The church receives from me—I'm dead—$21,000 as long as the stock market goes and until Jesus comes back"

#### Notes on Legacy Strategy
- "We think on it, it works, doesn't it? But as far as I know, I've never heard of anybody doing that"
- Church as nonprofit wouldn't be taxed on investment earnings
- "All I got to have is somebody behind me that will do what I told them to do and make sure it takes"
- Accounts would be "sitting in the same accounts that people's retirement sitting in"
- Mutual funds (not individual stocks) are much safer
- "Everything that I have, every investment account that I've had for the last 20, 15, and 10 years has made at least 8%"
- Some accounts have made more than 8%
- Mr. Ramsey uses higher numbers, but Pastor Mark's are "a little more conservative"

#### Mission Fund Application
- "When it came to missions money...if we took funds and put some away for the next generation of missionaries and let it earn, then that money would be a whole lot more valuable"
- "There's missionaries that need money right now, we need to give right now, but if you took some of it..."
- Some people might "feel weird if we were investing our church's money into the stock market"
- Clarification: "Not one stock—mutual funds that are much safer"
- After 60 years, one person giving just $50,000 properly invested could provide ongoing monthly support: "That's not including the people that attend the church and give to it already. That's over and above. And that's one person giving, just if they did their retirement right and didn't have to use it all up and still did something for their own family"

### Closing Application
- "You could still do something like that if you start early enough and you put that...whether it's one, two, five...15%"
- "Not everybody can do that in the beginning, but as time goes, if you discipline yourself, it can be done and it works"
- "So you see, we've come all the way to this from looking at the Tribulation period where they're supposed to have nothing, and that's why it's so important that you see the difference and you see things differently"
