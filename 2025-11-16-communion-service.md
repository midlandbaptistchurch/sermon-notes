## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne leads a communion service emphasizing the spiritual significance and solemnity of the Lord's Supper. He explores how observing communion involves not just personal remembrance of Christ's sacrifice, but also recognition of the church as the body of Christ, with angels observing the manifold wisdom of God displayed through believers. The sermon stresses the importance of discerning the Lord's body—both Christ's sacrificial body and the unified body of the church—when partaking of communion.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 11:23-29 — Paul's instruction regarding the institution and proper observance of the Lord's Supper, including warnings about taking it unworthily.

## Key Themes
- The mystery of godliness: Christ dwelling in believers and God's manifold wisdom revealed through the church
- The ordinance of communion as memorial and command, not merely ritual
- Discerning the Lord's body: recognizing both Christ's sacrifice and the unity of the church
- Angels observing the church and learning God's wisdom through believers' conduct
- Self-examination and the importance of right relationship before taking communion
- The connection between personal godliness and corporate unity in the body of Christ

## Notes

### The Mystery of Godliness (1 Timothy 3:15-16)
- Believers are temples of the Holy Spirit; Christ in us is "the hope of glory"
- The angels are watching the body of Christ to observe God's manifold wisdom working through the church
- Ephesians 3:9 reveals that through the church, principalities and powers learn about God's divided plan regarding Israel, the church, and different dispensations

### The Significance of the Lord's Supper
- The term "Lord's Supper" (1 Corinthians 11:20) emphasizes dining, eating, drinking, and communion together
- Observed in the evening because a supper is an evening meal
- Jesus instituted it the night before his crucifixion, giving thanks for his body that would be broken and his blood that would be shed
- Paul received this ordinance by direct revelation from the Lord Jesus Christ, not merely from the apostles

### The New Testament and Christ's Blood
- A testament cannot be enforced until the testator dies
- The New Testament could not begin until Christ's death and the shedding of his blood on the cross
- Believers today participate in the spiritual blessings of the New Testament through the Holy Spirit

### Communion as Memorial and Ordinance (Exodus 12)
- Like the Passover, communion is both a command (ordinance) and a memorial
- It memorializes the work of the cross and Christ's sacrifice
- Exodus 12:14 and 13:9 show how God uses "memorial" and "ordinance" together to emphasize the preciousness of what is being remembered

### Proper Observance: Discerning the Lord's Body (1 Corinthians 11:27-29)
- Taking communion unworthily means not recognizing the significance of Christ's death, sacrifice, and shed blood
- "Unworthily" includes lack of gratitude, lack of remembrance, and lack of understanding of spiritual significance
- Discerning the Lord's body means recognizing both:
  - The physical body Christ bore when he came to sacrifice on the cross
  - The spiritual body of Christ—the church—which he formed and of which we are members
- The Corinthian church failed to discern the Lord's body by allowing divisions and inequality to manifest during their communion observance
- Believers must recognize their baptism into one body by one Spirit and maintain unity, charity, grace, and forgiveness toward one another
- Taking communion unworthily brings condemnation—not eternal damnation, but conviction and self-judgment for failing to do right

### The Call to Self-Examination
- Before partaking, believers should confess sin and repent
- They should thank the Lord for his blood and forgiveness
- If there is conflict with others in the body of Christ, believers should seek reconciliation, at least in their hearts, even if full restoration cannot happen immediately
- The goal is to truly "discern" both the sacrifice of Jesus Christ and the unity of his church body
