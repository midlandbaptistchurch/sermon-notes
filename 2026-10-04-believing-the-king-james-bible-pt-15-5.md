## Summary
This lesson examines the qualifications and work of Desiderius Erasmus in compiling the first printed Greek New Testament (later known as the Textus Receptus). Taylor establishes the two opposing viewpoints in Erasmus's time—the Latin Vulgate position held by Roman Catholics versus the Greek text authority position—and demonstrates how Erasmus, despite Roman Catholic opposition, possessed the credentials, manuscripts, network of scholars, and dedication necessary to produce a reliable Greek New Testament that would later become the basis for the King James Bible.

## Scripture
No primary passage examined—historical study of Bible transmission and translation.

## Key Themes
- **Authority of Greek Text vs. Latin Vulgate**: Two competing views existed regarding textual authority, with Roman Catholics defending the Latin Vulgate to preserve church authority, while Erasmus and others believed the Greek manuscripts held superior authority.
- **Erasmus's Qualifications**: Erasmus dedicated over 40 years to studying Greek manuscripts, traveled extensively collecting manuscripts, maintained a vast network of learned friends who supplied him with readings, and approached his work with scholarly rigor and passion.
- **Opposition to Erasmus's Work**: Roman Catholic theologians like Martin Dorp and Peter Soutour opposed Erasmus's Greek text publication, arguing that any error in the Vulgate would undermine church authority, which they considered impossible.
- **God's Providence in Bible Preservation**: Despite opposition and obstacles (including customs officials confiscating his manuscripts), God used Erasmus's life work to produce the Greek text that became foundational for the King James Bible.

## Notes

### Historical Context of Textual Authority
- Two main positions existed regarding New Testament textual authority in Erasmus's time
- Latin Vulgate position: held by Roman Catholic Church as official Bible
- Greek text authority position: held by Erasmus and some others, including Pope Leo X
- Erasmus dedicated his first edition of the Greek text to Pope Leo X for political reasons

### Laurentius Vala's Influence on Erasmus
- Vala lived 1405-1457, just before Erasmus
- Emphasized importance of language, believing civilization's decline in Dark Ages was due to decay of Greek and Latin languages
- Possessed Greek New Testament manuscripts
- Maintained Latin text often differed from Greek with additions and omissions
- Argued Greek wording was generally better than Latin
- Erasmus studied Vala's writings and agreed with his positions

### Roman Catholic Opposition
- Martin Dorp: leading expert on Latin text, theologian at University of Louvain
  - Wrote to Erasmus in 1514 asking him not to publish Greek text
  - Argued that if Vulgate contained errors, the church would have been wrong for centuries, which he considered impossible
  - Insisted church councils approved Latin Vulgate as official Bible and rejected Greek text as corrupted by "heretical Greek church"
  - Defended Latin primarily to defend the church, not truth
- Peter Soutour: French Catholic theologian, outspoken critic of Erasmus
  - Declared "if at one point the Vulgate were an error, the entire authority of Holy Scripture would collapse"
  - Opposition tied to protecting Roman Catholic power structure built on tithes and offerings

### Scholars Who Validated Erasmus's Work
- **William James Durant** (1885-1981): American historian and philosopher
  - With wife Ariel, produced 11-volume "The Story of Civilization"
  - Volume 6, Chapter 14 extensively covers Erasmus
  - Work translated into nine languages, sold over two million copies
- **James Anthony Froud** (1818-1894): English historian, novelist, biographer
  - Wrote "The Life and Letters of Erasmus" (published 1894)
- **John Joseph Mangan**: 20th century author and biographer
  - Wrote "The Life, Character, and Influence of Desiderius Erasmus of Rotterdam" (1927)
  - Two volumes relying heavily on Erasmus's own writings and correspondence
  - Purpose: let Erasmus speak for himself through direct quotation
- **Frederick Nolan** (1784-1864): Irish-Anglican theologian, Greek and Latin scholar
  - Defender of Textus Receptus as superior to all other Greek New Testament editions
  - Spent 28 years tracing Textus Receptus to apostolic origins
  - Argued first editors of printed Greek New Testament intentionally selected superior texts and disregarded inferior ones

### Erasmus's Manuscripts and Resources
- When Erasmus went to Basel in July 1515, he had at least five Greek New Testament manuscripts (documented by Dr. Edward Hills in "The King James Version Defended")
- These five manuscripts together covered the entire New Testament with some overlap
- Strong indications he had additional manuscripts beyond these five
- Erasmus traveled extensively looking for manuscripts everywhere
- Borrowed manuscripts from everyone he could during his travels
- Was surrounded by Bible manuscripts from childhood (1460s) until publication of Greek text (1516)—over 40 years of exposure

### Erasmus's Preparation and Dedication
- Durant: "The preparation for his work had taken years"
- Years before 1507, Erasmus was already working directly on the text
- 1505: Erasmus wrote to friend, "I shall sit down to holy scriptures with my whole heart and devote the rest of my life to it. All these three years I have been working entirely at Greek and have not been playing with it"
- In Italy, spent all time "devouring the libraries"
- Wanted positions that covered living expenses but gave maximum free time for study
- Mangan: comparing manuscripts "for the most correct reading of some intricate passage was his passion"
- Wanted to determine which readings were correct and why differences existed

### Erasmus's Scholarly Network
- Frederick Nolan (1815): In addition to manuscripts Erasmus owned or saw himself, "he gathered readings from the whole of Europe through his broad friendships"
- Erasmus: "I have a room full of letters from men of learning"
- Sought third-party validation and critique of his work
- Froud: Erasmus corresponded "at length and elaborately with the learned men of his time on technical points of scholarship, biblical criticism, etc."
- Erasmus: "It may easily be guessed how large a part of the usefulness of my work would have been lacking if my learned friends had not supplied me with manuscripts"
- His work was somewhat of a team effort with vast network of supporting scholars

### Obstacles Overcome
- When leaving England to go to continent in 1514, customs agents seized his manuscript collection
- Erasmus said they had "stolen the labors of his life"
- Manuscripts were eventually returned, allowing work to continue
- Arrived at Basel "way down with books and copious notes on the New Testament"—extensive library

### Application and Comparison
- Parallel drawn to Pastor Jim Payne's decades of study and book collection
- Just as Erasmus built knowledge of Greek text over lifetime, Payne built knowledge of King James text
- Both faced detractors and criticism
- Importance of having final authority (King James Bible) for recalibrating our lives
- Erasmus lived a fulfilling life dedicated to his passion
- We are beneficiaries of his life's work through the King James Bible
