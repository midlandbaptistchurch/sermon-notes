## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson examines the master-servant relationship as the third relationship modeling Christ as head and the church as body, alongside marriage and parenting. He emphasizes that this relationship, though unfamiliar to modern culture, is essential for understanding our role as servants of Christ who serve Him by serving His body. The sermon highlights that true service requires humility and submission to one another, working as unto the Lord rather than merely pleasing people, regardless of whether our earthly authorities are good or difficult.

## Scripture
Ephesians 6:5-9 — Servants are commanded to obey their masters according to the flesh with fear and trembling, in singleness of heart as unto Christ, not with eye service as men pleasers, but as servants of Christ doing God's will from the heart, knowing they will receive reward from the Lord whether bond or free.

Colossians 3:22-24 — Servants are to obey their masters in all things, not with eye service as men pleasers, but in singleness of heart fearing God, doing all heartily as to the Lord, knowing they will receive the reward of inheritance for they serve the Lord Christ.

## Key Themes
- **Three Relationships Modeling Christ and the Church**: Marriage, parenting, and master-servant relationships all demonstrate Christ as head and the church as body
- **Servanthood in the Body of Christ**: To serve Christ, believers must serve His body; serving one another is the practical way we serve our Master
- **Working as Unto the Lord**: True service is done with the right spirit and attitude regardless of circumstances or whether authorities are godly
- **Ownership by Christ**: Believers are bought with a price and belong to Christ, which means belonging to the body of Christ
- **Submission to One Another**: Ephesians 5:21 establishes the foundation of mutual submission in the fear of God
- **Grace Changes Perspective**: Under grace, service is not obligation but privilege—"I get to do it" rather than "I have to do it"

## Notes

### The Master-Servant Relationship Context
- This is the third of three relationships that model Christ as head and the church as body (marriage, parenting, master-servant)
- Though unfamiliar to modern culture, this relationship is placed alongside marriage and parenting as essential for spiritual maturity
- The relationship goes beyond employer-employee dynamics to illustrate our relationship with Christ as Master
- Culturally, people tend to think of slavery when hearing master-servant, but must separate that and see the biblical role clearly

### The Challenge of Understanding This Relationship
- Most believers will never be in a literal master-servant relationship in their lifetime
- Unlike husband/wife or parent/child roles that many experience, this requires going to Scripture to understand
- Being "perfect" (mature, complete) requires understanding all three relationships, not just the ones we naturally relate to
- Paul uses this relationship because it was common and accepted in his day, making it a practical illustration

### Serving Christ by Serving His Body
- Disciples had Christ in the flesh and could serve Him directly
- Today, Christ sits at the right hand of the Father, so we serve Him by serving His body
- To truly serve Christ as Master, believers must serve other believers
- The cycle: we do for others, others do for us, creating continuous service throughout the body
- Some struggle with letting others serve them, which breaks the cycle God designed

### The Problem in the Early Church
- When masters and servants both got saved, they became equal members of the body
- Satan would tempt servants to think: "He's no better than you, saved by same grace, going to same heaven"
- This created tension in maintaining the earthly master-servant relationship after spiritual equality
- Paul addresses this issue in 1 Timothy 6

### Obedience with Right Spirit
- Servants commanded to obey masters "according to the flesh" (temporal, earthly masters)
- To obey "with fear and trembling and singleness of heart as unto Christ"
- Not with "eye service" (only when being watched) as "men pleasers"
- But as servants of Christ doing God's will from the heart
- Same character and desire whether master is good or difficult

### The Example of Christ as Servant
- Philippians 2:1-8 presents Christ's example
- Though equal with God, He "made himself of no reputation"
- Took upon Him "the form of a servant"
- Humbled Himself and became obedient unto death, even death of the cross
- This is the mind believers are to have

### The Difficulty of Service Under Hardship
- Like wives submitting to unsaved husbands or children to ungodly parents, servants had to serve difficult masters
- Sin always complicates things and makes God's commands harder to follow
- The believer's response: go back to the cross where Christ suffered unfairly yet said "Father forgive them"
- Not our job to change others or make everything perfect around us
- Our job: do what God requires with right spirit and attitude regardless of difficulty

### Personal Application
- Pastor Mark acknowledges his own struggle: "didn't have a problem with authority as long as you liked them"
- His wife identified this issue: if he didn't think they were right, they didn't get respect
- Lesson he should have learned: submit and follow even when it doesn't make sense, doing it as unto the Lord
- The thought of being "owned" by someone is difficult, but believers are bought with a price by Christ

### Belonging to Christ and His Body
- 1 Corinthians 6:19-20: "Ye are not your own, ye are bought with a price"
- Privilege to be owned by Christ who paid such a price
- Our bodies are His, purchased with the sacrifice of His own body
- Paul called himself "servant of Jesus Christ" and "prisoner of Jesus Christ"
- If we belong to Christ our Master, we belong to the body of Christ
- This means belonging to one another and serving one another

### The Joy of Servanthood
- Queen of Sheba's observation of Solomon's servants: "Happy are thy servants"
- Despite hard labor building temple and palace without modern tools, servants were happy
- Solomon's servants worked hard yet maintained joyful spirit
- Contrast with Rehoboam whose harshness split the kingdom
- True servanthood under godly leadership produces happiness and fulfillment

### Foundation of Mutual Submission
- Ephesians 5:21 (immediately before the three relationships): "Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God"
- This establishes the principle for all three relationships
- As Christ humbled Himself, we are to humble ourselves
- Called to be servants one to another

### Grace Changes Everything
- Under grace, service is not obligation but privilege
- Not "I have to do it" but "I get to do it"
- This perspective transforms how we view submission and service
- Makes possible joy in serving even when circumstances are difficult
