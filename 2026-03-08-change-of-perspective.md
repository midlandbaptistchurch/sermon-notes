## Summary
Youth Pastor Andrew Watt challenges young people and parents to allow God to shift their perspective through real-world experiences. Drawing from Psalm 73, he addresses the spiritual danger of envying the prosperity of the wicked and emphasizes that only God can truly change our perspective—not our own efforts or careful shielding. The sermon reflects on a recent mission trip and practical ways to help young people develop a robust, God-centered worldview before facing faith-testing trials in college and beyond.

## Scripture
Psalm 73 — Asaph's struggle with envy of the wicked's prosperity and his discovery that true perspective comes only in God's sanctuary.

## Key Themes
- The danger of an untested, protected faith
- Envy of the wicked and their material prosperity
- Only God can shift and solidify perspective
- The importance of world-shifting, perspective-changing experiences
- Making church a priority beyond mere attendance
- Finding godly mentors and role models
- Parents modeling faith during hardship
- Mission trips and service as tools for perspective change

## Notes

### The Problem: Protected but Untested Faith
- Many churched youth grow up protected by their parents' faith without having their own perspective-shifting events
- They haven't seen the kind of world-altering spiritual experiences that test and mature faith
- This creates a dangerous vulnerability when they leave for college and face compelling arguments against their faith

### The Root Issue: Envy (Psalm 73)
- Young people (and adults) become envious of those who reject God yet prosper materially
- They see people who pursue worldly pleasures and worldly advancement, and their flesh becomes envious
- This envy leads to engagement with worldly things, which leads to entrapment
- The challenge is not unique to teens—it's a universal human struggle

### The Only Solution: God Must Change Perspective (Psalm 73:17)
- "Until I went into the sanctuary of God, then understood I their end"
- Parents cannot manufacture or protect their children from needing a changed perspective
- Parents cannot ensure their child's perspective will be protected from the world's influence
- God alone can solidify faith and ground it in truth

### Practical Ways to Facilitate Perspective Change
1. **Mission trips, camps, and volunteering** — Get young people out of their comfort zone where God can work
2. **Make church a priority** — Not just the formal service, but real community and living out faith at home
3. **Understand the home is the church** — Faith cannot be compartmentalized; young people need to see faith lived out daily
4. **Find godly mentors** — Young people will listen to someone besides their parents; help them find pastors, youth pastors, teachers, and role models who speak God's Word into their lives
5. **Show them how God has changed your perspective** — Parents should share (appropriately) how God sustains them through trials, how their perspective has shifted through faith, and why following God is worth it despite worldly opposition

### Key Illustrations
- The mission trip to see the Gerards serving God in challenging circumstances
- A pastor-counselor's theology being shaped by surviving a crocodile attack in South Africa; his neighbor came to Christ by witnessing his refined faith afterward
- Youth seeing wealthy, successful people living contrary to God's Word while righteous people labor and struggle
- The generational difference: previous generations kept finances and faith separate, but teens need to see how faith sustains parents through real struggles

### The Challenge for Parents and Leaders
- Don't wait until college for young people to have their first major perspective shift about faith
- Help create safe environments where perspective can be challenged and refined
- Recognize that some trials are out of our control—that's not a problem if parents are loving, obedient, and setting a godly pattern
- Teens are processing enormous amounts of information from school, social media, and peers; they need trusted voices speaking God's truth
