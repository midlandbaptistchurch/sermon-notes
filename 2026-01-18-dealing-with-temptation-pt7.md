## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on temptation by examining the second "but" in 1 Corinthians 10:13—that God is faithful and will not allow us to be tempted beyond our ability to resist. He explores how God became personally involved in our struggle by sending His Son in human form to experience temptation without sin, demonstrating His faithfulness and providing us with the righteousness of Christ.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — A passage that divides into three key truths about temptation, framed by three conjunctive "buts" that reveal God's character and provision.

## Key Themes
- God's faithfulness and personal involvement in every believer's life
- Christ's voluntary submission to temptation in the flesh without sinning
- The extent of God's power and presence—each believer has access to all of God
- God's willingness to take our place and make us righteous in Him
- The meaning of "suffer" in Scripture: to feel pain, to endure and sustain, and to allow or permit
- God will not allow temptation to exceed our capacity to resist

## Notes

### God Gets in the Boat With Us
The first "but" in 1 Corinthians 10:13 establishes that all of humanity shares the common experience of temptation. The second "but"—"God is faithful"—reveals that God didn't leave us alone in this struggle. God sent His Son to join us in our condition.

### Christ's Incarnation and Learning Through Suffering
Though Christ is God and knows all things, He voluntarily subjected Himself to take human form and learn obedience through suffering. This is unprecedented—God experiencing human existence firsthand. By doing so, He became a better God to us, making salvation and eternal life possible.

### God's Personal Investment in Each Believer
God is omnipotent, omnipresent, and omniscient. Because of His infinite nature, there is enough of God to be completely and personally involved in each believer's life individually. He is not stretched thin—He is fully God to each person who believes in Him.

### Christ Tempted in All Points
Hebrews 4:15 states that Christ "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin." Whatever temptations we face, Christ faced them. He experienced the full spectrum of human temptation while remaining sinless, enabling Him to understand our struggles and help us through them.

### Condemned Sin in the Flesh
Romans 8:3 and Philippians 2:5-8 show that God sent His Son in the likeness of sinful flesh to condemn sin and conquer it on our behalf. Christ humbled Himself, took the form of a servant, and became obedient unto death—even the death of the cross.

### Made the Righteousness of God
2 Corinthians 5:21 reveals the exchange: Christ was made sin for us (though He knew no sin) so that we might be made the righteousness of God in Him. Just as God has infinite power to give to each believer, He has infinite righteousness to bestow. Our standing before God is secured in Christ.

### The Meaning of "Suffer"
The word "suffer" in Scripture has three primary meanings:
1. To feel or bear what is painful, disagreeable, or distressing—to undergo pain
2. To endure, support, and sustain—not to sink under a burden
3. To allow or permit—not to forbid or hinder

### God Will Not Allow Temptation Beyond Our Ability
When 1 Corinthians 10:13 says God "will not suffer you to be tempted above that you're able," it means God will not permit temptation to reach the point where our resistance necessarily crumbles. God knows our limits better than we do and maintains that boundary. If we give in to temptation, it is not God's fault—He has been faithful to His promise.

### The Faithfulness of Our God
God's faithfulness means:
- He gets in the boat of temptation with us
- He experienced temptation in human form without sinning
- He conquered sin and death on our behalf
- He personally sustains us in our struggles
- He will not allow temptation to exceed our capacity to resist
- He provides a way of escape (to be explored in the next sermon)
