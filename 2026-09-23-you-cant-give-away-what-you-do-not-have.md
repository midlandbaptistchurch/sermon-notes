## Summary
Willie Wackerly challenges the congregation to recognize that genuine spiritual fruit, gospel witness, and Christian living can only flow from what believers truly possess inwardly through the Holy Spirit and the Word of God. Drawing on Paul's final instructions in 2 Timothy, an analysis of dos and don'ts in Ephesians 4–6, personal evangelism stories, and a striking illustration from Vietnam-era POW camps, he urges believers to study Scripture diligently so they are equipped to share their faith when opportunity arises. The central, repeated refrain is: "You cannot give away what you do not have."

## Scripture
2 Timothy 3:16-17 — Paul's declaration that all Scripture is God-breathed and profitable, cited as a key verse believers should memorize for doctrine, correction, and equipping for every good work. Additional passages woven throughout include Titus 2:11-12, 2 Timothy 2:15, 2 Timothy 4:6-7, Ephesians 2:10, Ephesians 4–6 (the "duty" half of Ephesians), Galatians 5:22-23, James 3:10-12, Acts 3:6, Acts 9:20, 1 Corinthians 15:3-4, Colossians 3:21, Proverbs 15:1, and Proverbs 22:6.

## Key Themes
- You cannot give away what you do not have — spiritually, doctrinally, or personally
- The necessity of Spirit-empowered living over flesh-driven religious performance
- Scripture memory and Bible study as preparation for evangelism and daily witness
- The fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5) as the foundation of Christian duty (Ephesians 4–6)
- Speaking truth in love — balancing positive encouragement (dos) with correction (don'ts)
- Parenting and discipleship: modeling a two-thirds positive, one-third corrective approach
- Paul's progressive revelation as a model: he only gave what he had received

## Notes

### Review of Titus 2:11-12
Wackerly opens by referencing a prior message from Pastor Mark on Titus 2:11-12 — living soberly, righteously, and godly — noting that righteousness without godliness is insufficient; a person can know how Christians are supposed to act without truly being godlike.

### Paul's Final Words: 2 Timothy
2 Timothy is framed as Paul's deathbed instructions. Key verses highlighted for memorization:
- **2 Timothy 3:16-17** — All Scripture is profitable; the man of God may be thoroughly furnished.
- **2 Timothy 2:15** — Study to show yourself approved unto God, rightly dividing the word of truth.
- **2 Timothy 4:6-7** — Paul's confident farewell: "I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith."
- **Psalm 1:1** — Blessed is the man who walks not in the counsel of the ungodly.

### Rightly Dividing Ephesians: Doctrine and Duty
Ephesians is divided structurally:
- **Chapters 1–3** = Doctrine (the triune God and salvation)
- **Chapters 4–6** = Duty (how believers are to live)

Within the duty section (~89 verses), Wackerly counts approximately **62 dos** and **29 don'ts** — roughly two-thirds positive commands to one-third prohibitions. The point: God emphasizes what to *do* far more than what *not* to do.

### Parenting Application (Ephesians 6:4; Colossians 3:21)
The 2:1 ratio of dos to don'ts applies practically to parenting. Fathers are warned not to provoke children to wrath (Ephesians 6:4; Colossians 3:21). Children who hear only correction and never affirmation become discouraged. The principle: for every correction, offer at least two affirmations. Proverbs 15:1 ("A soft answer turneth away wrath") and Proverbs 22:6 ("Train up a child in the way he should go") reinforce speaking truth in love.

> *"Love without truth is hypocrisy. Truth without love is brutality."* — Wackerly's personal ditty

### Walking in the Spirit (Ephesians; Galatians 5)
Paul uses the word "walk" 37 times in his epistles, 7 times in Ephesians alone:
- Walk in good works (Ephesians 2:10)
- Walk worthy of your calling (Ephesians 4:1)
- Walk in love as Christ loved us (Ephesians 5:2)
- Walk circumspectly, not as fools but as wise (Ephesians 5:15)
- Walk as children of light (Ephesians 5:8)

Ephesians 5:9 — the fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness, righteousness, and truth — is connected to Galatians 5:22-23. The conclusion: genuine goodness, righteousness, and truth are impossible apart from the Holy Spirit's indwelling and empowering. The flesh can imitate Christian behavior, but only the Spirit produces real fruit.

### "You Cannot Give Away What You Do Not Have"
The sermon's core principle, illustrated multiple ways:

1. **Acts 3:6** — Peter and John had no silver or gold, but gave what they *did* have: healing in the name of Jesus Christ.
2. **Paul's progressive ministry** — In Acts 9:20, Paul's first sermon proclaimed only that Jesus is the Son of God. The full gospel of death, burial, and resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:3-4), justification, and the mystery of the Body of Christ came later through further revelation (2 Corinthians 12:1) and the prison epistles. He gave only what he had received.
3. **Assurance of salvation** — If you do not possess assurance of your own salvation, you cannot effectively share it with others.
4. **Conviction on the Word of God** — Without a settled belief in the preserved Word of God, you will be confused when confronted by competing Bible versions. You must possess that conviction before you can give it away.
5. **James 3:10-12** — A fountain cannot yield both sweet and bitter water; a fig tree cannot bear olive berries. What comes out in moments of anger or pride reveals what is actually inside.

### Personal Testimonies
- **Hospital visit**: As a young man without the gospel, Wackerly visited a dying, angry former employee — a man he knew needed what that beloved childhood preacher had — but he had nothing to offer. The man died without Christ, as far as Wackerly knows.
- **Kitchen table evangelism**: Years later, equipped through Bible study, Wackerly led a distant relative to Christ after she raised an objection about God condemning humanity for one sin. His knowledge of Romans 5 (one man's sin, one Savior's righteousness) gave him the answer, and she trusted Christ two minutes later.

### Closing Illustration: POW Camps in Vietnam
Wackerly was drafted during the Tet Offensive but did not go to Vietnam. He later read about conditions in North Vietnamese POW camps — daily torture, total isolation, divide-and-conquer tactics. Despite those conditions, some men shared the gospel through whispers and signals through walls, and men were saved. The challenge: if you were in total isolation, could you lead someone to Christ from memory? The closing prayer calls believers to read, study, and memorize Scripture so they are always ready to give away what they have.
