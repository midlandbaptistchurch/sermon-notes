## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson continues his series on biblical sobriety, exploring what it means to live as a sober Christian in the age of grace. Drawing on Paul's appearance before King Agrippa in Acts 26 as a living example of sobriety in action, he shows how truth paired with soberness produces clarity, confidence, and conviction without manipulation. The sermon also examines Titus 2:11-14, unpacking the coupling of "soberly, righteously, and godly" as three interlocking adverbs that together shape a sanctified life.

## Scripture
- **Acts 26:1-29** — Paul's defense before King Agrippa and Festus, used as the primary illustration of biblical sobriety lived out.
- **2 Corinthians 5:13** — "For whether we be beside ourselves, it is to God: or whether we be sober, it is for your cause." Provides a contrast defining sobriety against being "beside oneself."
- **2 Corinthians 11:3** — "But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtlety, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ." The thematic backdrop of the series.
- **Titus 2:11-14** — "Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present world." Central text for the three-adverb study.
- **2 Corinthians 7:9-11** — Godly sorrow and repentance illustrated as an example of the word "godly" in practice.

## Key Themes
- Biblical sobriety as a way of life, not merely abstinence from alcohol
- Truth combined with soberness produces clarity, conviction, and peace
- The danger of manipulation versus the power of genuine conviction
- Studying Bible words in context: the four C's (Context, Compare, Coupling, Contrast)
- Living soberly, righteously, and godly as three coupled adverbs shaping sanctification
- Godly repentance versus mere confession
- Personal responsibility to study Scripture under grace

## Notes

### The Problem Soberness Solves
- The series frames 2 Corinthians 11:3 as the backdrop: Satan's subtlety can corrupt minds away from the simplicity in Christ.
- Biblical sobriety is what neutralizes that subtlety — a sober person is not taken in by deception.
- Definition of sober offered: regular, calm, not under the influence of drama; serious, temperate, rational, not mad or heated with passion; having the regular exercise of cool, dispassionate reason.
- A sober person knows what they believe, why they believe it, and is more influenced by truth than by outward circumstances.

### The Four C's of Bible Word Study (Review)
1. **Context** — Always read a word within its surrounding passage.
2. **Compare** — Use a concordance to find every use of the word and read each in context.
3. **Coupling** — Note words paired with the target word (especially in Paul's writings).
4. **Contrast** — Look for places where the opposite of the word is shown (e.g., 2 Cor. 5:13: "beside ourselves" vs. "sober").

### Paul Before Agrippa — Sobriety in Action (Acts 26)
- Paul, a prisoner, appears before King Agrippa and Festus. Despite his circumstances, he speaks with happiness and confidence.
- He gives his full testimony without pulling punches — his life before salvation, his conversion, and his call to the Gentiles.
- When Festus interrupts and accuses Paul of being "beside himself" (mad, out of his mind), Paul calmly replies: *"I am not mad, most noble Festus; but speak forth the words of truth and soberness."* (Acts 26:25)
- Paul then turns straight back to Agrippa without missing a beat — soberness kept him from being derailed by the insult.
- Contrast drawn between **manipulation** (controlling others through guilt, deceit, subtlety) and **conviction** (presenting truth and allowing others to decide freely).
- Agrippa's response — "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian" — shows the power of truth with soberness even when rejected.

### Soberly, Righteously, and Godly (Titus 2:11-14)
- Three adverbs coupled together: soberly, righteously, godly.
- Adverbs modify — they restrain or enlarge the life they describe.
- **Righteously** — relates to judgments, decisions, discernment, one's walk; appears eight times in Scripture, always connected to judging or one's conduct.
- **Godly** (little g) — being set apart, holy, separated unto God; not just what you do but who you are.
- Soberness ties the two together: without it, a person may try to be righteous without godliness (self-righteous rule-keeping) or godly without righteousness (understanding truth but never applying it).
- Grace teaches this — it is not a list of commands but principles that believers work out with fear and trembling.

### Godly Sorrow and Repentance (2 Corinthians 7:9-11)
- "Godly" sorrow (sorrowed after a godly manner) means recognizing that sin creates distance from God.
- Repentance is not merely saying sorry at bedtime; it involves real actions and a turn back toward God.
- Result: being "cleared" in all things — already forgiven, but now also free of undealt-with sin.
- Soberness helps a believer see the difference between going through motions and genuine repentance.

### Call to Personal Study
- Believers are responsible to take up the Word themselves, not rely solely on the pastor's study.
- Study takes time and effort — the content of this message took the speaker hours to prepare.
- The Bible (King James) is treated as the final authority; English grammar study applied directly to the text is encouraged.
