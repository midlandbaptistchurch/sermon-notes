## Summary
Assoc. Pastor Mark Simpson teaches that living under grace requires an internal motivation that law-based living does not demand. He argues that believers must learn to assess their own value before God — not as worldly self-esteem, but as a biblically grounded recognition that each person is significant, relevant, and important to God's purposes. Without this self-understanding, Christians will fail to invest seriously in their walk with God.

## Scripture
Ephesians 4:16 — The whole body fitly joined together, each part contributing to the growth and edifying of the body in love; used to illustrate that every believer is an indispensable part of Christ's body.

Galatians 6:1 — "Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted" — cited to show that ministering to others requires first taking care of and considering oneself.

## Key Themes
- Grace motivation versus law motivation
- Every believer's significance to the body of Christ
- Self-worth rooted in what Christ paid, not worldly self-esteem
- Faithfulness and personal investment in one's walk with God
- The judgment seat of Christ as a sober reality for believers
- Each believer's unique, irreplaceable role in God's work

## Notes
**Grace Motivation**
- Under the law, fear of judgment provided external motivation to obey. Under grace, there is little immediate consequence for sin, so motivation must come from within — from a proper understanding of one's value to God.
- Grace does not force obedience; it invites willing, enthusiastic participation in God's work.

**Assessing Value to Yourself**
- The starting point for grace motivation is learning to see your own value as a believer.
- The world offers a counterfeit version of this (worldly self-esteem), but the biblical version is rooted in what Christ paid on the cross and in the believer's identity as the body of Christ, the temple of God, and the building of God.
- If you do not see yourself as valuable, you will not invest seriously in prayer, Bible study, church attendance, or ministry.

**First Key Word: Significance** *(Ephesians 4:16)*
- Significance means "having meaning."
- Every believer has meaning to God and to the age of grace. God's goal is for His body to be fitly joined together — every part matters.
- Even private acts of faithfulness (personal Bible study, choosing holiness when no one is watching, recognizing God as Creator in prayer) carry enormous significance to God, even when unnoticed by others.
- Illustrated by the idea that recognition from an unexpected or unlikely person often means more than recognition from those expected to give it.

**Second Key Word: Relevance**
- Relevance means "to have bearing upon or to be connected with the matter at hand."
- Believers should feel genuinely connected to the work of the Lord — to the body of Christ, to the time period they live in, and to the God who died for them.
- A sense of relevance produces ownership and responsibility: if it is God's and you are God's, then it is yours to steward.
- When believers do not feel relevant, they default to passivity — assuming someone else will handle it, or that their contribution does not matter.

**Third Key Word: Importance**
- Importance is the most direct of the three: Am I important to what God is doing?
- This must be answered personally, not just affirmed in group settings.
- Illustrated through the relationship with a spouse: if a husband recognizes his wife's significance, relevance, and importance to him — working actively to maintain that relationship — how much more should that same energy be directed toward one's relationship with the Lord?
- The very fact that God leaves saved believers in sin-cursed bodies means He values the choice to obey — obedience chosen freely, against the pull of the flesh, means more to God than obedience with no alternative.
- The example of Job: God pointed to Job before Satan as evidence of faithful devotion — suffering handled rightly is significant to the Lord.

**Practical Application**
- Ask yourself: What do I think about going to bed and waking up? What has my heart, mind, and material investment?
- Showing up to Bible study when you do not feel like it, serving quietly without seeking credit, teaching a difficult children's class, running the sound and video — all of these are acts of significance, relevance, and importance before God.
- Cited Galatians 6:1 — "considering thyself" — as a call for each believer to take personal stock and ensure they are spiritually strong and rightly motivated.
- Believers under grace should approach their walk not as something they *have to* do, but as something they *get to* do.
