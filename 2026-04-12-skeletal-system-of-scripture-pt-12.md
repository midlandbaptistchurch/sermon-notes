## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues part 12 of "The Skeletal System of Scripture" series, exploring the foundational structure of the Bible from Genesis to Revelation. He focuses on understanding God's purpose in creation, the judgment that followed Lucifer's rebellion, and the significance of light as God manifests His glory in the physical creation.

## Scripture
Colossians 1:9 — Paul's inspired prayer that believers be filled with the knowledge of God's will; Genesis 1:1-3 — The creation account and God's declaration of light.

## Key Themes
- The skeletal system of Scripture: understanding the Bible's structure from Genesis to Revelation
- Knowledge of God's will and increasing in the knowledge of God
- God's creative work and His judgment upon rebellion
- The three aspects of physical creation: the heaven and the earth, the heavens and the earth, and the new heaven and the new earth
- The three worlds: the angelic world, the old world (before Noah's flood), and the present evil world
- Genesis 1:2 as a declaration of God's judgment, not creation
- The light of God's glory and its significance in creation
- Living as children of light in a dark world

## Notes
- The goal of the series is to help believers be "filled with the knowledge of his will" (Colossians 1:9)
- Walking worthy of the Lord requires understanding God's will from Scripture
- The physical creation is divided into three stages: initial creation, the heavens and earth (present), and the new heaven and earth (future)
- Four worlds are presented: the angelic world, the old world (destroyed by flood in Noah's time), the present evil world, and the world to come (millennial kingdom)
- Genesis 1:2 describes God's judgment on the rebellion of Lucifer using terminology found only one other time in Scripture (Jeremiah 4:23)
- "Without form and void" and "darkness" indicate judgment, not creation
- The light spoken of in Genesis 1:3 is not the sun (created on day four) but God's glory shining from His throne
- God's throne dwells in a realm of light that no man can approach (1 Timothy 6:16)
- The waters above the firmament hide the face of God's throne from human sight
- Believers are translated from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of God's dear son and called to walk as light
- The King James Bible accurately preserves these distinctions in translation
