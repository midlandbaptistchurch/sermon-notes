## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne preaches that boldness is a missing but essential virtue in the Christian life, drawing on Paul's prayer request in Ephesians 6 that he might "speak boldly" the mystery of the Gospel. The sermon identifies four sources of spiritual boldness — the blood of Christ, the presence of Christ, the power of the Holy Spirit, and the practice of righteousness — and then challenges believers to apply that boldness in three areas: proclaiming God's Word, confessing Christ before the world, and facing the coming judgment seat of Christ with confidence.

## Scripture
Ephesians 6:18-20 — Paul's prayer request from a Roman prison, asking the Ephesian believers to intercede that he might open his mouth boldly to make known the mystery of the Gospel. Supporting passages include Hebrews 10:19, Acts 4:13, Acts 4:23-31, Proverbs 28:1, Hebrews 13:5-6, and 1 John 4:17.

## Key Themes
- Boldness as a daily, renewable spiritual virtue
- Prayer as the battleground of spiritual warfare, not merely a piece of armor
- The blood of Christ as the foundation of confident access to God
- Time spent in the presence of Christ producing boldness in believers
- The filling of the Holy Spirit evidenced by bold proclamation of God's Word
- Righteous living silencing a guilty conscience and freeing the believer to speak
- The judgment seat of Christ approached with boldness because of our standing in Christ

## Notes

### How Believers Obtain Boldness

**1. Through the blood of Christ (Hebrews 10:19)**
Boldness to enter the holy of holies belongs to every believer because of the sinless, powerful blood Christ shed on Calvary and sprinkled on the heavenly mercy seat. Knowing one is fully accepted by God — sealed by the Spirit, bought by the blood — is the daily foundation of bold Christian living.

**2. Through the presence of Christ (Acts 4:13)**
When the Sanhedrin observed Peter and John's boldness, they recognized that these men had been with Jesus. Time spent with Christ in prayer, Bible study, and fellowship with God's people produces boldness. Unlike human familiarity that can breed contempt, closeness with Christ only deepens courage.

**3. Through the power of the Holy Spirit (Acts 4:23-31)**
After the apostles prayed together under threat, the place was shaken and they were filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke the Word of God with boldness. Pastor Payne argues that the biblical evidence of Spirit-filling is bold, intelligible proclamation of Christ — not unintelligible speech. The Holy Spirit came to magnify Jesus (John 15), and a Spirit-filled believer will do the same.

**4. Through the practice of righteousness (Proverbs 28:1)**
"The righteous are bold as a lion." A guilty conscience is one of the quickest ways to silence a Christian witness. Conversely, those who hunger and thirst after righteousness, deal with sin, and seek to live godly lives find that practical holiness breeds boldness.

### Three Areas Requiring Boldness

**1. Boldness to preach and declare God's Word (Acts 9:26-29)**
Even after 30 years of ministry and imprisonment, Paul still asked for fresh boldness to preach. Pastor Payne calls preachers and all believers to bold, unapologetic declaration of truth, noting that Christ, John the Baptist, and Paul were never soft-spoken about the Gospel or sin.

**2. Boldness to profess Christ before the world (Hebrews 13:5-6)**
"The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me." This confession must move beyond the church walls into workplaces, family gatherings, and everyday conversations. The fear of man is a snare; the issue is never ability but dependability.

**3. Boldness to face the coming judgment (1 John 4:17)**
"As he is, so are we in this world." The judgment seat of Christ, while a time of accounting, can be faced with boldness by every believer because God sees us in His Son. Our sins have been dealt with at the cross; what remains is the joy of giving account before a Savior who is also our friend.
