## Summary
This sermon continues the missions week theme by examining the means God uses to accomplish His mission work. Building on previous sessions about motive, message, messenger, method, and mandate, this message focuses on three key means: prayer, provision (finances), and people. Using Philippians 4 as the primary text, the sermon emphasizes that all believers are called to participate in missions through partnership, and that such participation produces eternal fruit that will matter at the Judgment Seat of Christ.

## Scripture
Philippians 4:10-19 — Paul commends the Philippian church for their partnership in his ministry through financial giving, describing their gifts as a sweet-smelling sacrifice well-pleasing to God, and promises that God will supply all their needs according to His riches in glory.

## Key Themes
- **Missions Participation is Universal**: Christianity is participatory, not spectatorial; all believers are called to be laborers together with God in missions
- **The Power of Prayer**: Prayer is a foundational means of missions, illustrated by the speaker's grandmother whose prayers led to his salvation and eventual missionary service
- **Sacrificial Giving**: The Macedonian believers gave sacrificially despite their poverty, communicating with Paul in his affliction through financial partnership
- **Eternal Fruit**: Participation in missions produces eternal fruit that abounds to the giver's account, providing reward at the Judgment Seat of Christ
- **Partnership in Ministry**: God uses partnerships between local churches, individuals, and missionaries to accomplish His work
- **Contentment Through Christ**: Paul's example shows believers can learn to be content in any circumstance through Christ's strength

## Notes

### Introduction and Context
- Series theme: "Missions, God's Magnificent Obsession"
- God is obsessed with His Son, and missions brings honor and glory to Christ
- 1 Timothy 2:4: God "will have all men to be saved and come to the knowledge of the truth"
- Previous sessions covered: motive (God's will), message (the cross and resurrection according to Scripture), messenger (who God sends), method (how God evangelizes), mandate (the Great Commission vs. great commitment), and provision/finances

### Salvation is By Grace Alone
- Speaker's testimony: saved at age 25 after years of religious activity
- Baptized at age 9 but not saved
- Heard gospel teaching that made sense: living in the age of grace between the cross and the rapture
- Convicted for days, unable to sleep, finally called on Christ from Romans 10:9, 13
- Strong emphasis: salvation is by grace through faith, not works
- "I'm not a fruit inspector" - rejection of judging others' salvation by their works
- If someone gets saved and later backslides, they're still saved
- Grace saved us, and we should live by grace, give by grace, go by grace, serve by grace

### The Means of Missions: Prayer
- Someone was praying for Brother Tim before his salvation
- His Pentecostal grandmother prayed consistently: "God, save your boys in there. Make them preachers. Call them to be missionaries."
- Though her doctrine was flawed, she knew how to pray (talk to God)
- She signed every letter: "Jesus is my bestest friend"
- Her prayers were answered: speaker's brother got saved first and became a preacher; speaker got saved and became a missionary
- God hears and answers prayers even when doctrine isn't perfect
- "You can pray too little, but you can't pray too much"

### The Means of Missions: Provision
- Reference to Friday night's teaching on financial giving
- "You can never out-give the Lord"
- Financial provision is one ingredient God asks us to provide for missions
- After God has your heart, He has your pocketbook, time, talent, and treasure

### The Means of Missions: People
- God uses people to give, pray, go, send, and reach
- Some are called to go, some to send, but all are called to participate
- Christianity is participatory, not spectatorial
- Illustration: football players can't just watch the game without wanting to participate
- We are "laborers together with God" (1 Corinthians 3:9)
- Partnership exists between local churches, individuals, and with the Holy Spirit
- "You never can reach too many people"

### Philippians 4:10-19 - Partnership in Missions

#### Paul's Contentment (vv. 10-13)
- Paul rejoiced that the Philippians' care for him flourished again
- They wanted to help even when they lacked opportunity
- Paul learned to be content in whatever state he was in
- He learned both how to be abased and how to abound
- He was instructed how to be full and hungry, to abound and suffer need
- "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me" (v. 13) - this is the context
- Speaker visited the Mamertine prison in Rome where Paul wrote this
- Only 15 people at a time can go down because it's so tiny and wretched
- Paul was content even in that horrible place
- There's no excuse for Christians to be discontent

#### The Philippians' Communication (vv. 14-15)
- "You have well done that ye did communicate with my affliction"
- They communicated financially, not just with words
- "In the beginning of the gospel" - refers to Acts 16 when Paul first came to Philippi
- This was after Acts 15 when much doctrine was settled
- From Macedonia, no other church communicated with Paul concerning giving and receiving except the Philippians
- The poorest crowd was the only one giving to Paul's missions ministry

#### Eternal Fruit (vv. 16-17)
- They sent to Paul's necessity once and again
- Paul says: "Not that I desire a gift, but I desire fruit that may abound to your account"
- The fruit Paul refers to is eternal fruit
- This fruit abounds to the giver's account
- Example: 48 churches planted in Myanmar, people being saved
- Midland Baptist Church has strategic church planting with three-year declining financial support plan ($150/month year 1, $100/month year 2, $100/month year 3)
- Goal is indigenous churches that are self-supporting
- Prayer partners who never met Brother James have eternal fruit from his ministry
- All the statistics of salvations and church plants represent eternal fruit
- This matters at the Judgment Seat of Christ (2 Corinthians 5)
- "You'll be glad you participated in some of that eternal fruit at the Judgment Seat of Christ"
- "You're going to need every ace in the deck"

#### Illustration: The Grandmother's Eternal Reward
- Speaker's grandmother will have eternal fruit at the judgment
- Despite being "messed up on her doctrine," she prayed faithfully
- She died in 1987 but is still getting eternal reward
- The brown people in Myanmar are "fruit of your account, Granny"
- She participated in missions partnership through prayer for her grandson
- Her prayers continue to produce fruit decades after her death

#### A Sweet-Smelling Sacrifice (v. 18)
- "But I have all, and abound: I am full"
- Paul was in a cold, damp prison cell but said he had all and was full
- Epaphroditus brought gifts from the Philippians to Paul
- Paul described it as "an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God"
- The poor Macedonians gave sacrificially
- Paul accepted it as a sacrifice
- Result: Paul was full and it was well-pleasing to God, not just to Paul
- This is how they "communicated" with Paul's affliction

#### Illustration: The Sacrificial Grandmother
- Speaker's grandfather was a miser who never learned to tithe even after being saved late in life
- Grandmother had a small budget for food
- When grandfather wasn't looking, she would give her son Joe a check to put in the offering plate
- She gave sacrificially to their church
- This was well-pleasing unto God

#### God's Promise (v. 19)
- "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus"
- Speaker has this verse next to his kitchen sink
- Context matters: this promise comes with conditions
- God obligates Himself to meet your need as you meet other people's needs
- The condition: giving sacrificially in a way that's well-pleasing to the Lord
- Everybody can participate in this as long as they're determined to do so

### Closing Application
- God is interested in us giving, going, praying, sending, and reaching folks around the world
- This is God's magnificent obsession
- Goal for believers: determine to serve the Lord with fervor until Jesus comes
- Goal for the unsaved: hear, understand, and believe the gospel and be gloriously saved
