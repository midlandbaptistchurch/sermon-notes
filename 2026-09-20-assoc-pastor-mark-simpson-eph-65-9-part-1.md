## Summary
This sermon examines the master-servant relationship as the third example of how Christ is the head and we are the body. Pastor Simpson explores why this relationship was addressed so frequently in Scripture, showing how servants struggled with their position after salvation since they were now "brothers beloved" with their masters yet still expected to serve. The teaching prepares believers for spiritual warfare by establishing the proper mindset of humility and submission to Christ.

## Scripture
Ephesians 6:5-9 — Servants are instructed to be obedient to their masters with fear and trembling, as unto Christ, doing the will of God from the heart. Masters are commanded to do the same things toward their servants, forbearing threatening, knowing that both have a Master in heaven who shows no partiality.

## Key Themes
- **Master-Servant Relationship as Picture of Christ and the Church**: This relationship was a significant issue in the early church because servants struggled with remaining in their role after becoming "brothers beloved" with their masters in Christ.
- **Balance Under Grace**: Paul addressed this relationship in multiple letters (Ephesians, 1 Timothy, Titus, Philemon), showing both the temporal nature of earthly roles and the eternal value of faithful service unto the Lord.
- **Preparation for Spiritual Warfare**: The teaching on submission in these three relationships (husband-wife, parent-child, master-servant) prepares believers for true spiritual warfare against principalities and powers rather than flesh and blood.

## Notes

### The Master-Servant Relationship Context
- This was the third relationship Paul used to illustrate Christ as head and the church as body
- None of us live in this relationship today, making it less familiar
- This was a significant issue in the early church because the majority of people were either masters or servants
- The struggle: servants questioned why they should remain servants to fellow members of the body of Christ

### Paul's Instructions to Servants (Ephesians 6:5-8)
- Be obedient with fear and trembling in singleness of heart as unto Christ
- Not with eyeservice as men-pleasers, but as servants of Christ
- Do the will of God from the heart with good will
- Serve as to the Lord and not to men
- Whatever good thing any man does, he will receive the same from the Lord, whether bond or free

### Paul's Instructions to Masters (Ephesians 6:9)
- Do the same things unto servants
- Forbear threatening
- Remember your Master is in heaven
- There is no respect of persons with God

### Related Passages on Master-Servant Relationship

#### 1 Timothy 6:1-2
- Servants under the yoke should count their masters worthy of all honor
- Purpose: that the name of God and His doctrine not be blasphemed
- Those with believing masters should not despise them because they are brethren
- Rather serve them because they are faithful and beloved, partakers of the benefit
- Timothy instructed to teach and exhort these things

#### Titus 2:9-10
- Servants to be obedient to their own masters
- Please them well in all things
- Not answering again (talking back)
- Not purloining (holding back) - this word appears only once in Scripture
- Show all good fidelity
- Purpose: that they may adorn the doctrine of God our Savior in all things
- The servants were holding back because they didn't feel they should still be in that position

### The Transitional Context: 1 Corinthians 7
- The entire chapter deals with transition from law to grace
- Paul uses the phrase "the time is short" (verse 29) referring to the transitional period
- This was the time when Paul could still use signs, miracles, and wonders to reach Jewish people
- This period would end (evidenced by Paul leaving Trophimus sick, telling Timothy to use wine for stomach issues, and Paul remaining in prison without miraculous deliverance)

#### Specific Instructions in 1 Corinthians 7
- Verse 6: Paul speaks "by permission and not of commandment"
- Verse 27-29: Discusses marriage in context of "the time is short"
- Those with wives should be as though they had none (during this short transitional period)
- This doesn't contradict Ephesians 5:22-33 but was specific to that season
- Balance needed: neither family nor ministry should always come first, but both are important
- Verse 21: "Art thou called being a servant? Care not for it: but if thou mayest be made free, use it rather"
- If opportunity for freedom from servitude existed during this time, take it
- Verse 22: "He that is called in the Lord, being a servant, is the Lord's freeman"
- Verse 23: "Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men"
- Verse 40: "But she is happier if she so abide, after my judgment: and I think also that I have the Spirit of God"
- Paul acknowledges this is his judgment, though Spirit-inspired, not a direct commandment for all time

### The Book of Philemon: A Case Study
- Paul writes from prison about Onesimus, a runaway servant of Philemon
- Onesimus was likely saved and discipled while in prison with Paul
- Paul describes Onesimus as his "son whom I have begotten in my bonds" (verse 10)
- Onesimus was "in time past unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me" (verse 11)
- Paul wanted to keep Onesimus to minister to him but wouldn't do so without Philemon's consent (verse 13-14)
- Onesimus belonged to Philemon; Paul had no right to retain him
- Verse 15: "Perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever"
- Verse 16: "Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved"
- Paul commands Philemon to receive Onesimus as a "brother beloved," especially in the flesh and in the Lord
- Verse 17-18: "If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself. If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account"
- Paul even wrote in his own hand that he would repay what Onesimus owed
- This shows the tension: Onesimus was a "brother beloved" but still technically a servant

### Why This Teaching Matters for Spiritual Warfare
- The three relationships (husband-wife, parent-child, master-servant) prepare believers for verses 10-18
- Verse 10: "Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might"
- This leads into the armor of God and spiritual warfare
- Having the mindset to not wrestle against flesh and blood but against principalities, powers, rulers of darkness, and spiritual wickedness in high places
- Most people don't spend time in spiritual warfare because they spend all their time in temporal warfare and fleshly pursuits
- If you can't take on the role and commitment level required in these three relationships, you won't effectively engage in spiritual warfare

### The Connection to Humility and Submission
- The sin-cursed flesh does not like to submit
- Natural human desire: don't want to be told what to do, how to do it, or be corrected
- This doesn't line up with being the body of Christ
- Must fight against this and put it in its place
- Need to study these roles to understand the level of submission required
- This develops the humility demonstrated by Christ in Philippians 2 when He went to the cross
- Having this level of submission and humility prepares believers to be spiritually effective toward the Lord and the body of Christ

### The Difficulty for Servants
- Being a servant was hard enough
- After salvation, it became even harder to view the master and give work, reverence, and respect "as unto the Lord and not unto man"
- Required changing their mindset
- Had to value their relationship with the Lord so highly that they could serve in this way
- Didn't have to have what the master had
- Didn't have to be in control
- Didn't have to have the possessions
- Had to serve not as men-pleasers but even when the master wasn't looking, knowing God sees

### Application for Today
- We live in a whole different culture, government system, and environment
- Don't have to deal with master-servant relationships
- But looking back helps us understand how hard it would have been
- Particularly difficult after salvation to give this service "as unto the Lord"
- The same level of commitment and discipline required for servants is needed to walk in the Spirit and engage in spiritual warfare
