## Summary
Hugh Taylor examines the three-word catchphrase "remember my bonds" from Paul's personal handwritten salutation in Colossians 4:18. He explores three meanings: as a request for prayer, as recognition of the price Paul paid for his ministry to Gentiles, and as a rallying cry to participate in the cause of Christ. Taylor emphasizes that Paul suffered extensively for the gospel and encourages believers to pray for persecuted Christians, recognize the sacrifice made for their spiritual liberty, and remain faithful in their Christian walk.

## Scripture
Colossians 4:18 — "The salutation by the hand of me Paul, remember my bonds. Grace be with you. Amen." Paul's personal signature includes this unique three-word phrase found nowhere else in his epistles.

## Key Themes
- **Biblical Catchphrases**: Scripture contains memorable statements that stick with us and provide doctrine, principles, and practices for Christian living
- **Paul's Suffering**: Paul endured tremendous persecution including beatings, imprisonments, shipwrecks, and constant danger for his ministry to Gentiles
- **Prayer for the Persecuted**: Christians must remember and pray for believers suffering persecution in places like Nigeria, China, and Haiti
- **The Price of Ministry**: Paul paid an enormous personal price so Gentiles could receive the gospel and understand right division
- **Perseverance in Ministry**: The phrase serves as encouragement to continue faithful service despite difficulties

## Notes

### Introduction: Understanding Biblical Catchphrases
- A catchphrase is an attention-getting word or phrase used to publicize something
- Examples: "I'll be back" (Terminator), "What's in your wallet?" (Capital One)
- Biblical catchphrases include "handfuls of purpose" (Ruth 2:16), "a man after God's own heart" (1 Samuel 13:14, Acts 13:22), and "the Lord opened" hearts (Acts 16:14)
- When studying biblical catchphrases we: define meanings, derive principles for what we believe and value, develop practices for what we believe and how we behave

### Paul's Use of Personal Salutations
- Paul typically used a scribe (Onesimus, Titus, Timothy) to write his letters due to eyesight issues
- He personally signed each letter to validate its authenticity
- "Remember my bonds" appears only once in all of Paul's epistles, making it unique and significant
- Located in Paul's personal handwritten salutation at the end of Colossians

### Meaning #1: Remember My Bonds as a Request for Prayer
- At face value, Paul reminds readers he is in prison
- Beyond literal meaning, serves as request for prayer support
- Paul believed in prayer and practiced it consistently
  - Romans 1:9: "Without ceasing I make mention of you always in my prayers"
  - Ephesians 1:16: "Ceasing not to give thanks for you, making mention of you in my prayers"
  - Colossians 1:9: "We also do not cease to pray for you"
- Paul requested prayer from others
  - Colossians 4:2-3: "Continue in prayer and watch in the same with thanksgiving; Withal praying also for us"
  - 1 Thessalonians 5:25: "Brethren, pray for us"
  - 2 Thessalonians 3:1: "Finally, brethren, pray for us, that the word of the Lord may have free course, and be glorified"
- God wants us to care about each other through prayer
- Advice: "If someone bothers you, pray for them" - hard to maintain bitterness toward someone you pray for
- Application today: pray for persecuted believers in Nigeria, China, and places with Islamism or Communism
- Socialism leads to Communism just as peaceful Islam leads to Sharia law - both bring domination and persecution
- Specific need: pray for Brother Biz and his family in Haiti/Dominican Republic facing economism and corruption
- Prayer request for Brother Bill and family (Craig and Luella) - that God would open his heart to attend to hospice counsel

### Meaning #2: Remember My Bonds as Recognition of the Price
- Paul paid an enormous price for his ministry to Gentiles
- Paul's resume of suffering (2 Corinthians 11:23-28):
  - "In labours more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequent, in deaths oft"
  - Five times received 39 lashes from the Jews
  - Three times beaten with rods
  - Once stoned
  - Three times suffered shipwreck
  - A night and day in the deep
  - Perils of waters, robbers, own countrymen, heathen, city, wilderness, sea, false brethren
  - "In weariness and painfulness, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness"
- Ephesians 3:1: "For this cause I Paul, the prisoner of Jesus Christ for you Gentiles"
- No one suffered for humanity as much as Christ, but no man or woman this side of Christ suffered as much as Paul
- Paul's crime: simply preaching the gospel of the grace of God
- Colossians 4:3: "Withal praying also for us, that God would open unto us a door of utterance, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in bonds"
- Paul fought good fight, finished course, kept faith for our benefit as Gentiles
- Without Paul's willingness to suffer, Gentiles would have no hope - speaker has no Jewish heritage or connection to temple worship
- Paul's bonds were on our behalf and for our benefit

### Meaning #3: Remember My Bonds as a Rallying Cry to Participate
- A rallying cry makes people join together to support an idea or cause
- Historical example: "Remember the Alamo"
  - March 6, 1836: 1,500 Mexican troops under Santa Anna defeated 200 Texans at the Alamo
  - Santa Anna showed no mercy, killed even survivors who surrendered
  - April 21, 1836: Texas army under Sam Houston defeated Santa Anna at San Jacinto in 18 minutes
  - "Remember the Alamo" became rallying cry that fired up Texan soldiers
- For believers: rallying cry to identify and involve ourselves with cause of Christ
- Follow Paul as he followed Christ
- Hebrews 13:3: "Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body"
- Example of Reese Howells: known for intercessory prayer, empathized (not just sympathized) with those he prayed for
- Consider blessings we take for granted:
  - Can drive to church without walking
  - No roadblocks to pass
  - No guards at church doors
  - No fear of pastor being dragged out
  - Freedom not enjoyed by believers in China, Nigeria, Haiti, Dominican Republic
- Not intended as guilt trip - congregation present on third consecutive Wednesday without senior pastor, drove through miserable weather
- For faithful attenders: "remember my bonds" as rallying cry for affirmation
- Encouragement to carry on, keep pressing toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus
- "Don't faint. Keep fighting. Keep pressing."

### Application Summary
- Remember my bonds as request for prayer for those in need
- Remember my bonds as recognition of price paid for our liberty in Christ and right division
- Remember my bonds as rallying cry to participate in God's cause: reach lost world, minister to saints, encourage one another, make full proof of ministries, live godly lives, bring glory to Christ
