## Summary
Pastor Simpson explores Ephesians 5:5 and the warning that those engaged in sexual sin have no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God. He clarifies that this passage addresses believers, not just the lost, and explains that while salvation cannot be lost, a believer's inheritance and eternal rewards can be forfeited through persistent sin. The sermon emphasizes that our inheritance is secured by grace but grows through faithful works done as God works in and through us.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:5 — Paul warns the church at Ephesus that those who practice fornication, uncleanness, and covetousness will not inherit the kingdom of Christ and God.

## Key Themes
- Inheritance in the kingdom of Christ versus salvation
- The distinction between losing inheritance and losing salvation
- Sexual sin and its consequences for believers
- How God works through believers by grace to build eternal rewards
- The judgment seat of Christ and loss of rewards
- Working out salvation with fear and trembling

## Notes

### The Core Question
Pastor Simpson addresses a critical theological question: When Paul writes to believers about having "no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and God," is he speaking to lost people or to the church? The context shows he's addressing believers—those already saved—about how their choices affect their eternal inheritance.

### Inheritance: Guaranteed and Forfeitable
There are two aspects to a believer's inheritance:
- **Guaranteed portion**: Secured by salvation through grace; cannot be lost
- **Forfeitable portion**: Based on our works and obedience; can be lost or suffer loss

This aligns with 1 Corinthians 3, which describes believers whose works are tested by fire and who "suffer loss" while still being saved.

### The Wrath of God on Believers
Pastor Simpson examines Colossians 3:5-6, which speaks of God's wrath coming on "children of disobedience." He notes that this wrath is not the future wrath of tribulation (from which believers are exempt) but rather God's judgment experienced in this life—through the natural consequences of sin, discipline, and the loss of eternal rewards.

### Three Clarifying Passages
1. **2 Corinthians 12:20-21**: Paul expresses concern that he will find believers at Corinth continuing in sexual sins and not repenting. He warns of shame and humiliation at his arrival.
2. **Colossians 3:24-25**: "Ye shall receive the reward of the inheritance: for ye serve the Lord Christ." Those who do wrong will receive loss.
3. **Galatians 5:19-21**: Lists works of the flesh including sexual sins and declares, "they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God." This is not loss of salvation but loss of inheritance.

### How Inheritance Grows: Grace Working Through Us
Pastor Simpson distinguishes between the kingdom age (where God gave supernatural gifts) and our current age of grace:
- Under law/kingdom: God empowered people directly to perform miracles
- Under grace: God works *in and through* us as we yield ourselves, study, grow in obedience, and cooperate with His Spirit

Key passages:
- **Galatians 2:20**: "Yet not I, but Christ liveth in me"
- **Philippians 2:12-13**: "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you, both to will and to do, of his good pleasure"

Our inheritance grows when we:
- Allow God to work in and through us
- Do our works for the right reasons (to glorify God, not ourselves)
- Don't frustrate grace by pursuing works of the flesh
- Cooperate with God's Spirit as members of His body

### The Cost of Sexual Sin
Chosen sexual sin costs believers:
- Eternal inheritance in heaven
- The privilege and intimacy of God working through them to minister to His body
- Mental and spiritual purity
- Rewards at the judgment seat of Christ

### The Sober Call
Pastor Simpson closes with the gravity of this passage: believers must work hard to maintain obedience and purity, not merely to escape punishment, but because the value of inheritance with Christ is immeasurable. Our obedience positions us to receive what God longs to give us.
