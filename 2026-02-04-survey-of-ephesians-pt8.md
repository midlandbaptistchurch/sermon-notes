## Summary
Pastor Payne continues the survey of Ephesians by examining chapter 3, describing it as "the most important forgotten or ignored chapter in the Bible." He explains God's eternal purpose hidden from the foundation of the world—the mystery of the church as the body of Christ—and how believers display God's manifold wisdom to principalities and powers in heavenly places. The sermon emphasizes the unsearchable riches of grace, mercy, and glory available to believers through their access to God in Christ.

## Scripture
Ephesians 3:1-12 — Paul explains the dispensation of grace given to him and the mystery of the church, hidden from the ages but now revealed to the Gentiles.

## Key Themes
- The mystery of the church hidden from the foundation of the world
- God's eternal purpose and the dispensation of grace
- The unsearchable riches of Christ: grace, mercy, and glory
- The body of Christ displaying God's wisdom to principalities and powers
- Believer's boldness and confidence of access to God through faith in Christ
- God's dealings with nations, Israel, and the Gentiles across different dispensations

## Notes

### The Importance of Ephesians 3
- Pastor Payne presents a hypothetical: if God met you face-to-face and explained His work across 6,000 years, where would you go in Scripture to understand what He is doing today? The answer is Ephesians 3 and the book of Ephesians.
- This chapter reveals what God is currently doing versus what He did in past ages.

### The Dispensation of Grace (Ephesians 3:1-8)
- Paul identifies himself as "prisoner of Jesus Christ for you Gentiles" and speaks of the "dispensation of the grace of God."
- The mystery was hidden in ages past but is now revealed through Paul's apostolic ministry.
- Paul emphasizes he is "less than the least of all saints," yet grace was given to him to preach among the Gentiles.
- The gospel is the means by which Gentiles become "fellow heirs" and "partakers of his promise in Christ."

### Historical Context: God and the Nations
- At the Tower of Babel, God divided the nations and appointed angelic principalities to rule over them (Deuteronomy 32:8-9).
- God disinherited the Gentile nations and focused His covenant plan on Abraham and his descendants.
- God placed Israel at the center of His kingdom program for the earth.
- Today, God is no longer dealing with nations or Israel as He did in the past; instead, He deals with individual souls through the gospel.

### The Three Unsearchable Riches of Christ
1. **The Riches of His Grace** (Ephesians 1:7) — Redemption through His blood and forgiveness of sins according to the riches of His grace.
2. **The Riches of His Mercy** (Ephesians 2:4) — God's mercy toward us, distinct from grace; we do not receive what we deserve.
3. **The Riches of His Glory** (Ephesians 3:16) — The manifestation of God's perfections and virtues; believers will shine His light in heavenly places.

### The Mystery: Hidden from the Foundation of the World (Ephesians 3:9-10)
- The mystery was hidden from the beginning of the world but is now revealed.
- This mystery concerns the fellowship of the body of Christ—the oneness of believers, both Jew and Gentile, in one spiritual body.
- All things (thrones, dominions, principalities, powers) were created by Christ and for Christ.
- The church displays the "manifold wisdom of God" to principalities and powers in heavenly places, showing a greater wisdom than Satan's.

### Eternal Purpose and Access (Ephesians 3:11-12)
- The mystery is part of God's eternal purpose, purposed in Christ Jesus our Lord.
- Believers have boldness and access to God with confidence through the faith of Christ (His redemptive work and obedience).
- In Old Testament times, access to God was restricted and required complex rituals; today, believers have direct, unrestricted access to God.

### Application: Appreciating Our Access
- Believers often take for granted the blessings and access they have in Christ.
- The church should be a safe place where believers come into the kingdom of God together.
- Gratitude for our spiritual position and privileges in Christ should motivate holy living and faithful stewardship of the gospel.
