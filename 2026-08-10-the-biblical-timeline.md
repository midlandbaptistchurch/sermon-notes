## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson leads a Bible study session focused on building and understanding a biblical timeline as a tool for rightly dividing scripture. He walks through key dispensations, major biblical figures, and doctrinal distinctions — particularly the unique nature of the Age of Grace as a parenthetical mystery hidden from prior ages. The session emphasizes that clarity in understanding what portion of scripture is addressed to whom brings confidence and sound doctrine.

## Scripture
Romans 16:25 — Used to establish the definition of a biblical "mystery": something kept secret since the world began but now revealed, specifically the gospel of grace through Paul's ministry.

## Key Themes
- Rightly dividing scripture using a biblical timeline
- The Age of Grace as a parenthetical mystery not revealed in prior dispensations
- The rise and fall of Israel and its role in understanding prophecy
- Distinguishing the rapture from the second coming of Christ
- Key spokesmen across biblical dispensations (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, Elijah, Paul)
- Doctrinal distinctions unique to the Age of Grace (eternal security, the body of Christ, the mystery)

## Notes
- Pastor Simpson references and recommends the book *One Book Rightly Divided* (expanded edition) as a foundational study tool for understanding dispensations and biblical timelines. He cautions against the newer "prophetic edition" by the same author.
- The timeline exercise places the cross near center, with Genesis at the far left and the end of the millennial kingdom at the far right — representing approximately 7,000 years of biblical history.
- The Age of Grace is drawn as a parenthetical section: Israel's program was interrupted at the stoning of Stephen (Acts 7), the mystery age of grace was introduced through Paul, and Israel's program will resume in the tribulation period.
- Key dispensational labels discussed: Innocence (Garden of Eden), Conscience, Law, Age of Grace (Mystery), Tribulation, Millennial Kingdom.
- Spokesmen placed on the timeline: Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, Elijah/Elisha, John the Baptist, Peter (early Acts), Paul (Age of Grace), Moses and Elijah again as the two witnesses in the tribulation.
- Doctrinal distinctions unique to the Age of Grace placed on the timeline include: eternal security, the body of Christ (with Christ as the head), the mystery, and the judgment seat of Christ (as distinct from the great white throne judgment).
- The rapture ends the Age of Grace; believers meet Christ in the clouds for the judgment seat of Christ, while the second coming is a separate event — Christ returning to earth after the tribulation.
- Clarence Larkin is mentioned as a historical example of detailed, artistic biblical timeline work.
- Next week's study will cover chapters 4 and 5 of *One Book Rightly Divided*: "Reconciling the Scriptures" — examining passages that appear to contradict each other if the Bible is not rightly divided.
- The session underscores that the timeline is not just a teaching tool for others but a personal study discipline: being able to explain events on the timeline for 10–20 minutes demonstrates genuine comprehension.
