## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the "Members in Particular" series by exploring how spiritual gifts and abilities function within the body of Christ. The sermon emphasizes that gifts are given not for personal status or specialization, but as responsibilities to serve the broader congregation. Taylor also reflects on Brother Kevin Bunn as an exemplary member of the body who embodied humility, encouragement, and faithful stewardship.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 12:27 — "Now ye are the body of Christ and members in particular." The key verse establishing the sermon's focus on individual members' roles within Christ's body.

Romans 12:3-8 — Discusses spiritual gifts (prophecy, giving, mercy, exhortation, etc.) and the imperative not to think more highly of oneself than warranted.

Ephesians 5:15-16 — "See then that you walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise... Redeeming the time, because the days are evil."

Galatians 3:28 — "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither bond nor free, there is neither male nor female; for ye are all one in Christ Jesus."

## Key Themes
- **Gifts as Responsibility, Not Status** — Abilities given by God create obligations to serve others, not permission to boast or specialize exclusively in one area
- **All Members Called to All Gifts** — While some may have particular abilities, every member should develop and exercise all the gifts listed (giving, teaching, exhortation, mercy, etc.)
- **Humility in Ministry** — Avoid pride about spiritual abilities; recognize that God grants them and that they carry responsibility rather than privilege
- **Identity in Christ** — Members retain their individuality and identity while losing worldly labels and limitations through union with Christ
- **Serious Engagement with Church Life** — A call to move beyond casual church attendance to active, committed participation in the body
- **Brother Kevin Bunn as Model** — Exemplified comfort in Christ, unpretentiousness, trust in God's work, and faithful use of abilities to encourage others

## Notes

### Three Caveats for Understanding Spiritual Gifts

1. **Humility Over Pride** — Spiritual abilities should humble, not exalt. If God gives an ability, much is required; it is a responsibility, not a status symbol.

2. **Develop All Gifts, Don't Specialize Alone** — While some may have particular emphases, believers should pursue opportunities to exercise all mentioned gifts (prophecy, giving, exhorting, teaching, showing mercy, etc.). Not everyone gifted in giving should avoid giving; all are called to give (2 Corinthians 9:7). This applies to teaching moments with children, acts of mercy, and exhortation in conversation.

3. **Exercise Gifts as Ministry Responsibility** — Gifts must be used to strengthen the body. Avoiding service because "I've already done my part" neglects the calling to continual ministry. Members should fill gaps left by others and give their best effort.

### Brother Kevin Bunn: An Example of Members in Particular

Taylor dedicates this series to Kevin Bunn's memory and identifies four qualities that made him exemplary:

1. **Comfortable in Christ** — Kevin embodied peace and comfort in being a member of Christ's body, drawing on "the God of all comfort," especially as he faced illness.

2. **Unpretentious** — He never lorded his superior painting skills over others or made anyone feel inferior. He invited others to "come on, let's all do what we can do."

3. **Attentive to God's Work** — Kevin listened carefully in class, compared teachings to his own life experience, and trusted God in what He was doing.

4. **Used Abilities to Encourage** — Though skilled as a painter, Kevin's primary ministry was exhortation—encouraging others, particularly Taylor, to be all they could be as members of the body of Christ.

### Losing Labels and Limitations in Christ

- Members do not lose identity or individuality in the body; rather, they lose worldly **labels** (Galatians 3:28 — neither Jew nor Greek, male nor female, slave nor free).
- More significantly, believers lose their **limitations** — while sin-cursed flesh is limited, in Christ there is unlimited potential through living as a "living sacrifice" (Romans 12:1).

### Call to Seriousness

Taylor challenges the congregation:
- The body of Christ must stop "playing church."
- Preparation for the judgment seat of Christ requires genuine commitment, not casual attendance.
- Poor turnout for communion (Lord's Supper) on Sunday evening reflects lack of seriousness about the church's fundamental ordinance and its members' readiness.
- Are we truly "members," or merely "attendees"?
