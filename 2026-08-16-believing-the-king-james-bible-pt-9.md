## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues his study on the King James Bible by examining the development and global spread of the English language. He demonstrates how English has become the world's dominant lingua franca, with approximately 1.53 billion speakers (19% of humanity), making it the most strategic language for spreading the Gospel. Taylor argues this widespread adoption of English is not coincidental but part of God's design to prepare the world to receive His Word through the King James Bible before the return of Christ.

## Key Themes
- **God's Providence in Language Development**: The evolution and global spread of English over 1500 years was by divine design to facilitate Gospel propagation

## Notes

### Historical Development of English
- English evolved through three major periods: Old English (450-1066 AD), Middle English (1066-1500s), and Modern English (1500s-present)
- Old English brought by Angles, Saxons, and Jutes from Northwest Europe around 450-500 AD
- Norman Conquest (1066) and Battle of Hastings introduced French influence during Middle English period
- Around 1500s, England rejected French influence and solidified their own language
- Language maturity coincided with England's colonial expansion and cultural projection

### English Language Statistics
- Approximately 1.53 billion total English speakers worldwide (19% of human population)
- Native speakers: 390 million (about 1/4 of total)
- Second language speakers: 1.14 billion (about 3/4 of total)
- Third most common native language after Mandarin and Spanish
- Number of English speakers has doubled in the last 25 years (since 2000)
- English vocabulary includes 273,000 headwords in Oxford English Dictionary and 470,000 entries in Webster's Third New International Dictionary
- 78% of English words have origins in other languages

### English as Official Language
- 58 sovereign nations have English as official language
- 28 non-sovereign entities (territories, provinces) have English as official language
- Many administrative divisions at local/regional level declare English official
- United States has no official language (de facto English)
- Official language of major organizations: UN (one of 6 official languages), European Union (main language among 24 official), NATO (one of 2 official)

### Global Influence and Usage
- 60% of international companies use English as official company language
- De facto language of diplomacy, science, international trade, tourism, aviation, entertainment, and internet
- Internet: 52% of website content in English, next largest is Russian at 8%
- 96% of students in continental Europe learn English
- English taught as compulsory subject in 186 countries
- Duolingo teaches English in 122 countries
- 1.5 billion people currently learning English worldwide

### English in Employment
- 98.5% of employers across 38 surveyed countries assess English competency of potential employees
- 50% of companies offer higher starting salaries for strong English skills
- AI translation tools have not reduced demand for English speakers
- Physical verbal skill of real-time English communication remains highly marketable

### Personal Anecdotes
- Taylor shares his experience using his vocabulary knowledge early in his relationship with his wife Regina
- Regina's basic French helped them navigate Paris restaurants as young married couple when stationed in Europe
- Taylor notes how much easier international travel has become for English speakers over his lifetime
- Friend in North Carolina has ministry of giving away King James Bibles in retirement

### Missionary Application
- English predominance means more people learning English than Greek and Hebrew
- Non-native language speakers more likely to access Bible in English than in original languages
- Missionaries can serve in English-speaking foreign countries if unable to learn other languages
- Native English speakers have strategic advantage and responsibility to share the Gospel
- The King James Bible in English is positioned to reach unprecedented numbers globally

### End Times Implications
- Taylor wonders what language the Antichrist will use
- Notes that technology described in Revelation is becoming increasingly credible and conceivable
- Observes that things once considered science fiction (like Dick Tracy's communication watch) are now reality
- Suggests global unification through English and technology indicates proximity to Christ's return
- Emphasizes urgency to use English advantage to save souls before the rapture and tribulation
