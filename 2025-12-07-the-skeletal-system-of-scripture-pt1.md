## Summary
Pastor Payne introduces a new teaching series examining the foundational structure of Scripture from creation to consummation. He addresses the scoffers mentioned in 2 Peter 3 who claim "all things continue as they were from the beginning of creation," demonstrating how this uniformitarian view misunderstands both creation and God's ongoing work. The sermon establishes that Genesis 1:1 describes an initial creation of "the heaven" (singular) and earth, while the six days detail God's forming and filling of an earth that had become "without form and void."

## Scripture
2 Peter 3:1-7 — Peter's warning about scoffers who deny Christ's coming based on uniformitarian assumptions about creation.

Genesis 1:1-2 — The initial creation of heaven and earth, followed by the earth's formless and void state.

## Key Themes
- The Bible is about the Creator, not the creature
- Uniformitarianism versus biblical understanding of creation
- The distinction between initial creation (Genesis 1:1) and the six days of forming/filling
- The progression from "the heaven" to "the heavens" (plural)
- God's throne in heaven and the firmament as the second heaven

## Notes
- The series aims to provide the "skeletal system" of Scripture - the foundational structure that gives the Bible its form and protects its vital truths
- Uniformitarianism claims processes have remained constant since creation, but Peter refutes this in 2 Peter 3:5-7
- Genesis 1:1 uses "the heaven" (singular) while later passages refer to "the heavens" (plural)
- The six days of creation (Genesis 1:3-31) describe God giving form to the formless earth and filling the void
- The firmament created on day two becomes the second heaven, containing sun, moon, and stars
- Angels and the spiritual realm existed before the six days of creation
- The earth is the focal point of God's creation, with celestial bodies designed to give light upon it
