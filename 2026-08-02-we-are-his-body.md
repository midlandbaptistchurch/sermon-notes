## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson concludes the study of Ephesians 5 by drawing attention to the often-overlooked emphasis Paul places on Christ and the church, alongside the more familiar husband-wife instructions. He focuses on Ephesians 5:30-33 to show that believers are not merely in Christ's hand but are members of his very body — his flesh and bones — and therefore bear the profound responsibility of representing him on earth. The lesson connects Ephesians 5 with Romans 12 and 1 Corinthians 12 to reinforce the call to humility, unity, and faithful service within the body of Christ.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:30-33 — Paul's closing words on the husband-wife relationship, which he explicitly frames as a mystery pointing to the greater reality of Christ's union with the church as head and body.

## Key Themes
- Christ as head of the church, not just a concept but an eternal reality
- Believers as members of Christ's body — his flesh and bones upon the earth
- The distinction between being "in his hand" and being "his hand"
- Oneness and unity within the body of Christ (Ephesians 4, Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12)
- Humility in the body — not thinking more highly of oneself than one ought (Romans 12:3)
- The husband-wife relationship as a picture of Christ and the church, not an end in itself
- Identity: the believer's primary identity is as the body of Christ and his ambassador on earth

## Notes
**Ephesians 5:22-33 — More Than a Marriage Passage**
- Pastor Simpson emphasized that popular readings of Ephesians 5:22-33 focus almost exclusively on the husband-wife relationship, while Paul repeatedly uses the word "as" to compare that relationship to Christ and the church.
- The words *body*, *church*, and *as* appear throughout the passage intentionally — pointing the reader upward to an eternal reality, not just earthly roles.
- Earthly relationships (husband/wife, parent/child, master/servant) are temporal; the relationship of Christ as head and the church as body is eternal.

**Ephesians 5:30 — Members of His Body**
- "For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones."
- Pastor Simpson noted the difference between the common illustration of being "in his hand" (John 10) and Ephesians 5:30 — *we are his hand*. We are not merely with him; we are part of him.
- Believers are Christ's ambassadors — his hands, feet, mouth, and mind upon the earth while he is seated at the right hand of the Father.
- This is a sobering identity: sin-cursed, yet called to be his body. The tension requires ongoing meditation and memorization of this truth.
- Our home is in heaven (spiritual blessings in heavenly places, Ephesians 1); for this brief time on earth, we are his bodily presence here.

**Ephesians 5:32 — A Great Mystery**
- "This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church."
- The oneness of husband and wife is a beautiful picture, but it is secondary to the greater mystery: Christ making believers his own body.
- This mystery was hidden and is now revealed — a characteristic of Paul's gospel (grace/mystery doctrine).

**Romans 12:1-3 — Consecration and Humility in the Body**
- Romans 12:1-2 (the living sacrifice) is well-known, but verse 3 is less memorized: *"not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think."*
- In context, this is not just general anti-pride advice — it is specifically about one's role in the body of Christ.
- Because Christ is the head and each believer is a member, no member should elevate their role or gift above another.
- Romans 12:6-8 lists seven gifts (prophecy, ministry, teaching, exhorting, giving, ruling, showing mercy) — whatever the body needs, that is what each member is called to supply.

**1 Corinthians 12:14-18 — Many Members, One Body**
- "The body is not one member but many."
- The foot, ear, eye — each is essential. Some members are less "comely" but no less necessary.
- Applied back to Ephesians 5: whether one holds a leadership role (head) or a serving role (body), no position warrants pride over another.

**Practical Application**
- Christ nourishes and cherishes the church (v. 29) — believers are called to receive his love and allow that relationship to be real, not just theoretical.
- In times of difficulty, believers are most likely to draw close to Christ and depend on him as head.
- The call to be fitly joined to Christ and to one another brings glory to God.
- Preview of Ephesians 6: children/parents (vv. 1-4), servants/masters (vv. 5-9), and the armor of God/spiritual warfare (vv. 10-18) — the spiritual warfare section only makes full sense when the foundation of headship and body is understood.
