## Summary
Youth Pastor Andrew Watt challenges the church to examine their spiritual growth, asking when they last grew spiritually. Drawing on the metaphor of physical growth requiring pain, he identifies three growing pains that lead to spiritual maturity: godly sorrow that produces repentance, submission to one another in fear of God, and sacrifice of what we hold most precious. Through examples ranging from personal conviction to Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac, the sermon emphasizes that genuine spiritual growth often comes through discomfort and dying to ourselves.

## Scripture
Ephesians 4:11-16 — Paul describes the gifts given to the church for equipping the saints and building up the body of Christ until we mature in Him, no longer being "tossed to and fro" by false doctrine but speaking truth in love.

## Key Themes
- **Spiritual Growth**: Believers should continually grow spiritually and become more like Christ
- **Growing Pains**: True spiritual development often requires discomfort, difficulty, and sacrifice
- **Godly Sorrow**: Genuine repentance comes through sorrow over sin, not merely fear of consequences
- **Submission**: Humbling ourselves and submitting to one another mirrors Christ's submission to the Father
- **Sacrifice**: Presenting our bodies as living sacrifices means surrendering what we hold most dear
- **Complacency**: It's easy to become comfortable in church routines without allowing the Holy Spirit to convict and transform us

## Notes

### Opening Challenge
- Pastor Andrew asks a penetrating question: When is the last time you grew spiritually?
- Many believers have been in church for years yet feel they no longer need to grow
- The church at Midland Baptist is planted in "rich soil"—a pure Bible, truthful preaching, and freedom—yet growth is often absent
- The problem is often religious routine rather than genuine spiritual transformation

### Growing Pain #1: Godly Sorrow
- **Scripture**: 2 Corinthians 7:8-10 — "Godly sorrow worketh repentance to salvation, not to be repented of: but the sorrow of the world worketh death"
- Being caught or facing consequences for sin is not the same as repentance
- Teenagers (and adults) often apologize when caught but continue in the same behavior
- True repentance requires godly sorrow—a heart conviction that what we did was wrong against God, not just regret over consequences
- The pain of conviction is a growing pain that leads to genuine change

### Growing Pain #2: Submission
- **Scripture**: Ephesians 5:21-27 and Philippians 2:5-11
- Submission to one another in the fear of God is difficult but essential
- Christ Himself submitted to the Father's will, even unto death
- Contrast: Satan sought to exalt himself (Isaiah 14:12-15) and was brought low; Christ humbled Himself and was highly exalted
- Submitting to others in the church—especially those we disagree with—is painful but produces growth and unity
- Pastor Andrew emphasizes that relationships with others flow from our relationship with God

### Growing Pain #3: Sacrifice
- **Scripture**: Philippians 4:10-20 and Romans 12:1-2
- The Philippian church sacrificed financially to support Paul's ministry, and God promised to supply their needs
- Sacrifice is not always monetary; it may be time, location, or even our children
- Romans 12:1-2 calls believers to present their bodies as "living sacrifices," which requires dying to fleshly desires
- God often asks for the one thing we're most reluctant to give
- Abraham's offering of Isaac (Genesis 22) is the ultimate picture: God called Abraham to sacrifice the promise itself

### Abraham and Isaac
- Abraham had repeatedly failed to trust God throughout his life (lying in Egypt, going to Hagar instead of trusting for Isaac)
- When God finally asked him to offer Isaac, Abraham obeyed without withholding
- God's response: "Now I know that thou fearest God, seeing thou hast not withheld thy son, thine only son from me" (Genesis 22:12)
- This willingness to sacrifice revealed Abraham's transformed heart and fear of God

### The Central Challenge
- It's easy to attend church, sing songs, hear preaching, and take notes without allowing the Holy Spirit to actually change us
- We get caught up in the routine and structure of services, in what we do after church, rather than being transformed by God's Word
- True growth requires allowing ourselves to experience the growing pains of conviction, humility, and sacrifice
- The question remains: What is God asking you to give up today?
