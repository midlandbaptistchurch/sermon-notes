## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson walks through several apparent contradictions in Scripture, demonstrating how proper dispensational interpretation — distinguishing between the Law, the Kingdom program, and the Age of Grace — resolves them. Topics covered include Old Testament justice versus kingdom ethics in Matthew 5, principles of peacemaking and self-defense under grace, giving versus enabling, the commission to Jews versus Gentiles, and tithing versus grace-based generosity.

## Scripture
Various passages including Exodus (eye for eye), Matthew 5:38–42, Romans 12:17–21, 1 Timothy 5:8, 1 Corinthians 6, 2 Thessalonians 3:10, Galatians 6:1–5, Matthew 10:5–6, Acts 13, 18, and 28, Genesis 14:18–20, Leviticus 27:30–32, and 2 Corinthians 8–9. These passages are examined in their respective dispensational contexts to explain how they relate to one another without contradiction.

## Key Themes
- Reading Scripture literally and in context to avoid misapplication
- Distinguishing Old Testament Law, the coming Kingdom, and the present Age of Grace
- Resolving apparent contradictions through dispensational rightly-dividing
- Grace-age principles on peacemaking, self-defense, and accountability
- The transition of Paul's ministry from Jews to Gentiles (Acts 13, 18, 28)
- Tithing under the Law versus freewill, cheerful giving under grace (2 Corinthians 8–9)
- Helping others versus enabling continued irresponsibility

## Notes
- The class is working through the book *One Book Rightly Divided* (available on Amazon in expanded digital edition).
- **Old Testament Law**: Lex talionis (eye for eye, tooth for tooth) was literal; cities of refuge distinguished manslaughter from murder; Sabbath violations carried the death penalty — these show how seriously Israel was held to the law.
- **Matthew 5 (Sermon on the Mount)**: Understood as Kingdom teaching — instructions for how life will operate when Christ returns and establishes His earthly reign. "Turn the other cheek" and "give to him that asketh" are Kingdom commands, not directives for the present age.
- **Romans 12:17–21**: The grace-age principle is to live peaceably with all men as much as possible and leave vengeance to God, but this is acknowledged as a principle requiring wisdom rather than an absolute rule covering every situation.
- **1 Timothy 5:8 and 1 Corinthians 6**: Believers have a responsibility to provide for their own household and to resolve disputes within the church rather than going to secular courts.
- **2 Thessalonians 3:10 vs. Matthew 5:42**: "Give to him that asketh" (Kingdom) versus "if any would not work, neither should he eat" (Grace age) — these are reconciled by recognizing their different dispensational contexts. Helping someone is distinct from enabling continued irresponsibility; Galatians 6:5 ("every man shall bear his own burden") reinforces accountability under grace.
- **Matthew 10:5–6**: Jesus explicitly told the twelve not to go to Gentiles — a commission tied to the Jewish Kingdom program. This transitions through Acts 13, 18, and 28 as Paul progressively turns to the Gentiles.
- **Tithing**: Abraham's tithe to Melchizedek (Genesis 14) predates the Law; Leviticus establishes the tithe to support the Levites who had no land. Under grace, Paul never commands a tithe; 2 Corinthians 8–9 describes generous, cheerful, non-compelled giving — and the specific context there was relief for impoverished saints in Jerusalem during a transitional period.
- Pastor Simpson noted Midland Baptist Church does not command tithing but encourages grace-based generosity, including supporting pastoral staff and missionaries.
