## Summary
Pastor Simpson teaches on Ephesians 5:1, examining what it means to be "followers of God as dear children." He explains how following Christ in Paul's epistles differs from the literal, physical following in the Gospels, and emphasizes the importance of maintaining childlike faith, humility, and trust in God while growing in Christian maturity.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:1 — "Be ye therefore followers of God as dear children."

## Key Themes
- The importance of the word "therefore" in understanding context and biblical connections
- Distinction between following Christ in the Gospels versus in Paul's epistles
- Following God as principles and attributes rather than physical location
- Childlike faith combined with adult understanding
- Maintaining innocence and trust despite life's difficulties
- The danger of losing childlike faith as believers mature

## Notes

### Context from Ephesians 4
- The word "therefore" signals that the command is based on what came before
- Chapter 4 deals with moving from bad to good to spiritual and God-glorifying actions
- Example: not just moving from stealing to working, but to becoming a giver for the body of Christ
- Chapter 4 emphasizes forgiveness and dealing with specific sins: corrupt communication, stealing, lying

### Following in the Gospels vs. Paul's Epistles
- In Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, disciples followed Christ physically and literally
- They had to give up everything, leave families, and be physically present with Him
- That following was temporary, preparing them for the tribulation period
- In Hebrews through Revelation, following changes because Christ is ascended
- Instead of physical following, believers follow attributes: peace, holiness, righteousness, charity

### Following God in Paul's Writings
- Romans 14:19 — Follow after things that make for peace and edify others
- 1 Corinthians 14:1 — Follow after charity and spiritual gifts
- 1 Thessalonians 5:15 — Follow that which is good
- 1 Timothy 6:11 — Follow righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness
- 2 Timothy 2:22 — Follow righteousness, faith, charity, peace
- Following involves principles and attributes that control and shape our thinking
- We follow these attributes through faith and the renewing of our minds

### "As Dear Children" — Matthew 18 Context
- Jesus taught the disciples to "become as little children" (Matthew 18:3-4)
- This followed Peter's failure to accept Jesus's prediction of His death
- Peter rationalized instead of trusting: "Not so, Lord"
- Jesus called him "Satan" because he was thinking of man's ways, not God's
- The lesson: believers must humble themselves and trust God even when they don't understand
- Childlike faith means believing without needing to rationalize everything
- Mary's anointing of Jesus (for His burial) showed childlike trust; Peter's skepticism showed adult rationalization

### Practical Application for Believers Today
- Young believers often have strong faith and don't question God's direction
- As they mature and gain freedom and responsibility, they can lose that childlike trust
- Adults tend to condition their obedience: "If I have time," "If I can afford it," "If it fits my schedule"
- Children make simple decisions based on faith; they don't overthink priorities
- The goal is not to lose the essence of childlike innocence while gaining understanding
- Knowledge without maintained childlike faith leads to deprioritizing God in decision-making

### The Challenge of Maintaining Childlike Faith
- As believers gain knowledge and wisdom, it's easy to begin rationalizing away obedience
- Life's difficulties and unanswered questions can erode faith
- The renewing of the mind with God's Word must give God greater control in our thinking than worldly concerns
- Simply putting "no" to God through our actions and priorities, even if we don't say it with our mouths
- The goal: to stand before God one day and hear, "Well done... you followed me as a dear child"
