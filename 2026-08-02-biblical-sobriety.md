## Summary
Assoc Pastor Mark Simpson conducts an in-depth word study on the biblical meaning of "sober" and its related forms, demonstrating that Scripture never uses the term merely in reference to alcohol, but instead calls believers to a life marked by seriousness, rationality, groundedness, and doctrinal clarity. Drawing on passages from Titus, Romans, 1 Thessalonians, and Acts, he shows that biblical sobriety encompasses temperance, gravity, sound faith, and the ability to think and act with purpose regardless of circumstances. The sermon closes with a personal challenge for each listener to examine their own "biblical sobriety" — asking what it truly takes to move them off the truth of God's Word.

## Scripture
- **Titus 2:2** — Paul's instructions that aged men be sober, grave, temperate, and sound in faith, charity, and patience.
- **Titus 2:12** — The call to deny ungodliness and worldly lusts and to live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present world.
- **Romans 12:3** — The command not to think of oneself more highly than one ought, but to think soberly according to the measure of faith God has given.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:6-8** — The exhortation to watch and be sober, contrasted with those who sleep and are drunk in spiritual darkness.
- **Acts 26:25** — Paul's declaration before Festus and King Agrippa: "I speak forth the words of truth and soberness."
- **Philippians 4:6-7** — The peace of God that guards hearts and minds, connected to the practice of sober, prayerful living.

## Key Themes
- The biblical definition of sobriety goes far beyond abstaining from intoxication
- To be sober is to be serious, rational, transparent, and rooted in truth
- Temperance (the last fruit of the Spirit in Galatians 5) brings all the other fruits together in balance
- Sobriety involves knowing what you believe and why — not merely riding on others' faith
- Paul in Acts 26 is presented as sobriety in action — steadfast under false accusation and political pressure
- Sanctification after salvation is expressed through sober, righteous, and godly living (Titus 2:12)
- Biblical sobriety includes doctrinal clarity, especially regarding end-times events such as the rapture

## Notes
**Introduction — Why Word Studies Matter**
- Words in Scripture often carry a cultural or modern definition that differs significantly from their biblical meaning
- Example given: the word "perfect" in Paul's writing means complete/mature, not flawless
- The word "sober" similarly carries a modern connotation (not intoxicated) that obscures its deeper biblical meaning

**Biblical Definition of Sober**
- In every New Testament usage, "sober" never refers to alcohol; it is always about a state of mind and character
- Webster's 1828: to be sober is to be regular, calm, not under the influence of passion — i.e., the absence of drama
- Key words associated with biblical sobriety:
  - **Serious / Grave** — taking the things of God with appropriate weight
  - **Temperate** — bringing all the fruits of the Spirit together (cf. Galatians 5); more than just self-control
  - **Rational** — things that are sober make sense; a sober person operates with logic and biblical common sense
  - **Real / Transparent** — a sober person lives in reality, not in what they wish were true
  - **Rooted** — steadfast and unmovable

**Four Steps for Conducting a Word Study**
1. Read the word in its immediate context
2. Search for the word in other passages using a concordance
3. Observe words that are coupled with or listed alongside the word being studied
4. Look for contrasts — how the word is set against its opposite in Scripture

**Titus 2:2 — The Sober Aged Man**
- Aged men are commanded to be sober, grave, temperate, and sound in faith, charity, and patience
- Gravity means a seriousness befitting the weight of eternal things; it is not coldness but appropriate seriousness
- Sound faith means knowing what you believe and why; sound charity means love directed rightly; sound patience means not being controlled by emotion or ego
- The aged have a responsibility to model this package for those younger

**Titus 2:12 — Living Soberly After Salvation**
- Soberness is contrasted with ungodliness and worldly lusts
- This verse describes post-salvation sanctification — the ongoing work of living soberly, righteously, and godly

**Romans 12:3 — Thinking Soberly**
- Sobriety is tied to understanding one's place in the body of Christ
- Not thinking of oneself more highly than one ought is corrected by thinking soberly — knowing doctrine, knowing one's role
- Faith must be personally owned; you cannot ride on the faith of Christian parents or a godly church indefinitely

**1 Thessalonians 5:6-8 — Sober Doctrinal Clarity**
- Written in the context of Paul revealing the rapture and distinguishing it from the second coming
- Spiritual sleep = ignorance of doctrine and truth; drunkenness = being out of control and without discernment
- A doctrinally sober believer is not frightened by end-times events because they understand what Scripture actually teaches
- The rapture has no preceding prophetic signs; the last days have been ongoing for 2,000 years

**Acts 26:25 — Sobriety in Action**
- Paul, arrested and falsely accused, stands before Felix, Festus, and King Agrippa
- Festus accuses Paul of madness; Paul responds calmly: "I am not mad, most noble Festus, but speak forth the words of truth and soberness"
- Paul's sobriety allowed him to deal with Festus briefly and continue ministering to Agrippa
- Agrippa's response — "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian" — shows he could not deny Paul's argument, yet his pride prevented him from acting; he stopped short of his own sober moment
- Sobriety produces discernment and enables effective ministry even in extreme circumstances

**Philippians 4:6-7 — Sobriety and the Peace of God**
- The peace of God guarding hearts and minds is inseparable from the practice of sober, prayerful living
- Challenge questions posed to the congregation:
  - How easy is it to move you off the truth?
  - How grave are you about the things of God?
  - Is Sunday morning a box to check, or does all of life revolve around Scripture?
  - What has greater influence on you — truth or circumstances?

**Closing Challenge**
- A person comes to salvation through a moment of sobriety — realizing they are a sinner heading for eternity without Christ
- The speaker encouraged each listener to devise their own "biblical sobriety test": what does it take to discourage you, distract you, or move you from God's truth?
- Sermon noted as the first part of a continuing series on biblical sobriety
