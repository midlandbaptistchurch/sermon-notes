## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a series called "Guidelines of Grace," focusing on how believers must assess their own value in order to live faithfully under grace. Rather than relying on fear of judgment as a motivator (as under the law), Christians under grace must develop an internal sense of their significance, relevance, and importance to God and His work. Simpson uses everyday illustrations — marriage, physical fitness, church ministry — to help listeners see that what they do for the Lord genuinely matters, even when results are not immediately visible.

## Scripture
Ephesians 4:16 — Used to illustrate that every member of the body of Christ is necessary and fitly joined together, each part contributing to the whole.
Galatians 6:1 — Referenced in the context of "considering thyself" — maintaining personal spiritual health and meekness while ministering to others.

## Key Themes
- Grace motivation versus law motivation
- Assessing personal value and self-worth as a believer
- Significance: having meaning in God's work
- Relevance: being connected to and bearing weight in God's purposes
- Importance: recognizing that individual faithfulness matters to God personally
- The judgment seat of Christ as a future accountability for post-salvation living
- Faithfulness in personal Bible study, prayer, and church involvement

## Notes
**Grace Motivation vs. Law Motivation**
- Under the law, fear of judgment and consequence was a primary motivator for obedience.
- Under grace, there is far less immediate consequence for sin — making internal, grace-based motivation essential.
- The key motivator under grace is understanding and assessing value: to God, to His work, and to yourself.

**Three Critical Concepts for Living Under Grace**

1. **Significance** — Having meaning.
   - Every believer is significant to the body of Christ (Eph. 4:16).
   - Significance is not limited to those in visible ministry roles; faithful personal Bible study, prayer, and holy living are deeply meaningful to God.
   - Just as a person is moved when someone notices something about them that no one else sees, God is honored when believers recognize and affirm things about Him that others overlook — such as acknowledging Him as Creator.
   - When you choose not to sin in a moment where you easily could, that choice is significant to God.

2. **Relevance** — Having bearing upon or connection to the matter at hand.
   - Believers are to feel genuinely connected to the work of the Lord — not just when serving in a visible ministry, but in their personal walk.
   - A sense of ownership and responsibility for the church and its ministries flows naturally from understanding that we belong to God and thus His work is ours.
   - If believers do not see their relevance, they default to assuming someone else will do what needs to be done — and sometimes it simply does not get done.

3. **Importance** — Recognizing personal value to God.
   - God's conversation with Satan about Job illustrates that God notices and takes pride in individual believers who remain faithful.
   - Believers are left on earth in sin-cursed bodies precisely so that their choice to obey has meaning — a sinless being choosing God costs nothing; a fallen person choosing God costs something.
   - Just as Simpson's faithfulness in physical health matters to his wife because it signals that her security is important to him, faithfulness in spiritual disciplines signals to God that He is important to us.
   - Suffering handled rightly is also significant to the Lord.

**Practical Application**
- Assess value to your time in the Word, your prayer life, your attendance, your giving, and your service — not as obligations but as opportunities.
- When going through the motions in Bible reading, stop, go back, and re-engage intentionally.
- Ask yourself: "What else am I going to do that will count as much as this?"
- Recognize that fewer people think this way than we might assume — which makes individual faithfulness even more meaningful.
- Humility and a high view of one's value to God are not contradictory; both are necessary for grace-motivated living.
