## Summary
On the occasion of the Class of 2026 graduation celebration, Youth Pastor Andrew Watt delivered ten practical life lessons drawn from his own ten years since graduating high school. Drawing on personal testimony and a wide range of scripture, he encouraged graduates to anchor their lives in God's purpose and faithfulness, to pursue biblical rather than worldly success, and to face the challenges ahead with humility, generosity, and perseverance.

## Scripture
Multiple passages across the evening, including Ephesians 3:8–11 (God's eternal purpose), Philippians 3:4–8 (Paul forsaking worldly success), Proverbs 27:1 (do not boast about tomorrow), Romans 5:8 and John 15:13 (love and sacrifice), Acts 20:35 and 2 Corinthians 9:7 (generosity), 1 Timothy 3:6 and 2 Timothy 3:7 (humility and the danger of pride), Proverbs 23:23 (buy the truth and do not sell it), Romans 8:6 (spiritual vs. carnal mind), Psalm 37:23 and Proverbs 24:16 (falling and rising again), and 1 Corinthians 10:13 (God's faithfulness in temptation).

## Key Themes
- God has a sovereign purpose for every life
- Biblical success over worldly success
- Living in the present rather than wishing away time
- Love involves sacrifice and the willingness to be hurt
- Generosity as a spiritual habit and blessing
- Humility and the danger of a know-it-all attitude
- The enduring value of truth
- Calling sin what it is rather than reframing it as preference
- Failure is choosing not to get up, not the act of falling
- God's faithfulness through every season of life

## Notes
**Context:** Sunday evening graduation service for the Midland Baptist Church Class of 2026 (high school and college graduates).

**Lesson 1 — God Has a Purpose for Your Life** *(Ephesians 3:8–11)*
Graduates face a flood of major decisions in a short span of time. Without confidence that God has a purpose for them, they can spiral quickly. Andrew shared that within a month of his own high school graduation in 2016 he struggled deeply with purpose — and also shared the sobering contrast of a school friend who did not survive that same crisis. Ten years on, he pointed to marriage, children, college graduation, and youth ministry as evidence that God's purposes unfold in ways we cannot anticipate.

**Lesson 2 — Learn the Difference Between Worldly and Biblical Success** *(Philippians 3:4–8)*
Worldly success — status, reputation, influence, power — always leads to comparison. The Apostle Paul had all four, yet counted them loss for Christ. Biblical success, a life sold out for Jesus Christ, produces contentment instead of comparison.

**Lesson 3 — Stop Counting Tomorrows** *(Proverbs 27:1)*
We habitually wish away the present by counting down to the next milestone. Time is the only currency you spend and cannot earn back. Whatever season you are in — a small apartment, a difficult boss, a hard semester — thank God for today, because you are not guaranteed tomorrow.

**Lesson 4 — Love and Sacrifice** *(Romans 5:8; John 15:13; Ephesians 5)*
Loving people will cost you pain. Friends you invest in will hurt you; people you expect to walk alongside you for years may not. Yet the example of Christ — loving a lost and dying world at great personal cost — calls us to approach even difficult relationships with a soft heart rather than walls, because the world needs to receive the message of the cross.

**Lesson 5 — It Is More Blessed to Give Than to Receive** *(Acts 20:35; 2 Corinthians 9:7)*
Andrew shared that when he proposed to his wife at age 19 he was working at McDonald's for ten dollars an hour with no savings. The generosity shown to him by others over the years taught him more than the gifts themselves ever could. Make generosity a habit — there are people with needs who will never raise their hand to ask.

**Lesson 6 — Avoid Being a Know-It-All** *(1 Timothy 3:6; 2 Timothy 3:7)*
The devil wants graduates to exit school armed with credentials and book knowledge and approach life as though they already have all the answers. Andrew's personal rule: "I know enough to know that I don't know." Find people who know more than you, yoke up with them, and stay teachable — before God, before the church, and in every area of life.

**Lesson 7 — The Truth Stands the Test of Time** *(Proverbs 23:23)*
Using the analogy of an investment you are instructed never to sell, Andrew argued that truth appreciates in value over time while everything you trade it away for depreciates. In a world of deceit and confusion, those who have bought the truth and held it are able to say "let me show you." He also warned of the need to develop the ability to spot a lie — and then to actually stop believing it.

**Lesson 8 — Call Sin What It Is** *(Romans 8:6)*
Much of what the world now frames as "preference" is sin. Andrew drew the distinction between the spiritual mind and the carnal mind (Romans 8:6), arguing there is no neutral gray area. Honest, candid conversation about sin is only possible when we stop relabeling it. Having a different preference than God and choosing it to fulfill the lust of that preference is sin — call it that.

**Lesson 9 — Failure Is Choosing Not to Get Up** *(Psalm 37:23; Proverbs 24:16)*
A just man falls seven times and rises again. Falling is inevitable; staying down is the failure. Andrew encouraged graduates not to dwell in, cover up, or settle into their mistakes, but to get up, learn what caused the fall, and keep running the race. God's hand will hold you — you will not be utterly cast down.

**Lesson 10 — God Is Faithful** *(1 Corinthians 10:13)*
The bookend lesson. Through every season — high school, college, career, marriage, parenting — things will change, but God does not. Andrew closed with a detailed personal story about pursuing a workplace promotion over the course of several months, watching every door close one after another, yet choosing to text his wife "God is faithful" each time. The final outcome — a special director-level approval granting the promotion with a 15% raise — came only because every previous door had closed. God's plan required every "no" to arrive at his "yes."
