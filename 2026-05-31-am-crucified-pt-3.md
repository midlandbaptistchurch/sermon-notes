## Summary
Bill Baughman leads the third installment of the AM Crucified series, walking through a detailed chronological overview of the events surrounding the crucifixion of Jesus. Beginning with Jesus's journey from Jericho through Bethany and the triumphal entry into Jerusalem, the sermon moves through the Last Supper, Gethsemane, the trials, the scourging, and finally the crucifixion itself. Along the way, Baughman addresses the Good Friday timeline controversy, highlights fulfillments of Old Testament prophecy, and draws out spiritual and practical lessons from each event.

## Scripture
Matthew 27; Luke 23; John 18–19 — The sermon draws from all four Gospels to construct a harmonized chronological account of the final days and death of Jesus Christ, from His arrival in Jericho to His last words on the cross.

## Key Themes
- The chronological events of the crucifixion week
- Fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy in the passion narrative
- The Good Friday timeline controversy resolved by Scripture
- The humility and servanthood of Jesus
- Spiritual transformation through encountering the crucified Christ
- Divine sovereignty and spiritual intervention throughout the passion events

## Notes
**The Good Friday Controversy**
- Baughman opens by addressing the traditional Good Friday claim, arguing from Matthew 12 (the sign of Jonah — three days and three nights) that a Friday crucifixion is mathematically impossible given that the resurrection occurred on the first day of the week.
- He urges the congregation to let Scripture settle the matter definitively.

**The Journey from Jericho to Jerusalem**
- Jesus travels from Jericho (846 feet below sea level, the lowest city in the world) up to the Mount of Olives (2,710 feet above sea level) — a climb of approximately 3,500 feet over 15 miles.
- Baughman uses the comparison of Boyne Mountain (500 feet) to help the congregation appreciate the physical effort of this journey.
- Key stops: healing of two blind men near Jericho; encounter with Zacchaeus, the chief tax collector, who was saved and pledged restitution.

**Bethany and the Anointing**
- Six days before Passover, Jesus arrives in Bethany where Mary anoints His feet with costly spikenard.
- Judas objects, calling it wasteful; Jesus defends Mary, saying it was done in preparation for His burial.

**The Triumphal Entry**
- Jesus sends two disciples to fetch a donkey and unbroken colt, fulfilling Zechariah's prophecy: "Tell ye the daughter of Zion, behold thy king cometh unto thee, meek and sitting upon an ass."
- Baughman highlights the miracle of a never-ridden colt remaining calm as evidence of divine intervention.
- The crowds spread garments and palm branches, crying "Hosanna to the Son of David."
- Jesus weeps over Jerusalem, foreseeing its destruction by Titus in AD 70.

**The Fig Tree and Cleansing of the Temple**
- Jesus curses the barren fig tree — a symbol of Israel's religious hypocrisy: leaves (outward religion) but no fruit (no true righteousness or acceptance of the Messiah).
- Jesus drives out the money changers, declaring, "My house shall be called the house of prayer, but ye have made it a den of thieves" (fulfilling Isaiah 56 and Jeremiah).
- Satan enters Judas Iscariot, who then conspires with the chief priests to betray Jesus.

**The Last Supper**
- Held in the upper room, Jesus institutes the Lord's Supper — bread and cup as the New Testament in His blood.
- He washes the disciples' feet, modeling servant leadership consistent with Philippians 2: taking "the form of a servant."

**Gethsemane: Prayer, Betrayal, and Arrest**
- Jesus takes Peter, James, and John to pray; His sweat becomes as great drops of blood.
- He prays, "Nevertheless, not as I will, but as thou wilt."
- Judas betrays Him with a kiss; when Jesus declares "I am he," those who came to arrest Him fall backward to the ground.
- Peter cuts off the ear of Malchus; Jesus rebukes him and surrenders willingly.

**The Trials**
- Jesus is led to Caiaphas and the Sanhedrin; false witnesses testify; He is charged with blasphemy and condemned to death.
- Before Pilate, Jesus is largely silent; Pilate finds no fault but yields to the crowd's demand.
- Barabbas is released; Jesus is handed over to be crucified.
- Pilate washes his hands, declaring himself innocent of Jesus's blood.

**The Scourging and Mocking**
- Jesus is scourged with instruments embedded with bone and metal, leaving His back severely lacerated.
- A scarlet robe and crown of thorns are placed on Him; soldiers mock and strike Him.

**The Crucifixion**
- Simon of Cyrene is compelled to carry the cross to Golgotha ("the place of the skull").
- Key sayings from the cross: "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do"; care of His mother entrusted to the beloved disciple; promise to the repentant thief — "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise"; "Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani" (My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?); "Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit."
- Darkness covers the land from the sixth to the ninth hour.
- Jesus gives up the ghost after declaring all things accomplished.
