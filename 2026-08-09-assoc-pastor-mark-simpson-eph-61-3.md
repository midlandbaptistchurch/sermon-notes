## Summary
This sermon examines Ephesians 6:1-3 not primarily as parenting instruction, but as teaching about our relationship with Christ as Head. Just as children are called to obey and honor parents, believers are called to give Christ both obedience and reverent respect. The message emphasizes that while obedience can be forced, honor must be chosen, and explores how earthly relationships (husband-wife, parent-child) teach us principles about our relationship with God.

## Scripture
Ephesians 6:1-3 — Children are commanded to obey their parents in the Lord (for this is right) and to honor father and mother, which is the first commandment with promise: that it may be well with you and you may live long on the earth.

## Key Themes
- **Christ's Headship**: The parent-child relationship teaches us about seeing Christ as our head and giving Him reverent respect, not just obedience.
- **Obedience vs. Honor**: Obedience can be forced, but honor must be a choice and decision. God wants both from us.
- **Childlike Trust**: As children depend on parents without understanding everything, God wants us to trust Him even when we can't answer "why" about our circumstances.
- **Respect and Relationship**: Proper relationship with God requires both following principles (rules) and relationship, which generates respect and eliminates rebellion.
- **Grace and Reverence**: Living under grace doesn't eliminate reverent fear of God; we should respect Him not out of fear of judgment but out of proper relationship.

## Notes

### The Context: Second of Three Earthly Relationships
- Ephesians 5:22-33 dealt with husbands and wives
- Ephesians 6:1-3 deals with children and parents
- These earthly relationships teach us about Christ being the head and us being the body
- Not just for those currently in these relationships, but for all to learn from

### Learning from the Wife's Role
- Wife submits and reverences her husband
- This isn't just submission but exalting and making much of him
- Men are designed to need respect more than love
- Illustration: "I love you but don't respect you" to a man = "I respect you but don't love you" to a woman
- For men, it's more important to be respected than to be loved
- Application: How often do we tell God we love Him vs. showing Him reverence and respect?

### Children's Obedience and Honor (vv. 1-2)
- Two things commanded: obedience and honor
- You can make a child obey, but you cannot make them honor you
- Honor has to be a choice and decision
- Children have innocence and lack of wisdom, but they have trust and dependence
- Example: Children believing dad can fix anything, thinking parents are invincible
- Children don't worry about bills or world events because they trust parents have it handled

### Application to Our Relationship with God
- God wants that same all-in-all trust from us
- We go through things as adults we don't understand: trials, difficulties, conditions based on others' choices
- During these times, God wants us to trust Him even when we can't answer "why"
- Philippians 4: Let requests be made known to God, and the peace that passes understanding will keep our hearts and minds
- We need to find strength in Him as our head even when we can't control or understand circumstances

### The Promise (v. 3)
- "That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth"
- References Exodus 20:12 (fifth commandment)
- Difference between Exodus and Ephesians: Exodus says "upon the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee"
- Ephesians omits reference to the land because we're not promised earthly land but heavenly things
- "Land" was personal to Israel; "earth" is our temporary dwelling
- The promise seems general and somewhat vague in application today
- Under the law, certain sins by children were worthy of death, limiting "long life"
- For us under grace, this doesn't apply the same way
- The preacher admits this is one area where he hasn't achieved complete clarity and continues to study

### Rules, Relationship, and Respect
- Three critical elements: rules, relationship, respect
- Too many rules without relationship = dictatorship, leads to rebellion
- All relationship without rules = trying to be a buddy, leads to rebellion
- Balance of rules and relationship generates respect
- You can't have respect and rebellion together
- Application to youth ministry: "I don't care if you like me, you're going to respect me"
- Living with character and consistency generates respect

### Fathers Not Provoking Children (v. 4)
- Command to bring children up in nurture and admonition of the Lord
- (Not fully developed in this sermon)

### Old Testament vs. New Testament Understanding
- Under the law: children could be stoned for cursing parents (Levitical law)
- Religious exercise (1 Timothy 4:8) refers to continuing law practices, not physical exercise
- "Bodily exercise profiteth little" = religious rituals under law have little profit
- "Godliness is profitable unto all things, having promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come" (1 Timothy 4:8)

### Promises for the Body of Christ
- Living according to godliness gives us promises for this life and the life to come
- This life: security, hope, understanding, wisdom
- Life to come: rewards at judgment seat, ruling and reigning with Christ
- Critical to understand which promises are to us and which are not

### Moses and Paul Parallel
- Moses received the law on Mount Sinai - only person who had it, then gave it to others
- Paul received the mystery - only person who had it initially
- Some call this heretical, saying Paul should have been the 12th disciple
- But God duplicated the pattern: revelation given to one man, then distributed
