## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues the survey of Philippians by examining the opening greeting and establishing the foundational themes of the book: unity through lowliness of mind and the theme of joy. He emphasizes Paul's deep pastoral concern for the Philippian church and introduces the crucial concept of the judgment seat of Christ as the primary responsibility of pastoral ministry.

## Scripture
Philippians 1:1-8 — Paul's greeting to the saints at Philippi and his prayer for their spiritual growth.

## Key Themes
- Unity in the body of Christ
- Lowliness of mind (esteeming others better than ourselves)
- Joy and rejoicing as fruits of the Spirit
- The dispensation of grace
- Grace and peace from God the Father and Lord Jesus Christ
- The judgment seat of Christ
- Pastoral responsibility to prepare believers for eternity

## Notes

### The Nature and Themes of Philippians
- Philippians is a book of reproof concerning both doctrine and behavior, addressing divisions in the church
- The primary themes are: (1) lowliness of mind—esteeming others better than ourselves, and (2) joy or rejoicing
- Joy is appropriated, not manufactured; it is a fruit of the Holy Spirit

### The Greeting: Saints in Christ Jesus (1:1-2)
- Paul uses the distinct phrase "in Christ Jesus" rather than "in Jesus Christ," emphasizing believers' position in Christ's exalted, heavenly place
- This phrase is unique to Paul and appears frequently in Ephesians, Philippians, and Colossians
- The local church structure includes saints, bishops (overseers/pastors), and deacons, with saints mentioned first as a reminder of humility
- Paul's greeting of "grace and peace" is a declaration from heaven, rooted in the dispensation of grace

### Understanding Grace and Peace
- Grace precedes peace; one cannot have peace without grace
- Three types of peace in Scripture:
  - **Peace with God**: Made through surrender to Christ at salvation (Romans 5:1)
  - **Peace of God**: Knowing God is on the throne and in control of all things (Philippians 4:7)
  - **Peace with all men**: Pursued through spiritual maturity and the avoidance of pride (Romans 12:18)

### The Dispensation of Grace
- We are living in the day of salvation, also called the dispensation of grace
- This distinguishes our time from the day of visitation (Christ's earthly ministry), the last days (early Acts), and the future day of the Lord (millennial kingdom)
- Paul uniquely received revelation of God's grace and grace to communicate it to the Gentiles

### Different Biblical "Days" Explained
- **Day of Visitation**: Christ's earthly ministry (Luke 19:44)
- **Last Days**: The period from Pentecost onward if Israel had accepted Christ (Acts 2:17)
- **Day of Salvation**: The dispensation of grace, currently lasting 2,000 years
- **Day of Redemption**: The rapture, when believers receive redemption of the body (Ephesians 4:30)
- **Day of Christ**: Christ's appearing in the middle of the tribulation period (Philippians 1:6, 2:16)
- **Day of the Lord**: The thousand-year kingdom period, beginning with Christ's second coming (1 Thessalonians 5:2)
- **Day of God**: The final eternity beyond the millennial kingdom (2 Peter 3:12)

### Paul's Confidence and Prayer (1:6-8)
- Paul is confident that God will perform a good work in believers until the day of Christ
- The judgment seat of Christ is the pastor's primary responsibility in preparing believers for eternity
- Paul's love for the Philippians is expressed "in the bowels of Jesus Christ," meaning he loves them with Christ's love rather than carnal affection
- True Christian love transcends human emotion and is grounded in Christ's sacrificial love

### The Judgment Seat of Christ
- Following the rapture, believers will stand before Christ's judgment seat for evaluation of works
- Christ will then present believers before God the Father
- Eternal position and reward are determined by faithfulness in this present earthly life
- This truth should motivate believers to live with eternal perspective, realizing the brevity of earthly time compared to eternity
