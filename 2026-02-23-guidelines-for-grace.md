## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson introduces the Guidelines for Grace series by explaining why Christians must look beyond the Gospels to understand grace-based living. He addresses the problem of taking Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John out of context—passages written to people preparing for the tribulation, not to the church in the age of grace. Simpson emphasizes that living under grace requires thinking, discernment, and understanding biblical principles rather than following rigid rules.

## Scripture
Matthew 25:14-30 — The Parable of the Talents, used to illustrate how context determines interpretation; 1 Thessalonians 5:21 — "Prove all things and hold fast that which is good"; Romans 12:1-2 — On being transformed by the renewing of your mind to prove God's will; Philippians 4:11-13 — Paul's contentment in all circumstances.

## Key Themes
- Rightly dividing Scripture and understanding context
- The difference between law and grace
- Decision-making and discerning God's will
- Stewardship and finances under grace
- The balance between liberty and legalism
- God's concern with our motives, not just our actions
- The importance of developing biblical principles and a grace mindset

## Notes

### Why Context Matters in the Gospels
- Most churches preach heavily from Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, but these are historical books about Jesus preparing people for the tribulation period
- Paul's writings (Romans through Philemon) contain the doctrines for the church age, but are often avoided because they are difficult
- The Parable of the Talents appears to teach works-based salvation if taken out of context; it actually addresses endurance through the tribulation
- Taking Gospel passages out of context creates confusion about salvation, giving, and Christian living

### Living Under Law vs. Living Under Grace
- Under the law, God's expectations were simple and specific: do this, don't do this
- Under grace, living rightly requires thinking, developing a mindset, and understanding principles
- Grace is more complex because it involves discernment, wisdom, and understanding God's will through your renewed mind
- Many Christians struggle with grace because they prefer clear rules; controlling people through fear and guilt is easier than allowing liberty

### Discerning God's Will
- God wants you to figure out His will through principles and thinking, not through signs and circumstances
- Romans 12:2 teaches that you must prove what is God's good, acceptable, and perfect will through the renewing of your mind
- God doesn't always have one "perfect will" and one "permissible will"—sometimes He gives you freedom to choose based on your heart and life circumstances
- Developing biblical principles helps you make wise decisions without needing explicit Scripture for every situation

### Grace and Decision-Making
- Grace operates in the middle ground between legalism (rigid rules) and libertinism (doing whatever you want)
- God is more concerned with why you do something and your condition while doing it than with the action itself
- A lost person can do good works and appear saved; a saved person doing good works to please men receives no reward
- Using intelligence, logic, and experience to make decisions is godly—God wants you to think

### Principles for Relationships and Finances
- Biblical principles underlie concepts like boundaries, communication, security, conflict resolution, and the dangers of manipulation—even if these exact terms aren't in Scripture
- In finances, the principle is that money should not control you, whether you have little or much
- Contentment (Philippians 4) means you can be content in any circumstance—whether you have abundance or scarcity
- Stewardship includes thinking long-term: how can your resources continue serving God's kingdom after your lifetime through wise planning and generational giving?

### Your Value Under Grace
- Most believers don't understand their value as Christians under grace
- You don't need to be brilliant, have perfect memory, or compare yourself to others to serve God effectively
- God values the work He gives you to do and wants you to understand your unique role and purpose
- Fear and inadequacy should not prevent you from doing what God calls you to do
