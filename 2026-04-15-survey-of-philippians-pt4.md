## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues the survey of Philippians, emphasizing the book's role as one of reproof and correction addressing division in the church at Philippi. He explores the theme of prayer in chapter 4, the proper context of prayer promises, Christ's humility and exaltation in chapter 2, and the book's overarching message of joy despite suffering.

## Scripture
Philippians 4 — Paul addresses prayer and peace as solutions to division in the church.

## Key Themes
- Prayer as a means of addressing conflict and anxiety
- The proper context of prayer promises in Scripture
- Christ's humility and obedience as the model for Christian conduct
- The theme of joy in Philippians despite difficult circumstances
- Overcoming divisiveness through lowliness of mind and service

## Notes

### Philippians as a Book of Reproof
- The book addresses division and lack of humility in the church at Philippi
- Members failed to honor the body through humility, service, and lowliness of mind
- This theme is relevant to churches throughout history, not just the first century

### The Issue of Prayer in Philippians 4
- Paul instructs believers to "be careful for nothing" (Philippians 4:6)
- The concern is not avoiding all concern, but not allowing worry to separate us from God's sufficiency
- Key elements of prayer: supplication (asking for things), intercession (praying for others), and thanksgiving
- Thanksgiving keeps our thoughts toward God and acknowledges His recognition

### Rightly Dividing Prayer Promises
- Matthew 21:22 ("all things whatsoever ye shall ask in prayer, believing, ye shall receive") applies to Israel, not the church age
- That promise was specifically given to the disciples in their future tribulation period
- The church must understand the context and timing of different prayer promises
- Romans 8:26 acknowledges that we often don't know what to pray for as we ought

### The Lord's Prayer in Its Proper Context
- The Lord's Prayer teaches Israel how to pray during the tribulation period
- "Thy kingdom come, thy will be done in earth as it is in heaven" refers to Satan's expulsion from heaven during the tribulation and God's will being established on earth
- "Give us this day our daily bread" applies to Israel in the tribulation, not believers in the church age who have daily provision
- "Lead us not into temptation" addresses the intense trials of the tribulation period
- The prayer is not the model prayer for the church age but rather instruction for Israel's future

### Christ's Humility and Exaltation (Philippians 2)
- Seven statements of Christ's humility:
  1. Made Himself of no reputation
  2. Took upon Him the form of a servant
  3. Made in the likeness of men
  4. Found in fashion as a man
  5. Became obedient unto death
  6. Death of the cross (criminal's death)
  7. Overall emphasis on lowliness of mind and servitude

- Seven statements of Christ's exaltation:
  1. God highly exalted Him
  2. Given Him a name above every name
  3. That at His name every knee should bow
  4. Things in heaven bow
  5. Things in the earth bow
  6. Things under the earth bow
  7. Every tongue confesses Jesus Christ is Lord to the glory of God the Father

### Philippians as the Book of Joy
- Paul writes from prison, in chains and suffering
- Despite his circumstances, he calls believers to rejoice repeatedly
- The resolution to division is found in joy and freedom through Christ
- True joy comes through lowliness of mind, service to others, and unity
- Internal conflict with one person can rob our joy; resolution comes through unity

### Application
- Christians are called to live with lowliness of mind and service
- We find true joy in Christ not through exalting ourselves but through humility and serving others
- In a troubled world, believers can maintain joy through prayer, supplication, and intercession
- Keeping our hearts and minds requires making our requests known to God with thanksgiving
