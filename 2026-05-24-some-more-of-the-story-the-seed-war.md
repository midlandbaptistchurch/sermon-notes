## Summary
Pastor Jim continues his teaching series on the skeletal system of scripture, focusing on the spiritual warfare between the seed of the woman and the seed of the serpent. He explains how Satan's rebellion in heaven led to his attempt to corrupt humanity through the sons of God (angels) intermarrying with the daughters of men, producing the Nephilim giants.

## Scripture
Genesis 3:15 — God's promise that the seed of the woman would bruise the serpent's head, establishing the foundation for the ongoing spiritual war.

## Key Themes
- The gap between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2 revealing Satan's original rebellion
- God's will for reconciliation of all things in Christ
- The seed war between Satan and humanity
- The corruption of the human bloodline through angelic intervention
- God's judgment and grace throughout history

## Notes
### The Rebellion and God's Reconciliation Plan
- Satan (Lucifer) rebelled in heaven between Genesis 1:1 and 1:2
- God's will is reconciliation - bringing all things back under Christ
- 2 Corinthians 4:6 proves the gap theory - God commanded light to shine out of darkness
- Paul uses the creation account as an illustration of salvation

### The Seed War Begins
- Genesis 3:15 establishes enmity between the serpent's seed and woman's seed
- Satan immediately began trying to corrupt the human bloodline
- Cain (of the wicked one) killed Abel (righteous seed)
- God replaced Abel with Seth to continue the godly line

### The Sons of God Corruption
- Genesis 6: Sons of God (angels) saw daughters of men and took them as wives
- This produced the Nephilim - giants and men of renown
- Occurred during the days of Jared, continuing for hundreds of years
- Enoch walked with God and was taken out; Noah was preserved through judgment
- Total corruption required God's judgment through the flood

### God's Commands for Destruction
- Deuteronomy 7 and 20, 1 Samuel 15 - commands to destroy certain nations
- These were not acts of genocide but necessary judgment against corrupted bloodlines
- God's grace and mercy evident even in judgment
