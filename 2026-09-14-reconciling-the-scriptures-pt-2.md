## Summary
Associate Pastor Mark Simpson continues a multi-part study on reconciling seemingly contradictory passages of Scripture by examining how different portions of the Bible are addressed to different audiences and time periods. He contrasts Old Testament law and justice (eye for an eye, cities of refuge) with Jesus's kingdom teachings in Matthew 5–7, and explores the distinction between Israel and the Church. The session also addresses the audience and context of the Book of James, the Great Commission accounts across the Gospels, and the interpretation of Mark 16.

## Key Themes
- Rightly dividing Scripture by recognizing different audiences and dispensations
- Old Testament law versus New Testament grace and kingdom principles
- The distinct roles of Israel and the Church in God's redemptive plan
- The kingdom teachings of Matthew 5–7 as preparation for Christ's millennial reign
- Solomon's judgment as a type of Christ's future righteous reign
- The Book of James as written primarily to Jewish believers before Paul's epistles
- The danger of mixing passages meant for different audiences ("spiritual Israel" critique)
- Interpretation of Mark 16 in its proper context

## Notes
- **Review of Part 1:** Session picks up from a previous meeting; prior content covered an introduction and timeline overview.
- **Old Testament Law and Justice:** Discussed "eye for an eye" (Exodus 21), the role of the avenger of blood, and cities of refuge as protections for accidental killers. Joshua's military campaigns cited as God-ordained.
- **Matthew 5–7 in Context:** Simpson argues the Beatitudes and related teachings are specifically preparing Jewish disciples for life under Christ's millennial kingdom reign, not general instructions for the Church Age.
- **Adultery and Lustful Thoughts:** Matthew 5:28 interpreted in the context of kingdom-level righteousness, where even internal sin would be judged under Christ's direct rule.
- **Solomon as a Type:** The account of Solomon's judgment between two mothers (1 Kings 3) is presented as a picture of Christ's perfect, righteous judgment during the Millennium — "Solomon on steroids."
- **Romans 12:17 and Self-Defense:** Passage used to discuss whether believers today are called to live peaceably rather than seek personal vengeance, with acknowledgment that context differs from kingdom-era passages.
- **Book of James:** Argued to have been written before Paul's conversion, making it pre-Pauline and directed to the twelve scattered tribes — kingdom-preparation literature rather than Church Age doctrine.
- **Spiritual Israel Critique:** Simpson challenges the view that the Church has replaced or inherited Israel's promises, arguing this leads to improper mixing of passages and confused doctrine.
- **Galatians 3:28:** "Neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free, male nor female" interpreted as describing equality within the body of Christ during the Church Age, while noting these distinctions remain temporally meaningful on earth.
- **Gospel Commission Distinctions:** Matthew presents Christ as King; Mark as Servant; Luke as Man; John as Messiah. Matthew 10 commission (go only to Israel) contrasted with Mark 16 commission (preach to all). The 144,000 in Revelation identified as servants carrying the kingdom gospel during the Tribulation.
- **Mark 16 Interpretation:** Signs following believers (casting out demons, handling serpents, immunity to poison) discussed as belonging to a specific commission context; Simpson cautions against applying these signs indiscriminately to the Church Age.
- **Looking Ahead:** Simpson indicates the series will continue over the following weeks.
