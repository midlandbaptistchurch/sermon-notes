## Summary
Bill Baughman concludes the five-week Resurrection series by examining Jesus's exclusive claim to all authority in himself. Through the Road to Emmaus account and comparisons with other religious leaders, the sermon demonstrates that Jesus's identity and teachings point solely to himself as God, not merely a good teacher. The presentation emphasizes that believers' resurrection bodies will be transformed to be like Christ's glorified body, enabling believers to share in his eternal authority.

## Scripture
Luke 24:13-31 — The Road to Emmaus account where Jesus appears to two disciples after his resurrection, their hearts burning within them as he opens the scriptures to show how all things concerning himself were fulfilled.

## Key Themes
- Jesus claims all authority in himself, not in visions, logic, or experience
- Jesus modified the Mosaic Law with new, heart-centered commands
- Believers will receive glorified, eternal bodies like Christ's
- Other religious leaders (Buddha, Confucius, Muhammad) seat authority elsewhere
- Jesus's identity as God is demonstrated through his authoritative words and actions
- The ransom nature of Christ's sacrifice pays the debt for humanity's sins

## Notes

### Jesus's Claim to All Authority
- Jesus alone claims "all authority in eternity" (Matthew 28:18)
- Demonstrated through seven "I AM" statements: bread of life, light of the world, door of the sheep, good shepherd, resurrection and the life, the way/truth/life, true vine
- Unlike other religious leaders, Jesus points to himself as the source of authority, not to external systems

### Comparison with Other Religious Leaders
- **Buddha**: Seats authority in personal experience and understanding; teaches that enlightenment comes through self-reliance and the Eightfold Path
- **Confucius**: Seats authority in logic and ethical systems for social harmony; denies God and afterlife
- **Muhammad**: Seats authority in visions and revelations through the Archangel Gabriel compiled in the Quran
- Jesus differs by pointing all authority to himself personally

### Jesus Modified the Mosaic Law
After 1,500 years of the Torah, Jesus introduced modified, heart-centered laws:
- Mosaic: "Thou shalt not kill" → Jesus: Anger without cause brings judgment
- Mosaic: "Thou shalt not commit adultery" → Jesus: Lustful thoughts constitute adultery in the heart
- Mosaic: Eye for eye, tooth for tooth → Jesus: Turn the other cheek, love your enemies
- Mosaic: Love your neighbor → Jesus: Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you

### Jesus Did Not Ask Permission to Forgive
- When forgiving the paralytic, Jesus did not ask the Father's permission; he exercised divine authority directly
- To the rich young ruler who called him "good master," Jesus clarified: "There is none good but one, that is God"—implying that calling him good necessitates calling him God

### The Believer's Resurrection Body
- Believers will have bodies transformed to be like Christ's glorified body (Philippians 3:21)
- Attributes of the glorified body:
  - Physical and solid enough to be touched and to eat
  - Able to appear and disappear at will
  - Able to pass through solid objects
  - Eternal, unlike Lazarus who faced second death after resuscitation
  - Capable of traveling at great speeds across vast distances

### Jesus's Control Over His Death and Resurrection
- "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself" (John 10:18)
- Jesus decided when to die and when to rise; the Jewish leadership did not kill him
- His death and resurrection occurred on the third day according to prophecy (Jonah 3:1-3)

### The Ransom and Remission of Sins
- Jesus gave his life "a ransom for many" (Matthew 20:28), paying the debt owed by all people
- His blood is shed "for the remission of sins" (Matthew 26:28)
- Jews must see him as Son of God and Messiah; Gentiles believe in his death, burial, and resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:1-8)

### Heart-Burning Scripture
- From the Emmaus account: "Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures?"
- This should be believers' experience when encountering Scripture—hearts transformed by Jesus's truth
