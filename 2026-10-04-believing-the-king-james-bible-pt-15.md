## Summary
This lesson examines the qualifications and work of Desiderius Erasmus in compiling the first printed Greek New Testament (Textus Receptus). The session explores the historical conflict between those who held the Latin Vulgate as authoritative (primarily Roman Catholics) versus those who believed the Greek text had authority. Through quotations from four notable scholars (Durant, Froude, Mangan, and Nolan), the lesson establishes Erasmus's credentials as a dedicated scholar who spent over 40 years studying Greek manuscripts before publishing his work in 1516.

## Key Themes
- **Two Competing Authorities**: The fundamental conflict between those who believed the Latin Vulgate had authority (Roman Catholic Church) versus those who believed the Greek text was authoritative
- **Erasmus's Qualifications**: Erasmus spent over 40 years studying Greek manuscripts, had access to at least five key manuscripts covering the entire New Testament, and maintained an extensive network of learned friends who supplied manuscripts and scholarly input
- **Opposition to the Greek Text**: Roman Catholic theologians like Martin Dorp and Peter Soutour argued that any error in the Vulgate would undermine the entire authority of Holy Scripture and the Church itself
- **Dedicated Scholarship**: Erasmus's life work demonstrates the extensive preparation required to compile the Textus Receptus, comparable to modern biblical scholars who dedicate their lives to studying Scripture

## Notes

### Historical Context: Two Bodies of Belief
- One group believed the Latin Vulgate had authority for the definitive Word of God in the New Testament (primarily Roman Catholic Church)
- The other group believed the Greek text had the authority
- Erasmus was of the Greek text persuasion
- Some Catholics, including Pope Leo X, were of the Greek authority persuasion
- Erasmus's first edition was dedicated to Pope Leo X

### Laurentius Vala's Influence on Erasmus
- Vala lived 1405-1457, just before Erasmus was born
- Emphasized the importance of language, believing civilization's decline in the Dark Ages was due to the decay of Greek and Latin languages
- Possessed Greek New Testament manuscripts
- Maintained the Latin text was often different from the Greek, with additions and omissions
- Argued Greek wording was generally better than the Latin
- Erasmus studied Vala's writings and agreed with his positions

### Opposition from Latin Vulgate Supporters
- Martin Dorp, leading expert on Latin text view and theologian at University of Louvain
- In 1514, wrote to Erasmus asking him not to publish his Greek text
- Argued that if the Vulgate contained errors, the church would have been wrong for centuries, which he considered impossible
- Insisted church councils approved the Latin Vulgate as official Bible and rejected the Greek New Testament
- Claimed the Greek text had been corrupted by the heretical Greek church
- Dorp argued primarily to defend the church, not the truth

### Peter Soutour's Position
- French Catholic theologian and outspoken critic of Erasmus
- Declared: "If at one point the Vulgate were an error, the entire authority of Holy Scripture would collapse"
- Roman Catholic opposition viewed Erasmus as a threat to their power structure

### Four Scholars Validating Erasmus's Work

**William James Durant (1885-1981)**
- American historian and philosopher
- With wife Ariel, produced 11-volume work "The Story of Civilization"
- Work took 40 years, sold over 2 million copies, translated into 9 languages
- Volume 6, Chapter 14 contains extensive chapter on Erasmus
- Said of Erasmus: "wherever he was, he had one foot elsewhere"

**James Anthony Froude (1818-1894)**
- English historian, novelist, biographer, and magazine editor
- Wrote "The Life and Letters of Erasmus" published in 1894

**John Joseph Mangan (20th century)**
- Author and biographer focused on religious and historical subjects
- Little public biographical information available
- Wrote "The Life, Character, and Influence of Desiderius Erasmus of Rotterdam" (1927, two volumes)
- Let Erasmus speak for himself through extensive quotations from his own writings and correspondence

**Frederick Nolan (1784-1864)**
- Irish-Anglican theologian, Greek and Latin scholar, historian
- Great defender of the Textus Receptus
- Spent 28 years tracing the Textus Receptus to apostolic origins
- Argued first editors of printed Greek New Testament intentionally selected superior texts and disregarded inferior ones

### Erasmus's Manuscript Collection
- When he went to Basel in July 1515, he had at least five Greek New Testament manuscripts
- These five manuscripts provided complete coverage of the entire New Testament with some overlapping portions
- Strong indications he had additional manuscripts beyond these five
- The five manuscripts listed by Dr. Edward Hills in "The King James Version Defended"

### Erasmus's Extensive Preparation
- Surrounded with Bible manuscripts from childhood in 1460s until publication in 1516 (over 40 years)
- Traveled extensively looking for manuscripts everywhere and borrowed them from everyone he could
- Was "something of a rolling stone" but "certainly gathered manuscripts"
- Began working directly on the text before 1507
- In 1505 wrote to a friend: "I shall sit down to holy scriptures with my whole heart and devote the rest of my life to it. All these three years I have been working entirely at Greek and have not been playing with it"
- Durant said Erasmus "spent all of his time devouring the libraries" when in Italy
- Mangan noted "comparing two codices for the most correct reading of some intricate passage was his passion"

### Network of Learned Friends
- Had large network of other educated men and serious students of the text
- Gathered readings from whole of Europe through broad friendships
- Said: "I have a room full of letters from men of learning"
- Wanted validation and critique from others, not just his own perspective
- Corresponding "at length and elaborately with the learned men of his time on technical points of scholarship, biblical criticism, etc."
- Said: "it may easily be guessed how large a part of the usefulness of my work would have been lacking if my learned friends had not supplied me with manuscripts"

### Journey to Basel
- Arrived at Basel in 1515 "way down with books and copious notes on the New Testament"
- His manuscript collection was so large and valuable it was seized by customs agents when leaving England in 1514
- Erasmus said they had "stolen the labors of his life"
- Manuscripts were eventually returned

### Comparison to Modern Bible Study
- Contrasted with people who take "a couple of classes in Bible college and they think they're an expert"
- Some criticize the King James Bible based only on undergraduate Greek class
- Erasmus worked for a dozen years on the text itself
- Analogy made to Pastor Jim Payne's lifetime of collecting commentaries and studying the King James text

### The Importance of Final Authority
- The King James Bible is "our final authority, this is our true north, this is our standard for what we believe and live by"
- Satan's strategy is to undermine faith in the Bible through criticism
- Having this book allows believers to "recalibrate" their lives, hearts, minds, and thought patterns
- Church attendance is partly "to get recalibrated in line with this book"
