## Summary
Evan Watt preaches on the transforming power of salvation, centering on the past-tense word "were" in Romans 5 to show that believers are no longer defined by their former weakness, sin, or enmity toward God. Drawing on passages across Romans, 1 Corinthians, Colossians, and Titus, he argues that God's finished work in Christ has fundamentally changed who believers are — not merely what they do. The sermon closes with a challenge to live daily in the new identity God has declared over every saved person.

## Scripture
Romans 5:1-11 — Paul's declaration that those once without strength, sinners, and enemies of God have been justified and reconciled through Christ's death. Supporting passages include Romans 7:15-24, Romans 8:33-37, 1 Corinthians 6:9-11, Colossians 1:21, and Titus 3:3-7.

## Key Themes
- Christian identity is rooted in what God has done, not in past sin or ongoing struggle
- Justification means God declares believers righteous — they are no longer identified as sinners
- Believers are reconciled to God once and for all through Christ's finished work
- Sanctification involves both God's completed work and the believer's daily responsibility to live accordingly
- Grace reigns where sin once reigned; believers must actively choose to walk in that grace

## Notes

### The Significance of "Were"
- The word "were" in Romans 5 is past tense — describing what believers were before salvation, not what they are now
- We are prone to identify with our weaknesses, failures, and sins, but Scripture calls us to identify with what God has made us
- Central question: do our sins *define* who we are, or merely *describe* what we were?

### Point 1: We Were Without Strength — Now We Are More Than Conquerors
- Romans 5:6 — "when we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly"
- Our flesh remains weak (Matthew 26:41), but weakness of the flesh does not mean we are without strength
- 2 Corinthians 12:9 — God's strength is made perfect in weakness; "when I am weak, then am I strong"
- Romans 8:37 — "we are more than conquerors through him that loved us"

### Point 2: We Were Sinners — Now We Are Justified
- Romans 5:8 — "while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us"
- Romans 5:9 — we are "now justified by his blood" — justification means being declared righteous and not guilty before God
- 1 Corinthians 6:9-11 — "such were some of you, but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus"
- To be washed = cleansed; to be sanctified = set apart as a saint; to be justified = declared righteous
- Titus 3:3-7 — we were foolish and disobedient, but God saved us by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost, not by our works
- Romans 7:15-24 — Paul acknowledges his struggle with sin but refuses to let it define him: "it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me"
- Calling ourselves "sinners" after salvation risks taking God's finished work for granted and using sin as a crutch; better to say "I was a sinner — I am saved by grace"

### Point 3: We Were Enemies — Now We Are Reconciled
- Romans 5:10 — "when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son"
- Colossians 1:21 — "ye that were sometime alienated and enemies in your mind by wicked works, yet now hath he reconciled"
- Romans 8:7 — the carnal mind is enmity against God; without the Spirit, no one can be subject to God's law
- Reconciliation is God's finished work — believers do not need to earn or re-earn it through moral performance
- We should repent and deal with sin, but sin does not place us back at enmity with God; we can pray without ceasing precisely because we are reconciled

### Application: Live Like What You Are
- Ephesians 5:8 — "ye were sometimes darkness, but now are ye light in the Lord: walk as children of light"
- 2 Corinthians 7:1 — "let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God"
- Ephesians 6:10 — "be strong in the Lord and in the power of his might" — we must choose to take up his strength daily
- Romans 5:20-21 — sin reigned, but now grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life
- Romans 6:12 — "let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body" — believers who live carelessly in sin are not sinners by nature; they are letting sin reign when they do not have to
- The call is to put off the old man and put on the new man — to crucify the flesh daily
- We are not what we were: let us walk like it, live like it, act like it
