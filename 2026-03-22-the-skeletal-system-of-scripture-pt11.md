## Summary
Pastor Payne continues examining Lucifer's rebellion against God's throne, focusing on his role as a "covering cherub" who led worship in the pre-Adamic world. The sermon explores how Lucifer's pride and beauty led to his corruption, and how he merchandised lies and flattery among the angels to build support for his violent insurrection against God.

## Scripture
Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28 — Passages describing Lucifer's original perfection, his corruption through pride and merchandising, and his five "I will" declarations of rebellion against God's throne.

## Key Themes
- Lucifer's pre-fall perfection as the covering cherub
- The nature of sin's origin before Adam
- Merchandising and tale-bearing as tools of deception
- Pride as the root of Lucifer's downfall
- God's sovereignty as possessor of heaven and earth

## Notes

### Review: Sin's Origin Before Adam
- Sin did not originate with Adam in the Garden of Eden
- Before the Adamic world existed the Luciferian world with angels
- Lucifer sinned before Eve, who sinned before Adam
- Satan beguiled Eve through subtlety and deception (2 Corinthians 11:3)
- One-third of angels followed Lucifer in rebellion

### Ezekiel 28: Lucifer's Perfection and Corruption
- Created perfect in all his ways until iniquity was found (v.15)
- Corrupted through merchandising and traffic among angels (v.16)
- Defiled the sanctuaries where angelic worship occurred
- Violence filled him through his merchandising activities
- Cast out as "profane" - lacking reverence for God
- Pride lifted him up because of his beauty and brightness (v.17)

### Lucifer as Tale-Bearer and Merchandiser
- Etymology connects "merchandise," "traffic," and "tale-bearer" (Leviticus 19:16)
- Went throughout the angelic world spreading lies and flattery
- Used same deceptive tactics later employed with Eve
- Spread gossip and revealed secrets to build following
- Flattered angels while undermining God's authority

### Isaiah 14: The Five "I Will" Declarations
1. "I will ascend into heaven" - seeking elevation above current position
2. "I will exalt my throne above the stars of God" - wanting supremacy over other angels
3. "I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north" - desiring to rule God's heavenly assembly
4. "I will ascend above the heights of the clouds" - attempting to penetrate God's glory
5. "I will be like the most high" - ultimate goal of replacing God as possessor of heaven and earth

### God's Response to Rebellion
- Cast Lucifer out of the mountain of God as profane
- Destroyed him from his position as covering cherub
- Brought him down from heaven to earth
- Demonstrated that pride goes before destruction (Proverbs 16:18)
- Maintained His position as Most High God, possessor of heaven and earth
