## Summary
Pastor Mark Simpson examines four biblical terms—overseer, elder, bishop, and pastor—demonstrating that they all refer to the same office in the local church. He explains that while "pastor" appears only once in the New Testament (Ephesians 4:11), it is used eight times in Jeremiah. The sermon focuses on the qualifications for this office from 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1, clarifying misconceptions about church leadership roles and explaining the differences between pastors, evangelists, missionaries, and apostles.

## Scripture
**1 Timothy 3:1-7** — Provides the qualifications for a bishop, including being blameless, husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behavior, given to hospitality, apt to teach, not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre, patient, not a brawler, not covetous, ruling his house well, not a novice, and having a good report of those outside the church.

**Titus 1:5-16** — Instructions for ordaining elders in every city with similar qualifications, with specific application to dealing with false teachers and difficult situations in Crete.

## Key Themes
- **Four Terms, One Office**: The terms overseer, elder, bishop, and pastor all refer to the same church leadership role, emphasizing different aspects of the position.
- **Biblical Qualifications**: Scripture provides specific character and lifestyle requirements for church leaders that must be maintained.
- **Distinction of Offices**: Apostles and prophets are no longer active offices since the Bible is complete; evangelists, pastors, and teachers continue today.
- **Authority of Scripture**: Church practices and terminology should be based on what the Bible actually teaches rather than tradition or assumption.

## Notes

### Four Biblical Terms for Church Leadership
- Overseer, elder, bishop, and pastor all refer to Pastor Payne's job
- The word "overseer" is used only once in Acts 20:28
- The word "pastor" appears only once in the New Testament (Ephesians 4:11) and eight times in Jeremiah
- The word "bishop" is used multiple times, particularly in 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1
- The word "elder" appears 194 times throughout the entire Bible (126 times in Old Testament)

### Gifts to the Body of Christ (Ephesians 4)
- Five categories listed: apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers
- Apostles and prophets no longer exist because the Bible has been completed
- Apostle means "sent one" with specific authority
- An apostle had the role of a prophet as well
- The term "missionary" does not exist in the Bible

### The Role of Evangelist
- The word "evangelist" appears only three times in the Bible
- Philip was called "the evangelist" as one of the seven men appointed in Acts
- Evangelist refers to winning people to Christ, doing the work of an evangelist
- Billy Graham held evangelistic crusades focused on gospel preaching
- 2 Timothy 4:5 instructs Timothy to "do the work of an evangelist"
- Modern "evangelists" who preach from church to church are not necessarily the biblical definition
- Brother Pat is described as "probably more of a true evangelist than most evangelists"

### Missionaries vs. Evangelists
- Missionaries typically go to other countries to reach people and establish churches
- They work to make churches indigenous (able to operate on their own)
- Their first role is seeing people saved—doing the work of an evangelist
- Midland Baptist Church supports many missionaries who "do not rightly divide the Bible"
- The church supports them because they do the work of an evangelist in places where others don't go
- Some Bible churches have no missions program because missionaries don't align perfectly with their doctrine
- Pastor Mark states: "I would rather take supporting them to the judgment seat than not supporting them and not doing anything about it"

### Qualifications of a Bishop (1 Timothy 3:1-7)
- "This is a true saying, if a man desire the office of a bishop, he desireth a good work"
- Must begin with a desire—there must be a want to
- **Blameless**: Covers a lot of different areas; a principle to live by
- **Husband of one wife**: 
  - Two interpretations: (1) never been divorced, or (2) only having one wife at a time
  - More popular view is that divorce disqualifies from pastoral ministry
  - Question raised about divorce before salvation and remarriage after salvation
  - Romans 7:1-4 uses marriage as an illustration of the law, not necessarily a direct teaching for the church age
  - Under grace, we live according to principles rather than rigid law
- **Vigilant, sober, of good behavior**
- **Given to hospitality**: Can be expressed in different ways (taking people out to eat vs. having people in your home)
- **Apt to teach**: Also mentioned in 2 Timothy 2:24—"servant of the Lord must not strive but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient"
- **Not given to wine**
- **No striker** (not violent)
- **Not greedy of filthy lucre**: 
  - Filthy lucre is crooked money
  - Proverbs speaks of "false balance" and "divers weights"—dishonest scales in the marketplace
  - Means not making money in an ungodly or dishonest way
- **Patient, not a brawler, not covetous**
- **Rules well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity**:
  - Verse 5: "For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?"
  - While children are at home, there should be order and direction
  - Children will make their own decisions when older
  - Sometimes churches have "adults that act like children"
- **Not a novice** (not inexperienced):
  - Doesn't necessarily mean young—Timothy was young but experienced
  - Timothy had spent significant time with Paul and was wise beyond his years
  - Many situations require experience to navigate properly
  - "Sin is always, the Bible says Satan's the author of confusion"
  - Pastors deal with complex situations during the week that congregations don't see
- **Not lifted up with pride, lest he fall into the condemnation of the devil**:
  - People will say wonderful things that can lead to pride
  - Pride is "being more concerned about what man thinks than what God thinks"
- **Must have a good report of them which are without** (a good reputation with the lost)

### Titus and the Qualifications in Titus 1
- Titus was left in Crete to "set in order the things that are wanting and ordain elders in every city"
- As an apostle, Titus had authority to ordain elders (pastors)
- Similar list of qualifications given (verses 6-9)
- Specific challenges in Crete:
  - Port cities with rough environments where ships came in
  - "Cretians are always liars, evil beasts, slow bellies" (verse 12)
  - Many "unruly and vain talkers and deceivers" (verse 10)
  - Required sharp rebuke and firmness
- Titus described as "a bad dude" who could handle the difficult situation

### Balance in Pastoral Approach
- 2 Timothy 2:20-26 gives instructions to Timothy with a gentler approach than Titus
- Verse 24: "The servant of the Lord must not strive but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient"
- Verse 25: "In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves"
- Different situations require different approaches:
  - Some need firm confrontation
  - Others need gentle, patient instruction over time
- Pastor Payne example: Has yelled at men in serious situations (like adultery) when needed
- Some people respond to firmness, others need gentle patience
- "People are going to make their own choice"

### Understanding "Elder" in Scripture
- Elder can refer to someone who is old (age)
- Pastor Mark: "I am an elder to each one of you. It doesn't mean I'm your pastor, that means I'm old"
- Some churches have created an "elder" position not specifically found in Scripture
- Midland has people who are elders in the sense of being older, wise, knowing their Bible
- Not everyone who is old is wise—"just because somebody's old does not mean that they're wise"
- Acts 16:4 refers to "apostles and elders which were at Jerusalem"—these were ordained pastors
- Context determines whether "elder" refers to age or office

### The Term "Bishop" vs. "Pastor"
- Biblically, calling someone "Bishop Payne" would not be incorrect
- The term "pastor" is more practical and describes the shepherding role
- The Bible uses "bishop" more often than "pastor"
- Philippians 1:1: "To all the saints in Christ Jesus which are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons"
- Paul and Timothy were called "servants of Jesus Christ" but were also apostles

### Differences in Pastoral Personalities
- Pastor Payne and Pastor Mark are very different in personality
- Pastor Payne: "Very much a people person. He's always on the phone. He's always talking, always visiting"
- Pastor Mark: More focused on administrative matters, building maintenance, logistics, finances
- The two complement each other in ministry
- If Pastor Mark became the senior pastor, he would need to relinquish administrative responsibilities and focus more on personal ministry
- People come not just for good preaching but because the pastor shows up at the hospital, makes calls, prays with people

### Church Support of Missionaries
- Midland Baptist Church supports many missionaries on their missionary wall
- "The majority of those missionaries out there that we support do not rightly divide the Bible"
- However, they "do the work of an evangelist"
- They go to places where Midland doesn't have anyone going
- Many Berean, Grace, and Bible churches have no missionaries and no missions program
- Pastor Mark's position: "I would rather take supporting them to the judgment seat than not supporting them and not doing anything about it"
- Some supported missionaries are 78-81 years old with no retirement because they were "sheep" who listened to wrong advice about finances

### Accountability and Transparency
- Congregations should know the biblical qualifications for pastors
- This knowledge keeps pastors accountable
- "Some pastors don't like preaching on this or teaching on it. They don't want to tell anybody about it"
- Pastor Mark's perspective: "You're going to preach it to them and tell them all their bad stuff. You better let it come back to you too as well"
- Purpose of teaching: "That's like anti-stupid pills...keeps you from being stupid"

### Additional Practical Notes
- Pastor Payne never has people stay at his house; Pastor Mark's house has been "like a hotel"
- When making major decisions, consult the right people: "If it's a business decision, your pastor's not a business man, don't talk to him"
- Parents called Pastor Mark as youth pastor to tell their kids what to do because kids wouldn't listen to them
- Pastors spend the week "putting out fires" that the congregation doesn't see on Sunday morning
- The seven men in Acts were not deacons—they were appointed for specific daily administration
- The only two offices in the church are pastor and deacon
