## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne introduces the book of 1 Thessalonians, placing it within the broader arc of Paul's letters and their overarching themes: the cross, the church, and the coming of Christ. He traces the historical background of the Thessalonian church through Paul's second missionary journey before giving particular attention to 1 Thessalonians 4, where Paul reveals the mystery of the rapture and the resurrection of the dead in Christ.

## Scripture
1 Thessalonians 1–4 — Paul's letter to the young church at Thessalonica, written from Corinth during his second missionary journey, addressing questions about deceased believers and the coming of Christ.

## Key Themes
- The coming of Christ as the unifying theme of 1 Thessalonians
- The rapture and resurrection of the dead in Christ (1 Thessalonians 4:13–17)
- Paul's unique apostolic ministry and his reception of revelation directly from the Lord Jesus Christ
- The historical and geographical context of Paul's second missionary journey
- The importance of doctrinal soundness and being established in the faith
- The gospel of the cross as the foundation of salvation today

## Notes

### Overview of Paul's Letters
- Pastor Payne placed 1 Thessalonians within a four-part thematic framework seen across Paul's thirteen letters:
  1. **The Cross** — Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians
  2. **The Church** — Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians
  3. **The Coming of Christ** — 1 & 2 Thessalonians
  4. (Future study)
- 1 Thessalonians: 5 chapters, 89 verses, 1,837 words
- Described as one of Paul's most readable and enjoyable letters, alongside Philippians

### The Theme of the Coming of Christ
- Each of the five chapters of 1 Thessalonians ends with a statement or teaching about the coming of Christ:
  - Ch. 1:9–10 — waiting for His Son from heaven
  - Ch. 2:19 — the Lord's coming as Paul's hope and crown of rejoicing
  - Ch. 3:13 — establishing hearts at the coming of the Lord with all His saints
  - Ch. 4:16–17 — the Lord's descent from heaven and the catching up of believers
  - Ch. 5:23 — (referenced)

### Historical and Geographical Context
- Paul's second missionary journey: departed Antioch with Silas, picked up Timothy at Lystra
- Prevented by the Spirit from entering Asia and Bithynia; received the Macedonian vision at Troas
- Ministered at Philippi (Acts 16), then traveled to Thessalonica (Acts 17)
- At Thessalonica, Paul reasoned in the synagogue for **three Sabbath days (three weeks)**
- Converts won from Jews, devout Greeks, and leading women; opposition arose from unbelieving Jews who stirred up the city against the household of Jason
- Paul and Silas were sent away by night to Berea, then Paul went on to Athens alone
- From Athens, Paul sent Timothy (and Silas) back to Thessalonica to check on the young church
- Paul then moved to Corinth (Acts 18); Timothy and Silas returned to him there from Macedonia with a good report
- It was at **Corinth** that Paul wrote 1 Thessalonians in response to that report and the questions brought back from the church

### Paul's Unique Apostolic Ministry
- Paul's revelation about the rapture was received directly from the Lord Jesus Christ, not from the Twelve (1 Thess. 4:15 — "by the word of the Lord")
- Paul is identified as "that other apostle" — distinct from the Twelve, the key to understanding the Bible's message for today
- 1 Corinthians 15:51–52 — Paul introduces the rapture as a **mystery**: "Behold, I show you a mystery; we shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed"
- Paul continued to receive visions and revelations of the Lord throughout his ministry (2 Cor. 12:1–2), especially during his prison ministry

### The Rapture — 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17
- The Thessalonians' question: what has happened to believers who have already died?
- Paul's answer: "I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren" — the largest Christian denomination is ignorant brethren; God wants His people to know
- **The dead in Christ:** their spirits are immediately present with the Lord at death; their bodies rest in the ground (cemetery = "sleeping place") awaiting resurrection
- **The order of events at the rapture:**
  1. The Lord Himself descends from heaven with a shout, the voice of the archangel (Michael), and the trump of God
  2. The dead in Christ rise first
  3. The living believers are caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air
- The trump signals a **summoned convocation** — God has the entire event planned and ordered
- At the rapture, living believers' mortal bodies will be transformed into immortal, incorruptible bodies — like Christ's resurrected body (flesh and bone, no blood)
- Christ's blood was taken to heaven and placed on the mercy seat; salvation is by grace and according to mercy
- Paul himself lived in the hope of being among the living at the rapture ("we which are alive and remain")
- Comfort for believers: "so shall we ever be with the Lord; wherefore comfort one another with these words" (1 Thess. 4:17–18)

### Additional Notes
- Jason (Acts 17) remained faithful even after his house was attacked; he is later mentioned by Paul in Romans 16:21
- Believers are called to be **established** in faith — per Romans 16: through Paul's gospel, the preaching of Jesus Christ according to the revelation of the mystery, and the scriptures of the prophets
- The Great Commission (Matthew 28, Mark 16) was concluded at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15); God now uses Paul — not Israel — to reach the Gentiles
- Part 2 will cover 2 Thessalonians, including a deeper study of the Day of Christ and the prophetic passage in 2 Thessalonians 2
