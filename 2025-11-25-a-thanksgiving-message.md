## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne explores the challenging command in Ephesians 5:20 to give thanks "always for all things." He contrasts this with the more accessible instruction in 1 Thessalonians 5:18 to give thanks "in everything," explaining that Paul elevates believers to a higher spiritual responsibility in Ephesians. The sermon examines five key reasons God uses trials and difficulties: opportunity, correction, testimony, maturity, and glory.

## Scripture
Ephesians 5:20 — "Giving thanks always for all things unto God and the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ."

Also references: 1 Thessalonians 5:18, Romans 8:28, 2 Corinthians 1:3, Hebrews 12:5-11, James 1:2-4, 1 Peter 4:12-14, Luke 2:52, Acts 7:54-60.

## Key Themes
- The command to give thanks in all circumstances, not just good ones
- Five spiritual benefits of trials: opportunity for witness, divine correction, powerful testimony, spiritual maturity, and eternal glory
- The contrast between gratitude *in* difficulties versus gratitude *for* difficulties
- God's goodness and sovereignty even in the midst of worldly evil and suffering
- The role of suffering in Christian maturity and conformity to Christ
- The spirit of glory that rests upon believers who suffer faithfully

## Notes

### The Challenge of Thanksgiving
- Ephesians 5:20 commands giving thanks "always for all things"—a higher standard than simply not complaining
- Many believers live at lower levels of thankfulness: some grumble constantly, others are simply indifferent, and many give thanks only for good circumstances
- The call is to mature spiritually by reaching a place where thanksgiving is possible even in adversity

### Why God Allows Trials

**Opportunity:** Difficult circumstances create unexpected moments to share faith and witness. Example: Jerry Cloutier led someone to Christ while in the hospital facing heart problems.

**Correction:** God disciplines His children like a loving parent to keep them on the right path and to produce "the peaceable fruit of righteousness" (Hebrews 12). Correction is not punishment but redirection toward what the inner person truly desires.

**Testimony:** God comforts us in our troubles so we can minister comfort to others going through similar trials (2 Corinthians 1:3-4). Those who have suffered loss, illness, or hardship are best equipped to help others facing the same challenges.

**Maturity:** God's practical plan is not necessarily health, wealth, or prosperity, but spiritual maturity. Jesus Himself "learned perfection through the things which he suffered" (Hebrews 5:8-9). Trials teach what prosperity cannot; they develop character and conformation to Christ.

**Glory:** Both present and future. Now, the glory of God's spirit rests upon those who suffer faithfully (1 Peter 4:12-14). In eternity, the sufferings of this present time will be revealed as producing "glory which shall be revealed in us" (Romans 8:18).

### God's Goodness in a Fallen World
- The world "lies in wickedness," but God is good and works all things—even evil—toward His purposes
- God uses trials to help us understand goodness, to demonstrate His strength in our weakness, and to refine us like carbon under pressure becoming a diamond
- Every good and perfect gift comes from God (James 1:17); He will ultimately eliminate evil, sorrow, and pain in eternity

### On Celebrating Thanksgiving
- The holiday of Thanksgiving is uniquely suited for Christian celebration—there is no legitimate reason to avoid expressing gratitude to God
- Thanksgiving is connected to being filled with the Holy Spirit; without the Spirit, we cannot fulfill this command
