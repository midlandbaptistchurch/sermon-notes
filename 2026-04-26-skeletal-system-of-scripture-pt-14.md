## Summary
Pastor Jim Payne continues his series on the skeletal system of Scripture by examining Genesis 1 and the six days of creation. He explains how the firmament divides the waters, creating the universe, and how God's creative order demonstrates his character and plan. The sermon emphasizes the distinction between the initial creation (Genesis 1:1) and the six days of creative activity (Genesis 1:3-31), and introduces how God's purpose includes humanity's dominion over the earth.

## Scripture
Genesis 1 — The account of creation over six days, establishing the foundational structure of Scripture.

## Key Themes
- The firmament as a divider of waters, separating the heavenly realm from the earthly realm
- Multiple heavens and the cosmic structure of creation
- God's creative order: each day builds upon the previous, with everything reproducing after its kind
- The rebellion of Lucifer and its effect on creation
- Humanity created in God's image with dominion over the earth
- The contrast between the Luciferian world (Genesis 1:1-2) and the new creation (Genesis 1:3-31)

## Notes

### The Skeletal System of Scripture
The "skeletal system" refers to foundational biblical truths that give structure and support to all of Scripture. Understanding God's will requires knowledge of creation and the overall framework of God's plan.

### Genesis 1:1-2: The Initial Creation and Judgment
- Genesis 1:1 describes God's creation of heaven and earth
- Genesis 1:2 describes the earth becoming "without form and void" — the result of judgment on Lucifer's rebellion
- The waters of "the deep" covered the earth after this judgment
- This differs from the six days of creative activity that follow

### The Firmament (Genesis 1:6-8)
- God creates the firmament (the universe/space) on the second day
- The firmament divides waters above from waters below
- Waters above the firmament exist beyond the visible universe
- The firmament is called "heaven" and is stretched out like a curtain or tent
- God opens part of the firmament to create the atmosphere around Earth, allowing life to exist
- This creates three heavens: the atmosphere, the firmament/universe, and the throne of God above

### The Heavens
- The waters above the firmament form a crystalline or ice-like structure where God's throne resides
- The visible universe reflects back to us; we cannot see beyond it because God has hidden it
- The darkness of night sky is the same darkness that was originally on the face of the deep

### Days Three-Six: Populating Creation
- **Day 3**: Dry land appears, vegetation brought forth (grass, herbs, fruit trees)
- **Day 4**: Lights (sun, moon, stars) placed in the firmament to give light upon the earth
- **Day 5**: Marine creatures and fowl created — the first use of "created" since Genesis 1:1, indicating these are newly formed species
- **Day 6**: Land animals in three categories — cattle (domesticated), beasts (wild), creeping things (reptiles, insects); then mankind

### The Law of Kind
Seven times in Genesis 1, God emphasizes that creatures reproduce "after their kind." This establishes the principle that kind produces kind — an apple tree produces apples, not oranges; species do not evolve into other species.

### Humanity (Genesis 1:26-27)
- Man is created in God's image, making him a moral, free-will agent capable of choosing
- God breathes life into Adam, making him a living soul
- Humanity receives dominion over the earth — the dominion that Lucifer had lost through rebellion
- Man is created with a spirit, soul, and body (a tripartite being, reflecting God's trinitarian nature)
- Both male and female are created to replenish the earth and subdue it

### The Continuous Rebellion
- Lucifer rebelled in the heavenly realm (Genesis 1:1-2)
- After Adam and Eve are created, Satan tempts Eve in the garden (implying rebellion occurs on the sixth day or shortly after)
- The pattern of rebellion appears both in heaven and on earth
- God's purpose includes redemption through Christ to restore what was lost

### Continental Division
- The earth was originally one landmass
- During the days of Peleg (Genesis 10:25), God divided the earth into the continents we know today
- There are twelve natural land divisions: seven continents plus Central America, Indonesian Islands, and other regions
- This division was God's judgment on human rebellion at Babel

### The Gap Theory Framework
The sermon presents the "gap theory" interpretation: a gap exists between Genesis 1:1 (initial creation) and Genesis 1:3 (six days of restoration/reformation), during which Lucifer's rebellion occurred and was judged.
