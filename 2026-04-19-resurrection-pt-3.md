## Summary
In this third installment of the Resurrection series, Bill Baughman examines extra-biblical historical evidence for the resurrection of Jesus Christ, drawing on sources such as Tacitus, Josephus, the Babylonian Talmud, and Lucian of Samosata. He also explores the "liar, lunatic, or Lord" argument by surveying several of Jesus's radical and culturally jarring sayings, arguing that these statements are "organically outrageous" and incompatible with the claim that Jesus was merely a good and wise teacher.

## Scripture
- **Acts 1:3** — The opening anchor verse, cited as evidence that Christ appeared to his disciples by "many infallible proofs" over forty days.
- **Matthew 16:13** — Jesus asks "Whom do men say that I, the Son of Man, am?" — used to frame the contemporary question of Christ's identity.

## Key Themes
- Extra-biblical historical evidence for the resurrection
- Limitations of science regarding supernatural events; the law of authenticity and historical certainty
- The "liar, lunatic, or Lord" trilemma
- The "organically outrageous" nature of Jesus's teachings and their implications for his identity
- The cost of discipleship
- Living out a bold, visible faith today

## Notes
### Review of Previous Sessions
- Previous weeks addressed circular reasoning and scientific objections to the resurrection.
- Science's methodology (natural, observable, repeatable processes) cannot evaluate a one-time supernatural event; therefore science is silent — not negative — on the resurrection.
- The legal "law of authenticity" is offered as an alternative framework: historical certainty can be reached through evidence even for unrepeatable events.

### Extra-Biblical Evidence for Christ
Four non-Christian historical sources were presented as corroborating evidence:
1. **Tacitus** (c. 116 AD) — A Roman historian hostile to Christians who nonetheless records Christ's execution.
2. **Josephus** (c. 93–94 AD) — Jewish historian whose *Antiquities* states that Jesus "appeared to them alive again the third day."
3. **Babylonian Talmud** (2nd–4th centuries AD) — Jewish source that explicitly references Jesus's death, lending credibility through opposition.
4. **Lucian of Samosata** (c. 125–175 AD) — A Greek satirist who mocked Christians but in doing so acknowledged their devotion to Christ as a executed sage.

Key point: hostile or indifferent sources corroborating the core facts strengthen the historical case.

### Book Recommendation
- *Cold-Case Christianity* was recommended as a resource for engaging skeptics with evidence-based apologetics.

### Organically Outrageous — Jesus's Identity
- "Organically outrageous" was defined as something arising naturally from a situation, without artificiality or force, yet producing a shocking result.
- Examples of Jesus's radical sayings discussed:
  - Calling followers to "hate" family in comparison to devotion to him (Luke 14)
  - "I came not to send peace, but a sword" (Matthew 10:34)
  - "Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, ye have no life in you" (John 6)
  - "Let the dead bury the dead" (Luke 9:60)
  - Calling a Gentile woman a "dog" before praising her great faith (Matthew 15)
  - "Cut off the hand that offends thee" (Matthew 5:30)
- These statements are presented as incompatible with the label of merely a "good and wise teacher" — Jesus must be a liar, a lunatic, or the Lord.

### Application
- Believers are challenged to be "organically outrageous" in their own witness — visibly different, bold in prayer, and unashamed in daily life.
