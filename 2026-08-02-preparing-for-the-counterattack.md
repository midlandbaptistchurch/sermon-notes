## Summary
Following a youth camp week of gospel preaching and spiritual decisions, Youth Pastor Andrew Watt challenges parents and the congregation to prepare for the inevitable spiritual counterattack that follows revival. Drawing from Joshua 24, he calls the church to tear down modern idols — particularly social media, screens, materialism, and vanity — so that teenagers can fully surrender to God without being hindered by the example or habits of those at home.

## Scripture
Joshua 24:14-15 — Joshua's charge to the nation of Israel after their conquest of Canaan, calling them to choose whom they will serve; the passage frames the sermon's central challenge to choose God completely over all competing loyalties.

Additional scriptures referenced:
- Exodus 20:3 — "Thou shalt have no other gods before me."
- Matthew 6:24 — "No man can serve two masters."
- Matthew 16:24 — "Let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me."
- 2 Corinthians 6:14-17 — "Be not unequally yoked... come out from among them and be ye separate."

## Key Themes
- The spiritual counterattack that follows genuine revival and camp decisions
- The lie in contemporary Christianity that one can serve both God and the world
- Parents as the primary spiritual pattern for their children
- Social media and smartphones as a dangerous, addiction-forming idol for young people
- Materialism and consumerism as gods of American culture
- Vanity and pride as sins to be called out regardless of cultural or political affiliation
- The call for fathers to lead family devotions, prayer, and Bible memorization
- Complete surrender to God rather than partial commitment

## Notes
**The Context of Joshua's Charge**
- Joshua 24 is not a pre-battle speech — it is given *after* great victory: crossing the Jordan, the fall of Jericho, the sun and moon standing still, and the full conquest of Canaan.
- Following received promises and completed victories, Joshua still calls Israel to choose whom they will serve — a pattern directly applicable to teens returning from a victorious week at camp.

**The Counterattack Is Coming**
- Spiritual enemies do not stand idly by after ground is taken. Just as Israel's post-conquest history in Judges shows the pull of foreign gods, teens who made decisions at camp will face immediate pressure upon returning home.
- The church wrestles not against flesh and blood but against principalities and powers (Ephesians 6).

**The Central Lie**
- The most widely believed lie in today's Christianity is not that God is unreal or unkind — it is that a person can serve both the God of the Bible and the god of this world simultaneously.
- Scriptures from Exodus, Matthew, and 2 Corinthians collectively refute this: God demands complete allegiance, not partial devotion.
- The pendulum swing from legalism to lawlessness has left many Christians asking "how much can I keep?" rather than "what must I lay down?"

**The Gods of Their Fathers (Parental Responsibility)**
- Israel was asked to choose between the God of the Bible and the gods *their fathers* had served — a pointed challenge for parents today.
- Are teens being asked to choose between a parent's ungodly habits and the God they heard preached at camp?
- Specific areas of concern: inappropriate media left on screens, worldly music playing in the family car, content parents ask children to leave the room for.
- A challenge to parents: if you handed your teenager your exact walk with God, would Jesus approve of what they received?

**Social Media and Smartphones as Modern Idols**
- The speaker describes smartphones as "a nuclear bomb in the hand of a young person."
- References *The Shallows* (Nicholas Carr) on how internet use rewires the brain, *The Coddling of the American Mind* on a generation that fails to launch, and *The Anxious Generation* (Jonathan Haidt) on how phone-based childhood has replaced play-based childhood, creating epidemic anxiety and depression.
- Two stories from camp involved 12- and 13-year-olds who had taken their own lives — attributed in part to unchecked social media exposure.
- The author of *The Anxious Generation* recommends no social media before age 16; the current legal minimum of 13 has no meaningful enforcement.
- The reason restrictions are not implemented faster: adults enjoy the devices themselves.

**The Gods of the World Around Them**
- Materialism: American culture constantly promotes "more" — consumerism, hustle culture, and the habit of impulse purchasing train young people to satisfy flesh rather than deny it.
- Contentment is the biblical counter: "godliness with contentment is great gain" (1 Timothy 6:6).
- Vanity and pride are identified as prominent gods of current culture, including in political life — the speaker calls Christians to distinguish between national identity and biblical values.
- Busyness itself is identified as a cultural god that crowds out family devotions and prayer.

**Practical Charge to Parents**
- Fathers should initiate and lead family devotions, not wait for children to develop the habit independently.
- Learn to say "no" to activities, schedules, and media that displace time in the Word.
- Be willing to remove or restrict screens in the home for the spiritual benefit of children.
- Pray with and for the young people in the church, especially in the days following camp.

**Closing Personal Testimony**
- The speaker shared that while traveling for work, his wife called to report that their four-year-old daughter Aurora had come to saving faith after a series of gospel conversations connected to VBS classes and questions prompted by the Lord's Supper.
- He used this as a final appeal: parents should want their children to choose *their* God — and that God must be the God of the Bible.

**Call to Action**
- Three youth from the camp week surrendered to ministry or Bible college — the speaker asked the congregation to consider what full surrender across the whole youth group could accomplish.
- Encouragement to pull aside returning teens and pray with them individually before the counterattack strikes.
