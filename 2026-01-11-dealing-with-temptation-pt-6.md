## Summary
Hugh Taylor continues the series on temptation by exploring 1 Corinthians 10:13 as "a tale of three buts." He examines the three meanings of temptation in Scripture—trial, provocation, and enticement to sin—and emphasizes that while temptation is common to all people, God's faithfulness ensures we are never tempted beyond our ability to resist. Taylor reflects on Christ's incarnation as God getting into the boat with us and living in obedience to the Father.

## Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:13 — "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it."

## Key Themes
- The three meanings of temptation: trial/test, provocation, and enticement to sin
- Temptation as common to all humanity throughout history
- God's faithfulness as unchanging in His attributes
- Christ's incarnation and obedience to the Father
- Living consistently with our faith at home, church, and in the world
- The struggle with sin that "easily besets us"
- God's fellowship calling through Christ is never diminished

## Notes

### The Three Meanings of Temptation
- Every sin has a precursor of temptation
- All temptation is a departure from sound doctrine
- The three definitions: (1) trial or test of whether we adhere to Scripture, (2) provocation or tempting of God through disobedience, (3) enticement to sin or evil
- Most believers consciously deal with the third definition, but often overlook the first two

### Living Consistently in Faith
- Taylor emphasizes the importance of consistency—not just talking the talk at church but walking it at home and in the community
- The challenge is maintaining this consistency when distracted by worldly affairs
- Personal example: we may speak differently to family than to others, yet should maintain consistent behavior
- Temptation seeks to derail us from our consistency and entangle us in the affairs of this world

### God's Faithfulness
- God is faithful and does not change in His attributes, though He may change His actions
- His faithfulness means He stays true to His character: He is "the same yesterday, today, and forever"
- James 1:17 speaks of no "variableness" or "shadow of turning" in God
- 1 Corinthians 1:9 affirms that God's calling into fellowship with Christ is never diminished or thwarted
- Our sins are all forgiven, which should liberate us and energize us to serve Him faithfully

### Christ's Incarnation and Obedience
- Christ literally "got into the boat with us" through His incarnation
- He came in obedience to the Father, subjecting Himself to His earthly parents
- Hebrews 5:8 says He "learned obedience by the things which he suffered"
- John 6:38 and John 8:29 emphasize His perfect obedience: "I do always those things that please him"
- His perfection was not a liability to His siblings but an asset—He encouraged them and reminded them of God's love

### Personal Reflection
- Taylor shares memories of family gatherings and how his children viewed their older brother as a model
- He reflects on Christ's special love for children and how He welcomed them without scrutiny
- Christ's tenderness and blessing to those around Him made His perfection an encouragement rather than a burden

### Closing Exhortation
- All believers struggle with "the sin that so easily besets us," though it may take different forms
- The good news is that no temptation is beyond our ability to resist because God is faithful
- We must integrate this truth into the very fabric of our being and minds
- This understanding should help us make "full proof of the ministry" God has called us to
